# The penultimate minimum at the width-506 restart

The inequality $\Pr(B)<0.009$ remains open. Here $B$ requires both an old witness plane and a penultimate minimum $1<t\le\sqrt{(0.954h)^2+1}$. Two partial results locate the gap.

* The last-used-coordinate count reaches $0.009$ at physical radius $1.19806583$ and one at $1.20927112$, even with a sharper determinant recurrence. The requested radius is greater than $30.5692$ on every history. More than 96.29% of the computed majorant comes from a latest used column that supplies the first direction of a long flag.
* At the requested radius, three events involving the **406th suffix selection** together cost less than $2.400\cdot10^{-9}$. Two involve a fresh coordinate after a horizontal selection. The third is a short vertical winner. The outstanding terms are an inherited plane on a horizontal selection and a short quotient after a vertical selection in an explicit length interval. The bounds here do not identify which has greater probability.

## Law and scales

## 1. Where the last-used-coordinate count exhausts its budget

For $g_l$ take the Hermite caps of Part 147, including $g_{24}=4$. Define
$$
 B_l=\sqrt{g_l}\prod_{m=2}^lg_m^{1/(2(m-1))},\qquad
 P_k=\sum_{l=1}^k\log B_l,\qquad P_0=0.
$$
For a fixed physical cutoff $u\ge1$ with $u\max_{l\le T+1}B_l<q$, the following is an upper bound on $\Pr(t\le u)$. Include
$$
 E_0(u)=V_d(u B_{T+1}+\sqrt d/2)^d/(2Q).
$$
For $1\le i\le T$, $k=T-i+2$, $0\le j<k$, set $p=d-j$, $w=B_{k-j}$, $z=\max(1,w\sqrt{2\pi/(p+1)})$, and $\Theta(y)=1+2e^{-\pi y^2}/(1-e^{-3\pi y^2})$. Add the terms
$$
 E_{ij}(u)=
 \frac{u^{b}}{2}\exp\!\left(\frac{\pi w^2}{z^2}-L_{i-1}+P_k-P_{k-j}\right)
 z^{p+1}\Theta(uz)^p.
 \tag{3}
$$
Write $S(u)=E_0(u)+\sum E_{ij}(u)$.

**Proof.** Append $x$ to the list of $T$ winners. If $i$ is the last suffix coordinate used by this flag, its final $k$ vectors lift to the window present at $i$. Later coordinate axes are perpendicular to every preceding removal that has zero in those coordinates. Consequently a lift of a flag vector with zero later coordinates also has zero raw coefficients there. In each length-$l$ tail, the first vector is shortest in the sublattice generated by lifts of that tail. Hermite's inequality bounds its length by $\sqrt{g_l}$ times the geometric mean of the $l$ Gram–Schmidt lengths. The log mean exceeds the log mean of the remaining $l-1$ by at most $\log(g_l)/(2(l-1))$. Starting at $t\le u$ bounds the first length of each tail by $u B_l$.

If exactly $j$ initial directions of that flag omit coordinate $i$, their signed selections agree with successive shortest projections in the old lattice alone. They can therefore be computed before revealing column $i$. Reject the history if any exceeds its cap. The remaining old determinant is at least $e^{L_{i-1}-P_k+P_{k-j}}u^{-j}$. The first vector to use $i$ has length at most $uw$. Each possible positive height is less than $q$ and permutes the projected mark. For the common Gaussian estimate used here and below, a projected old lattice $\Gamma$ of rank $p$ has mark extension $K=P_F\mathbb Z^N$ with $\det K=\det\Gamma/|H_F|$. At a height prime to $q$, averaging the Gaussian over the uniform marked shift gives $\rho_y(K)/|H_F|$, where $\rho_y$ has weight $e^{-\pi\|v\|^2/y^2}$. Poisson summation bounds this by $y^p\Theta(y)^p/\det\Gamma$. Indeed $K^*=F\cap\mathbb Z^N$ injects into $p$ integer coordinates and coordinate restriction only shortens vectors. Summing the Gaussian weights of positive integer heights costs at most $y/2$ by integration. Majorize the ball indicator by the Gaussian times its inverse boundary weight, and take $y=uz$. This proves (3), including $u^{j+p+1}=u^b$. No conditioning on a later column or on which column is last used enters the expectation. If the flag uses no suffix coordinate, the initial graph has a vector of length at most $uB_{T+1}<q$. Each possible sign pair has nonzero input residue if it belongs, and then costs $1/Q$. Enclosing disjoint unit cubes gives $E_0(u)$. [Last-used-coordinate argument and audit](wall1_uncovered_lines.md) · [Finite mark law](wall1_first_tour.md) · [Gaussian Poisson formula](https://cims.nyu.edu/~regev/teaching/lattices_fall_2004/ln/transference.pdf)

**Numerical enclosure.** Let $S_-$ be the sum of (3) at one after replacing every $\Theta$ by one and omitting $E_0$. Since $\Theta$ decreases and $E_0(u)\le u^d E_0(1)$,
$$
 u^{506} S_-\ \le\ S(u)\ \le\ u^{506}S(1).
 \tag{4}
$$
Both $\log_2 S_-$ and $\log_2 S(1)$ belong to $(-138.713695261,-138.713695260)$. The crossing of the two envelopes brackets the transition of the evaluated bound as follows.

| Level | Below the level through radius | Above the level from radius, within the cap range |
|---|---:|---:|
| $0.009$ | $1.19806583315$ | $1.19806583317$ |
| $1$ | $1.20927112242$ | $1.20927112245$ |

The largest term of $S(1)$ has $(i,j)=(218,0)$, $k=190$, $B_{190}\in(144.78324,144.78325)$, and base-two log in $(-142.55570,-142.55569)$. The second-largest log is below $-142.57507$. Replacing the largest term's theta factor by one leaves it above that second bound, so the same term dominates throughout the scaled range. The theta-free subtotal for $175\le i\le260$, $j=0$, exceeds $0.9629649\,S(1)$. The count loses control when the freshly revealed column can supply the **first** direction and the other directions of a long flag can already lift to that window. The history probabilities of these configurations are unknown. Even at physical radius ten, the single $(218,0)$ calculation has $10B_{190}<q$ and exceeds $2^{1538}$; other terms need not remain valid there. At the requested radius even this term's untruncated length cap exceeds $q$. Continuing (3) across multiples of $q$ would incorrectly permute a zero-height residue. The budget is exhausted well before that issue. [Unit-threshold audit](wall1_uncovered_lines.md) · [Earlier scaling to $2/\sqrt3$](wall1_long_lines.md)

## 2. What the final independent column can pay for

Let $S$ now denote the history immediately **before selection 406**, let $M$ be its rank-$d$ retained lattice, and put $D_-=\det M$. Write $v$ for the next signed winner, $s=\|v\|$, and $k$ for its unsigned height in the new coordinate. Define two disjoint residual events, both subsets of $B$.

* $H_{\rm old}$ requires $k=0$, that $x$ in the resulting $L$ has zero new coordinate, and that some witness in $\pi_{x^\perp}L$ also has zero new coordinate. Thus the winner, $x$, and a witness lift give three directions from $M$.
* $V_{\rm mid}$ requires $k>0$ and $0.954<s/(G_bD_-^{1/b})<1.097193$.

Then
$$
 0\le\Pr(B)-\Pr(H_{\rm old})-\Pr(V_{\rm mid})
 <2.400\cdot10^{-9}.
 \tag{5}
$$
In particular, $\Pr(H_{\rm old})+\Pr(V_{\rm mid})\le0.0089999976$ would suffice. The presence of $B$ in both definitions retains its joint old-plane and length tests.

**Horizontal selection.** Force the signed old shortest vector of $M$ before reading the column. Its projection leaves an old lattice of rank $d-1$ and determinant $D=D_-/s$. The actual quotient on a horizontal win appends the projected uniform mark at height one. Thus $D,h,r(h)$ can be tested before the draw. A hypothetical forced $D$ outside (2) contributes nothing on an actual horizontal win. Put $J_n=e^{n/2}(2\pi/n)^{n/2}/V_n$. If $x$ uses the new coordinate and $B$ occurs, there is a positive-height vector of length at most $r(h)<q$. Dropping the actual winner tests, the count in the proof of (3), at $z=r(h)\sqrt{2\pi/d}$, bounds its joint probability by
$$
 \frac{J_d}{2}(r(h)/h)^d\Theta(z)^{d-1}
 <1.226519\cdot10^{-9}.
 \tag{6}
$$
Here $r(h)/h$ decreases and $z$ increases with $h$, so (2) permits evaluation at $H_*$.

If $x$ is horizontal, it equals the signed shortest vector of that fixed rank-$(d-1)$ old lattice. Force this second projection **before** the same draw. The old part now has rank $d-2$ and determinant $D/t$. Whenever $B$ occurs, $1<t\le q$. The upper bound follows also by projecting the raw $q$-axes, which span the retained space. A new-coordinate witness would have length below $R=ch\,t^{-1/d}<q$. Use $n=d-1$, $R_*=cH_*q^{-1/d}$ and $z_*=R_*\sqrt{2\pi/n}$. The same count with the scale $R\sqrt{2\pi/n}$ is at most
$$
 \frac{e^{n/2}(2\pi/n)^{n/2}(cG_d)^d}{2R_*}\Theta(z_*)^{d-2}
 <2.789500\cdot10^{-10}.
 \tag{7}
$$
Indeed $R^d=(cG_d)^dD/t$ and $R\ge R_*$. If these two fresh incidences are absent, a horizontal win in $B$ belongs to $H_{\rm old}$. Both counts concern joint probabilities given $S$. Conditioning first on winning would bias the mark. [Exact one-step geometry, Theorem 4(b)](wall1_lyapunov.md) · [Projected mark counts](wall1_uncovered_lines.md)

**Vertical selection.** With $u=cG_bD_-^{1/b}$ and $z=u\sqrt{2\pi/b}$, the chance of a positive-height winner of length $s\le u$ is bounded, uniformly in $S$, by $J_b c^b\Theta(z)^d/2<8.938011\cdot10^{-10}$. This is the same fresh count in the full window. Equation (1) at step 405 gives $z>3.42155$ and $D_-\le Q$ gives $u<3185<q$. For every shortest $v$, rounding a lift of $x$ along $v$ gives a nonzero vector of squared length at most $t^2+s^2/4$ in the window. Thus $t\ge\sqrt3\,s/2$. On $B$, $t\le c_*G_d(D_-/s)^{1/d}$ for $c_*=\sqrt{c^2+H_*^{-2}}$. Therefore
$$
 \frac{s}{G_bD_-^{1/b}}\le\frac{(2c_*G_d/\sqrt3)^{d/b}}{G_b}<1.097193.
$$
The three bounds sum to less than $2.400\cdot10^{-9}$, proving (5). In the remaining vertical interval the exact output is the image of a cyclic extension of $M$ under the contraction determined by $v$. Its short-vector count depends jointly on old directions and the selected $v$. ## 3. A bound on the whole event from reachable histories

Let $p_j=Q^{-511}\binom{511}{j}(Q-1)^j$. An unconditional two-sided bound obtained here is
$$
 \frac{p_{105}}{(Q-1)^2}\ \le\ \Pr(B)\ \le\
 1-\sum_{j=0}^{104}p_j-(1-\eta)p_{105},\qquad
 \eta=\frac{V_d(r(h_0)+\sqrt d/2)^d}{2(Q-1)}<1.3630\cdot10^{-10}.
 \tag{8}
$$
The lower endpoint exceeds $2^{-1909212.581}$ and the upper endpoint is less than $1-2^{-1899851.882}$. Neither endpoint decides a percent-level probability.

**Proof.** Condition on $j\le105$ nonzero columns among the 511. Every appended coordinate window contains 106 active graph columns before its selection and therefore has a zero column. Its shortest vectors are precisely the signed unit axes of zero columns. The signed tie deletes one such coordinate, leaving a graph in the ordinary coordinate metric. Inductively all nonzero columns survive. For $j\le104$ an old unit remains and $B$ fails. For $j=105$ the remaining columns are independent uniform nonzero vectors conditional on their locations, and the determinant is $Q$. A nonzero integer vector of length at most $r(h_0)<q$ that belongs must have nonzero graph-input residue. Fix all columns except one having a nonzero input coefficient. At most one of its $Q-1$ values realizes the vector. The enclosing-cube sign-pair count gives $\eta$, proving the upper bound.

For the lower bound, let the first three surviving columns coincide. This has conditional probability $(Q-1)^{-2}$. Their input-axis differences give two independent vectors of length $\sqrt2$. There are no units, so $t=\sqrt2$. At least one difference projects nontrivially off the designated $x$, with length at most $\sqrt2<R(t)$. Also $t<r(h_0)$. This works with every tie and with the other columns unrestricted. Part 149's separate family attains $t/h\in(0.934297,0.934298)$ much closer to the requested cutoff. Both constructions have negligible mass at this scale. [Zero-column deletion and audit](wall1_horizontal_core.md) · [Part 149 family](wall1_long_lines.md)

## 4. Dual and Haar comparisons

Part 142 Theorem C, used at time 406 alone, gives $\lambda_1(L^*)\ge R_0(406)>0.2009091560$ outside probability $10^{-3}/408$. Indeed its raw-graph first moment uses $N=911$ and 511 independent columns, and the selected dual is contained in the raw dual. The rank-505 Hermite cap gives $\lambda_1(L^*)\le \sqrt{g_{505}}D^{-1/d}$ with $\log\sqrt{g_{505}}\le1016.893214923/505$. Hence $h<204.219$ on this event. This can localize both residuals of (5) with a single additive cost. A lower bound for the first dual minimum alone gives no bound on the number of short primal flags. Part 101's ambient dual count likewise does not weight those flags by selection. [Dual floor and audit](wall1_dual_cusp.md) · [Hermite caps](wall1_hermite_caps.md) · [Mixed modular count](wall1_qary_rogers.md)

For comparison one can compute the **joint** Haar first moment for the actual two cutoffs. Fix $D$, and give $D^{-1/d}L$ Haar law. Count primitive unoriented marks $v$ with $\|v\|\le r(h)$, each weighted by the number of primitive quotient sign pairs of length below $R(\|v\|)$. Denote this flag count by $N$. On $B$ the mark $x$ supplies at least one pair. Primitive unfolding has marginal $dv/(2\zeta(d)D)$ and gives a Haar quotient of determinant $D/\|v\|$ for the primitive incidence measure. Applying primitive Siegel there, and integrating radially, gives exactly
$$
 \mathbb E_{\rm Haar}N=
 \frac{V_dr(h)^d}{2\zeta(d)D}
 \frac{V_{d-1}(cG_d)^{d-1}}{2\zeta(d-1)}
 \left(\frac{r(h)}{D}\right)^{1/d}
 \frac{d}{d+1/d}.
$$
Indeed the inner mean is proportional to $\|v\|^{1/d}$, whose average in the ball gives the last factor. For $h\ge H_*$ both $r(h)/h$ and $r(h)/D$ decrease. Dropping both zeta denominators at $H_*$ bounds the mean by $2.138\cdot10^{-22}$. This is a benchmark for a single observable. The finite restart has no proved comparison for its expectation. Part 105 unfolds primitive marks on a Haar parent, Part 107 treats Haar minima, and Part 136 Theorem 8 certifies a rule which may try several marks on a Haar window. None gives the corresponding mean for 406 selected projections. The missing estimate concerns which old directions survive selection on those histories. Even a proof of this conjecture alone would leave the charge on $B^c$ in Part 149.

## Audit

**Verdict — SOUND.** All six requested checks pass for Part 117’s unconditioned restart and Part 108’s signed selector. No numerical correction is needed. The inequality $\Pr(B)<0.009$ remains open.

**Determinant and radius.** The new axis gives $s_i\le q$ and the rank-506 Hermite cap gives $s_i\le CD_{i-1}^{1/506}$. Both resulting lower bounds for $D_i$ increase with $D_{i-1}$, so the maximum in (1) can be iterated. The integral exterior product of the frozen lifts has norm $\prod_i s_i\ge1$, giving $D_i\le Q$. Independent 200-bit outward-rounded arithmetic gave $G_d e^{L_{406}/d}\in(32.02610188,32.02610189)$ and $r(h_0)\in(3221.6434060,3221.6434062)$. **First-moment crossings and decomposition.** A scratch computation formed the ball volumes by the two-step recurrence and summed all 83,027 pairs in (3), keeping separate totals with and without the theta factors.
