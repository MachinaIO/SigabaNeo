# Greedy selection on random inputs and the first BKZ pass

**Research judgment.** The closest rigorous precedent for the desired *output law* is Vallée–Vera’s two-dimensional Gauss reduction. The closest precedent for handling *new randomness* is deferred exposure in random SAT. Together they suggest counting the input mass of a prescribed sequence of winners, with competitor exclusions imposed on that same input. Neither supplies the missing estimate for an old lattice selected by BKZ. The first test worth doing is a two-selection calculation retaining the rank-three incidence of Part 126. It can expose exactly where an attempted probability bound loses the winner information.

## The observable and its probability budget

In Part 108 the unread graph column is independent of the past. After passing to the retained torus its displacement is uniform on the **current finite image** $H$. The frame, lattice $L$, and subgroup have already been selected. The window is generated by $L$ at height zero and one lift $(U,1)$. Select its shortest line with the prescribed tie rule and project; the next subgroup is the projected image of $H$. Thus one may defer the column until use. One cannot defer the emptiness of the balls that certified earlier winners and then treat the retained lattice as an unconditional draw. [First-pass law](wall1_first_tour.md)

At width 506 the reduced experiment starts from a uniform $400\times105$ graph and has 407 remaining draws. Suffix failure at most 0.00977696 suffices. In the final window, a short quotient line is equivalent to a saturated plane *through the winner* of length $s$, of area below $sR$, where $R$ is the target quotient length. Part 120 splits failure into a horizontal hit and residual positive-height witnesses. The first conditional probability is $I_0(L)w_0(L,H)$: the designated old shortest line has a bad old plane and survives the fresh draw. The second is bounded by a count on the actual displacement fibers. A sufficient equal allocation is 0.00488848 for each history average. The physical threshold $R\approx27.4538$ is weaker than requiring 0.954 times the terminal lattice’s own GH radius. [Restart](wall1_idea_round2.md) · [Plane formula](wall1_plane_count.md) · [Physical cutoff](wall1_band_moments.md)

This distinction matters for interpreting Part 123. In its proportional-width experiments at $q=3329$, there were zero physical-floor failures at the tested sizes from 20 through 36, with just 48 runs at 36. The 0.954 ratio failed 7/512 times at 30 and 3/48 at 36. The physical floor is a necessary height-zero condition. These observations neither certify the width-506 tail nor justify concentrating the proof on sub-GH winners. [Experiments](wall1_experiments2.md) · [Winner gate](wall1_annealed_selection.md)

## What the Gauss and LLL analyses actually transfer

**Gauss reduction and continued fractions.** Vallée and Vera analyze execution parameters and the geometry of the returned reduced basis for specified two-dimensional input distributions. After quotienting out similarity, a complex shape parameter encodes the pair. Integer translations and inversion give explicit fractional-linear branches, analogous to continued-fraction digits. Their Theorem 1 expresses the output density on the reduction domain as a sum over inverse branches, each weighted by the input density and area Jacobian. The branch domain enforces the algorithm’s decisions. This is precisely the feature needed when many different inputs reach one output. It depends on explicit inverse branches and control of their weighted sum. The BKZ branch also has a known weight, but it is a finite fiber count, depends on the old frame and torsion subgroup, and can carry an entire nested flag of incidences. The cubic start has an atomic law. A transfer operator on Haar densities would lose its initial condition (Part 111). [Vallée–Vera, paper and Theorem 1](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/3549/pdf) · [Hecke obstruction](wall1_hecke_gap.md)

**Higher-rank LLL.** Daudé and Vallée bound the average number of LLL iterations for random input bases. The proof bounds the iteration count deterministically in terms of input geometry and estimates the lower tail of the smallest input Gram–Schmidt length under the random-ball model. It does not need the distribution of every swap-selected intermediate basis. The analogous shortcut here would be a deterministic implication from terminal failure to a *rare input event*. A prefix determinant alone cannot provide it: Part 116 constructs bad admissible prefixes for every input under the available cap. A candidate certificate must include facts distinguishing the prefix actually taken. [Daudé–Vallée, 1994](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(94)90071-X) · [Prefix obstruction](wall1_flag_prefix.md)

Two nearby LLL results answer different questions. Akhavi–Marckert–Rouault prove limits for the probability that a sampled basis already satisfies a reduction condition, using spherical input and the distribution of Gram–Schmidt ratios. Nguyen–Stehlé investigate typical LLL behavior experimentally. Neither result gives a distribution of quotients after a chain of greedy SVP projections. In particular, matching ratios in small dimensions would test a model without establishing its conditional law. [Akhavi–Marckert–Rouault](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0604331) · [Nguyen–Stehlé, corrected paper](https://perso.ens-lyon.fr/damien.stehle/downloads/average-corrected.pdf)

## Deferred decisions, exploration and revealment

**Where the SAT argument closes.** In Achlioptas’s analysis of random 3-SAT heuristics, uniform randomness of the residual clauses conditional on their size counts permits the next update to be generated on demand. The exposure and choice rule preserve the symmetry of unused literal positions. This gives computable conditional drifts even though the variable assignment is adaptive. For BKZ the analogous symmetry holds for *unread columns*. Within a column, all height layers use multiples of the same $U$. Across columns, observing the winner imposes comparisons against all competitors in that window. Even a horizontal win discloses a void in the positive-height layers. The exact transition can be sampled on demand, but Part 121 gives old states with equal determinant, subgroup and two minimum statistics that have different next quotient statistics. Uniform generation of a transition does not make those statistics a sufficient state. [Achlioptas, random 3-SAT via differential equations](https://web.stevens.edu/algebraic/Files/SAT/SATlbdiff.pdf) · [Counterexample](wall1_global_quotient.md)

**A two-step test combining the two methods (proposal, unverified).** Work first at enumerable ranks on the exact graph ensemble, with a fixed physical cutoff. Compare the following upper bounds with the *exact* two-step horizontal hit on identical inputs.

1. Trace a final horizontal witness back through the preceding winner. Keep its lifted saturated triple, its nested lines/planes, both winners, and the two actual length-dependent area cutoffs. Sum over disjoint winner branches with their finite input weights. Within a branch test existence, or explicitly count witnesses for an upper bound. This is the discrete counterpart of retaining the Gauss branch domain. Part 126 already gives the exact recursion; the question here is how cheaply one can certify exclusion on its branches.
2. For a proposed winner, exhibit only a subset of competitors capable of defeating it. For a horizontal candidate of length $t$, for example, a height $k>0$ defeats it strictly when $k<t$ and the distance of $kU$ to zero in the old torus is less than $\sqrt{t^2-k^2}$. Start with $k=1$, then include other feasible heights. For a marked positive-height witness impose its required congruence $jU=y+L$ first. Count the **union** of defeating events on that fiber; the complement is an upper bound on survival. Omitting equality competitors is safe for an upper bound; exact branch counts require the tie rule. A nonempty fiber has size $|H[j]|$. In particular, for exponent $q$ prime and $q\nmid j$, it is a singleton. In that common case the gain must come from discarding incompatible marks or earlier inputs; there is no independent randomness left in this draw.
3. Partition preceding inputs by the proposed first winner and compute the second exclusion on the corresponding child. Compare the exact branch mass, the bound using selected competitors, and the bound after forgetting the triple incidence. Report their **unconditional probability contributions**, including branches with tiny mass. A candidate family merits extension only if it removes the excess on the branches that dominate the weighted error. Neither a high overall rejection fraction nor agreement on typical states controls that error.

This test addresses the first omitted incidence in Part 126, where dropping the rank-three mark yields a Haar mean over $10^{45}$ times one budget. A successful two-step calculation would still need a summable error estimate through the suffix under the actual history law. The depth-103 reachable history in Part 122 also precludes a deterministic short certificate based only on truncating horizontal ancestry. [Depth obstruction](wall1_depth_truncation.md) · [Marked recursion and loss](wall1_band_moments.md)

**Decision-tree revealment.** O’Donnell–Saks–Schramm–Servedio bound the variance of a Boolean function under a product input by a sum of coordinate influences weighted by query probabilities (in the paper’s normalization). The original graph columns are product coordinates. The forward algorithm reads each suffix column, so that decision tree offers no small column revealment. A backward witness search might certify some failures early, but a decision algorithm must also certify the complementary event. Even a variance estimate alone does not locate the mean on the small-probability side. Low priority unless a different exact decision tree and quantitative influence estimates can be exhibited. [OSSS](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~rocco/Public/osss-focs_z.pdf)

## Other adjacent methods

| Method and rigorous result | Hypothesis that pays for adaptivity | Assessment for the first pass |
|---|---|---|
| Wormald’s differential equations track scaled random-process observables with high probability over a controlled time interval. [Wormald, 1995](https://users.monash.edu/~nwormald/) | Conditional mean increments approximate a Lipschitz trend in the tracked variables, with bounded or otherwise controlled jumps up to a stopping time. | The exact determinant update still depends on the selected length. Band counts can have enormous individual contributions. A stopped process would need bounds on both exceptional-history mass and conditional drift of a winner-marked observable. Fitting drift curves to Part 123 supplies neither. |
| Benaïm’s stochastic approximation relates asymptotic limit sets of noisy recursions to invariant sets of an ODE. [Benaïm](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0363012993253534) | Suitable shrinking gains, noise control and precompactness allow interpolated trajectories to follow the flow. | One projection can change the identity of the minimum outright. No shrinking-gain scaling or closed drift for marked incidences has been established. Even asymptotic tracking would need finite-horizon tail constants here. |
| Random sequential adsorption admits laws of large numbers and central limit theorems for accepted packing counts. [Penrose–Yukich](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/limit-theory-for-random-sequential-packing-and-deposition/) | With independent spatial arrivals, local acceptance can be resolved by exploring earlier interacting arrivals within a random stabilizing neighborhood. | A change in an early winner changes the projection of every later candidate. A BKZ analogue would bound the *probability mass of unresolved witness dependencies* after backward exploration. Part 122 bounds one kind of chain but leaves even the depth-zero old-plane count uncontrolled. A chain-length tail alone cannot suffice. |
| Walks on finite Grassmann posets have spectral descriptions for up/down transitions along incident subspaces. [Grassmann walks and spectral decay](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol245-approx-random2022/LIPIcs.APPROX-RANDOM.2022.16/LIPIcs.APPROX-RANDOM.2022.16.pdf) | Uniform choices among incident subspaces yield symmetric transition operators relative to the reference measure. | The Euclidean shortest line in a framed lattice is selected by its length, not uniformly among incident lines. A finite-field flag also omits Euclidean lengths. Even a gap for an append correspondence gives no estimate for the selected projection from a singular start (Part 111). One would first need a comparison for the actual marked observable. |
| Random-lattice sieving. Doulgerakis–Laarhoven report a modified sieve solving SVP in time $2^{0.2924\ldots n+o(n)}$ with high probability over Haar lattice and algorithmic randomness. [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37482) | Their modifications maintain independent uniform shell samples conditional on the lattice. **Scope checked at abstract level; proof not audited here.** | The BKZ reducer has fresh uniform columns but must keep the exact winning projection. Resampling shell points changes that kernel. An auxiliary randomized competitor set could certify that a proposed mark loses when it finds a shorter vector; its miss probability under the marked input law is the required new estimate. |
| Södergren proves a Poisson limit of volume-scaled short-vector lengths (one per sign pair, intensity $1/2$) for Haar unimodular lattices as dimension tends to infinity. Strömbergsson gives finite-dimensional avoidance bounds, including disintegration conditioned on containing a specified point. [Södergren](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.3623) · [Strömbergsson](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/pdr021) | Haar averaging permits integration over lattices with a prescribed point. Prescribing a point is a different conditioning from prescribing it **and** excluding all earlier competitors. | These are reference laws for one marked vector and a void. Part 105 already exploits that distinction for one Haar shortest selection. Weak convergence of unconditioned counts gives no relative error after conditioning on rare transcripts; a transfer would need a joint finite-ensemble bound for incidence and void, with the accumulated error charged in probability units. [One-step law](wall1_selection_law.md) |

**Decision.** Prioritize exact branch mass and competitor exclusion on two successive selections. Use the physical cutoff first because it tests the necessary condition directly and retains the joint effect of length and determinant when compared against the relative cutoff. The unresolved quantity is the weighted mass of bad old flags that survive those exclusions. No result checked here bounds it over the width-506 suffix. [Parts 105–126](../PROOF_GAP.md)
