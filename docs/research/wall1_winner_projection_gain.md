# Actual winner, radius, and projection in a solvable rectangular subclass

This stage proves actual power drift in a rank-one-marked rectangular family
at the corridor scale. It does not decide drift for the previous 400-marked-axis
obstruction or for reachable algorithmic histories. The two families have the
same ambient dimension and determinant convention, but different mark ranks.
The distinction is essential: the present calculation works because the
nonhorizontal winner reduces the marked problem to a one-dimensional quotient.

## A deterministic sufficient condition

Let M=ell Z direct-sum c Z^(n-1), K=(ell/q) Z direct-sum c Z^(n-1), q prime.
For an input mark u, the graph lattice splits orthogonally into the planar
lattice generated by (ell,0), (ell*u/q,1), and the unmarked c-axes. Suppose
every shortest winner lies in that plane and has nonzero height j, with
1<=s<=S and j not divisible by q. Shortest vectors are primitive, including
ties. Orthogonal projection of the planar graph lattice off a winner has
spacing ell/s, while the image of a marked vector (ell*v/q,0) has signed
coordinate -j*ell*v/(q*s). Thus its exact quotient distance is

    d_u(v)=ell*|jv|_q/(q*s).

The unmarked axes do not affect these nearest distances. Since multiplication
by j permutes residues modulo q, the outgoing void at radius B(s) is exactly

    p'_u(B(s)) = max(0,q-1-2 floor(q*s*B(s)/ell))/q,
    B(s)=sqrt(L^2*s^(-2/506)-1),   L=1.10g.

The formula uses the actual winner-dependent radius, not a conservative cap
radius. A sufficient branchwise nonincrease condition is

    s*B(s)>=R,  where R=sqrt(L^2-1).

This explicitly combines the radial loss with projection gain. It follows
throughout 1<=s<=S whenever

    L^2*(1-1/506)*S^(-2/506)>=1,

because the derivative of s^2 B(s)^2=L^2*s^(2-2/506)-s^2 is nonnegative in
that range, and equality sB(s)=R holds at s=1. Under these premises the
outgoing void is at most the incoming void on every branch, so every power
k>0 has nonpositive conditional drift with no shell condition. Full coverage
holds whenever sB(s)>=ell*(q-1)/(2q); ell/2 is a simpler sufficient threshold.

These hypotheses are special to this planar winner geometry. A shortest
unmarked horizontal winner has no such marked projection gain, so the lemma
cannot be applied to it. Nor does it assert that multiplying a generic
multidimensional marked radius by s captures its projection.

## Concrete exact verification at corridor scale

Take n=505, q=3329, ell=1475, g=550 and

    c=(V506*g^506/ell)^(1/504),  V506=pi^253/253!.

Then det M=V506*g^506, c=100.69678529..., and the initial horizontal test
radius is R=604.99917355.... Its exact void is 598/3329, less than .18.
For every u, enumerate heights 1<=j<=60 and choose the nearest horizontal
coordinate. Its squared length is exactly

    [ell^2*|ju|_q^2+q^2*j^2]/q^2.

Integer numerator comparisons select a true minimizing candidate. The largest
selected length over all 3329 marks is less than 41.01120. This certifies
completeness of the enumeration: every unsearched height has length at least
61, every nonzero unmarked coordinate has length at least c>100, and a purely
horizontal planar vector has length at least ell. Negative heights give the
same candidate lengths. Hence every actual shortest winner is planar and
nonhorizontal, with 1<=j<=60<q; ties have the same s and therefore the same
void formula.

Interval evaluation of the actual B(s), with certified integer floor bounds,
gives the exact outgoing fractions:

| input marks | outgoing void |
|---|---:|
| u=0 | 598/3329 |
| u=1 or -1 | 342/3329 |
| all other marks | 0 |

Consequently, for the exact uniform fresh input and k=24,

    E[p'_U(B(s_U))^24]/p(R)^24
      = [1+2*(342/598)^24]/3329
      = 0.00030039140822343553... < 1/3300.

Thus this solvable subclass has strict expected power contraction as well as
branchwise void nonincrease. No independence between winner and input was
assumed; the calculation enumerates their actual dependence.

## Relation to the earlier obstruction

The earlier 400-marked-axis example still has an unresolved actual transition.
Its unmarked horizontal vectors give a cap around 98.85, better than its
conservative S around 752, but projecting away such a vector does not shorten
any marked representative. Several height layers may nevertheless greatly
improve its void. Deciding that case requires their joint occupancy, or a
separate multidimensional projection argument. Neither the earlier bad shell
at the conservative radius nor the present rank-one contraction settles it.

The practical lemma supplied here is therefore conditional on verified planar,
nonhorizontal winner geometry. It illustrates a concrete way to replace shell
regularity by actual projection gain, but not a proof for generic BKZ histories.
