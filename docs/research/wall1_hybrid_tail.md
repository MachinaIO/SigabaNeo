# A fixed coordinate prefix and an adaptive terminal tail

The horizons 164 and 171 count selections **from a specified random marked lattice**. The calendar index before that lattice is reached does not enter the theorem. The distribution at entry does. In the width-506 first tour, forcing horizontal coordinates for longer changes that distribution even when the retained rank stays 505 and every unread column stays independent. At one endpoint there are only seven adaptive selections and the terminal minimum is one with probability one. Thus tail length alone cannot imply either the small-plane bound or a small terminal lower tail.

## What can be restarted

Use the unplanted, independent-column graph of Parts 108, 117 and 183. A state consists of the physical retained lattice, the subgroup of its torus on which the next displacement is uniform, and the ambient frame used for signed ties. In the unit-height graph phase let the transition be the append, select and project rule of Part 108. Denote the Part 117 entry law by $\mu$. Its lattice and mark are
$$
 M(B)=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb Z^{400}\oplus\mathbb Z^{105}:x=By\pmod q\},
 \qquad H=(\mathbb Z^{400}\times0+M(B))/M(B),
$$
with uniform $B$, $q=3329$, and the stated horizontal-first order. Let $\tau_X$ be the first retained state with a saturated plane of area at most $X$, counting the entry state as time zero. The audited Part 198 bounds are $\Pr_\mu(\tau_X\le164)<8.336\cdot10^{-13}$ at $X=676\sqrt2$ and $\Pr_\mu(\tau_X\le171)<5.055\cdot10^{-12}$ at $X=1352/\sqrt3$. These round the audit enclosures upwards. [Part 108](wall1_first_tour.md#filtration-and-append-law) · [Part 117](wall1_idea_round2.md#coordinate-prefix-theorem-and-its-proof) · [Part 198 audit](wall1_flag_horizon.md#audit)

**Transfer lemma.** Suppose the hybrid entry law is $\nu$ and its subsequent transition, including ties, agrees with the reference transition under a marked isometry. The incoming columns must have the stipulated fresh law given the available history. For a path event $E$ in the next $t$ selections, let $f_E(s)$ be its probability from state $s$. Then
$$
 \Pr_\nu(E)=\int f_E\,d\nu,\qquad
 |\Pr_\nu(E)-\Pr_\mu(E)|\le d_{\rm TV}(\nu,\mu).
$$
Indeed the same transition iterated from $s$ defines both conditional probabilities, and $0\le f_E\le1$. In particular the horizon transfers verbatim from an identical entry law, or with an additive charge under a coupling. Part 117 couples the ordinary first tour to the *unconditioned* forced-prefix experiment with disagreement below $0.00022304$. It leaves 407 adaptive selections. Conditioning the entry matrix on agreement would give a different experiment.

The distinction is visible in the proof. With a hit at tail draw $h$, last used tail column $i$, and terminal witness rank $b$, the lifted flag in that window has rank $h-i+1+b$. Shortestness in the successive projections of that flag only uses the adaptive tail. The branch with no used tail column must already have its lifts in the entry lattice. Its raw short-vector estimate uses the entry graph membership probability $q^{-400}$. The other branches use the inverse determinant moment starting at $D_0=q^{400}$, the fresh mark, and the covering bound for the projected integer grid. Forced prefix directions need not be shortest, so peeling a KZ flag backwards through them would be invalid. Nor does survival of a fixed vector supply the missing entry estimate. A positive-height winner is killed by its own projection, while a different vector in the same window can survive. ## The determinant paid by a fixed prefix

Consider the direct extension of Part 117. After the cubic freezes let $d=505$, so the retained lattice is $q\mathbb Z^d$. For each of the first $p$ graph appends, $0\le p\le d$, prescribe deletion of one surviving horizontal axis in a fixed order. Immediately after these appends the retained lattice and its determinant are
$$
 M_p=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb Z^{d-p}\oplus\mathbb Z^p:x=B_py\pmod q\},
 \qquad D_{\rm in}=q^{d-p}.
$$
Deleting a horizontal coordinate forgets one equation. Every solution to the remaining equations extends by choosing the deleted horizontal entry in its required residue class. Induction leaves precisely the indicated submatrix of independent uniform entries. The mark consists of the $d-p$ horizontal unit classes, with order $q^{d-p}$, because the horizontal section at $y=0$ is $q\mathbb Z^{d-p}$. Each forced direction has primitive lattice length $q$ in its window, and each unit-height append preserves covolume before projection. Thus each forced step divides the determinant by $q$ regardless of the true shortest length. For adaptive tail winners of lengths $s_1,\ldots,s_j$ the exact retained determinant is
$$
 D_j^{\rm tail}=q^{d-p}/(s_1\cdots s_j).
$$
This follows by completing each primitive winner to a basis and factoring volume into its length and the determinant of the projected complement. In the raw entry graph a test with nonzero input residue now has membership probability $q^{-(d-p)}$. Even the first term and initial condition of the horizon calculation have changed.

Keeping all 512 original graph columns gives $t=512-p$ adaptive selections and $d-p=t-7$ horizontal checks at entry. The reference $400\times105$ law occurs at $p=105$, where $t=407$. For every other $p$ the physical entry determinant differs deterministically from $q^{400}$, so the two entry laws have total variation distance one. Stopping that very law after a short tail instead leaves some original input columns unread. Forcing extra rows away so as to finish all columns changes both the covolume and the mark. A normalized shape comparison would also have to rescale physical radii and the unit append height.

**Counterexample on the full column schedule.** Take $p=d$. The entry lattice is $\mathbb Z^{505}$ with trivial mark. Each of the remaining seven windows is an orthogonal $\mathbb Z^{506}$ in its lattice coordinates. Every shortest vector is a signed coordinate unit, and projecting off any such vector returns an isometric $\mathbb Z^{505}$. Induction gives terminal minimum one and a saturated coordinate plane of area one at entry and at every subsequent draw. The area lies below both Part 198 cutoffs, and the minimum lies below the Part 183 radius of 26. Each event has probability one, even if entry is excluded. This prefix is deterministic and uses the standard Euclidean metric throughout.

For a general fixed rational projection the prefix determinant can also be random. Write the initial full lattice as the kernel of a surjective syndrome map $\sigma:\mathbb Z^N\to\mathbb F_q^m$. For a rational frozen space $P$, set $S=P\cap\mathbb Z^N$, $\rho=\dim_{\mathbb F_q}\sigma(S)$ and $Q=P_{P^\perp}$. The section $L\cap P$ has index $q^\rho$ in $S$. Extending a basis of the primitive section in $L$ and factoring volumes gives $\det QL=q^{m-\rho}/\det S$. For pure sample-axis deletions $\det S=1$ and each deletion removes one independent check. For deletions that also remove input axes, the omitted columns can change $\rho$ and the residual syndrome space. An exactly computable law for this projected lattice does not imply the entry law $\mu$. This formula concerns whichever raw graph is being projected, so its $m$ and $N$ must refer to that graph. ## The terminal event and its dependence on the entry state

Let $t\ge1$, let $M$ be the state after $t-1$ adaptive selections, and let $x$ be its designated shortest vector. Write $F$ for the event that the minimum after the final selection is at most physical radius $r$, and $J$ for $\lambda_1(P_{x^\perp}M)\le r$. Put $R=2r/\sqrt3$ and $a_r=Rr$. For the same height-one transition and tie rule, Part 183 gives the pathwise inclusions
$$
 F\triangle J\subseteq\{N_+(R)>0\},\qquad
 J\subseteq\{M\text{ has a saturated plane of area }\le a_r\}.
$$
Here $N_+$ counts positive-height window points. For the first inclusion, round a lift of a short quotient point along the winner of length $s$. The rounded lift has squared length at most $r^2+s^2/4$ and at least $s^2$, so both lengths are at most $R$. On a horizontal win, old lifts give $J$ and a fresh lift gives a point counted by $N_+$ after a possible sign change. On a vertical win with $F$, the winner itself is counted. On a vertical win with $J$, the same rounding in $M$ gives $s\le\|x\|\le R$. For the second inclusion, $x$ and a lift of the old projected point span area at most $\|x\|r\le a_r$. Saturating only decreases area. The converse would require the plane to contain the designated shortest line and have sufficiently small spacing modulo that line. [Part 183](wall1_terminal_tail_047.md#last-draw-one-exceptional-ball-covers-every-origin) · [Part 192](wall1_rank2_tail.md)

Consequently every such hybrid satisfies
$$
 \Pr_\nu(F)\le \Pr_\nu(\tau_{a_r}\le t-1)
 +\mathbb E_\nu\!\left[\min\{1,\mathbb E[N_+(R)\mid M,H,\text{frame}]\}\right].
$$
The horizon relevant to the old event ends on the **penultimate** draw. For $r=26$ its area cutoff is $1352/\sqrt3$. In particular, stopping the *reference* restart with $t-1\le171$ pays the Part 198 bound for the old event directly, including all the first-hit cases distinguished in Part 192. For this early stop the Part 183 last-draw discrepancy bound also applies: its fixed-radius Gaussian count uses the rank-505 projected integer grid dual, $R<q$, and an inverse determinant factor. The grid dual still embeds in integer coordinates by restriction to a full-rank coordinate minor. The certified log-determinant floors $L_i$ decrease through 406 (the recurrence in Part 183 has both alternatives below $L_{i-1}$ for $L_{i-1}>0$). Thus the earlier floor is at least $L_{406}$, and the same fixed-radius count is at most $3.210\cdot10^{-14}$. Adding the two charges gives $\Pr_\mu(F)<5.088\cdot10^{-12}$ for that early stop. Part 192's comparison with a determinant-matched Haar probability has a different, determinant-dependent right-hand side. An absolute prefix budget alone does not prove that comparison. [Part 183, conditional count](wall1_terminal_tail_047.md#conditional-count-at-a-fixed-physical-radius) · [Part 192, terminal charges](wall1_rank2_tail.md#summing-charges-along-the-tour)

For a changed coordinate prefix there is still an exact conditional count when $R<q$. Let $K$ be the preimage of $H$ in the old Euclidean space. At positive integer height $j\le R$, multiplication by $j$ permutes the finite mark group. Each point of $K$ lies in precisely one mark coset, so its contribution is weighted by $1/|H|$ inside the horizontal ball of radius $\sqrt{R^2-j^2}$. The identity $|H|\det K=\det M$ explains why a smaller entry determinant matters even when every future mark is fresh. The hybrid must supply its own bound for this count and the old-plane mass. The cubic counterexample even has $F=J$ surely. A perfect discrepancy estimate there would coexist with terminal failure probability one for the test $\lambda_1>26$. The Part 183 event is a physical minimum in an unplanted horizontal quotient. A planted terminal SVP criterion also involves the projected plant, the height layers and lifting.

## Which obstructions apply

* **Recursive deferral (Part 130).** If one projector is held fixed while batches of unread columns are appended, the zero-fresh slice embeds the old quotient isometrically. A short old vector is already a witness at the same physical radius. All shifts use the same leaf displacement group, so subdividing the appends preserves the pooled collision test in the audit. In a genuine adaptive tail, projection off each winner can shorten or kill old vectors and pushes the mark group forward. This invalidates the fixed-projector premise behind pooling all tail columns. Each individual append still contains its old lattice at height zero before the freeze, as the event $J$ records. Escaping the pooling argument gives no estimate of $J$. [Part 130 and audit](wall1_recursive_deferral.md)
* **Finite families (Part 40).** Here the relevant plane is the *total* frozen space in the original planted embedding. It contains the fixed prefix and also the lifts of every adaptive winner. If it ranges over a predetermined family of $N$ planes, success of the width-406 unique-terminal-pair criterion implies success for at least one family member. The audited fixed-plane estimate therefore bounds success by $N s_*$ with $s_*<2^{-678.61}$, regardless of the selection rule. The number of adaptive selections does not bound $N$ by the number of draws: a single draw can have many possible winning lines. Fixing just the prefix neither invokes Part 40 for the final plane nor demonstrates that the tail selects a good plane. The bound concerns planted uniqueness at 406, not the width-506 horizontal shape event above. [Part 40](wall1_finite_family.md#a-rigorous-small-family-obstruction-at-406) · [Audit summary](../PROOF_GAP.md)

**Exact small check.** A scratch enumeration over $\mathbb F_3$ used two horizontal axes and three input columns. For all $3^6=729$ matrices, projecting away one prescribed row after each of the first two appends gave retained residue indices 3 and 1. After the last append every triple of input residues occurred. The graph contains all period axes, so the final window is the integral cubic lattice, as predicted by the endpoint proof.

The transfer lemma, determinant identities, terminal inclusions and counterexample are paper arguments. For a nondegenerate changed prefix, a useful terminal bound requires estimates under its actual marked entry law. Whether a particular such prefix attains a proposed terminal budget remains open. A uniform claim based solely on the length of the adaptive tail is false.

## Audit

**Verdict — SOUND.** The early-stop inequality and the seven-draw counterexample both hold. They use different entry laws. The determinant and column counts below specify the scope of the early-stop result.

**Where the clock starts.** Draw zero in Part 198 is the unconditioned Part 117 graph with 400 checks, 105 inputs, its horizontal mark and the signed tie frame. With the same subsequent kernel and conditionally fresh columns, equality of marked entry laws gives equality of path probabilities. An exact description of another entry law gives the integral against that law. In the coordinate family the determinant alone distinguishes every $p\ne105$ from the reference. The $0.00022304$ in Part 117 bounds disagreement in the coupling to the ordinary tour. The Part 197 flag argument uses shortestness at each adaptive selection, and Part 199's fixed-vector survival condition supplies no such comparison for a prescribed deletion. [Part 117](wall1_idea_round2.md#coordinate-prefix-theorem-and-its-proof) · [Part 198 audit](wall1_flag_horizon.md#audit) · [Part 199 audit](wall1_survival_constraint.md#audit)

**The last two ranks and scales.** Let $i\le171$ be the number of restart selections completed before revealing one final independent column. Its incoming window has rank 506 and covolume $D_i$. Projecting off its primitive winner gives the retained horizontal lattice $M_{i+1}$ of rank 505, the lattice whose minimum is tested. Its covolume is exactly $D_{i+1}=q^{400}/\prod_{j=1}^{i+1}s_j$. As physical scale checks, iterating the pathwise Part 183 recurrence gives $L_{171}/\log 10>878.5248$ and $L_{172}/\log 10>875.9137$. The floors decrease over these draws, so the incoming window has Gaussian-heuristic radius $(D_i/V_{506})^{1/506}>298.68$ and the retained lattice has $(D_{i+1}/V_{505})^{1/505}>297.20$, where $V_n$ is unit-ball volume. These are lower bounds on scale. At the latest stop, $105+172=277$ graph columns have entered and 235 remain unread. Parts 182–183 use $H_{407}$ after all 512 columns. Parts 180–182 test a full terminal block with its plant height and additional conditions. Rank 505 at the early stop gives neither the law of $H_{407}$ nor the score for that block. [Part 182](wall1_restart_passes_test.md) · [Part 183](wall1_terminal_tail_047.md)

**Probability at that stop.** The old event $J$ implies $\tau_{1352/\sqrt3}\le i$. At fixed physical radius $52/\sqrt3<q$, the Part 183 conditional count has the same rank-505 dual-grid estimate at every eligible history and is proportional to $1/D_i$. Since $L_i\ge L_{406}$, the audited discrepancy bound applies before the final reveal. Adding the two audited upper endpoints gives
$$
\Pr_\mu\!\left(\lambda_1(M_{i+1})\le26\right)
<5.05421450\cdot10^{-12}+3.20907865\cdot10^{-14}
=5.0863052865\cdot10^{-12}<5.088\cdot10^{-12}.
$$
The argument also permits a bounded stopping time $i$ for the revealed history, followed by one fresh draw. The old event is covered by one cumulative horizon and the discrepancy by one conditional expectation. Choosing a final draw retrospectively would require a separate bound. [Part 198 audit](wall1_flag_horizon.md#audit) · [Part 183 conditional count and audit](wall1_terminal_tail_047.md#audit)

**Coordinate endpoint and exact check.** Deleting all 505 sample coordinates leaves free integral inputs and a trivial mark. Each remaining append gives a cubic rank-506 window, and deleting any winning coordinate line returns a cubic rank-505 lattice. Its minimum is one. The squared area of two independent integer vectors is the sum of squares of their integral two-by-two minors, so it is at least one. A coordinate pair attains equality and spans a saturated plane. An independent scratch enumeration projected the graph residues for all 729 two-by-three matrices over $\mathbb F_3$. Every matrix gave indices 3 and 1 after the two deletions, then all 27 input triples in the last window. The period axes lift this residue conclusion to the integer lattice.

**Scope of the obstructions.** Part 130 pools columns conditional on one projector fixed before all those columns. Updating the projector after each winner removes that common-projector premise and leaves open the frequency of a surviving old witness. At width 406, Part 40 bounds the unique-terminal-pair probability by $Ns_*$ when the **total** frozen plane takes values in a predetermined family of size $N$. Its union bound permits an adaptive choice within that family. A fixed initial portion alone supplies no bound on the size of the family of completed planes.
