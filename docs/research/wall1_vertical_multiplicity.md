# Positive-height plane multiplicity on a non-horizontal win

In the marginal width-506 restart, the contribution from winners of absolute height at least **363** is less than **$1.872\cdot10^{-9}$**, counting every witness plane. The same bound holds on the larger stratum where the terminal cutoff radius is at least $314.36214$. Consequently large winning heights can be removed with an explicit charge. The full non-horizontal first moment is below $2^{456.548}$, improving the $2^{1316.021}$ majorant of Part 147. A budget-sized estimate for the remaining small-height moment is open.

## The observable and the exact conditional count

Use the marginal $400\times105$ restart graph and 407 independent suffix columns, with the exact forward selector and inherited signed ambient lexicographic ties. The probability space consists of the design alone. The independent CBD(3) plant does not enter. Write $S$ for the complete history before the last column, $L\subset E$ for the old lattice, $D=\det L$, $H\subset E/L$ for the mark, $d=505$, $b=d+1$, $q=3329$, and $c=0.954$. Conditional on $S$, the displacement $U$ is uniform on $H$. Let $V_j$ be unit-ball volume and $G_j=V_j^{-1/j}$. For a representative $\tilde u$ let $W=L+\mathbb Z(\tilde u,1)$, identifying $L$ with the height-zero lattice. Denote the selected length by $s$, the absolute winning height by $k$, and the projected lattice by $Q$. Then $\delta=\det Q=D/s$ and $R=cG_d\delta^{1/d}$. A witness is a saturated plane through the winner with area strictly below $sR$. For $k>0$ every such plane has positive height image. Write $Z=\mathbf1_{k>0}N_+$ and $F_{\rm nh}=\mathbb E Z$. [First-pass law](wall1_first_tour.md) · [Plane convention](wall1_plane_count.md) · [Part 147 and audit](wall1_uncovered_lines.md)

**Proposition 1.** For each displacement giving $k>0$, orient the selected vector to positive height and write $v=(y,k)$, $y=\lambda+k\tilde u$, $\lambda\in L$. For an old primitive vector $x$ up to sign define
$$
 e_x=\max\{j:j\mid k,\ x\equiv a\,q y\pmod{jL}
                    \text{ for some integer }a\text{ coprime to }j\}.
$$
The divisor $1$ always qualifies. The exact conditional mean is
$$
 \mathbb E[Z\mid S]=\frac{1}{|H|}
 \sum_{k=1}^{(q-1)/2}\ \sum_{u\in H:\, k(u)=k}
 \ \sum_{x\in L_{\rm prim}/\{\pm1\}}
 \mathbf1\!\left\{\|x\|^2-\frac{\langle x,y\rangle^2}{s^2}
                             < e_x^2 R^2\right\}.                 \tag{1}
$$
Only $\|x\|<sR$ need be tested. At fixed $k$, the map $u\mapsto ku$ is a permutation of $H$. The inner displacement sum may thus be taken over the chosen shortest representatives of precisely the classes for which height $k$ wins. The denominator for this *joint* moment is $|H|$.

*Proof.* The projected $q$-axes span the old space, so the old minimum is at most $q$. Positive heights with the same residue up to sign have the same sets of horizontal parts up to sign. Keeping the least absolute height strictly improves a nonminimal representative. Height $q$ could tie only with zero horizontal part and loses the signed tie to the old winner. Thus the stated range is exhaustive. At a winning height, $y$ is a shortest representative of $ku$ with the inherited tie rule. Distinct $u$ at this fixed height give distinct classes. Once the winning representative is specified it also fixes $u=k^{-1}(y+L)$, so there is no additional independent coset draw. [Projective-line formula](wall1_subspace_void.md) · [Voronoi law, Theorem B](wall1_dual_cusp.md)

Projection orthogonal to $v$, restricted to $E$, has squared norm $\|x\|^2-\langle x,y\rangle^2/s^2$ and least singular value $k/s$. It maps $L'=L+\mathbb Z\lambda/k$ onto $Q$ because the projection of $(\tilde u,1)$ equals that of $-\lambda/k$. Primitivity of $v$ in $W$ means that $k$ and the content of $\lambda$ in $L$ are coprime. Hence $L'/L$ is cyclic of order $k$. [One-step geometry, Theorem 4](wall1_lyapunov.md)

Each rational line of $L'$ has a unique primitive old direction $\pm x$. On it, $L'\cap\mathbb Rx=\mathbb Z x/e$ for a divisor $e$ of $k$. For $j\mid k$ the class $x/j$ has order $j$ modulo $L$. It belongs to $L'/L$ exactly when it generates the unique subgroup there of order $j$, generated by $\lambda/j$. This is equivalent to $x\equiv a\lambda\pmod{jL}$ for some unit $a$ modulo $j$. Since $q\tilde u\in L$, $q y\equiv q\lambda\pmod{jL}$, and $q$ is a unit modulo $j$. The largest qualifying $j$ is therefore $e_x=e$. This proves independence of all lift choices. The image of $x/e_x$ generates the corresponding primitive quotient line. Its inverse image is the unique saturated plane over that line, of area $s$ times its primitive length. The norm test in (1) follows. The least singular value and $e_x\le k$ imply $\|x\|<sR$ for every witness. Summing over the uniform mark proves (1). ∎

## A bound for the entire large-height contribution

Put $N=912$, $m=400$, $z_N=q^{m/N}$, $\alpha=\sqrt3/2$, and
$$
 R_* = z_N\sqrt{d/(2\pi)},\qquad
 J_d=\Gamma(1+d/2)(2\mathrm e/d)^{d/2},\qquad
 \theta(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\exp(-\pi z^2 n^2).
$$

**Theorem 2.** The following bounds charge multiplicity under the restart design law:
$$
 \mathbb E[Z\mathbf1_{k\ge363}]
 \ \le\ \mathbb E[Z\mathbf1_{R\ge R_*}]
 \ \le\ \frac{J_dc^d}{2}
       \bigl(\theta(z_N)^N+\theta(q/z_N)^N\bigr)
 \ <\ 1.871946\cdot10^{-9}.                                      \tag{2}
$$

*Proof.* Each nonzero quotient vector has length at least $\alpha s$. Indeed its lift can be rounded along $v$ to have parallel component at most $s/2$, and the resulting nonzero window vector has length at least $s$. Therefore $Z>0$ requires $R>\alpha s\ge\alpha k$. The interval check below gives $R_*/\alpha<363$.

Let $T_B(z)=\sum_{\xi\in B}\exp(-\pi z^2\|\xi\|^2)$. Counting opposite primitive vectors once, Gaussian majorization and Poisson summation give, for every $z>0$,
$$
 Z\le \frac{\exp(\pi R^2/z^2)z^d}{2\delta}\,T_{Q^*}(z).          \tag{3}
$$
Use $z=R\sqrt{2\pi/d}$. The prefactor is $J_dc^d/2$ on every draw. On $R\ge R_*$ we have $z\ge z_N$. If $M$ is the final raw graph lattice then $Q^*=M^*\cap E_{\rm out}$, by duality of orthogonal projection. Thus the theta factor on this stratum is bounded by $T_{M^*}(z_N)$, which may be averaged over the full design after dropping the indicator. No conditioning of the raw matrix is needed.

For completeness, for a graph with $m$ rows, $n-m$ independent input columns and ambient dimension $n$, its dual consists of $(p,w)/q$ with $w\equiv-A^\top p\pmod q$. At $z_n=q^{m/n}$, the residue-zero contribution to its expected theta sum is $\theta(z_n)^n$. For each nonzero residue of $p$ the $n-m$ prescribed residues are uniform and independent. Their total contribution is at most
$$
 q^{-(n-m)}\theta(z_n/q)^n=\theta(q/z_n)^n
$$
by one-dimensional Poisson summation and $z_n^n=q^m$. This proves (2) with $n=N$. The full-design average controls a selected stratum because every theta summand is nonnegative. ∎

Part 147 used scale at least $z_N$ even at the worst permitted output determinant and paid $(z_N/f)^d$ with $f$ the root-determinant floor. Equation (2) identifies a stratum where that cost disappears. The earlier majorant gives no lower bound on the frequency of any determinant or height. ## An unconditional first-moment bound

**Theorem 3.** $F_{\rm nh}<2^{456.548}$. For $u_0=\sqrt{b/(2\pi)}$, the two contributions from $s\le u_0$ and $s>u_0$ are respectively less than $2^{456.54708}$ and $2^{414.70069}$.

*Proof.* Write $p_S(u)=\Pr(k>0,\ s\le u\mid S)$. For every $u,z>0$,
$$
 p_S(u)\le \frac{\exp(\pi u^2/z^2) z^b\theta(z)^d}{2D}.          \tag{4}
$$
To see this, sum Gaussian weights over the possible positive winning heights. In each such layer $j$, the class $jU$ is uniform. Put $K=L+H$ in the sense of the full inverse image of $H$ in $E$. The expected horizontal Gaussian mass in layer $j$ is $z^d T_{K^*}(z)/D$ by equal fibers and Poisson summation. Here $K^*=\mathbb Z^{911}\cap E$. Some $d$ coordinate functionals are injective together on $E$, so deleting the other coordinates bounds $T_{K^*}(z)$ by $\theta(z)^d$. Finally $\sum_{j\ge1}\exp(-\pi j^2/z^2)\le z/2$ by the integral test. The extension of this last numerical sum to all $j$ requires uniformity only for the possible winning heights. [Projected mark identity](wall1_dual_cusp.md) · [Projected Gaussian count](wall1_uncovered_lines.md)

Use the old determinant floor $D\ge D_*$ with
$$
 a=d/b,\qquad \log D_*=a^{406}m\log q-\tfrac b2(1-a^{406})\log(56.2142).
$$
Indeed the window Hermite cap gives $s_i\le\sqrt{56.2142}\,D_{i-1}^{1/b}$, and $D_i=D_{i-1}/s_i$ starting from $q^m$. Iterating gives the displayed floor. [Hermite certificates](wall1_hermite_caps.md) · [Restart determinant recursion](wall1_uncovered_lines.md)

For $s\le u_0$, put $f=(D_*/u_0)^{1/d}$, $n=911$ and $z'=q^{m/n}$. In (3) take $z=z'\delta^{1/d}/f\ge z'$. On vertical winners the old-coordinate map on $Q^*$ is injective into $L^*$ and shortens norms: the last dual coordinate is uniquely determined by orthogonality to $(y,k)$. It follows that $T_{Q^*}(z)\le T_{L^*}(z')\le T_{M_{\rm old}^*}(z')$. Crucially this last theta sum is measurable **before** the final draw. Retaining the indicator of the short vertical win, applying (4) at $(u_0,1)$ conditionally, and then averaging the old graph give
$$
 B_{\rm small}=
 \frac{\exp(b/2)\theta(1)^d}{2D_*}
 \frac{\exp(\pi(cG_df/z')^2)(z'/f)^d}{2}
 \bigl(\theta(z')^n+\theta(q/z')^n\bigr)                         \tag{5}
$$
as an upper bound. The raw-graph estimate in Theorem 2 uses just the 511 columns already revealed here. Multiplying the analogous *final* ambient theta expectation by $p_S(u_0)$ would require an extra correlation estimate.

For $s>u_0$ we use a packing bound. If points in a $d$-ball of radius $R$ are mutually at distance at least $h$, with $0<h\le R$, their number is at most
$$
 \pi d(d+1)(\sqrt2 R/h)^d .
$$
Lift $x$ to $(x,\sqrt{R^2-\|x\|^2})/R$ on the unit sphere in $\mathbb R^{d+1}$. Distinct lifted points have inner product at most $1-h^2/(2R^2)$. In any open cap of angular radius $\gamma$ with $\sin\gamma=h/(\sqrt2 R)$, orthogonal projections off the cap axis have pairwise strictly negative inner products. There are at most $d+1$ such points in $\mathbb R^d$: an affine dependence among $d+2$ points would give two disjoint convex combinations equal to each other, whose mutual inner product is strictly negative. The normalized cap area is at least $\sin^d\gamma/(\pi d)$ by integrating $\sin^{d-1}t\cos t$ on $[0,\gamma]$ and bounding the normalization integral by $\pi$. Averaging the cap occupancy proves the claim. The ball-to-sphere lifting argument also appears in [Cohn and Zhao, *Sphere packing bounds via spherical codes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.5966).

Apply this to both signs of the primitive quotient vectors with $h=\alpha s$ and divide by two. If $R\le h$ the count already vanishes. On every vertical winner this gives
$$
 N_+\le \frac{\pi d(d+1)}2 (\sqrt2 cG_d/\alpha)^d D s^{-b}.
$$
For $u\ge u_0$, (4) at $z=u/u_0$ gives $p_S(u)\le C u^b/D$, where
$$ C=\tfrac12\exp(b/2)(2\pi/b)^{b/2}\theta(1)^d. $$
Since $s\le q$, integrate $s^{-b}=q^{-b}+b\int_s^q u^{-b-1}\,du$ on $\{k>0,s>u_0\}$. The conditional expectation of this restricted weight is at most $C[1+b\log(q/u_0)]/D$. Thus even conditionally on $S$ the large-length contribution is at most
$$
 B_{\rm large}=\frac{\pi d(d+1)}2(\sqrt2 cG_d/\alpha)^d
                  C[1+b\log(q/u_0)].                                \tag{6}
$$
The numerical sum of the upper bounds (5) and (6) gives the theorem. ∎

**A probability-only exit.** Set $u=cG_bD^{1/b}$ in (4) and use $z=u/u_0$. The determinant floor gives $z>3.38611$, so $p_S(u)\le J_bc^b\theta(z)^d/2<8.939\cdot10^{-10}$, uniformly in $S$. For the probability of a terminal witness one can discard all such short vertical wins at this cost. For $F_{\rm nh}$ the discarded quantity is $\mathbb E[Z\mathbf1_{s\le u}]$ and each draw can contribute several planes. Equations (2) and (5) charge the actual multiplicity on their stated strata. ## Audit

**Verdict — SOUND.** The large-height and full non-horizontal first-moment bounds hold for the marginal width-506 restart. The only clarification is the scope of “larger stratum” in the opening paragraph. The containment proved in (2) holds on draws with a witness. The remaining budget is a sufficient condition and is still open.

**Exact count and signed ties.** Part 108 supplies the uniform mark after fixing the complete history. Part 146 permits the least absolute height representative and settles the height-$q$ tie in favor of the old winner. Once the signed winner is selected and oriented upwards, projection identifies the output with the image of $L'=L+\mathbb Z\lambda/k$. For primitive old $x$, the point $x/j$ has order $j$ modulo $L$. It lies in the cyclic extension precisely when it generates the same order-$j$ subgroup as $\lambda/j$. Multiplication by $q$ preserves that subgroup since $j\mid k<q$. This proves the divisor test even for composite $k$. The projected generator on this line is the image of $x/e_x$, so each accepted old direction identifies exactly one saturated plane through the selected winner. The joint count retains the winner test and the denominator $|H|$. These steps use Part 139, Theorem 4, and Part 142's exact mark and dual identities.

**Independent integer check.** For all $5^2+17^2=314$ displacements, a scratch script enumerated the signed window vectors in the closed ball of radius $q$ and selected the minimum by squared length and ambient coordinates. For each other vector $w$ it tested $0<2(s^2\|w\|^2-\langle v,w\rangle^2)<q^2s^2$ and identified the plane by the primitive direction of its intersection with the old space. This search is exhaustive because every primitive quotient witness lifts, after rounding along $v$, to a vector of squared norm below $q^2/2+s^2/4\le3q^2/4$. The resulting **sets of planes** agreed with (1) on every displacement, including four draws at each prime with tied shortest lines. Both printed rows agree in every height column. At $q=17$, the eight height-six wins gave respectively $8,8,16$ incidences with $e_x=2,3,6$.

**Moment estimates.** Rounding a lift of a nonzero quotient vector gives the separation $\alpha s$. Thus $Z>0$ and $k\ge363$ imply $R>363\alpha>314.36214>R_*$. Equation (3) counts every primitive pair below the strict cutoff. On $R\ge R_*$ the chosen Gaussian scale is at least $z_N$, so the full raw dual theta sum dominates pointwise. Averaging it over the marginal graph uses all 512 independent columns. In (5) orthogonality at $k>0$ fixes the last dual coordinate from the old ones. This gives a dominating raw theta sum measurable after only 511 columns, allowing (4) to be applied before averaging that sum. In (4) the equal-fiber average uses $\det K=D/|H|$, and every possible positive winning height permutes $H$. In (6) the lift to the hemisphere preserves the separation bound. Inside an open cap the axial components exceed $\cos\gamma$, which gives strictly negative inner products after projection and bounds occupancy by $d+1$. The endpoint $q^{-b}p_S(q)$ and the integral over $[u_0,q]$ together give the factor $1+b\log(q/u_0)$. The probability-only exit bounds the probability of a short vertical win and is separate from both moment estimates.
