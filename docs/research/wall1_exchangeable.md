# What randomizing the input basis can make exchangeable

Write bases as column matrices. There are three different laws here: the input basis, its residues in a finite quotient, and the prefix after reduction. Finite randomization gives an exact arithmetic calculation for the second law. Even exact independence from the presentation for the third law gives no positive lower bound on the terminal success probability from symmetry and full block reducedness alone.

## Finite randomization retains the presentation

Let the rank be at least two and take any nonempty finite family of unimodular matrices with support $F$. Starting from $B$ or $BT$ gives supports $BF$ and $BTF$. For the shear $S=I+E_{12}$, choose $T=S^j$ outside the finite set $FF^{-1}$ (the powers are distinct). The supports are then disjoint, so the worst-case total variation distance over presentations is exactly one for every family size. The same supremum is one for any probability on the discrete group: truncate to a finite set of mass at least $1-\epsilon$, separate its translates as above, and test membership in the first finite set to get distance at least $1-2\epsilon$. This argument concerns the input. Reduction may erase the difference, for instance by first constructing the column Hermite normal form in the fixed ambient integer coordinates. Canonicalizing before randomizing really does give a lattice-only kernel.

## An exact before-reduction bound

Fix any primitive $u\in L$, any basis $B$, a prime $p$, and terminal width $1\le\beta<d$. Sample uniformly one integral lift for each element of $\mathrm{SL}_d(\mathbb F_p)$, with determinant one, and use $BU$ as the input. Such lifts exist because row additions generate the field group: elimination uses signed row interchanges and paired scalings, both generated by additions. Explicitly, with $E_{ij}(a)=I+a e_i e_j^t$, the two-row product $E_{12}(a)E_{21}(-1/a)E_{12}(a)$ has rows $(0,a),(-1/a,0)$, and multiplying it by the inverse of the same product at $a=1$ gives the paired scaling.

The coordinates $U^{-1}B^{-1}u$ are uniform modulo $p$ on the nonzero vectors. Indeed primitivity excludes zero, and completion to a determinant-one field basis proves transitivity (rescale a complementary column). Thus the probability that the suffix vanishes modulo $p$ is exactly
$$
\frac{p^{d-\beta}-1}{p^d-1}.
$$
This is a Part 8 failure event for the **input** prefix: either the projection is zero or its generator multiplicity is divisible by $p$. It is only a one-prime test. In particular this exchangeability neither proves primitivity on its complement nor controls the lengths of competing lines.

## A reduced output for which the probability changes to one

Use the plain embedding of usvp_planted.md with $m=n=1$, zero error and secret, and a uniform scalar $A$ modulo an odd prime $q$. The plant is $u=e_3$ and $L=H_A\perp\mathbb Zu$, where $H_A$ is the planar kernel $x+Ay=0\pmod q$. This is a fixed support planting. For every $A\ne0$, neither planar unit vector is in the kernel, so $u$ generates the unique ambient shortest line. The gap can be arbitrarily large on typical instances of this family. For $1\le R<q$, a nonzero horizontal vector of length at most $R$ must have $y\ne0\pmod q$ to belong, and then occurs for exactly one $A$. A union bound gives probability at most $((2\lfloor R\rfloor+1)^2-1)/q$ for any such vector. For instance $R=q^{1/4}$ gives a gap exceeding $R$ with probability tending to one.

On the unique-shortest event take the lattice-only reducer that outputs a full successive projected shortest-vector basis (HKZ), with size reduction and deterministic ambient-coordinate tie rules. Existence follows recursively by lifting a primitive shortest quotient vector to extend the primitive prefix, then rounding earlier Gram–Schmidt coefficients in reverse order. Such a basis is fully BKZ reduced at every width, since restricting a projected suffix to a block containing its first vector retains its minimum. Its first column is $\pm u$. Thus at $d=3,\beta=2$ the terminal projection is zero with conditional probability **one**, for every input randomization. The input one-prime failure probability was $(p-1)/(p^3-1)$ even conditional on each such lattice. A concrete instance is $q=5,A=2$, with orthogonal horizontal basis $(1,2),(-2,1)$ of determinant five and squared lengths five. Taking the plant first gives an explicit fully reduced basis. The output prefix line is forced and is invariant even under every lattice isometry. The failure counted here is exactly terminal failure in Part 8; recovery earlier in the basis succeeds.

## What a symmetry proof would still need

For a lattice-only prefix kernel $K_L$ using fresh coins, the conditional success probability given $(L,u)$ is exactly $K_L(G(L,u))$, where $G$ is the set of prefixes passing both Part 8 tests. Equivariance under a finite group fixing the marked lattice makes masses equal within each prefix orbit. Each orbit lies wholly inside or wholly outside $G$, since projection commutes with isometries fixing the planted line. Hence equivariance leaves the total mass on successful orbits undetermined. In the example the forced first line is a singleton failure orbit. The example does not model the running time or the output distribution of the verified slide loop. For its canonical input, or for a specified randomized initialization, a bound near the GSA width still requires control of the mass its actual reduction places on good prefixes, including the probability that the plant already lies in the prefix. No transfer of the finite-field calculation through reduction has been established.
