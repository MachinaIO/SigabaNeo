# A fixed sample prefix gives a terminal reference bound

At the plain independent-matrix shape $(m,n,q)=(511,512,3329)$ with independent CBD(3) error and secret, deleting 377 fixed sample coordinates gives an exact SVP problem in dimension **647**. With probability at least **0.9951664** the projected plant is the unique shortest pair up to sign. This is the smallest dimension meeting the 99% target with the integer-ball certificate below. Deleting samples implements the familiar optimization over sample count. The probability certificate concerns a prescribed prefix and one terminal call. [Estimator dimension optimization](https://lattice-estimator.readthedocs.io/en/latest/algorithms/lwe-primal.html)

## Coordinate deletion theorem

Use prime $q$, $d=m+n+1$, $b=As+e$, and the embedding $x+Ay=tb$ modulo $q$. Condition on $u=(e,s,1)$ throughout the design count. Delete a fixed set of $k$ coordinates of $x$, putting $r=m-k$, $D=d-k$, and writing primes for restrictions to the remaining samples. The deleted axes have prefix lattice generated by their $q$-scaled units. Coordinate projection maps the embedding onto precisely the integral solutions of $x'+A'y=tb'$. Indeed every solution extends by taking the deleted entries of $x$ in the required residue classes. This proves surjectivity even for classes with long ambient lifts. The projected covolume is $q^r$ by the identity block, and $A'$ is uniform. The argument also works conditional on a plant-dependent choice independent of $A$ given $u$. The CBD averages below use a predetermined set.

Write $v=(e',s,1)$, $R=\|v\|\le\|u\|$, and $B=\|(e',s)\|_\infty$. Enumerate one of each signed pair of integer points $z=(x',y,t)$ in the closed ball of radius $R$, omitting zero and $\pm v$. For each representative put $Y=y-ts$, $X=x'-te'$ modulo $q$. The **exact expected number** of competing pairs is
$$
 M=\#\{Y=0,X=0\}+q^{-r}\#\{Y\ne0\}.
$$
Each surviving row sends a fixed nonzero $Y$ to a uniform field element. For $Y=0$ the condition is deterministic. Hence $h\le\min(1,M)$ including ties. The height one of $v$ excludes vanishing and shorter fractional multiples. If $R\sqrt{1+B^2}<q$, Cauchy–Schwarz bounds each integral discrepancy by less than $q$, so simultaneous zero residues force $z=tv$. There are no such competitors. With $N_D(j)$ the integer population of the closed squared-radius-$j$ ball, this gives the convenient certificate
$$
 h\le \min\{1,(N_D(R^2)-1)/(2q^r)\}.
$$
Replacing $R$ by the ambient norm in the last numerator gives a looser norm-only estimate. Unit cubes give the elementary upper bound $N_D(R^2)\le V_D(R+\sqrt D/2)^D$, where $V_D=\pi^{D/2}/\Gamma(1+D/2)$. The integer count avoids the cube inflation. Under success orient the SVP output to height one, read $s$ and recover all deleted errors as the centered residues of $b-As$ when $B<q/2$ (for deleted errors use their original support bound). Thus the lift ambiguity is resolved in this coordinate family.

## Dimensions, confidence and cost

The Gaussian heuristic radius in the volume-one convention is $G_D=(q^r/V_D)^{1/D}$. For any particular planting the comparison is $R<G_D$. For independent CBD($\eta$) coordinates the RMS comparison uses $R_{\rm rms}=\sqrt{1+(D-1)\eta/2}$. Scanning $r=0,\ldots,511$, the first strict RMS crossings are $D=641$ ($k=383$, $31.000<31.127$) for CBD(3) and $D=622$ ($k=402$, $24.940<25.151$) for CBD(2). These locate the largest deletions for the RMS comparison. Actual radii fluctuate.

The table averages a capped conditional bound, allowing rare large plantings to contribute at most their probability. Here is reproducible integer pseudocode. Use $J=2000$, $C(z)=\sum_{a=-\eta}^{\eta}\binom{2\eta}{\eta+a}z^{a^2}$ and $Q=4^\eta$. For $j\le J$, $j(1+\eta^2)<q^2$ throughout. Assign conditional failure bound one beyond $J$. In the loop, coefficient arrays can be updated by multiplying once per dimension and truncating after degree $J$. The recurrence for $N$ follows by conditioning on the last coordinate.

```text
N[0,j] = 1 for 0 <= j <= J
N[D,j] = N[D-1,j] + 2 sum_{1 <= a <= floor(sqrt(j))} N[D-1,j-a*a]
for D from 513 upwards:
    r = D - 513
    H = 2 * 3329^r
    T = Q^(D-1)
    gain = 0
    for j from 1 to J:
        w = coefficient of z^(j-1) in C(z)^(D-1)
        gain += w * max(0, H - (N[D,j]-1))
    failure_bound = (H*T - gain)/(H*T)
```

Both thresholds are minima of this particular certificate across coordinate sample counts. A bound above the target gives no converse for actual success. These are joint probabilities over the independent CBD planting and design. For a prescribed planting, including conditioning on a norm shell, the conditional count above applies directly. For example the Part 7 shape with 188 kept samples and dimension 701 is already available by deleting 323 coordinates from the original dimension 1024. Its coarser high-confidence bound is consistent with this calculation. [Part 7 calculation](ratio_unique.md)

The cost is one exact $\mathrm{SVP}_D$ call plus polynomial work. For example the deterministic Micciancio–Voulgaris bound gives $\widetilde O(4^{647})$ time and $\widetilde O(2^{647})$ space at the CBD(3) 99% point (time exponential factor $2^{1294}$ versus $2^{2048}$ at full dimension). The randomized worst-case $2^{D+o(D)}$ time and space bound carries its own solver error. Under a heuristic $2^{0.2925D}$ time model the leading bits at $D=647,662,627,641$ are respectively 189.25, 193.64, 183.40, 187.49, versus 299.52 at 1024. These exponents are cost models, without a finite operation count or a proof of planted sieve performance. ## General rational planes

For fixed $P$ let $S=P\cap\mathbb Z^d$, $G=\mathbb Z^d/S$ with norm induced by orthogonal projection, and $T$ the reduction of $S$ modulo $q$. Rationality makes $G$ a Euclidean lattice in the quotient. If $[u]=0$, charge failure one for vanishing. Otherwise enumerate the finitely many nonzero signed classes $g\ne\pm[u]$ in its closed ball of radius $\|[u]\|$, once per pair. Choose any integral representative $z_g$, however long. With $D_A(x,y,t)=x-te+A(y-ts)$ and $F(V)=\mathbb E_A\dim D_A(V)$, the exact expected number of competing pairs is
$$
 \sum_g \big(1+F(T)-F(T+\mathbb F_q\bar z_g)\big).
$$
Indeed $g$ has some lattice lift exactly when $D_A(\bar z_g)\in D_A(T)$. Adjoining that column changes rank by the indicator of nonmembership. This proves lift invariance and counts collinear competitors too. Cap the sum at one to bound $h$. Each $F$ reduces to the uniform finite-matrix rank sum in the [interior note](wall1_interior.md). Averaging fixed-plane bounds under the conditional marginal of the selector gives the reference input of [Part 38](wall1_analyzable_reducer.md).

Even rank one can have $h=1$ for every planting. With two sample axes $a_1,a_2$, choose $P=\mathbb R(a_1+Na_2)$ for integer $N>q$. The projected vector of $qa_2$ is nonzero of length $q/\sqrt{1+N^2}<1$ and height zero, while the plant retains height one. This fixed rational plane has full lattice intersection rank in its span for every design. Thus independence and prefix rank alone cannot yield a uniformly small reference probability. The quotient geometry matters.

The coordinate result improves on $\beta=d$ relative to the original 511-sample embedding. In the punctured instance it is full-dimensional SVP. It does not certify the Part 38 reference law of an arbitrary slide selector, the actual selected terminal block, or the registered $\beta=512$ claim. The analysis uses uniform plain matrices, and the sample-deletion proof depends on deleting error axes while retaining all secret coordinates and the height.
