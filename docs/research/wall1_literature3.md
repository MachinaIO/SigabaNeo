Throughout, the terminal *height* is the zero-based GS length at index $n-b$, the head of the last width-$b$ window. A lower bound on this height bounds the terminal minimum if the head is shortest. If the head only satisfies a $c$-approximate SVP condition, divide the height bound by $c$. For an unrestricted head the implication fails. All numerical Haar radii below use covolume one.

## What an actual reducer certifies

**Micciancio–Walter and Walter’s dissertation.** Algorithm 1 ends a tour with dual reductions all the way back to the first block. Lemma 1 proves the exact recursive predicate when the projected basis beyond the first block is identical before and after a complete loop. The paragraph following the proof explicitly leaves open whether this stopping test ever fires on every input. Section 4 caps the tour count for a Hermite-quality guarantee. Thus the audit wording in Part 86 that the predicate is “attained by a real algorithm” needs a stopping hypothesis. The 99% radii 0.12656799 and 0.36551297 at widths 406 and 506 hold simultaneously for predicate witnesses on a Haar event. They apply to any returned witness on that event. Conditioning on termination could itself reweight the input. At 647 and 800 a basis change inside the first block preserves the predicate while making the terminal height arbitrarily small. A fixed completion rule might exclude that example. Walter’s 2017 thesis identifies Gaussian-heuristic estimates for the oracle calls as the extra input for typical quality. Neither the dissertation nor the polynomial tour cap supplies a void estimate or a distributional transfer at finite modulus. [Original paper, Algorithm 1, Lemma 1 and §4](https://eprint.iacr.org/2015/1123.pdf) · [Walter, dissertation, Part I](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/5n51z56s) · [Part 86](wall1_dual_pinning.md)

**Li–Walter (2023), specializing the generalized slide of Aggarwal–Li–Nguyen–Stephens-Davidowitz.** Write $n=pb+s$, $0\le s<b$, $n\ge2b$. For an $(h,b,r)$-slide-reduced basis, $1\le r<b$, Theorem 2 gives $D_j\le h^{j(n-j)}D_n^{j/n}$ at every cut $j=ib+s$, $1\le i<p$, where $D_j$ is the rank-$j$ prefix covolume. Hence the terminal block has geometric mean at least $D_n^{1/n}h^{-(n-b)}$. For the ordinary generalized slide predicate with SVP factor $c$, one can take $r=1$ and $h=(c^2\gamma_b)^{1/(2(b-1))}$. Algorithm 1 with integral input, slack $0<\epsilon\le1$ and a rank-$b$ $\delta$-SVP oracle produces this predicate with $c=(1+\epsilon)\delta$ in polynomially many tours. At rank 1023 the two applicable cuts are **617** ($b=406,s=211$) and **517** ($b=506,s=11$). Both have $p=2$, so Definition 1 has a first twin condition and a terminal primal condition, with no intervening shifted dual block. Importing all of Part 86’s dual-window constraints here would give an invalid inference. The determinant lower bound still allows a short direction. The inequalities hold directly on integral q-ary inputs, but supply neither a height quantile nor the shear probabilities needed to refine them. [Li–Walter, Definitions 1 and 3, Theorems 1–2, Fact 3](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/140.pdf) · [ALNS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.03724)

**Albrecht–Ducas on the dual profile.** For the q-ary basis with initial plateaux at $q$ and 1, §4.3 explains an actual LLL constraint. The first length cannot increase and the last GS length cannot decrease. Reciprocal reversal preserves the relevant GS constraints. The length and slope of the intervening segment and the extrapolation to BKZ enter their ZGSA model. Even at exactly $q=3329$ the endpoint inequalities alone do not constrain a specified interior height to the predicted segment. There is no probability measure on fixed shears in this argument. [Albrecht–Ducas, §§4.2–4.3](https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/799.pdf)

## Void estimates on the fixed-profile space

**Kelmer, Theorem 1.** Use $d$ for the dimension of the window. Put $A=\operatorname{diag}(g_0,\ldots,g_{d-1})$, $L=A U\mathbb Z^d$, with $U$ upper unitriangular. Independent uniform entries above the diagonal in a unit interval give the invariant probability on the unipotent quotient: reduction by integer column additions successively chooses one representative off the boundary, and Haar measure in these coordinates is Lebesgue. Kelmer uses row lattices with the upper triangular group on the other side. Transposing and reversing coordinate and basis order gives his convention with diagonal entries in reverse order. Thus this theorem applies before imposing any shortest-head tests.

For a ball of radius $T\ge2$, let $N_T$ include the origin and put $V_T=\omega_d T^d/\det A$. The theorem gives
$$
\mathbb E_U |N_T-V_T|^2\le C(A)T^{d-1}\log^2 T.
$$
Take $2\le T<g_0$ and $V_T>1$. On the repository’s open-ball void event at $g_0$, $N_T=1$, so
$$
p(g)\le\min\left(1,\frac{C(A)T^{d-1}\log^2 T}{(V_T-1)^2}\right).
$$
Using a strictly smaller ball avoids any convention about the two deterministic head vectors on the boundary. One can rescale all lengths to arrange $T\ge2$, but this changes $C(A)$. The centering is by Euclidean volume, which can differ from the fixed-profile mean count. For a fixed lattice shape the bound is a large-radius discrepancy estimate. Here the test radius moves with the head, and the aim is suppression uniform over entire profile boxes. Neither the required constant nor exponential suppression in the fixed-profile competitor mean follows. An algorithm’s chosen shears have undergone additional selection, and uniform shear coordinates are continuous even when $q$ is fixed. [Kelmer, Theorem 1 and proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.06012) · [Repository’s fixed-profile event](wall1_void_haar_transfer.md)

**Dabbs–Kelly–Li, Theorem 2.** The same fixed-profile chart also admits a comparison for *smooth lattice observables*. Normalize $a=A/(\det A)^{1/d}$, and set $M(a)=\min_{1\le j<d}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}a_{ii}$. In the upper triangular column convention these are the proper fundamental weights. For fixed $d$, smooth compactly supported $f$ on the unimodular lattice space, the difference between its averages on $aU\mathbb Z^d$ and under Haar is bounded by $C(f,d)M(a)^{-\delta(d)}$ with $\delta(d)>0$. Decay requires **every** proper normalized prefix product to be large. For a normalized geometric diagonal $a_{ii}=\exp(t(d-1-2i)/2)$ with $t>0$, the products are $\exp(tj(d-j)/2)$, so the worst cut is an endpoint and $M=\exp(t(d-1)/2)$. Taking a huge middle product would greatly overstate the rate. The test $\lambda_1\ge a_{00}$ has a moving threshold and requires smoothing. In particular $\lambda_1\le a_{00}$ everywhere on this orbit, and equality can have positive orbit mass. A majorant must include equality. No effective 1% output bound or finite-$q$ transfer follows. [Dabbs–Kelly–Li, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.04341)

**Druţu–Peyerimhoff (2026), Theorem 1.6.** For $d\ge2$, the geometric horospheres of their specific maximal singular ray in the space of unimodular positive definite forms have error at most
$(C_d\|f\|_2+4\|\nabla f\|_\infty)\exp(-t\sqrt{d(d-1)}/8)$ for $t\ge T_d$ and $C^1\cap L^2$ tests, with explicit constants in (1.3)–(1.4). Their horosphere is a torus bundle over the full shape space in dimension $d-1$. It averages over the transverse shape that the repository fixes. This is an explicit comparison for a coarser conditioning, potentially relevant when integrating out a whole quotient. Applying that rate to a single GS diagonal or to a discrete q-ary orbit would require another theorem. [Druţu–Peyerimhoff, §1.2 and Theorem 1.6](https://doi.org/10.1515/forum-2024-0498)

## The quotient of a shortest vector and counts of reduced flags

Strömbergsson treats Haar avoidance conditioned on containing a specified point. The primitive component yields the precise primitive-pair integration formula in Laarhoven’s Proposition 1. For rank $d\ge2$ and radius $s$, the count-weighted intensity of an unoriented primitive vector and normalized orthogonal quotient $Q$ is
$d\omega_d s^{d-1}\,ds\,d\mu_{d-1}(Q)/(2\zeta(d))$ for rotation-invariant tests. To obtain the *shortest*-pair law, multiply this intensity by $w(s,Q)$, the conditional probability over the lift torus that the open $s$-ball contains no competitor. This follows by inserting the shortest indicator before integrating out the lifts. Ties between distinct lines have Haar probability zero. The resulting joint measure has total mass one because almost every lattice contributes exactly one pair. [Strömbergsson, §5](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/pdr021) · [Laarhoven, Proposition 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15265v1)

A useful support check follows immediately. Every projected vector lifts with parallel component of magnitude at most $s/2$ after adding a multiple of the marked vector. Thus $w=0$ if $\lambda_1(Q)<(\sqrt3/2)s^{d/(d-1)}$. Conversely $w=1$ if $\lambda_1(Q)\ge s^{d/(d-1)}$. The exponent includes the normalization of $Q$ by $s^{1/(d-1)}$. Only the intervening band needs integration over lifts for this single selection. This argument applies to a shortest vector of the entire space being projected, or locally to the quotient *within its tested block*. Treating that vector as globally shortest after a finite-width step would incorrectly exclude valid continuations. For finite $q$, the support inequalities persist geometrically, but the primitive intensity and lift law must be replaced.

## A rigorous primal decoding result with a different algorithm

Bai–Stehlé–Wen give a randomized polynomial-time reduction from BDD at relative radius $1/(\sqrt2\gamma)$ to $\gamma$-uSVP for polynomially bounded $\gamma>1$. The improvement over deterministic embedding uses random lattice sparsification before embedding. The guarantee concerns an oracle on the resulting promised instances. It does not assign a success probability to progressive BKZ on the original embedding. This is applicable to a q-ary base lattice directly **if the BDD promise is proved** and the prescribed randomized reduction is used. It supplies neither a terminal-height distribution nor a fixed-shear void bound. In particular, a better BDD radius is insufficient to improve the repository’s exact-SVP dimension certificate without accounting for the new calls. [Bai–Stehlé–Wen, ICALP 2016](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2016.76)
