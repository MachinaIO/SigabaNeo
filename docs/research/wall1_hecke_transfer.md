# Hecke transfer for the null lattice

For fixed dimension, the determinant-one graph lattices converge to Haar. This transfers the joint HKZ length law in Part 46 and, qualitatively, even the probability of an existential exact-BKZ terminal-length event. At rank 1023 the available sharp-cutoff estimate gives no numerical terminal quantile. The loss occurs between an operator estimate and point evaluation of a discontinuous statistic. One can see the loss already for the ambient ball, where the geometry of the boundary is known.

## Which orbit is averaged?

Let $q$ be prime, $n=N-m$, $0<m<N$, and write the null congruence as $x=Ay\pmod q$ in $\mathbb Z^m\times\mathbb Z^n$ (a sign change gives the same ensemble). Its kernel lattice has index $q^m$. Write $\nu$ for the law after multiplication by $q^{-m/N}$, and $\eta$ for the uniform measure obtained by lifting **all** $n$-planes in $\mathbb F_q^N$ and applying the same scaling. Smith normal form identifies the latter with the Hecke neighbors of $x_0=\mathbb Z^N$ of type $(q^m,1^n)$, where superscripts denote multiplicities. Indeed their quotients are elementary abelian of rank $m$, hence every lattice contains $q\mathbb Z^N$. The real scalar selects the unit-covolume representative of the rational diagonal correspondence. An average over all sublattices of index $q^m$ also includes other Smith types when $m>1$.

The graph law is $\eta$ conditioned on projection onto the last $n$ coordinates being invertible. There are $q^{mn}$ graphs, each with precisely one matrix. Dividing by the Gaussian binomial coefficient (count ordered independent frames and divide by the number of bases) gives
$$
p=\frac{q^{mn}}{{N\brack n}_q}
 =\prod_{i=1}^n\frac{1-q^{-i}}{1-q^{-(m+i)}}.
$$
Thus $\|\nu-\eta\|_{\rm TV}=1-p<1/(q-1)$, by the union bound for the numerator product. For bounded real tests the additive cost is at most $(1-p)\operatorname{osc}(f)$. For a rare event one can instead use $\nu(E)\le\eta(E)/p$. At 3329 the additive cost for an event is below 0.000301.

## Smooth tests at the standard lattice

Put $G=SL_N(\mathbb R)$, $X=G/SL_N(\mathbb Z)$, and let $\mu$ be Haar probability. For $N\ge3$ and $h=\min(m,n)$, the normalized Hecke averaging operator satisfies
$$
 \|T\|_{L^2_0(X)\to L^2_0(X)}\le B_q=
 \left(\frac{2\sqrt q}{q+1}\right)^h .
$$
Use the split, level-one case of Clozel–Oh–Ullmo and their extension to $GL_N$ modulo center. The strongly orthogonal roots pairing entries $i,N+1-i$ give $h$ valuation differences of one and all others zero. Their displayed rank-one spherical function at distance one is $2\sqrt q/(q+1)$. This retains the finite-prime factor usually absorbed into $O_N(q^{-h/2})$. The rank-one group case $N=2$ requires a different local bound. The qualitative Eskin–Oh theorem suffices for weak convergence of the full orbit. [Clozel–Oh–Ullmo, Theorems 1.1 and 1.7, split-case remark, §§4.7 and 5.1, Corollary 1.8](https://ullmo.ihes.fr/publications/COU-Invent.pdf) · [Eskin–Oh](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/ergodic-theory-and-dynamical-systems/article/abs/ergodic-theoretic-proof-of-equidistribution-of-hecke-points/1A965B731D15006602497C9833C0554A)

For example, fix a Lie algebra basis, $d=N^2-1$, integer $s>d/2$, and let $S_s$ be the square root of the sum of squared $L^2(\mu)$ norms of all derivative words through order $s$ for the real group action (including order zero). Then for smooth bounded $f$ of finite indicated norm,
$$
 |\nu(f)-\mu(f)|\le(1-p)\operatorname{osc}(f)+
 C_{x_0,s}B_q S_s(f-\mu(f)).
$$
Indeed $\eta(f)=Tf(x_0)$, point evaluation at this fixed lattice is bounded by local Sobolev embedding, and $T$ commutes with the real action. One can take $C_{x_0,s}$ to be the norm of point evaluation on this Sobolev space. It depends on the derivative normalization and has not been numerically bounded here. This argument requires no uniform Sobolev embedding in the cusp. Strömbergsson–Venkatesh give the sharper interpolated form using effective order $d/2+\epsilon$. [Small solutions to linear congruences and Hecke equidistribution, §3, (13)–(14)](https://math.stanford.edu/~akshay/research/andreas.pdf)

## Sharp thresholds

Take a symmetric exponential neighborhood $U_\delta$ of the identity of radius $\delta$ in fixed Lie coordinates. Smooth the indicator of $U_\delta E$ from the outside by convolution with a nonnegative unit-mass bump supported in $U_\delta$, and apply the same construction to the complement for the minorant. These bracket the indicator, and their Haar integral errors are bounded by the mass $b_E(C\delta)$ of the region where $U_{C\delta}$ can move a lattice across the event boundary. Here $C$ absorbs products of small neighborhoods. Rescaling a fixed chart bump gives derivative $L^1$ norms $O_{N,s}(\delta^{-j})$ at order $j$ by change of variables. Young's inequality gives the following sufficient bound:
$$
 |\nu(E)-\mu(E)|\le 1-p+b_E(C\delta)+C'_{N,s}B_q\delta^{-s}.
$$
There is also a finite inequality with no unspecified multiplicative constant. Choose a symmetric relatively compact identity neighborhood $U$ that embeds by $g\mapsto gx_0$, let $v=\mu(Ux_0)>0$, and set $E^+=UE$, $E^-=X\setminus U(X\setminus E)$ (using open $U$). For $x=gx_0$ in the patch, equivariance gives $T1_{E^-}(x)\le\eta(E)\le T1_{E^+}(x)$. Integrate against $1_{Ux_0}/v$, subtract the respective Haar means, and use Cauchy–Schwarz on the centered functions. In particular
$$
 \nu(E)\le\frac{\mu(E^+)+B_q\sqrt{(v^{-1}-1)\mu(E^+)(1-\mu(E^+))}}{p}.
$$
Applying this to the complement gives a lower bound. This formulation exhibits both required geometric quantities explicitly. For a fixed small chart ball scaled by $\delta$, $v\asymp_{N,x_0}\delta^d$ by smoothness of Haar density.

If $b_E(t)\ll_E t$, derivatives of positive order are supported in that layer and their $L^2$ norms are $O_{N,j,E}(\delta^{1/2-j})$. Interpolation and balancing yield the sharper $O_{N,E,\epsilon}(q^{-h/N^2+\epsilon})$ full-orbit discrepancy. The constants include the boundary estimate.

* **Ambient minimum and counts.** For a ball of fixed normalized radius $u$, changes in its count require a point in a shell with radii $u e^{\pm c\delta}$ by the singular-value bound. Siegel averaging bounds the layer by the volume of that shell, hence by $O_{N,u}(\delta)$. The same argument covers primitive counts, fixed annuli away from zero, and bounded tests of finitely many such counts. For the ordinary count including zero, Strömbergsson–Venkatesh prove the sharp-cutoff bound directly for compact regions containing a neighborhood of zero with Lebesgue boundary layer of order $\delta$. Their Lemma 5 and its optimization give the exponent $2\beta(m,N)/N^2$ with $\beta(m,N)=-h/2$ in higher rank. This applies in particular to the void event by requiring exactly one point including zero. For an unbounded factorial moment, convergence of bounded count tests gives convergence after a fixed truncation. Removing the truncation requires a uniform tail estimate under the discrete measures. Haar Rogers integrability alone supplies only the Haar tail. [Strömbergsson–Venkatesh, Lemmas 1, 4 and 5 and proof of (14)](https://math.stanford.edu/~akshay/research/andreas.pdf)
* **HKZ.** Away from ties of distinct shortest lines, the first shortest line is locally constant in integer coordinates. Locally the competing vectors in a bounded ball form a finite list, by bounded inverse matrix norm. Fixing that line gives continuous projected lattice coordinates in a local frame. Repeating in the quotient shows continuity of the full length profile at every lattice with unique shortest lines along its flag. Part 46 gives Haar-null ties even after selection, and a joint density for the normalized successive minima. Thus bounded continuous profile tests and threshold events with Haar-null level sets converge to the selected Haar law. Rectangles for the joint first length and intrinsic quotient shape likewise transfer at continuity boundaries. This is weak convergence of joint laws. Conditional laws at an exact length or vector require separate disintegration or a shrinking-window estimate. [Part 46](wall1_haar_prefix_law.md)
* **Existence of a fully BKZ basis.** Fix $2\le\beta\le N$ and $\rho>0$. Consider the intrinsic event that some basis satisfying all exact projected block-head minimum tests has $g_{N-\beta}<\rho$ (zero-based index). Its boundary is Haar-null. To see local finiteness, size-reduce by integral earlier-column additions, which preserve every prefix, projected block lattice and GS length. On a compact matrix chart the first basis length is at least the uniform positive ambient minimum. The two-vector comparison inside each block gives $g_{i+1}\ge\sqrt3\,g_i/2$. Hence all lengths have local positive lower bounds and, by their product one, local upper bounds. Size reduction now bounds the actual column lengths, giving finitely many integer basis labels on the chart. For each such basis, projected block coordinates vary continuously with bounded inverse on their span locally, so only finitely many integer competitors can beat the head. Squared GS lengths and squared competitor lengths are rational functions of the Gram entries with positive Gram-determinant denominators. Locally the event is a finite Boolean combination of analytic inequalities. Its boundary lies in nonidentically-zero equality sets (identical comparisons have constant truth value), hence has measure zero. A countable chart cover finishes the proof. Portmanteau therefore transfers its probability at fixed normalized threshold. This proof gives neither a dimension-controlled tube-volume constant nor the Haar probability of this event.
* **A particular reducer.** For a fixed basis section and algorithm the same conclusion holds if its output event is Haar-almost-everywhere continuous. For independent coins it suffices to prove this for almost every coin realization and integrate by bounded convergence. A $q$-dependent graph basis used for initialization is extra data. Weak convergence on $X$ does not prescribe a limiting basis section. The Part 47 independence statement concerns the plant conditional on the null input and reducer coins. It supplies no Haar estimate for the selected terminal minimum. [Part 47](wall1_decoupled.md)

## Size of the error at rank 1023

Here $h=511$, $\log_{10} B_q\simeq-746.19$, and the smallest integer Sobolev order above $d/2$ is **523265**. Already at $\delta=1/2$, the formal product $B_q\delta^{-s}$ exceeds $10^{156000}$ before chart and boundary constants. This illustrates the scale cost only. Even for a ball, the sharper optimized power $3329^{-511/1023^2}$ is about **0.9960**, before the implicit constant and $\epsilon$ loss. Rotation-invariant events can use local embedding on the shape space (symmetric determinant-one metrics, modulo a discrete group with finite local stabilizers); its dimension $N(N+1)/2-1=523775$ still costs order $261888$. None of these estimates certifies a numerical improvement of the Part 48 terminal bound.

A non-vacuous finite comparison *is* possible for one ambient event using its special arithmetic. At physical radius $R=443.0664$, Part 48 bounds the graph probability of $\lambda_1\le R$ by $0.01$. On Haar input of covolume $q^m$, primitive Siegel and Markov bound the same probability by $V_NR^N/(2\zeta(N)q^m)<0.00018$ (the expression without $\zeta(N)$ is about $0.00017374$). Thus the absolute discrepancy at this threshold is at most $0.01$. This uses two direct one-sided bounds and certifies no relative approximation of the probabilities. The known width-1000 certificate already uses the discrete ambient estimate. [Part 48, ambient count and deterministic propagation](wall1_null_terminal.md)

All convergence assertions above hold along primes with $N,m,\beta$ fixed and lengths measured at determinant one. A fixed normalized threshold $\rho$ corresponds to physical length $q^{m/N}\rho$. A fixed physical threshold instead shrinks to zero in normalized units. For the existential event, local finiteness and positivity proved above imply its Haar probability tends to zero as $\rho\downarrow0$; monotonicity and fixed-threshold convergence then give the same qualitative limit for a fixed physical threshold. Neither limit provides a rate at the physical threshold of interest. The remaining Wall 1 inputs are a quantitative Haar lower-tail law for the relevant finite-width selection and a transfer error small on that probability scale, with initialization accounted for. No GSA or independence heuristic enters the transfer.
