# What is known about the minimum of a BKZ terminal block?

**22 November 2026.** For a fresh Haar lattice of rank 1000, the elementary second-moment argument below certifies that the shortest length lies between 0.99540 and 1.00601 times the Gaussian-heuristic radius with probability at least 99%. There is also a finite-rank result for a genuinely adaptive projection: after removing **one globally shortest vector** from a Haar lattice of rank 1000, the normalized quotient minimum lies within 2% of the heuristic with probability above 99%. The second result is a deduction from primitive-vector integration. I found no corresponding theorem for the last full block after an ordinary finite-width BKZ tour. The distinction is which prefix the algorithm selects.

Write $n$ for ambient rank, $b=\beta$ for block rank, $P$ for the primitive sublattice generated by the first $n-b$ output vectors, and $Q=\pi_{P^\perp}L$. Then $\det Q=\det L/\det P$. Put $v_b=\pi^{b/2}/\Gamma(1+b/2)$ and $h(Q)=(\det Q/v_b)^{1/b}$. The target statistic is $R=\lambda_1(Q)/h(Q)$. Even when $R$ is known, an absolute terminal-height estimate needs $\det P$. An exact SVP-reduced terminal block has first GS length $\lambda_1(Q)$ at the time it is reduced. Specify input basis, oracle, tour count or stopping rule, and tie-breaking to define an algorithmic output law on Haar lattices. The results below also distinguish that law from a statement simultaneous over all fully reduced bases. ## Which profiles does the reduced-basis counting measure favor?

Kim–Venkatesh, IMRN 2018, Theorem 1.1, use reverse GS order. Write $a_i=\|x_i^*\|$ with projection off the *later* vectors. Their Siegel set has $\prod a_i=1$, all size-reduction coefficients of absolute value at most $1/2$, and $a_i/a_{i+1}\ge T^{-1}$ for fixed $T>1$. For Haar lattices, the number $N$ of such bases satisfies $n^{-3}\log E N\to(\log T)/6$. Under the Riemann hypothesis, $\operatorname{sd}(N)/E N\le e^{-c n^2}$ for sufficiently large $n$. In normalized Haar volume on the Siegel set, $a_n/T^{(n-1)/2}\to1$ in probability, unconditionally. Passing to a Haar lattice followed by a uniform reduced basis uses the concentration result (and one can discard empty fibers on the exceptional set). Part (iv)'s 99.9% concerns *existence* at $T=1.0001$ for sufficiently large $n$. No explicit rank-1000 threshold follows from the statement. For Lovász-LLL, the authors give the corresponding mean-count and mean-length calculations but leave the count concentration open. [Theorem 1.1, §§1.1, 3.5–3.6](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnx074)

The requested phrase “most bases are not GSA” needs a slope, a measure, and an approximation tolerance. In fact the paper's integration formula gives a profile close to an **extremal geometric progression**. Under Siegel-set volume the variables $Y_i=\log(Ta_i/a_{i+1})$ are independent exponentials of rates $i(n-i)$: substitute the simple-root ratios into the product Haar density in (3.8). Hence $E\sum Y_i=2H_{n-1}/n$. Since both profiles have determinant one, the maximum log-length deviation from the progression with ratios $1/T$ is bounded by $\sum Y_i$ (its range is that sum and its mean across coordinates is zero). The probability of deviation greater than $u$ is thus at most $2H_{n-1}/(nu)$. This finite-rank corollary concerns counted bases. The discrepancy observed for LLL is in the **slope**: the measured first length around $1.02^n$ is exponentially smaller than the typical counted length at $T=2/\sqrt3$. Neither a universal rejection of approximate GSA nor a BKZ sampling law follows. [Kim–Venkatesh, §§1, 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.00767)

Kim's *On the shape of a high-dimensional random lattice* is a 2015 dissertation preceding the joint article. Its reduced-basis counts and experiments on how often LLL reaches a basis motivate weighting by the product of the successive flag covolumes. The proposed relationship to output frequency is empirical. No calibrated likelihood or finite-dimensional error for that weighting was verified. Its separate short-vector sieve work became the articles below. [Dissertation](https://newton.kias.re.kr/~seungkikim/thesis.pdf)

A closer positive result for **projected** minima is Laarhoven's Proposition 3. With Haar probability $1-e^{-\Omega(n)}$, simultaneously for quasi-HKZ bases, the normalized log GS profile converges in bulk mean absolute deviation to $\frac12(1+\log(1-x))$, with $x=(i-1)/n$ bounded away from 1 and normalization $\log g_i/\log n$. In the HKZ tail these GS lengths are projected shortest lengths. Averaging permits an exceptional prescribed index, and log-scale convergence is much coarser than a fixed relative error in length. Constants certifying 99% at rank 1000 were not verified. [Proposition 3, eq. (4.10)](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15265v1) · [Repository discussion of the quantifiers](wall1_literature2.md)

## Haar short-vector laws and an explicit 99% benchmark

Let $X_b$ carry Haar probability on unimodular lattices. The volume coordinate $t=v_b r^b$ counts both signs geometrically. The mean number of nonzero pairs in a ball of volume $t$ is $t/2$. A primitive pair has mean density $1/(2\zeta(b))$.

* **Rogers, Schmidt, Södergren.** Rogers' integration formulas compute moments by separating linearly independent and dependent tuples. For fixed ball volume the pair counts converge to Poisson; Södergren proves weak convergence of the whole ordered volume process to rate $1/2$. In particular $\Pr(R>t^{1/b})\to e^{-t/2}$ for fixed $t$. Schmidt's void estimate, in Kim's Thm. 1.5 formulation, has additive error $O(e^{-cb})$ for $b\ge13$ and $t<Cb$. Without concrete constants this is insufficient for a numerical tail certificate. The limiting equal-tail 99% interval uses volumes $-2\log(.995)$ and $-2\log(.005)$, not a point mass at volume one. [Rogers and Schmidt results and references, Kim §§1–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.02880) · [Södergren](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.3623)
* **Kim.** The earlier inclusion–exclusion estimates treat counts in symmetric sets of volume $o(\sqrt b)$; the dissertation's Thm. 2.4 gives alternating finite sums with an explicit remainder. The later paper extends count probabilities to volume and count up to small absolute multiples of $b$: for a sign half of a symmetric set, volume $s<cb$, and integer $k<c'b$, the Poisson mass at $k$ with mean $s$ has additive error $O(e^{-c''b})$ (Prop. 3.3). Linear independence of representatives is a separate issue for higher successive minima (Props. 3.1–3.2). These estimates concern a fixed test set before basis selection. [Earlier paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.2005) · [Later paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02880)
* **Strömbergsson–Södergren.** For $f(b)\to\infty$ of subexponential growth, center the nonzero count in the ball of volume $t f(b)$ by that volume and divide by $\sqrt{2f(b)}$. On $0\le t\le1$ the process converges in distribution to Brownian motion. This describes larger-volume fluctuations; weak convergence gives no rank-1000 additive error. [Functional central limit theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.06332)
* **Klartag (2026).** Thm. 1.2 gives total variation at most $Ce^{-c'b}$ between $L\cap S$ and a unit-intensity Poisson process restricted to a **fixed** measurable $S$ with $S\cap(-S)=\varnothing$ and $0<|S|<cb$. It controls any statistic measurable from that point configuration, including data-dependent choices made entirely from it. A full BKZ trajectory uses information outside such a set. The theorem states universal constants, without optimizing them. [Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10479)

**Finite-rank deduction.** Count primitive vectors in a half-ball, one per sign pair, and denote this count by $M(t)$ for full ball volume $t$. For $b\ge3$, put $m=t/(2\zeta(b))$. Primitive Siegel integration gives $E M=m$. Rogers' independent ordered-pair integral gives $E[M(M-1)]=m^2$: distinct primitive representatives in the half-ball are independent, and Möbius inversion on the two integer contents multiplies the unrestricted independent-pair integral by $\zeta(b)^{-2}$. Consequently $\operatorname{Var}M=m$. Markov and Cauchy–Schwarz give $\Pr(M\ge1)\le m$ and $\Pr(M=0)\le1/(1+m)$. A ball contains a nonzero vector exactly when it contains a primitive pair. These are finite-dimensional inequalities with no asymptotic constants. [Rogers independent-tuple integral, Kim Lemma 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.02880) · [Primitive Siegel moment formulas](https://pure.tue.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/344597735/2310.03459v3.pdf)

Take $m_-=0.005$, $m_+=199$. A union bound gives at least 99% probability for
$$
 (0.01\zeta(b))^{1/b}\le R\le (398\zeta(b))^{1/b}.
$$
At $b=406$ this is contained in $[0.98872,1.01486]$, and at $b=1000$ in $[0.99540,1.00601]$ (rounded outwards). It applies directly at $b=n$ even after a basis change because the whole lattice survives. Using $n=1000$ in the exponent for a much smaller projected block would give a spurious improvement.

## What survives conditioning on a flag?

**Schmidt; Horesh–Karasik.** Schmidt studies the similarity classes of sublattices of $\mathbb Z^n$ ordered by covolume. The Horesh–Karasik refinement jointly equidistributes the span, primitive sublattice shape, and complementary shape as the height cutoff $X\to\infty$ at fixed ranks; the orthogonal integer lattice is dual to the projected factor lattice. The limiting measure is the product of the invariant measures. For bounded boundary-controllable shape domains their count has main term of order $X^n$ (positive-measure domains) and error $O_\epsilon(X^{n-1/(2n(n+1))+\epsilon})$ or better. One samples all eligible primitive sublattices by counting. Neither the height limit nor its implied constant certifies anything at a BKZ-selected height and rank 1000. [Schmidt, bibliographic record](https://geodesic.mathdoc.fr/item/MOMA_1998__125_4_178800/) · [Horesh–Karasik, joint counting theorem and error estimates](https://academic.oup.com/qjmath/article/74/4/1253/7191043)

**Kim's Haar flag formula.** For $0=d_0<d_1<\cdots<d_k<d_{k+1}=n$, the expected number of rational primitive flags with successive determinants at most $H_i$ is exactly $a(n,\mathbf d)\prod_i H_i^{d_{i+1}-d_{i-1}}$ (Thm. 5, with explicit normalization in the paper). It holds at finite rank. Bounding only the product height $\prod_i(\det P_i)^{d_{i+1}-d_{i-1}}$ gives infinite mean for $k\ge2$. For Wall 1, separate prefix determinant caps can restrict a first-moment sum over possible flags. A total flag count alone does not measure the fraction with a bad projected minimum. [Kim, Theorem 5 and corollary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.10874)

**One selection step with a finite error.** In the Haar integral summed over unoriented primitive vectors of length at most $s$, their determinant-normalized orthogonal quotient has Haar shape in rank $n-1$. The total intensity is $m=v_n s^n/(2\zeta(n))$. Let $A$ be a rotation-invariant bad quotient event of Haar probability $p$. When the globally shortest vector has length at most $s$, a bad selected quotient contributes at least one to the count of marked pairs with bad quotient. Otherwise the ball is empty. Integration and the finite-rank void bound therefore give
$$
 \Pr(Q_{\rm shortest}\in A)\le (1+m)^{-1}+mp .
$$
For $n=1000$, take $m=199$ and $A=\{R<0.98\text{ or }R>1.02\}$. At rank 999 the moment inequalities give $p\le 0.98^{999}/(2\zeta(999))+(1+1.02^{999}/(2\zeta(999)))^{-1}<10^{-8}$. The selected-quotient failure probability is below 0.005002. This deduction covers an exact SVP step in the whole Haar parent, or a first HKZ step there. Its proof integrates all candidate marks, so it never requires the shortest-selected quotient itself to be Haar. An ordinary BKZ head can be shortest only in its tested block; using the ambient void event for that selector would require a separate bound on its length. Iterating also changes the parent law. [Strömbergsson, conditional lattice measure, §5](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/pdr021) · [Primitive-vector quotient integral, Proposition 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15265v1)

**Remaining proof obligation.** For a finite-width Haar run one could seek a selector-specific tail comparison $\Pr(R_{\rm out}\in A)\le\eta+K\mu_b(A)$ for the two radius tails above. The one-step calculation exhibits actual values of $\eta$ and $K$ in a special case. For higher-rank prefixes the analogous calculation needs a usable bound on the probability the selected prefix escapes the covolume caps, and an integral counting the eligible prefixes with bad normalized quotient. Too many eligible flags can exhaust the 1% budget. Another sufficient route is a bound on the first and second moments of the primitive half-ball counts **under the selected quotient law** itself. This needs only two radii and avoids trying to identify the entire output distribution.

For fixed finite $q$ the normalized q-ary ensemble is discrete, so comparison in total variation with continuous Haar shape is generally impossible. An event-specific count or effective equidistribution for the selected-radius tests could still work. Even a pre-reduction coordinate quotient and a BKZ-selected quotient on the same modular input can have different laws. No numerical bound on $\eta+K\mu_b(A)$ for the repository's $q=3329$ terminal selector, or direct estimate of its two count moments, was verified. [Wall 1, Parts 1–3](../PROOF_GAP.md) · [Earlier q-ary equidistribution review](wall1_literature2.md)
