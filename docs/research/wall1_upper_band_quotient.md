# The upper-band quotient count at width 506

The plane in Part 161 lives in the rank-505 quotient *after* draw 406. Its short transverse line lives in rank 504. There is an exact finite-mark first moment for this line. Replacing shortestness of the first vector by membership in the band, however, makes even the Haar comparison too large. For every determinant in the good-history range, the Haar mean number of band candidates passing both gates exceeds **0.00152687**, even with the second vector required to be shortest and at most one hit counted per first vector. This is more than **7.72 times the entire shared allowance**. For a Haar window with its first shortest vector actually selected, the probability of the same gates is below **$4.361\cdot10^{-11}$**. The corresponding restart probability remains open. [Part 161 and audit](wall1_band_plane_hits.md)

## Which quotient contains the witness?

Fix a history $S$ immediately before draw 406 of the marginal restart for plain LWE $(511,512,3329)$, CBD(3). Use $b=506$, $d=505$, $D=\det M$ for the incoming rank-$d$ lattice, $V_i$ for unit ball volume, $g=(D/V_b)^{1/b}$, and $c=477/500$. Throughout the good-history band,
$$
 32.18553<g<203.888,\qquad a=0.990<\rho=s/g\le e=1.097193.
$$
Draw a uniform class $U\in H=K/M$, where $K=P_E\mathbb Z^{910}$, and form $W_U=(M,0)+\mathbb Z(\widetilde U,1)$. The first designated shortest line has generator $v$ of length $s$. For a vertical line orient it to positive height. Set
$$
 L_v=P_{v^\perp}W_U,\quad x=\text{designated shortest vector in }L_v,\quad
 t=|x|,\quad Q_{v,x}=P_{x^\perp}L_v,\quad h=(D/(sV_d))^{1/d}.
$$
The two gates are $1<t\le r=\sqrt{1+c^2h^2}$ and $\lambda_1(Q_{v,x})<R=ch\,t^{-1/d}$. The word “old” refers to $L_v$ before draw 407. The two successive quotient determinants are $D/s$ and $D/(st)$, respectively. [Part 120](wall1_plane_count.md) · [Part 153 and audit](wall1_neargh_vertical.md) · [Part 159](wall1_plane_hit_rounding.md)

**Witness correspondence.** Primitive unoriented lines of length less than $R$ in $Q_{v,x}$ correspond bijectively to saturated planes of area less than $tR$ through $x$ in $L_v$. Their inverse images in $W_U$ are saturated rank-three lattices of covolume less than $stR$. Indeed, quotienting a free lattice by a primitive generator identifies its quotient group with its orthogonal projection. A primitive line has a saturated inverse image. Adjoin a lift of a generator to the kernel basis and use Gram–Schmidt to multiply covolumes by $t$, then by $s$. Counting all primitive lifts $w$ separately would give infinitely many representatives $w+nx$ of a single witness.

There is a second issue if “old vector” is read as a vector of the incoming $M$. At positive height $k$, the index of $P_{v^\perp}(M,0)$ in $L_v$ is exactly $k$. Indeed the cokernel is $W_U/((M,0)+\mathbb Zv)\cong\mathbb Z/k\mathbb Z$ by height. An example with prime-order marks is $M=(15/2)\mathbb Z$, with displacement $3\bmod M$ in the order-five subgroup generated by $3/2$. Minimizing squared length in heights 1 through 5 gives $10,25/4,45/4,25,25$. The horizontal minimum has squared length $225/4$, and higher heights have length at least six. Thus $v=(-3/2,2)$ wins. The full projected spacing is $(15/2)/(5/2)=3$, whereas the projected old spacing is 6. A cutoff of 4 sees exactly one primitive sign pair in the full quotient and none in the old image. The example concerns the group identity, independently of the width-506 history law.

## Exact conditional first moment and its excess over event probability

Let $\mathcal V_S$ consist of all $v=(y,k)\in K\times\mathbb Z_{>0}$ in the given shell, keeping $v$ only if primitive in $W_{u_v}$, where $u_v=k^{-1}[y]\in H$. This is well defined because $k\le eg<3329$ and $3329H=0$. Force the projection along each such $v$ in its own window $W_{u_v}$. Determine $x,t,Q_{v,x}$ by the rule above. Let $G_v=\mathbf1_{1<t\le r}$, and let $N_v$ count primitive sign pairs in $Q_{v,x}$ of length strictly less than $R$. Write $J_v=1$ if $v$'s sign pair wins in $W_{u_v}$ under the prescribed ambient tie rule, and zero otherwise.

**Finite identity.** The requested first moment with only the band retained on $v$, the corresponding count with at most one hit per candidate, and the conditional selected probability are respectively
$$
 F_S=\frac1{|H|}\sum_{v\in\mathcal V_S}G_vN_v,\qquad
 A_S=\frac1{|H|}\sum_{v\in\mathcal V_S}G_v\mathbf1_{N_v>0},\qquad
 P_S=\frac1{|H|}\sum_{v\in\mathcal V_S}J_vG_v\mathbf1_{N_v>0}.
 \tag{1}
$$
In particular the exact overcount is
$$
 F_S-P_S=\frac1{|H|}\sum_{v\in\mathcal V_S}G_v
 \left[(N_v-1)_++(1-J_v)\mathbf1_{N_v>0}\right].
 \tag{2}
$$
The two summands distinguish several witnesses for one candidate from one or more witnesses for a losing candidate.

*Proof.* A candidate is present precisely for $kU=[y]$, so summing over the uniform displacement contributes exactly $1/|H|$ for that candidate, including all of its witnesses. Positive height orients the sign pair uniquely. Each successful window has a unique designated first line. Subtract the indicator for that line from the candidate count to obtain (2). In particular there is no second independent displacement to average after specifying $v$. Part 142's Voronoi representative selects a lift of each uniform class. Conditioning on a specified height winning restricts the admissible classes, so uniformity on the original set cannot be reused under that conditioning. [Part 108](wall1_first_tour.md) · [Part 142, Theorem B and audit](wall1_dual_cusp.md) · [Part 161, Proposition 4](wall1_band_plane_hits.md)

For comparison, the literal count of primitive old vectors in $M$ projected along the first candidate has conditional mean
$$
 \frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{v=(y,k)\in\mathcal V_S}
 \sum_{w\in M_{\rm prim}/\{\pm1\}}
 \mathbf1_{\,s^2|w|^2-\langle w,y\rangle^2<s^2 T_v^2}
$$
for any positive physical cutoff $T_v$ fixed by that candidate and history. This follows from the same mark identity and the squared projection formula. The inner sum is finite since $s^2=|y|^2+k^2$ implies $|w|<sT_v/k$ for each hit. It computes a statistic in the index-$k$ image described above. Even when $k=1$, its transverse rank is 505 and a short vector there alone does not test the plane gate in rank 504.

The sums admit a finite raw-vector implementation. For the gated $x$, round a lift along $v$ to squared norm at most $t^2+s^2/4$. For a witness, round first along $x$ in $L_v$, then along $v$ in $W_{u_v}$, obtaining squared norm less than $R^2+t^2/4+s^2/4$. Project these lists, identify equal vectors and signs, and retain primitive lines in the final quotient. The unconditional upper-band probability is exactly $\mathbb E_S[\mathbf1_{g<203.888}P_S]$. The expectation is over the uniform $400\times510$ graph prefix and its deterministic selections. Equations (1)–(2) evaluate the last random draw symbolically. Bounding the prefix average is a separate step.

## The Haar comparison after dropping the first selection

Use the Haar shape at fixed determinant $D$, scaled to give the same $g$. Count each primitive first line once, with its length in the specified band. Haar-almost surely no line lies in a fixed horizontal hyperplane. This is the rotationally invariant comparison for the band-only count. Haar has no distinguished integral height or mark group. Denote its mean number of successful candidates by $A_{\rm H}(D)$, and its mean number of witnesses by $F_{\rm H}(D)$, using the shortest $x$ in both.

The Haar primitive-line incidence in rank $n$, covolume $\Delta$, has mean $V_n u^n/(2\zeta(n)\Delta)$ in a radius-$u$ ball. Under the measure counting those marks, a mark of length $u$ has transverse Haar shape at determinant $\Delta/u$, with uniform lift character on its torus. This statement applies again to a primitive line in the transverse shape. It follows by the stabilizer disintegration in Part 105, then by scaling, or equivalently by iterating the marked integral for nonnegative functions. Lean's `part105` bounds an **oriented** primitive count with a short-quotient indicator. The sign-pair counts below divide the first orientation by two. The theorem `haarWindowTemplate_mu` bounds failure to find *some* acceptable candidate in a Haar window. It does not impose the prescribed shortest-line selector on the finite graph window. [Haar selection law and audit](wall1_selection_law.md) · [Primitive mean-value formula](https://doi.org/10.3934/jmd.2025015) · [TransverseMinimumBound.lean](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/TransverseMinimumBound.lean) · [HaarWindowTemplate.lean](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/HaarWindowTemplate.lean) · [Part 136 audit](wall1_restart_certificate.md)

Two scale checks explain why the quoted 0.006237% does not answer the question.

* That bound uses the first **selected** shortest vector and cutoff $0.925$ times the quotient's own GH. With the first selection removed, the exact Haar mean of primitive line pairs $(v,x)$ for this same one-quotient cutoff over the band is $(e^b-a^b)0.925^d/(4\zeta(b)\zeta(d))\in(481.64526,481.64528)$. This counts witnesses, whereas the probability bound in Part 105 includes the shortest-vector restriction.
* For the actual second quotient the own-GH ratio $\beta=R/\mathrm{GH}(Q_{v,x})$ satisfies $\beta^{d-1}=(V_{d-1}/V_d)c^{d-1}t^{1/d}/h$ by the determinant identity. On the gate $t>1$ and $h<204$, so $\beta>0.94810$. A bound on the event below 0.925 omits part of the requested ball.

**Theorem.** Uniformly for $32.18553<g<203.888$,
$$
 0.00152687<A_{\rm H}(D)\le F_{\rm H}(D)<0.025923.
 \tag{3}
$$
The lower bound already counts each first candidate at most once. Both gates and shortestness of $x$ are retained.

*Lower bound.* Put $n=d-1$, $B=(e^b-a^b)/(2\zeta(b))$. This is the exact mean of first lines in the band. For each marked $v$, count primitive lines $x$ in the shell $0.99ch<t<ch$. Its mean is $(1-0.99^d)c^d/(2\zeta(d))$. We have $32.02610<h<204$ on the whole band by $h=g(V_b/(\rho V_d))^{1/d}$. Hence $t>1$ and the length gate holds. In the quotient by each marked $x$, count primitive lines $z$ with $0.98R<|z|<R$. Their mean is at least
$$
 (1-0.98^n)\frac{V_n c^n}{2\zeta(n)V_d\,204},
$$
using $t^{1/d}>1$ in the scale identity above.

For this twice-marked transverse calculation, impose two further conditions. First require the lift character of $x$ evaluated at $z$ to have distance at least $1/4$ from zero modulo one. This costs exactly half of the character measure. The value is uniform even given the transverse lattice and $z$, since a primitive $z$ extends to a basis. Second require that the quotient by $z$ in $Q_{v,x}$ have minimum greater than $ch$. Under primitive incidence at $z$ this quotient is Haar of determinant $D/(st|z|)$. By the primitive mean and Markov, the excluded mass is at most
$$
 \frac{V_{d-2}(ch)^{d-2}st|z|}{2\zeta(d-2)D}
 \le \delta:=\frac{V_{d-2}}{2V_d}c^d<1.889\cdot10^{-9},
$$
since $t,|z|\le ch$ and $D=sV_dh^d$. Both conditions thus hold on at least $1/2-\delta$ of the incidence measure at the fixed lengths.

On this set every vector of $Q_{v,x}$ independent of $z$ has length greater than $ch$. Also
$$
 |z|/t>0.98\,204^{-1/d}>0.96973>\sqrt{15}/4 .
$$
All lifts of $\pm z$ into $L_v$ therefore have squared length at least $|z|^2+t^2/16>t^2$. Lifts of higher nonzero multiples have length at least $2|z|>t$. Vectors projecting to zero are multiples of $x$. Thus $x$ is the unique shortest line, and $z$ is the unique primitive witness below $R$. Counting these flags counts distinct successful first candidates. Multiplying the three shell means or lower bounds by $1/2-\delta$ gives a lower bound in $(0.00152687791,0.00152687793)$. This proves the first inequality of (3).

*Upper bound.* Drop shortestness of $x$ as well. At $\rho$, the mean number of first lines has density $b\rho^{b-1}/(2\zeta(b))$. For each first line the density for $x$ at length $t$ is $d\,t^{d-1}/(2\zeta(d)h^d)$. The mean number of last lines is $V_{d-1}c^{d-1}t^{1/d}/(2\zeta(d-1)V_d h)$. Integrating the product over $a<\rho\le e,\ 1<t\le r$ gives the exact Haar mean with both first and second selections removed. To bound it set
$$
 h_0=32.02610,\qquad X=\sqrt{c^2+h_0^{-2}},\qquad
 Z=\frac{V_{d-1} c^{d-1}\,204^{1/d}}{2V_d h_0}.
$$
Here $r/h\le X$, $r<204$, and the last-line mean is at most $Z<6.970\cdot10^{-12}$. Dropping the zeta denominators gives $(e^b-a^b)X^d Z/4<0.025923$, as claimed.

## What first selection costs in the Haar comparison

**Corollary.** If $v$ is actually the shortest line of the Haar window, the probability of both gates and the upper band is less than $4.361\cdot10^{-11}$, uniformly over the same determinants.

*Proof.* Split the band at $u=1.05$, the internal radius already used in Part 161. Through $u$ the upper bound just proved with $e$ replaced by $u$ is below $5.653\cdot10^{-12}$. Above $u$, shortestness implies an empty open $ug$-ball of primitive lines. The Haar primitive-ball second moment gives probability at most $1/(1+u^b/(2\zeta(b)))<3.796\cdot10^{-11}$. Adding the unrounded quantities gives $4.36056\cdot10^{-11}$ rounded up. [HaarMinimumLower.lean, primitiveBallPairs_hit_lower](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/HaarMinimumLower.lean) · [Part 161, Proposition 3](wall1_band_plane_hits.md)

Thus forgetting first selection changes the Haar mean of successful candidates by a factor exceeding $3.50\cdot10^7$ relative to the selected probability. A bound of the form “restart band-only moment at most $C$ times the Haar band-only moment” with $C\ge1$ cannot by itself certify even the shared $0.00019770868$. This conclusion applies to the specified band-only relaxation. Retaining smaller-key exclusions or the within-height nearest-representative condition gives a different count. In particular (3) gives no lower bound on the actual restart count.
