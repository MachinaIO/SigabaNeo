# What the width-506 failure budget certifies

**There is no feasible allowance for 99% overall success from the stated Lean bound.** Even granting zero probability to the upper band and the inherited event, the uniqueness and Babai error bounds in `restartReduction_lwe_babai` cannot simultaneously fit 1% on any accepted physical basis. This holds for every disjoint partition of the 511 rows at the specified width. It is a limitation of the certificate. It gives no upper bound on the success probability of the actual decoder.

There is a separate numerical answer for the Part 151 subproblem. Using the audited constants without intermediate rounding, **0.0001977086810795** suffices as an allowance for the upper band plus residual (a) to establish the local bound on $\Pr(B)$. Even establishing that local bound would leave two terms open in the terminal-plane estimate.

## The two height-zero thresholds

Write $d=505$, $\mu=1+3d/2=758.5$, $v=9d/2=2272.5$. Part 94 requires the horizontal minimum to exceed the projected plant length, as well as conditions at height one and higher heights. Part 103 uses the *lower* tail of the squared projected plant length. If the squared minimum is $\mu-t$ with $t>0$, height-zero success has probability at most $v/(v+t^2)$ by one-sided Cantelli. For this upper bound to reach 0.99 requires $t^2\le v/99$. This gives the necessary floor $\sqrt{\mu-\sqrt{v/99}}\in(27.45375,27.45377)$. Part 114 shows that $0.954$ times its guaranteed terminal GH radius $28.8095$ exceeds this floor. [Part 94](wall1_threshold.md) · [Part 103](wall1_width_scan.md) · [Part 114 and audit](wall1_idea_drift.md)

Lean's sufficient bound uses the *upper* tail. It requires $a=L^2-\mu>0$ and charges $v/(v+a^2)$ before any theta, Babai or design error. The certified minimum inferred from the two reported floors alone is $0.954\cdot28.8095=27.484263$, whose square minus $\mu$ is $-3.115287346831$. Moreover, even allocating the full 1% to this one term requires $a^2\ge99v$ by cross multiplication, hence $L\ge\sqrt{\mu+\sqrt{99v}}\in(35.11150,35.11152)$. These statements use the published deterministic GH floor. An individual design can have a larger physical GH radius. No relaxation of 0.954 using that floor gives the proposed sufficient criterion. ## Incompatibility on a complete physical basis

Consider any block with $0\le r\le511$ rows, $N=512+r$ horizontal coordinates, terminal horizontal rank $d=505$ and prefix rank $J=N-d=r+7$. Use the full prefix for the physical Babai lift and the projected horizontal lattice of that same block for the terminal test. These identifications matter because the abstract Lean theorem takes the prefix and final data as arguments. Denote the certified terminal Gram–Schmidt floor by $g$ and the individual prefix floors by $g_j$. All floors are nonnegative. [Restart theorem and prefix test](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/RestartLift.lean) · [Physical decoder](wall1_decoupled.md)

**Determinant identity.** The horizontal graph lattice has index $3329^r$ in $\mathbb Z^{N}$: the map $(x,s)\mapsto x-As\pmod{3329}$ is onto by varying $x$, and the lattice is its kernel. Its covolume is therefore $3329^r$. Complete the primitive prefix to a lattice basis. The orthogonal projections of the remaining columns form a basis of the terminal lattice. Gram–Schmidt in this order factors the original covolume into the prefix product and the terminal covolume. Either factor is invariant under a basis change for its lattice. The physical prefix is primitive because each selected shortest vector is primitive in its window and extends to a basis there, with the unread columns completing the basis. Consequently on every accepted block,
$$
  \left(\prod_{j<J}g_j\right)g^{505}\le3329^r. \tag{1}
$$

**No accepted block can meet both numerical error bounds.** Suppose $\varepsilon,\ell\le0.01$ satisfy the theorem's error hypotheses. The Babai sum is nonnegative term by term, so $2\exp(-g_j^2/12)\le0.01$ for every $j$. At $g_j=7$ the term exceeds $0.03370$, and it decreases with the nonnegative floor. Hence every $g_j>7$.

The height $67$ occurs in the Lean sum for every block, since $67^2\le9\cdot512+1\le9N+1$. Every theta excess is nonnegative by its term at zero. For the height-67 excess alone, retain the integer indices $0,\pm1$ in the Gaussian sum and apply $(1+x)^{505}\ge1+505x$ for $x\ge0$. This gives
$$
 \varepsilon\ \ge\ \operatorname{reductionError}(N,505,g,L)
 \ \ge\ 1010\exp\!\left(-g^2/(12\cdot67^2)\right).
$$
At $g=700$ the last quantity exceeds $0.11319$. Thus $g>700$. These loose constants already contradict (1). Indeed
$$
 \frac{7^{r+7}700^{505}}{3329^r}
 \ \ge\ \frac{7^{518}700^{505}}{3329^{511}}>1,
$$
since the ratio decreases with $r$, and the log of its value at 511 lies in $(171.84,171.86)$. This argument uses the actual height range of the formal bound, including its worst-case norm cap. Omitting a high-height summand changes the error bound. [Definition of thetaExcess](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/UniquenessProbability.lean) · [Definition of reductionError](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/ConditionalReduction.lean)

For the theorem's right-hand side $\prod_i\delta_i+\varepsilon+\ell$ to be at most 0.01, nonnegativity forces both error allowances to be at most 0.01. Under those allowances the previous argument makes acceptance empty on every block, so every rejection probability is one. The product is then one for every number of blocks. In particular the feasible set is empty even at $P+p_a=0$. Repeating the full-row run would also fail the theorem's injective row-index hypothesis for two such blocks. The “restart” of Part 117 is a coupling within one pass, while the product in Part 134 is over independent disjoint row blocks. [Part 117](wall1_idea_round2.md) · [Part 134](wall1_breakthrough_r1.md) · [Formal restart bound](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/RestartReduction.lean)

## Following the event through the plane counts

1. **Original pass to suffix.** Part 117 bounds the probability of a coupling discrepancy by $0.00022304$. The indicators of a terminal event agree off the discrepancy, hence its probability on the original pass is at most its suffix probability plus this charge. This gives a $0.00977696$ suffix budget for a 1% *terminal-ratio* failure target. Part 120 already allows any split whose sum fits this budget. Redistributing its two equal shares leaves their sum unchanged. [Part 117](wall1_idea_round2.md) · [Part 120 and audit](wall1_plane_count.md)

2. **Final draw (407).** Let $h_{\rm long}$ be the long-line horizontal charge and $F_{\rm rem}$ the remaining positive-height witness moment. Parts 147, 148 and 150 give a sufficient sum constraint $h_{\rm long}+F_{\rm rem}\le0.0097738000$. Indeed their other majorants add at most $2^{-135.60}+3.114918\cdot10^{-6}+9.35974\cdot10^{-10}+1.872\cdot10^{-9}+4.21822\cdot10^{-8}+2^{-909.45}$, which together with that sum is below $0.00977696$. The small terms bound disjoint or successively removed classes in those notes. The coupling is charged once to the terminal event. [Part 147](wall1_uncovered_lines.md) · [Part 148](wall1_vertical_multiplicity.md) · [Part 150](wall1_shortened_lines.md)

3. **Earlier history (406).** Part 149's $B$ requires an old plane witness and a penultimate minimum in its specified short range. If $A(S)$ is the conditional long-line charge, then $0\le A(S)\le1$ and
   $h_{\rm long}=\mathbb E[1_B A(S)]+\mathbb E[1_{B^c}A(S)]$.
   An upper bound on $\Pr(B)$ bounds only the first summand, since $1_B A(S)\le1_B$. Part 149 also gives a lower bound on this first summand, which explains the *necessary* smallness of $\Pr(B)$. Part 151 decomposes $B$. Its audit explicitly retains the $B^c$ issue. The positive-height moment $F_{\rm rem}$ at draw 407 is a second outstanding obligation. Thus the room between $0.009$ and $0.0097738000$ is not established disposable slack. 4. **Lower strip and large-GH exit inside $B$.** Write $P$ for the two-gate, good-history upper-band mass above $0.990$ GH and $p_a=\Pr(H_{\rm old})$. By Part 151's union bound, the three small draw-406 charges have sum less than $2.399269327\cdot10^{-9}$. Part 153's exit costs at most $1/816000$. On its complement Part 161 bounds the entire lower strip by $C(0.990)^{506}$, where its audited enclosure is $1.42274374393705<C<1.42274374393706$. A selected lower-strip hit has at least one positive-height candidate inside this ball, so the candidate first moment bounds its probability. This cumulative charge includes the earlier Part 159 strip. Tightening the printed $0.008801064$ by direct substitution gives a charge below $0.008801063429455084$. Consequently
   $$
   \Pr(B) < P+p_a+2.399269327\cdot10^{-9}
              +1/816000+C(0.990)^{506}.
   $$
   The remainder below $0.009$ lies in $(0.00019770868107951,0.00019770868107958)$. With Part 151's rounded $2.4\cdot10^{-9}$ it lies in $(0.00019770868034883,0.00019770868034891)$, recovering Part 161's figure. The first remainder is the supremum obtained by subtracting these particular majorants. It makes no optimality claim about radial majorants. Even deleting the lower-strip charge altogether would affect only this line of the argument. ## Numerical verification

All displayed numerical comparisons were checked in scratch space with 70-decimal-digit outward interval arithmetic (`mpmath.iv`). For reproduction, enter decimals as strings, take $C$ as the interval with the two audited endpoints, and compute $0.009-u-1/816000-C(0.990)^{506}$ once for each stated $u$. For the determinant comparison evaluate $518\log7+505\log700-511\log3329$, or compare the integer powers directly. The determinant incompatibility is a mathematical deduction for the physical instantiation of the cited theorem. It has not been formalized in Lean.

## Audit

**Verdict — SOUND for the physical width-506 certificate.** Every ordered basis of every eligible row block fails at least one acceptance test when the two numerical error allowances fit within 1%. Thus the product of rejection probabilities is exactly one. The result concerns the theorem’s upper bound on failure. The actual probability of successful decoding remains undetermined.

**Which basis the theorem tests.** In `restartReduction_lwe_babai`, the acceptance predicate is the conjunction of `FinalBlock.Good` and `prefixFloor`. It tests a possibly different nonnegative floor $g_m$ at each $0\le m<J$, one positive common floor $g$ at *every* terminal GS index $0\le i<d$, and the independent norm floor $L$ on every nonzero terminal integer-lattice vector. The gap hypothesis requires $a=L^2-1-3d/2>0$. Only the Cantelli term depends on $L$. The theta terms depend on $d,g,k$, with $N$ determining the last height. The abstract theorem accepts $R$, the prefix, and $J$ as separate inputs. The physical identification follows directly in `restartAttack_lwe`, where the reducer returns one ordered integer basis and $J+d=N$. Primitivity of its prefix follows from basis completion alone. [Restart acceptance](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/RestartReduction.lean) · [Prefix and allowances](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/RestartLift.lean) · [Terminal tests](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/ConditionalReduction.lean) · [Canonical physical basis](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/AttackLattice.lean)

**Covolume and numerical margin.** Lean uses the residue $z_{\rm err}+Az_{\rm sec}$, which is surjective by setting the secret coordinates to zero. Changing the sign convention preserves the index. The kernel has rank $N=512+r$ and covolume $3329^r$. The height-zero embedding is isometric. Projecting the last 505 columns off the first $J=r+7$ leaves the remaining diagonal lengths in Gram–Schmidt unchanged, so the two GS products together equal this covolume. The unit height gives the oracle its 506th dimension and contributes no terminal horizontal GS floor.

An independent 80-decimal-digit outward interval calculation gives the following **necessary** componentwise floors when each indicated term is granted the entire 1% budget. The prefix term gives $p=\sqrt{12\log 200}\in(7.973694,7.973695)$ for each $g_m$. The linear lower bound on the height-67 term gives $t=67\sqrt{12\log 101000}\in(787.854220,787.854221)$ for $g$. The Cantelli term gives $L\ge\sqrt{758.5+\sqrt{99\cdot2272.5}}\in(35.111507,35.111508)$. The log of $p^{r+7}t^{505}/3329^r$ decreases with $r$ and is in $(299.019432,299.019433)$ at $r=511$. Even the note’s weaker integer floors give $(171.848708,171.848709)$ there. These are relaxations of the joint budget. There is no jointly feasible minimum on a physical basis.

As a cross-check, the strictly decreasing **full theta sum alone** crosses 0.01 at $g\in(823.751016,823.751017)$ for $N=512$ and at $g\in(1180.672365,1180.672366)$ for $N=1023$. To reproduce the enclosures, set $u=\exp(-g^2/(12k^2))$ at each height and enclose the one-dimensional theta sum between $1+2u$ and $1+2u+2u^4/(1-u^5)$ before taking the 505th power and subtracting one. The upper endpoint follows because consecutive square exponents from $2^2$ onward differ by at least five. Sum over heights $1$ through $\lfloor\sqrt{9N+1}\rfloor$ and check the signs at each pair of endpoints.

**Certificate scope and correction.** To avoid this specific obstruction in a certificate statement, express the final-block obligation as a bound on the conditional probability of failing Part 94’s all-heights event. Keep that obligation explicit until it is independently proved. This removes the inference that all 505 terminal GS lengths must exceed $t$. Substituting actual prefix lengths in the existing exponential sum leaves the contradiction intact, since each actual length must still exceed $p$. Dropping early prefix terms would require a separate proof of successful lifting on those indices. Size reduction alone supplies no such probability bound.
