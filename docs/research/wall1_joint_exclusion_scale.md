# Joint exclusion at draws 289–406 and 290–406

Let $p(S,k)$ be the conditional probability that the next $k$ signed winners all come from the window already read at the framed prefix $S$. Take $(i,k)=(288,118)$ or $(289,117)$, where $S$ is the retained state after draw $i$. For each pair the following extrema over **reachable prefixes of the specified Part 108 restart** are exact:
$$
                 \min_S p(S,k)=0,\qquad \max_S p(S,k)=1.                 \tag{1}
$$
Thus the largest divisor guaranteed on every prefix by testing the later marks is exactly **one**. At the opposite extreme the tests can eliminate an entire putative flag. Even requiring a positive-height winner at $i$, maximal mark order, and a terminal old plane with minimum above 24 and area below both cutoffs permits $p(S,k)>1-10^{-561}$. The constructions below distinguish these pointwise statements from the probability weighted by first-hit geometry on the actual restart.

## Which probability multiplies

At entry let $M\subset K$ be the retained lattice and projected integer grid, $H=K/M$, $h=|H|$. Freeze the designated shortest lines in the **old section alone**. Write $M_t^o\subset K_t^o$ for the old lattices after $t$ such freezes, $\rho_t:H\to K_t^o/M_t^o$ for the quotient map, and $s_t=\|w_t\|$ for the old winning length at step $t$ (before its freeze). The $k$ independent marks at entry are the columns of a uniform map $U:\mathbb F_q^k\to H$. At step $t$, a candidate with nonzero fresh coefficient tuple $b\in\mathbb Z^t$ and old component $z\in K_{t-1}^o$ occurs precisely when $\rho_{t-1}(U\bar b)=[z]$, with zero padding in $\bar b$. Enumerate those candidates whose signed key precedes the old winner and let $D_{t,z,b}$ be the set of solutions of this equation. First disagreement with the old flag gives
$$
       p(S,k)=1-\frac{|\bigcup_{t,z,b}D_{t,z,b}|}{h^k}.                 \tag{2}
$$
Keeping only supports contained in a single coordinate gives exactly $\prod_{j=1}^k(1-|A_j|/h)$, where $A_j\subset H$ is the **union of distinct forbidden mark values** for coordinate $j$, over heights and stages. With a single stage these fractions can equivalently be computed in the image groups of orders $h_j$. Counting short points with multiplicity can overcount a forbidden value. The singleton product bounds (2) from above. For the full event a product is exact if its factors are the successive *conditional* survival probabilities given all earlier successes. A zero prefix probability terminates that product. [Part 201, joint count and audit](wall1_multistep_branch.md#audit) · [Part 176, inverse fibers](wall1_inverse_branch.md#audit)

At the original cutoff the $j=0$ radial term requires a factor below $10^{-620.27531922}$ if it alone receives the entire quoted allowance. Its 118th root is about $5.5390\cdot10^{-6}$. The corresponding 117th root at the enlarged cutoff is about $3.2183\cdot10^{-6}$. These are geometric means. A single impossible selection suffices, and the remaining conditional factors need not be small. [Part 201, audited divisors](wall1_multistep_branch.md#audit)

## A sufficient geometric ceiling near the Gaussian heuristic

There is a checkable regime in which even the full joint test can remove at most half the mass. Let $D_t=\det M^o_{t-1}$, $G_t=(D_t/V_{506})^{1/506}$ and $R_t$ be a covering-radius upper bound for the grid $K_{t-1}^o\times\mathbb Z^t$. One may take $R_t=\sqrt{505+i+t}/2$ by rounding in the original ambient integer grid before projection. For $s_t<q$ at every step,
$$
 p(S,k)\ \ge\ \max\left(0,1-\sum_{t=1}^k
          \left(\frac{\sqrt{s_t^2+R_t^2}}{G_t}\right)^{506}\right).     \tag{3}
$$
Indeed, at stage $t$ every nonzero $b$ in the ball is nonzero modulo $q$. Each candidate sign pair occurs with probability $1/|K_{t-1}^o/M_{t-1}^o|$ under the original uniform $U$. In the rank-506 grid, at least the inward half of the centered Voronoi cell at each candidate fits inside radius $\sqrt{s_t^2+R_t^2}$, by the squared norm identity. The cells have disjoint interiors. Counting sign pairs cancels the factor two, and multiplying the grid determinant by the image group order gives $D_t$. Sum over stages before conditioning on any success. This is a union bound allowing all ties. [Part 198, radial cell estimate](wall1_flag_horizon.md#the-finite-sum-and-the-dual-test)

In particular, $\sqrt{s_t^2+R_t^2}\le (2k)^{-1/506}G_t$ throughout implies $p(S,k)\ge1/2$. The thresholds lie in $(0.9892600,0.9892601)$ for 118 and $(0.9892766,0.9892767)$ for 117. This sufficient condition uses the physical cell radius and every forced old section. A qualitative assertion that winners are typical near GH supplies neither these inequalities nor a bound on the weight of prefixes satisfying them.

## Prefixes attaining survival one

Use the graph sign convention $x=Ay\pmod q$, $q=3329$, with 400 horizontal axes and 105 initial graph inputs. Put $a=k-106$ (12 or 11). Reserve one shared horizontal axis and $a$ auxiliary horizontal axes. The initial inputs have distinct coefficients on the shared axis from $1532,1533,\ldots$ and zero elsewhere. Use successive unused labels in this list when adding further shared inputs. Even after the last such addition the largest label is below $q/2$.

Here is the one-dimensional calculation needed to verify the construction. A free horizontal row starts with period $L=q/\sqrt n$ at $n=1$. After height-one pivots confined to that row and its own appended coordinates, its original horizontal unit projects to signed length $1/\sqrt n$ (choose the positive orientation). Appending coefficient $m<q/2$ on this row gives the vector $(m/\sqrt n,1)$. If it wins, the unit normal in this two-dimensional summand is proportional to $(1,-m/\sqrt n)$. The new period and horizontal generator therefore have the same form with $n\gets n+m^2$. A stored shared input with coefficient $c$ has old component $c/\sqrt n$ modulo the current period when this calculation is applied to the shared row. Its own input axis stays orthogonal to the row.

* Process each auxiliary row separately. While $q^2\ge3n$, append $m=\min(\lfloor7\sqrt n/5\rfloor,1498)$ on that row. The proposed vector has squared length at most $1+49/25<3$, less than $L^2$. It has the unique shortest horizontal representative at height one, and heights of magnitude at least two are too long. The independent shared graph has minimum at least $\sqrt3$: no input unit qualifies, and the distinct unsigned labels exclude two-input vectors of squared norm two with zero horizontal part. There is also no input with horizontal part of magnitude one at squared norm two. After 16 pivots $n=3902799$ and $1<L^2<3$. Append the next shared input. The auxiliary period now wins and projecting it out leaves that input in the shared graph. Each auxiliary row thus takes 17 draws and adds one stored input.
* Process the shared row itself. While $250^2q^2>281^2 n$, append $m=\min(\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor,1498)$. The pivot has squared length at most two. The integer recurrence satisfies $n+m^2<q^2$ at every such step, so the period is longer. Each stored singleton has centered horizontal magnitude at least $1532/\sqrt n>m/\sqrt n$. Two input coefficients of magnitude one can tie at squared norm two only with zero horizontal displacement, excluded by the distinct unsigned labels, including the new $m$. Larger input norms cannot compete. After 27 pivots $n=10597823$. Append the last shared input. Now $q^2<\min(2n,n+1532^2)$, so the period is strictly shortest. Its projection deletes the shared row. All $105+a+1=k$ stored input axes become ordinary orthogonal unit axes.

This takes 232 or 215 draws. Pad **at the beginning** by $i$ minus this number of zero appends. Each padding unit wins uniquely and its deletion preserves the initial graph. At entry the lattice is an orthogonal sum of $k$ input units and $400-a-1$ horizontal periods $q$. All later marks live on the remaining horizontal axes. On every continuation the lattice in these coordinates is integral. Any new unit ties after the earliest old unit in the signed ambient order. Deletion of the latter preserves this description until all $k$ have won. This proves the maximum in (1), including ties and the full mixed-support tests. This prefix already contains a small plane.

## Prefixes attaining survival zero

Use $k-1$ separate horizontal rows and put the initial 105 graph inputs on the other $r=401-k$ rows. Their graph $C$ of dimension $d=r+105$ can be chosen with $\lambda_1(C)>1200$. Indeed every eligible nonzero integer point in that ball either has a nonzero input modulo $q$, with membership probability $q^{-r}$ for a random core matrix, or cannot belong. Disjoint centered unit cubes give the first-moment upper bound $V_d(1200+\sqrt d/2)^d/q^r<1$.

After initial zero padding, append coefficient 52 on each separate row once, ending at $i$. Its vector $(52,1)$ wins on that row. For any competitor of at most its length both integer coordinates have magnitude at most 52, hence $|x-52b|<q$ forces $x=52b$. The processed rows have period $L=q/\sqrt{2705}>52.1$, larger than the pivot, and the core is orthogonal. At entry the $k-1$ forced old winners are these periods in tie order. After their deletion the old section is precisely $C$. If the flag survives that far, the last full window has rank 506 and covolume $q^r$, since its $k$ fresh heights form the standard unit grid above $C$. Minkowski's ball bound gives a vector of length at most $2(q^r/V_{506})^{1/506}<1040$. Every old vector has length above 1200. Thus **every** tuple loses either at an earlier stage or at this final comparison. This proves the minimum in (1). In particular, zero describes an impossible continuation of a reachable entry, with no positive-mass selected flag charged there. ## A positive-height entry with a surviving rank-two witness

For a comparison retaining the plane geometry, use $k+2$ separate horizontal rows and a core on the other $r=398-k$ rows. Choose the same type of 105-input core with minimum above 1200. After initial zero padding use coefficient 52 once on each separate row, then coefficient 112 once on each in the same order, finishing at $i$. On the second pass the pivot is $(112/\sqrt{2705},1)$ in a row of period $q/\sqrt{2705}$. Its length is below 2.4. At height two the centered numerator is 224, so it cannot beat the pivot. Higher heights, other rows and the core are too long. All pivots have height one, and the unused raw residue group at entry has full order $q^{400}$ by the coprime transport. [Part 176, transport](wall1_inverse_branch.md#the-mass-of-one-inverse-branch)

All $k+2$ resulting periods have length $L=q/\sqrt{2705+112^2}\in(26.9583,26.9584)$. The first $k$ forced old winners delete $k$ distinct periods. The two remaining ones span a saturated plane of area $L^2\in(726.7519,726.7520)$, below both cutoffs. It remains saturated in the full terminal output whenever the flag wins, since the height quotient is free.

A losing competitor at **any** stage, after discarding the separate-row components, gives an integer triple $(x,y,b)$ in the ball of radius $L$, where $x\in\mathbb Z^r$, $y\in\mathbb Z^{105}$ and $0\ne b\in\mathbb Z^k$ has been zero padded. All forced projections act outside the core. Thus $x=Ay+U_{\rm core}b\pmod q$ is necessary in the original core frame. Each fixed triple satisfies it with probability $q^{-r}$, since $b$ is nonzero modulo $q$. A **single** ball of dimension $r+105+k=503$ contains the projected candidates for every stage. Therefore
$$
             1-p(S,k)\le V_{503}(L+\sqrt{503}/2)^{503}/q^r.              \tag{4}
$$
The base-ten logarithm of the right side is below $-561.70$ at $k=118$ and $-565.22$ at $k=117$. Consequently even on positive-height entries with this terminal plane geometry, a guaranteed divisor cannot exceed $(1-10^{-561})^{-1}$. The resulting old plane is already present at entry. The prefix also contains short raw pivots, so it does not certify any mass in the gated first-hit residual.

## What remains at draw 406

The audited radial term $T$ is a majorant obtained after averaging and relaxing prefix information. It does not specify a framed lattice or a probability measure on the flags it counts. For example, if $W(S)$ counts eligible terminal planes determined by an old forced path, and $J(S,U)$ retains other history-dependent gates, its selected contribution uses
$$
 \mathbb E_S\big[W(S)\,\mathbb E_U[\mathbf1_{\rm flag}J(S,U)\mid S]\big].
$$
The outer law includes the probability of reaching the prefix. A prefix-uniform discount gives precisely the obstruction (1) on unrestricted states. A discount for the actual radial estimate requires a bound weighted by its eligible geometry, or a direct estimate of the selected contribution. Neither extreme in (1) determines that average. The $10^{-620}$ scale is attainable as pointwise exclusion, impossible as a uniform guarantee, and **unresolved for the gated term**. The draw-406 budget remains conjectural. ## Audit

**Verdict — SOUND.** For each of the two block lengths, reachable prefixes attain both endpoints of (1). The largest exclusion divisor valid on every reachable prefix is therefore exactly one. The plane example also passes both cutoff tests. Its earlier hit leaves the first-hit estimate at draw 406 open.

**Survival one.** An exact integer check compared each proposed pivot with the centered residue of every height that could match its length. The auxiliary recurrence takes 16 pivots to reach $n=3902799$, and the shared recurrence takes 27 to reach $n=10597823$. In the shared summand, an old singleton has greater horizontal displacement than the pivot. The only possible mixed tie has input squared norm two and horizontal displacement zero, which the distinct labels modulo sign exclude. The initial padding is 56 draws for $k=118$ and 74 for $k=117$. After the shared period is frozen, each of the $k$ stored inputs projects to its original coordinate unit. Integrality forces every future length-one competitor onto a coordinate axis, and signed lexicographic order selects the earliest remaining old unit at every step.

**Survival zero.** The core first moments in the table are below one, so a core with minimum above 1200 exists for each block length. The separate-row pivots leave $k-1$ orthogonal periods shorter than the core minimum. For any tuple surviving their deletion, projection onto the fresh heights maps the final window onto $\mathbb Z^k$ with kernel $C$ and orthogonal height covolume one. Its rank is 506 and its covolume is $q^{401-k}$. Applying the convex body theorem to the open ball of radius equal to the window minimum gives the stated length cap, below 1040 in both cases. The resulting vector must use a fresh coordinate, so even this last comparison forces a loss. [Minkowski’s convex body theorem, Theorem 9](https://cims.nyu.edu/~regev/teaching/lattices_fall_2004/ln/introduction.pdf)

**Plane and first hit.** The height-one pivots on both passes are strict up to sign, so the incoming mark order stays $q^{400}$ through draw $i$. The surviving pair of row periods is an orthogonal direct summand of the terminal old section and remains saturated after adjoining the free heights. Its minimum is $3329/\sqrt{15249}>24$ and its area is $3329^2/15249$, below $1352/\sqrt3$ and $676\sqrt2$. Already after the **second pivot of the second pass**, two completed rows span a saturated plane of this area. That draw is $i-k$, namely 170 or 172 respectively. The plane at that earlier draw can use a different pair of rows from the terminal witness. For any first disagreement the nonzero fresh height tuple survives projection onto the integer core and height coordinates. Zero padding therefore permits one union bound in dimension 503 across all stages. The base-ten logarithms of the resulting failure bounds lie in $(-561.72,-561.70)$ and $(-565.24,-565.22)$ respectively.
