# Joint inverse branches and the draw-406 plane count

**Result.** A block of $k$ coprime positive-height winners has conditional mass $h^{-k}$ per reachable decorated output, where $h$ is the incoming mark order. Grouping those draws gives exactly the same mass as successive inversion. The large draw-406 terms in Part 198 have a different structure. After the last used column, the 117 or 118 remaining winners all come from the window already seen. Their membership imposes **zero new mark equations**. Their selection imposes a joint exclusion of shorter or tie-preferred competitors. Below is an exact finite-group count of that exclusion. It can improve on separate competitor tests, but its improvement depends on the incoming framed lattice. No numerical reduction of the width-506 per-draw loss or extension of the certified horizon follows from the inverse fiber sizes.

## Counting a block from its two ends

Use the unconditioned Part 108 marginal restart, $q=3329$, $V=\mathbb F_q^{400}$ and $Q=|V|$. After draw $t$ the framed state $S=(E,M)$ has read $n=105+t$ columns. Write $K=P_E\mathbb Z^{505+t}$, $H=K/M$, $h=|H|$, and $N_t(S)$ for the number of raw prefixes reaching $S$. Use the graph sign convention in which the fresh displacement is $\phi_S(c)\in H$. The frame includes the original ordered axes and physical scale. Each unread raw column is uniform independently of the prefix. [Part 108](wall1_first_tour.md) · [Part 188, reach bound](wall1_state_counting.md#the-reach-bound)

**Block fiber.** Record the terminal embedded lattice and the ordered winners in their respective embedded windows. Resolve the signed norm and lexicographic comparison before orienting a winning pair to positive height. Suppose the record is reachable and every newest-coordinate height $m_j$ is positive and coprime to $q$. Backward application of Part 176 recovers a unique starting state and ordered actual marks. At each step with winner $(y,m)$ and output $L$, the horizontal intercept $B(z,b)=z-by/m$ gives $M=B(L)\cap K$ and $u=m^{-1}[y]$. Projection transports the old mark group onto the next one. Its kernel is trivial here because $P_{v^\perp}(z,0)\in L$ for $z\in K$ implies $z\in B(L)\cap K=M$. Thus the mark order is $h$ all along this block. The exact number and probability of raw designs for **one** record are
$$
 N_t(S)(Q/h)^k,\qquad \frac{N_t(S)}{Q^n}h^{-k}.                    \tag{1}
$$
Indeed each successive recovered mark has exactly $Q/h$ raw lifts at its recovered parent. Part 188 gives $N_t(S)/Q^n\le h^{-n}$, since the framed projection of each graph column fixes its coset modulo $\ker\phi_S$. For a block ending at 406, the resulting bound per record is $h^{-511}$ for **every** $k$. At maximal mark order it is exact. Let $C_k(S)$ count the distinct reachable records in (1) passing a specified terminal event. A plane existence test uses one indicator per record. A plane first moment counts each saturated plane in that record once. For the existence test, the all-positive contribution is exactly $\sum_S N_t(S)C_k(S)/(Q^n h_S^k)$ and $0\le C_k(S)\le h_S^k$. Thus the per-draw factor $h^{-1}$ for one specified record does not bound the fraction of successful continuations. At $k=406$ the entry law is explicit, but $C_{406}$ still entails all the selections and this sector omits paths with horizontal wins.

There is an exact forward sum for **all** height patterns. At fixed $S$, sample the $k$ unread columns as $U=(u_1,\ldots,u_k)\in H^k$ in the starting frame. At each later draw use the image of $u_j$ under the intervening mark transports. For any test $f$ on the realized block, its conditional average is $h^{-k}\sum_{U\in H^k} f(S,U)$. A fixed forward path of actual marks has probability $\prod_{j=1}^k h_{j-1}^{-1}$, with the actual intermediate orders. On horizontal or noncoprime steps Part 176 does not identify that path from the decorated terminal output. Sum over its preimages there. For a first-hit test weight each entry by the count of prefixes surviving to entry, then test the earlier draws *inside* the block. Retain any additional history or exit indicators required by the residual. Repartitioning any exact sum of this kind into blocks leaves it invariant. The parent at $406-k$ has its actual selected law. Restarting it with the draw-zero law would change the sum. ## Where joint selection can save mass

For fixed lifted candidates in the unprojected $k$-column extension of $S$, collect their new-coordinate coefficients as columns of $B$ over $\mathbb F_q$ and their horizontal classes in $H$ as columns of $C$. Regard $U$ as a uniform linear map $\mathbb F_q^k\to H$. The joint membership test $UB=C$ has probability zero if $\ker B\not\subseteq\ker C$ and $h^{-\operatorname{rank} B}$ otherwise. This follows by extending a basis of $\operatorname{im}B$. For lifts of successive positive coprime winners, the coefficient matrix is triangular with invertible diagonal. All $k$ marks are pinned. Testing the selector on that fiber can reject the record, but charging a second $h^{-k}$ for those pivots would reuse the same randomness. This rank calculation applies to fixed lifts. Summing arbitrary lifts of the same projected winner would introduce multiplicities.

Now fix the realized prefix through the last used column $i$ of a Part 198 flag, including its winner. Later flag winners have lifts from the window at $i$. In $M_i$ and its successive projections they must therefore be the designated shortest vectors of the old lattice alone, by restriction of the signed comparison. Their lifts have zero coefficients in *every* column after $i$. This applies also to the $j=1$ branch, where the first use of column $i$ is delayed. If $j=0$, the radial candidate at $i$ is killed by its own projection. All later comparisons concern other directions. The overlapping Korkin–Zolotarev inequalities bound lengths within that forced old flag. What they omit is the comparison against vectors using the later columns. [Part 198, flag construction and audit](wall1_flag_horizon.md) · [Part 199, survival audit](wall1_survival_constraint.md#audit) · [Pendavingh and van Zwam, §§1, 6](https://www.matroidunion.org/stefan/pdf/065879.pdf)

**Exact joint exclusion for an old flag.** Start at this fixed $S=S_i$ and prescribe $k$ successive winners from $M$ alone, where $k\le\operatorname{rank}M$. Project off the designated old winner each time, without reading a new column. Write $P_{s-1}$ for the resulting old-space projection before step $s$, $M_{s-1}^o=P_{s-1}M$, $K_{s-1}^o=P_{s-1}K$, and $\rho_{s-1}:H\twoheadrightarrow K_{s-1}^o/M_{s-1}^o$ for the induced map. These objects and the signed old winner $w_s$ are determined at entry. Along that prescribed path, all $s$ new coordinate axes are untouched. The window at step $s$ consists exactly of
$$
 (z,b)\in K_{s-1}^o\times\mathbb Z^s,\qquad
 [z]=\rho_{s-1}(U\bar b),                                         \tag{2}
$$
where $\bar b$ is reduced modulo $q$ and zero-padded to length $k$. Indeed a point of height tuple $b$ is the projection of a lift of $\sum b_j u_j$ plus the projected old lattice. This proves (2) even when an old projection shrinks the mark group.

At each $s$ list every $(z,b)$ with $b\ne0$ whose signed key precedes $(w_s,0)$, using the same frame for both keys. The list is finite because the grid is discrete and only norms at most $\|w_s\|$ enter. Old competitors were already tested when choosing $w_s$. Each listed competitor forbids the affine subset $D_e=\{U:\rho_{s-1}(U\bar b)=[z]\}$ of $H^k$. By induction through the first disagreement, the conditional probability of selecting the entire prescribed old flag is
$$
 p_{\rm flag}(S,k)=1-h^{-k}\left|\bigcup_e D_e\right|.             \tag{3}
$$
This includes later competitors that use several earlier new coordinates. It also handles $\bar b=0$. A compatible such competitor excludes every tuple. For an explicit evaluation by inclusion–exclusion, stack the maps $U\mapsto\rho_{s-1}(U\bar b)$ for a subset of competitors into a linear map $L$. Their common intersection has normalized size $q^{-\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_q}L}$ if the stacked target belongs to its image, and zero otherwise. This offers a count without enumerating winners anew for each tuple.

Equation (3) gives a precise dependence on block size for a relaxation of the competitor tests. Partition the $k$ *unread coordinate indices* into consecutive blocks. Retain the competitors whose integer coefficient support lies inside one block, at every stage at which they are tested. Their avoidance probabilities multiply across blocks at this fixed entry and forced flag, since they use disjoint $u_j$. Merging adjacent blocks adds the mixed-support competitors and weakly decreases the bound. One block gives (3) exactly. The size of this decrease is the conditional mass removed by those extra competitors. Neither the group order nor the number of blocks determines it. The Part 198 radial calculation does not already include the singleton version of (3).

One upper bound avoids inclusion–exclusion. First handle any deterministic competitor with $\bar b=0$. For each line $\ell\subset\mathbb F_q^k$ with a listed coefficient, choose $b_\ell\ne0$ on it. Collect in $A_\ell\subset H$ the values of $Ub_\ell$ causing any competitor on this line, taking its scalar multiple and its stage map $\rho_{s-1}$ into account. Put $p_\ell=|A_\ell|/h$. A full $A_\ell$ rules out the flag. Otherwise
$$
 p_{\rm flag}(S,k)\le\left(1+\sum_\ell\frac{p_\ell}{1-p_\ell}\right)^{-1}.       \tag{4}
$$
Indeed $Ub_\ell$ and $Ub_{\ell'}$ are independent for distinct lines, since their coefficient vectors extend to a basis. With $I_\ell=\mathbf1_{Ub_\ell\in A_\ell}$, the weighted sum $Y=\sum I_\ell/(1-p_\ell)$ has mean and variance both equal to $R=\sum p_\ell/(1-p_\ell)$. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\Pr(Y>0)\ge R/(1+R)$ for $R>0$, and the bound is trivial at $R=0$. This uses only pairwise independence. It turns the outstanding geometric input into a lower bound on the coverage of the mark group by competitors in each coefficient direction, on the prefixes carrying a terminal plane.

For the terminal old-plane test, the section at $b=0$ after the last projection is $P_k M$. Its quotient in the full retained lattice is free on the $k$ height coordinates, so a saturated plane of this old section remains saturated in the full output. Both the forced flag and its old terminal plane count can thus be evaluated from the entry. The exact conditional contribution for this run is the old plane count times (3), or its nonempty indicator times (3) for existence. Further first-hit, last-use and terminal gates must be retained for an exact residual, including gates on $U$ if appropriate. The missing estimate is the **average of this joint selection weight with eligible old geometry** over the true prefixes. Using a radial upper sum times an unweighted average of (3) would assert a correlation bound. For mixed flags the same issue occurs in the forward sum, or in the selector fraction conditional on the pinned parity equations of Part 197. ## Exact checks

* **Two coprime selections and a plane.** Use $7\mathbb Z$ in one active axis, an orthogonal spectator $8\mathbb Z$, and two new columns with active residues $a,b\bmod7$. Each raw column is a pair in $\mathbb F_7^2$ mapping to its first component. For the first window enumerate $x\equiv ay\pmod7$ through squared norm 49. Orient the selected pair to $y>0$ and put $m=x^2+y^2$. In the next window the old active vector is $(y,-x)n/m$ at new height $t$, with $n\equiv ybt\pmod7$. This follows by taking the dot product with $(y,-x)$ and using $\gcd(y,(ya-x)/7)=1$ by primitivity. Enumerate $n^2/m+t^2\le49$ with the original signed lexicographic ties. The modulus witness suffices for both searches and the spectator cannot win. The height pairs $(1,1),(1,2),(2,1)$ occur on 29, 6, 14 of the 49 mark pairs. The residues recovered by $x/y$ and $n/(yt)$ modulo 7 agree on every record. All 49 records have exactly 49 raw preimages among $7^4$ designs. The retained rank-two area squared is $56^2/(n^2+mt^2)$ by the two determinant updates. Eight records, or 392 designs, have area at most 16.

* **A mixed competitor changes the rate.** Take $M=(2\mathbb Z)^3\oplus11\mathbb Z$ with the old axes first and mark generated by the last unit class. Force the first $k\le3$ length-two old axes in order. At tied norm any competitor using new coordinates loses to the designated old axis (its coordinate there has magnitude less than two). For marks $(u_1,\ldots,u_k)$ enumerate $b\in\{-1,0,1\}^k$ with $0<\|b\|^2<4$. It beats the old winner precisely when $\|b\|^2+|\sum b_j u_j|_{11}^2<4$, taking the centered residue magnitude and the shortest old lift. Checking at the last stage includes every earlier support. The exact surviving counts for $k=1,2,3$ are $8/11$, $24/121$, and $0/1331$ as fractions of tuples. For $k=2$ the two singleton tests leave $64/121$. Here (4) gives $2/5$, since each of the four coefficient lines forbids three marks. The mixed tests exclude $u_1\pm u_2\in\{0,\pm1\}\bmod11$. At $k=3$ even the pair tests suffice, since three magnitudes in $\{2,3,4,5\}$ cannot be pairwise separated by at least two. This is an abstract marked-lattice check of (3).

* **No uniform strict discount from length alone.** In the actual width-506 support take all 105 entry columns zero. The lattice contains 105 orthogonal old input units. For each of the first 105 draws, every continuation selects the earliest remaining such axis: after coordinate deletion the graph is still integral, and any new unit ties after it. Thus the probability in (3) is exactly one for each of these block lengths at this parent. Its entry already has a small plane and it fails the raw-good test. It disproves a uniform block-length discount on unrestricted parents and gives no lower bound on the residual.

The block identities and exclusions are paper results. The Lean path constructs the specified 105-column entry and the 406 freezes. Its concrete terminal discrepancy concerns the separate 407th append. The one-step inverse is formalized, while the interval arithmetic and finite enumerations above were performed in scratch. ## Audit

**Verdict — SOUND.** Grouping draws preserves their exact mass. Equation (1) gives the weight of one reachable decorated record conditional on its recovered parent after removing the prefix factor. Equation (3) gives the exact selection probability for a prescribed old flag at a fixed incoming prefix. The numerical budgets at draw 406 remain open.

**Record mass.** The last embedded lattice and the last recorded winner recover the preceding framed lattice and mark by Part 176. Repeating this argument fixes every mark back to entry. At a coprime positive height the horizontal intercept proves that the mark transport has trivial kernel, and the transported old classes generate the next group. Thus each of the $k$ draws has exactly $Q/h$ raw lifts. With the Part 188 prefix weight this gives $N_t(S)(Q/h)^k$ designs out of $Q^{105+t+k}$. Summing over the disjoint records preserves the successive one-step sum. An independent enumeration of raw projected triples in the modulus-seven example reproduced the height-pair counts $29,6,14$, the 49 raw designs per record, and the eight records meeting the area cutoff.

**Last use and selection.** For $i=288$ the later draws are 289–406 inclusive, or 118 draws. For $i=289$ they are 290–406, or 117. By the last-coordinate construction in Part 198, the lifts of those winners and the terminal witness all have zero coordinates after $i$. This also holds for $j=1$: the first use of coordinate $i$ is delayed, but it is still in the window already read by the end of draw $i$. Consequently membership of the later winners imposes zero equations on the unread marks. To compute the chance that they win, fix the prefix and test the proposed continuation. Conditioning in advance on $i$ actually being the last use would already restrict the continuations. [Part 198 flag construction](wall1_flag_horizon.md#within-one-window) · [Part 199 audit](wall1_survival_constraint.md#audit)

**Exact joint count.** Before the first disagreement with the forced flag, each projection acts only on old coordinates. At a fixed later height tuple $b$, the possible old components therefore fill exactly the coset in (2). A tuple fails precisely when it lies in at least one of the forbidden sets $D_e$, including a tie won by a competitor. A consistent stacked system of rank $r$ has $h^k/q^r$ solutions, which verifies the intersection sizes in (3). In scratch, enumeration of every signed vector through squared norm four at each stage gave 64 of the 121 two-mark tuples after singleton tests and 24 after all tests. The mixed competitors remove exactly 40 additional tuples. The corresponding full counts for one and three marks were 8 of 11 and 0 of 1,331. Distinct coefficient lines give pairwise independent evaluations, which is precisely the independence used in (4).

**Radial divisor.** A separate 75-decimal-digit outward interval calculation rebuilt the three individual terms from the Part 77 and 82 cap data and Part 198 equations (1)–(5). At the original cutoff, $(b,i,j)=(2,288,0)$ gave $610.54197888<\log_{10}T<610.54197889$. Assigning it the full $B_0=1.84782\cdot10^{-10}$ gives $620.27531922<\log_{10}(T/B_0)<620.27531923$, hence a required divisor greater than $10^{620.27}$. The 118th root lies between $180538.77$ and $180538.79$. The other two rows gave log divisors in $(617.94787863,617.94787865)$ and $(642.60714175,642.60714176)$. All are diagnostics for discounting those particular radial terms. A width-506 probability bound still requires joint selection averaged with eligible geometry under the actual prefix law, retaining the first-hit, terminal and good-event gates.
