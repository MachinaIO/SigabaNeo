# Positive success probability at final widths 506 and 406

For the original Part 108 forward pass followed by the Part 47 decoder, at width **506** the probability of returning the secret exceeds **$2^{-1531}$**. For the Part 47 decoder using the recorded two-block slide reduction, at width **406** it exceeds **$2^{-1717}$**. These bounds include the lift and hold for every choice of the final exact SVP oracle. Both come from rare plants. An estimator that always guesses the modal secret already succeeds with probability $(5/16)^{512}>2^{-860}$. Thus these results answer the literal positivity question but give no evidence of an advantage over the prior. In particular, an inverse-polynomial lower bound for the specified BKZ trajectory remains open.

Here $A$ is uniform of shape $511\times512$ over $\mathbb F_{3329}$, and $X=(e,s)$ consists of 1023 independent CBD(3) coordinates independent of $A$. Write $p_0=\binom63/64=5/16$. The final width $\beta$ includes the embedding height, so the horizontal quotient $H$ has rank $d=\beta-1$. All results below are analytic consequences of the cited estimates. None is a new Lean theorem or a running-time claim.

## 1. Width 506 on the recorded forward pass

Use the exact, $qI$-first, no-lookahead pass with the inherited signed lexicographic tie rule of Part 108. Apply the Part 47 final SVP, orientation and prefix Babai lift immediately after that pass. Write $W$ for the prefix span and $Q$ for horizontal projection onto its orthogonal complement. A plant with $QX=0$ has final lattice $H\oplus\mathbb Z(0,1)$. If $\lambda_1(H)>1$, its unique shortest pair is $\pm(0,1)$. The fiber over $(0,1)$ is the plant plus the integral prefix lattice, since the frozen directions form a primitive prefix. Consequently the lift succeeds whenever $|\langle X,b_i^*\rangle|<\|b_i^*\|^2/2$ on every prefix Gram–Schmidt vector. These are the exact height and lift tests at this particular plant. [Part 47 and audit](wall1_decoupled.md) · [Part 94](wall1_threshold.md)

**Lemma 1.** In a unit-height graph append, let $t$ be the old physical minimum. If $t>2/\sqrt3$, the quotient after selecting a shortest line has minimum strictly greater than one, for every draw and every tie choice.

*Proof.* Write $v$ for the selected primitive vector, $s_*=\|v\|\le t$. A nonzero quotient vector of norm at most one has a lift $w$ independent of $v$, with component parallel to $v$ of length at most $s_*/2$. Thus $s_*^2\le\|w\|^2\le1+s_*^2/4$, giving $s_*\le2/\sqrt3<t$. The winner must have nonzero height. The plane lattice generated by $v,w$ has area at most $s_*$ by orthogonal projection. Its image under the integer height map is $j\mathbb Z$, $j\ge1$. A primitive generator $x$ of the kernel and a vector of height $j$ form a basis of this plane lattice. The former is an old vector, and the latter has distance at least $j$ from its line, since the new height coordinate is orthogonal to the old space. Thus the same area is at least $j\|x\|\ge t>s_*$. This contradiction proves the strict claim. ∎

**Theorem 2.** Put $a=1-0.00022304-2^{-138}(4/3)^{253}$. The width-506 forward pass and decoder satisfy
$$
 \Pr[\widehat s=s]\ \ge\ a\,p_0^{912}\ >\ 2^{-1531}.
$$

*Proof.* Six initial cubic steps remove sample axes. Couple the next 105 freezes to their prescribed coordinate choices using Part 117. The chance of disagreement is less than $0.00022304$. In the marginal restart experiment, Part 151, equation (4), gives $\Pr[t\le u]\le S(u)\le u^{506}S(1)$ in its cap range, with $\log_2 S(1)<-138.713695260$. The recorded range includes $u=2/\sqrt3$ (it extends through $1.19806583$). Thus the chance of $t\le2/\sqrt3$ is less than $2^{-138}(4/3)^{253}<1.2\cdot10^{-10}$. Here $t$ is the minimum **before** the last draw. By Lemma 1 the resulting terminal minimum exceeds one. The union bound on this coupling gives probability at least $a$ that the actual first 111 freezes are the prescribed sample axes and the actual terminal minimum exceeds one. No independence between coordinate agreement and the terminal geometry is needed. [Part 108](wall1_first_tour.md) · [Part 117 and audit](wall1_idea_round2.md) · [Part 147](wall1_uncovered_lines.md) · [Part 151, equation (4)](wall1_penultimate_tail.md)

Independently, require $s=0$ and the 400 sample errors on surviving rows to be zero. The other 111 errors have their full CBD law. This event has probability $p_0^{512+400}$. On the design event just proved, $X$ lies in the span of the first 111 freezes. For each of those freezes the Gram–Schmidt vector is a signed $q$-axis and $|e_i|\le3<q/2$. All subsequent Gram–Schmidt vectors are orthogonal to $X$. The lift test holds strictly throughout. The final oracle and Babai therefore return the actual plant, including its 111 unrestricted errors. Multiplying the independent design and plant bounds proves the result. ∎

## 2. Width 406 and an exactly countable history at both widths

**Proposition 3 (two-block slide).** With the two-block null reduction of Parts 88 and 92, followed by the Part 47 decoder, at either recorded width
$$
 \Pr[\widehat s=s] > 0.99\,p_0^{1023} > 2^{-1717}.
$$

*Proof.* Use the recorded exact terminal head and 1% twin slack. With probability greater than $0.99$ over $A$, the horizontal block of rank 406 or 506 has minimum at least $3.36337038$ or $7.41883519$, respectively. Freeze its shortest head as an additional prefix vector. If the old minimum is $g$, any nonzero vector in the resulting quotient lifts to an independent vector of length at least $g$ and parallel component of length at most $g/2$. Its projected length is therefore at least $\sqrt3\,g/2>1$ at both widths. This is the Part 94 rank correction. The new horizontal rank is 405 or 505, and the decoding call has rank 406 or 506. The recorded slide subroutines also use oracle width at most the corresponding cap. On the independent event $X=0$, the vertical unit is the unique shortest pair up to sign and every prefix Babai inequality is strict. This proves full recovery. The Part 151 bound used in Theorem 2 concerns the 506 restart law. Proposition 3 uses the slide guarantee to cover 406. **Proposition 4 (original first pass, both widths).** With $q=3329$, the unmodified Part 108 pass followed by the Part 47 decoder satisfies
$$
 \Pr[\widehat s=s]\ \ge\ q^{-512d}p_0^{512}.
$$
This gives a positive bound for the specific width-406 forward pass as well as an example of what counting a single history proves.

*Proof.* After the cubic steps the old lattice is $q\mathbb Z^d$, and its mark consists of all $q^d$ torsion classes. Require zero displacement at each of the 512 graph appends. On each zero draw the new unit axis is uniquely shortest up to sign, so its removal returns the same cube and full mark. Part 108 gives mass $q^{-d}$ at each step conditional on the cylinder so far. Equivalently the $d$ surviving rows of $A$ are zero and the removed rows are unrestricted. Multiplication gives $q^{-512d}$. This extends the 511-draw cylinder of Part 144 by its last zero draw. [Part 108](wall1_first_tour.md) · [Part 144, Theorem 2](wall1_horizontal_core.md)

Require independently that $s=0$, allowing every error vector. The horizontal component of the projected plant is the surviving error vector $y$, with $R^2=1+\|y\|^2\le1+9d<68^2$. At any nonzero integer height $|k|\le R$, each coordinate of $ky$ has magnitude at most $3\cdot68<q/2$. Thus the unique nearest point to $ky$ in $q\mathbb Z^d$ is zero. The shortest length in that layer is $|k|R$. Heights $|k|>R$ are longer already in the height coordinate, and height zero has minimum $q>R$. Only the signed plant pair attains $R$. The prefix Gram–Schmidt vectors are the removed sample $q$-axes followed by signed secret unit axes. The samples satisfy the strict $q/2$ test and the secret coefficients vanish. Thus Babai returns the full plant. ∎

**Proposition 5 (a nonzero secret on the Part 144 cylinder).** At either width the same first pass satisfies
$$
 \Pr[\widehat s=s,\ s\ne0]\ \ge\ q^{-511d}p_0^{511}(1-2^{-300})\frac{11}{16}
 \left(1-\frac{1+3(d-1)/2+24/11}{q}\right).
$$
In particular the exponent of this joint bound is below $2{,}422{,}414$ bits at 406 and $3{,}020{,}328$ bits at 506.

*Proof.* Stop the zero-displacement condition one draw earlier, exactly at the Part 144 cylinder of mass $q^{-511d}$. The last null window is generated by the old $q$-axes and one column $(a,1)$ with uniform $a\bmod q$. Consider the projection deleting the **earliest** surviving sample axis, before deciding the winner. The resulting graph has $r=d-1$ rows $a'$ and still only one secret coordinate. For any $1\le k<q$, the centered coordinates of $ka'$ are independent uniform residues. A vector at that height shorter than $q$ has at most 15 coordinates of absolute value at least 833, since $16\cdot833^2>q^2$. At height zero no such nonzero vector exists, and heights of absolute value at least $q$ cannot be shorter. Thus the probability that this projected graph has minimum below $q$ is at most
$$
 (q-1)q^{-r}\sum_{j=0}^{15}\binom rj1664^j1665^{r-j}<2^{-300}
$$
at both recorded ranks, by integer comparison. The projected graph contains sample $q$-axes, so its minimum on the complement equals $q$. The unprojected window then has no vector shorter than $q$ either: those with zero projection are multiples of the deleted $q$-axis. The signed lexicographic tie rule selects that earliest negative $q$-axis among all length-$q$ vectors. The actual terminal null lattice is therefore precisely the projected graph just bounded. The earlier frozen coordinates are zero in the frame of this last window. [Part 144, cubic cylinder and signed tie rule](wall1_horizontal_core.md)

Independently require that the first 511 secret coordinates vanish and the last one be nonzero. The prefix Gram–Schmidt vectors are sample $q$-axes and the first 511 secret unit axes, so all lift inequalities hold. The projected plant has $R^2=1+\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}e_i^2+s_{512}^2$. Its conditional mean is $1+3(d-1)/2+24/11$, since $\Pr[s_{512}\ne0]=11/16$ and $\mathbb E s_{512}^2=3/2$. Markov gives the displayed lower bound for $R^2<q$ conditional on this secret event. On that cap the Part 94 sufficient threshold satisfies $T(R)\le R^2<\lambda_1(H)$: for each $0\le k\le R$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives $k\sqrt{R^2-1}+\sqrt{R^2-k^2}\le R^2$. All heights and the lift succeed. Multiplying proves the bound. ∎

The large *bad* conditional horizontal charge in Part 144 concerns a normalized quotient-shape test. It does not assert that an atypical independent plant fails to decode. Conversely an atom of mass $q^{-k}$ supplies a success bound only after proving conditional decoding and lifting on that atom.

## 3. Which success question the certificates answer

For plant-blind data $D=(A,\text{reducer coins})$, set $G(D)=\Pr_X[\widehat s=s\mid D]$, including any fresh decoder coins in this inner probability. The unconditional answer is $\mathbb E G$. If $\Pr[D\in E]\ge a$ and $G\ge g$ on $E$, then $\mathbb E G\ge ag$ by restricting the integral. Conversely, for $0<\epsilon\le1$, if $\mathbb E G\ge\epsilon$ then $\Pr[G\ge\epsilon/2]\ge\epsilon/(2-\epsilon)$, by bounding the mean above by $\epsilon/2+(1-\epsilon/2)\Pr[G\ge\epsilon/2]$. Thus proving inverse-polynomial one-shot success needs only inverse-polynomial mass and conditional success. The near-total design coverage needed for 99% recovery is a stronger obligation. [Part 134 and audit](wall1_breakthrough_r1.md)

For a single Part 136 Monte Carlo trial with $M$ fake plants and rejection on more than $k$ failures, its **joint acceptance and real recovery** probability is exactly
$$
 \mathbb E\!\left[G\,\Pr\{\mathrm{Bin}(M,1-G)\le k\mid G\}\right].
$$
Given $D$, the real and fake outcomes are independent with the same success parameter. Requiring success of the first $M-k$ fakes suffices for acceptance, giving a lower bound $a g^{M-k+1}$ under the preceding hypotheses. With the recorded $M=100000$, $k=290$ this is explicit even for Theorem 2 ($g=p_0^{912}$). Checking a rare-success design with a gate intended for near-certain per-design success can cost most of its contribution. A shared-design restart cannot count the same rare design as fresh at each trial. For $T$ conditionally independent trials with conditional acceptance probability $\alpha(A)$, exhaustion has probability $\mathbb E[(1-\alpha(A))^T]$. [Part 136, Theorems 1–2 and audit](wall1_restart_certificate.md)

For the homogeneous bad-count method of Part 136, Proposition 6, a sufficient test based on $F(r)=0.01(r/r_0)^D$ can certify success $\epsilon$ by Markov only up to $r/r_0\le[100(1-\epsilon)]^{1/D}$. At $D=405,505$ even the limiting factors are only about $1.01144,1.00917$. Counting *good* plants or good histories directly incurs no such radius restriction. The price is visible in Theorem 2: a very common geometric event is paired with 912 prescribed coordinate values. Better geometry for one atom gives no lower bound for the much larger set of ordinary plants. [Part 136, Proposition 6](wall1_restart_certificate.md)

The Haar template has two separate limitations. Part 105 and Part 136, Theorem 8, integrate over Haar windows. For a deterministic tie rule the actual Part 108 path law has finite support. A literal positive-probability Haar component is impossible: the complement of that finite support has Haar mass one and finite-law mass zero. An inequality for one event under both laws remains possible. Even under Haar, the recorded $0.954$ test bounds a relative minimum. At 506 its physical floor $27.4843$ clears the recorded necessary $27.4538$ threshold for the 99% height-zero question. The audit supplies no conditional end-to-end recovery probability from this fact alone. The Part 162 Haar bound below $4.361\cdot10^{-11}$ likewise concerns a selected upper-band event. Parts 147–163 charge pieces of the Part 120 failure decomposition; $0.00019770868$ is a remaining **allowance**, not a measured failure probability or a recovery probability. ## 4. Interpretation and exact checks

The best explicit bounds obtained here for the two widths are Theorem 2 and Proposition 3. Neither asserts optimality. For perspective, for a fixed guess $s_0$ independent of the instance, the product CBD law gives $\Pr[s=s_0]\le p_0^{512}$, attained by zero. Averaging gives the same upper bound for any randomized independent guess. The prior benchmark is a different estimator; it cannot be attributed to the plain decoder. The leading bounds in Theorem 2 and Proposition 3 use $s=0$. Proposition 5 separately certifies actual recovery of a nonzero secret on a much rarer family. Part 135 also shows that in plant-last BKZ a visible nonhorizontal vector can alter the trajectory before the final call. The results for a null-only prefix do not cover that subsequent trajectory. [Part 135 and audit](wall1_plant_symmetry.md)

**Open conjectural target.** For a specified growing family of designs and widths, prove that $\Pr_D[G(D)\ge n^{-c_1}]\ge n^{-c_2}$ for fixed $c_1,c_2$. This would give $n^{-(c_1+c_2)}$ unconditional success. Neither the required family nor such constants follow from the two fixed widths. Along a proportional family the prescribed zero-secret event alone has mass $(5/16)^n$, smaller than every inverse polynomial eventually. That establishes a limitation of these witnesses, not an upper bound on the attack. No inverse-polynomial guarantee for the plain attack or these decoupled procedures follows from the cited frontier.

Scratch checks enumerated all 64 outcomes for one CBD coordinate and all 625 two-row, two-column designs at $q=5$, width two. Exactly 25 designs had both surviving graph displacements zero, as predicted by $5^{-2}$. Exact enumeration of the toy terminal competitors for surviving error $-1,0,1$ gave only the signed plant at or below its length. Integer and rational comparisons verified $2^{-138}(4/3)^{253}<1.2\cdot10^{-10}$, $2^{1531}a p_0^{912}>1$, $2^{1717}(99/100)p_0^{1023}>1$, and $2^{859}p_0^{512}<1<2^{860}p_0^{512}$. The Part 144 integer sum with $r=404,504$ was checked to be below $2^{-300}$ at both ranks. The high-dimensional analytic inputs are the recorded estimates; no parameter search was used.

## Audit

**Verdict — MINOR CAVEATS.** All four recovery bounds hold for the specified reducers, including final SVP and lifting. The opening paragraph gives the right benchmark. Always guessing zero succeeds with probability $(5/16)^{512}=2^{-859.172815\ldots}$, above both leading certificates. The certificates establish positive recovery probability at these fixed widths. Whether either decoder beats that benchmark remains open here.

**Width 506.** The key strict inequality in Lemma 1 survives ties. A quotient witness of length at most one would force the winner length $s_*\le2/\sqrt3<t$. Its nonzero integer height gives the plane generated with the rounded witness a rank-one horizontal kernel. The plane area would be at least $t$ from the kernel and at most $s_*$ from the witness. This contradiction applies to every final draw. Part 151 bounds the physical minimum before that draw in the marginal restart. Part 117 charges disagreement with the prescribed axes once, by a union bound. On agreement the 400 surviving sample labels are fixed. Setting those errors and the 512 secret coordinates to zero gives $QX=0$, a unique signed vertical shortest pair, and a strict Babai lift even for the 111 unrestricted errors. The negative base-two logarithm of $a p_0^{912}$ is approximately $1530.401899$.

**Width 406.** Part 92 supplies the physical rank-406 head bound for the Part 88 slide with exact terminal SVP and the recorded twin slack. Freezing that head leaves horizontal rank 405 and minimum at least $(\sqrt3/2)3.36337038>1$. Restoring the embedding height makes the decoding rank 406, as required by the rank correction in Parts 94 and 143. At $X=0$ the signed vertical pair is unique and all Babai coefficients vanish. The negative base-two logarithm of $0.99p_0^{1023}$ is approximately $1716.682058$. This argument and the width-506 leading bound both use the zero secret.

**Nonzero-secret count.** On Part 144's cylinder the first 511 zero displacements have probability $q^{-511d}$ and the last column remains uniform. Deleting the predetermined earliest surviving sample axis leaves $r=d-1$ uniform residues. For each positive height below $q$, scalar multiplication permutes those residues coordinatewise. The count uses 1664 large residues, 1665 small residues, and at most 15 large coordinates. Exact integer comparisons in scratch verified the displayed union bound is below $2^{-300}$ at both ranks. Its negative base-two logarithm is approximately $302.837540$ at $r=404$ and $397.943819$ at $r=504$. On the complementary event the earliest negative sample axis wins the signed tie. Conditional on the specified secret event, the exact means of $R^2$ are $6701/11$ and $8351/11$, respectively. On $R^2<q$, Part 94 gives uniqueness at every height and the frozen secret coordinates vanish in the Babai tests. Exact comparisons of the full products confirm the stated inequalities for the **joint** event of recovery and a nonzero secret. Their negative base-two logarithms are approximately $2{,}422{,}412.530713$ and $3{,}020{,}327.230784$, respectively.

**Wording corrections.** In §1 the strict Babai inequalities guarantee recovery for every rounding convention. Equality in a prefix coordinate can still succeed under a particular tie rule, so “exact” there should be read as a tie-independent test. Every witness used above lies strictly inside the Babai box. In §3 the Haar test gives a relative minimum. The physical comparison uses the separate first-pass GH scale bound $28.8095$, and the product of the displayed constants is $0.954\cdot28.8095=27.484263$. Thus $27.4843$ is a rounded comparison value. The product still exceeds the quoted necessary threshold $27.4538$. Parts 136 and 138 supply no transfer of the Haar success law to the actual forward pass.
