# What winner exchange bounds in the terminal window

The selected vector is shortest in the entire window, so Gauss reduction applies in every witness plane. It produces a short representative of the **primitive quotient generator**. This representative can have nonzero height even when the winner has height one. In that case the old primitive vector on the same plane can be arbitrarily longer than the cutoff in a marked window. The distinction prevents the proposed reduction of the full moment to an old short-vector count.

## Exact exchange in a saturated plane

**Lemma.** Subtract a nearest integer multiple of $v$ from $u$, retaining the name $u$ for the resulting vector. Write $u=u_\perp+\delta v$, with $\|u_\perp\|=p$ and $|\delta|\le1/2$. Then $v$ is a shortest vector of $P$ if and only if
$$
                    p^2+s^2\delta^2\ge s^2.                         \tag{1}
$$
In particular every accepted plane satisfies
$$
  \sqrt3\,s/2\le p<R,\qquad s\le\|u\|<\sqrt{R^2+s^2/4},
  \qquad \det P=sp.
$$

*Proof.* Necessity compares $v$ with $u$. Conversely, (1) gives $p^2\ge3s^2/4$. For $a,b\in\mathbb Z$ the squared norm of $av+bu$ is $s^2(a+b\delta)^2+b^2p^2$. When $b=0$ a nonzero vector has length at least $s$. For $|b|=1$ the nearest-integer choice makes the minimum over $a$ equal to $\|u\|^2$. For $|b|\ge2$ the perpendicular part alone has squared length at least $3s^2$. The displayed bounds and the area formula follow by orthogonality. This includes ties. The test decides shortestness within $P$ only. For example, adjoining an orthogonal summand generated by a vector shorter than $s$ leaves (1) unchanged and defeats $v$ in the larger lattice. Thus (1) cannot replace Part 152's signed global winner test. [Part 148, Theorem 2](wall1_vertical_multiplicity.md) · [Part 150, Lemma 2](wall1_shortened_lines.md)

**Proposition (recovery of the old vector).** Let $\ell$ be the signed height of this rounded $u$, $j=\gcd(k,\ell)>0$, $e=k/j$ and $b=\ell/j$. Up to sign the primitive old vector $x$ in the plane satisfies
$$
 (x,0)=eu-bv,\qquad
 \|x\|^2=e^2p^2+s^2(e\delta-b)^2.                                  \tag{2}
$$
The old vector projects to $e$ times a primitive generator. In particular $p$ is the projection length of $(x/e,0)$, even though $(x/e,0)$ need not belong to $W$.

*Proof.* Height in the basis $(v,u)$ is the row $(k,\ell)$. Its image is $j\mathbb Z$ and its integer kernel is generated by the coprime pair $(-b,e)$. Saturation gives $P\cap(E,0)=(L\cap\mathbb Rx,0)$, identifying the kernel generator with a primitive old vector. Orthogonal decomposition gives (2). Changing the lift by $mv$ changes $\delta$ by $m$ and $b$ by $me$, leaving $e\delta-b$ fixed. Equivalently, for $\lambda=y-k\tilde U\in L$, subtracting $(a/k)v$ from a window vector of height $a$ leaves a horizontal vector in $L+\mathbb Z\lambda/k$ with the same projection. Every element of this extension arises in this way, and projection is injective on $E$ because $k>0$. The intersection of the extension with $\mathbb Rx$ is therefore generated by $x/e$. This also distinguishes the plane height $j$ from the winning height $k$. [Part 139, Theorem 4](wall1_lyapunov.md) · [Part 148, equation (1) and audit](wall1_vertical_multiplicity.md)

There are two disjoint cases, assigning nearest-integer ties to the second whenever possible.

* **Only horizontal rounded lifts.** This occurs exactly when $e=1$ and $2|\langle x,y\rangle|<s^2$. Here $u=\pm(x,0)$, so $s\le\|x\|<\sqrt{R^2+s^2/4}$. Every such witness is counted by a distinct primitive old sign pair below that radius.
* **A nonhorizontal rounded lift exists.** This occurs when $e>1$ or $2|\langle x,y\rangle|\ge s^2$. One can choose $u$ of positive height and norm below the same radius. Formula (2) has no corresponding bound of that size on $\|x\|$. Distinct planes give distinct chosen lifts, since a lift and $v$ determine their real span.

Indeed, a horizontal lift of a primitive generator exists exactly when $e=1$, and then the only such lifts up to sign are $(x,0)$. Its parallel coefficient has absolute value $|\langle x,y\rangle|/s^2$. At equality two nearest lifts differ by $v$ and one has nonzero height. This proves the classification. The global winner also enforces $\|v\|\le\|v\pm(x,0)\|$, or $2|\langle x,y\rangle|\le\|x\|^2$. For a long $x$ this permits a parallel coefficient much larger than $1/2$ before reduction.

## A height-one counterexample

For an integer $n\ge3$ set $Q=n^2+1$, $L=Q\mathbb Z^2$, take the full mark $\mathbb Z^2/L$ and draw $U=(n,1)+L$. In the resulting rank-three window take
$$
 v=(n,1,1),\quad s^2=n^2+2,\quad (x,0)=(Q,0,0),\quad R=3s/2.
$$
Here $R$ is a toy cutoff. The height-one line $\mathbb Zv$ is the **unique** shortest line. At height zero the minimum is $Q$. At height one both centered residues in $v$ are unique. At heights $2\le a\le n-1$ the first coordinate has absolute value at least $\min(an,Q-an)\ge n+1$. At height $n$ the second coordinate has absolute value at least $n$, so squared length is at least $2n^2$. At all higher positive heights the height alone exceeds $s$. Sign symmetry handles negative heights.

The plane generated by $v,(x,0)$ is saturated: subtract the height times $v$ from any window vector in its real span and the result belongs to $L$ on the first axis. Since $k=1$, the quotient generator has $e=1$. The parallel coefficient of $(x,0)$ is $n-n/(n^2+2)$, so its rounded lift is
$$
 u=(x,0)-nv=(1,-n,-n),\qquad \|u\|^2=2n^2+1<R^2 .
$$
Thus $p<R$ and $\sqrt3/2<R/s<8$. The marked exclusion does not apply: $U$ has second residue $1$, off the marked first axis. Yet the shortest nonzero old vector has length $Q$, and $Q/R\to\infty$. There is no universal constant bounding the required old length by a multiple of $R$ from shortestness and the cylinder test alone. The instance $n=4$ already has prime modulus $17$. This family tests a deterministic inference in marked windows; it makes no claim about the probability of any history of the width-506 restart.

## Audit

**Verdict — SOUND.** The exchange bound, the height-one counterexample and the conclusion about the Part 152 shell hold as stated. No correction is required.

**Shortestness and exchange.** The signed window selector compares every nonzero vector before breaking ties. Restricting its winner to any incident plane therefore preserves shortestness. Part 120's saturation and primitive quotient generator give a basis $(v,u)$ of that plane. For $u=u_\perp+\delta v$ with $|\delta|\le 1/2$, Gauss reduction gives $\sqrt3\,s/2\le p$ and $\|u\|^2=p^2+\delta^2s^2<R^2+s^2/4$. The primitive height kernel in this basis is generated by $(-\ell/j,k/j)$, giving (2) and Part 139's divisor $e$. A horizontal rounded lift is consequently $\pm(x,0)$. When $e=1$ and $2|\langle x,y\rangle|=s^2$, translating that lift by one copy of $v$ gives the other, nonhorizontal nearest lift. Assigning the tie to the second case counts the plane once.

**Counterexample.** For every $n\ge3$, $Q>s$ and $(n,1)$ is strictly inside the centered coordinate ranges at height one. For $2\le a<n$, both $an$ and $Q-an$ are at least $n+1>s$. At height $n$ the last two coordinates give squared norm at least $2n^2>s^2$, and heights $a\ge n+1$ already exceed $s$. This proves uniqueness up to sign. In the saturated plane the unique rounded lift of the chosen quotient generator has height $-n$ and $4(R^2-\|u\|^2)=n^2+14>0$. Both the winning representative and its marked class have second coordinate $1$, excluding alignment with the witnessing first axis. The exact ratio $2(n^2+1)/(3\sqrt{n^2+2})$ diverges for this family with its stated toy cutoff.

**Shell and remaining moment.** Use Part 152's scalar $u=R(T)$ on the Proposition 5 stratum. A horizontal rounded lift would imply $B/u<\sqrt{363^{2/505}+1/(4\alpha^2)}<1.165$, since $1\le s\le T<363$ and $T/u\le1/\alpha$. The inclusive inner endpoint of the shell already gives $B/u\ge T/8\ge0.9h/8>3.592$. Thus every actual shell incidence admits a nonhorizontal rounded lift, including when the plane has height image $\mathbb Z$ and the winner has $k>1$. Part 150's $2^{71.640}$ upper bound covers these incidences. Part 152's $2^{617.94}$ lower bound concerns its relaxed estimate. The exchange supplies no budget-sized bound on $F_{\rm rem}$, and Part 153 concerns a separate penultimate event.

The old sign pair, divisor and lift classification agreed on every one of the 339 case–draw pairs. The three count triples in table order were $(0,8,0)$, $(0,576,328)$ and $(10,92,12)$.
