# Fractional multiples at the modulus prime

For a fixed rational prefix plane, the interior test has an exact probability over the planted matrix, including at the exceptional prime equal to the modulus. It requires two finite-field rank expectations. The prefix lattice itself may vary with the matrix. For a prefix plane selected by reduction the same rank test holds pointwise, but the rank expectations need the selected law.

## From saturation to a single exceptional test

Fix prime $q$, a planting $u=(e,s,1)$ and uniform $A$ as in [the planted model](usvp_planted.md). Let $P$ be a rational plane of codimension $β>0$, $S=P\cap\mathbb Z^d$, $W=P\cap L_A$, $G=\mathbb Z^d/S$ and $H=(L_A+S)/S$. For an output basis prefix, its integer span is exactly $W$ because the basis extends to all of $L_A$. Exclude $u\in P$, the separate vanishing stratum. Write $[u]=c h$ in $G$ with $h$ primitive and $c>0$. The integer $c$ is computable as the gcd of the coordinates in any integral basis of $G$.

Put $j=\operatorname{ord}(h+H)$ and $a=c/j$. Since $qG\subset H$ and $c h\in H$, $j$ divides both $q$ and $c$. On the marked real line the projected lattice is generated by $jh$, using the orthogonal identification of $H$ with the projected lattice. Thus the interior occurs exactly when $a>1$, and all attainable signed fractions are $k/a$ for integers $0<|k|<a$. Completing $jh$ to a basis of $H$ gives
$$
L_A/(W+\mathbb Zu)\simeq\mathbb Z^{β-1}\oplus\mathbb Z/a.
$$
In particular $a$ is the index of $W+\mathbb Zu$ in its saturation in $L_A$. This recovers the [fixed-plane multiplicity](wall1_reachable.md). For $c=1$ the interior probability is zero. For $c>1$ with $c\ne q$ it is one, since $j=1$ or $q$ and $j\mid c$. Only $c=q$ requires a probability calculation.

## Fixed-plane formula when $c=q$

Choose an integral lift $z$ of $h$, and reduce all coordinates modulo $q$. Write $T$ for the image of $S$ modulo $q$ and $T'=T+\mathbb F_q\bar z$. Use the linear maps
$$
X(x,y,t)=x-te,\qquad Y(x,y,t)=y-ts,\qquad D_A=X+AY.
$$
Then $D_A(u)=0$ and $L_A$ is the integral preimage of its kernel. The condition $a>1$ now means $j=1$, or $z\in L_A+S$. This holds precisely when $D_A(\bar z)\in D_A(T)$, because every element of $T$ lifts to $S$. Adjoining one column raises rank by zero or one. Hence the fixed-plane probability is exactly
$$
1-F(T')+F(T),\qquad F(V)=\mathbb E_A\dim D_A(V).
$$
This expression is independent of the lift.

For each fixed $V$, put $k=\dim Y(V)$ and $t_0=\dim X(V\cap\ker Y)$. Then $F(V)=t_0+\mathbb E\operatorname{rank} M$ for a uniform $(m-t_0)\times k$ matrix $M$ over $\mathbb F_q$. Indeed, first take the image of $V\cap\ker Y$, and quotient the codomain by this fixed image. Lift a basis of $Y(V)$ to $V$. Applying $A$ to those basis vectors gives independent uniform columns, since the basis extends to one of the secret-coordinate space. Adding their fixed $X$ offsets and passing to the quotient preserves joint uniformity. The rank expectation for dimensions $b\times k$ is the finite sum
$$
q^{-bk}\sum_{i=1}^{\min(b,k)}i\prod_{l=0}^{i-1}
\frac{(q^b-q^l)(q^k-q^l)}{q^i-q^l}.
$$
Indeed each rank-$i$ matrix factors as a full-column-rank matrix times a full-row-rank matrix, uniquely up to change of the intermediate basis. The empty sum handles zero dimensions.

## A selection obstruction with a unique shortest plant

Take $m=n=1$, $q=3$, $u=(0,0,1)$ and write $A$ as a scalar. For each $v\in\{0,1,2\}$ set $b_v=(-v,1,0)$ and $P_v=\mathbb R(u+3b_v)$. The generator has last coordinate one, so it spans the full integral intersection. In the quotient $[u]=-3[b_v]$ with $[b_v]$ primitive, since the three columns $(1,0,0),b_v,u+3b_v$ have determinant one. Modulo three the prefix generator reduces to $u$, of syndrome zero. Thus the interior occurs exactly when $D_A(b_v)=A-v=0$. Each fixed plane has probability $1/3$. Selecting $P_A$ gives probability one, and selecting $P_{A+1\bmod 3}$ gives zero. For $A=1,2$ the plant is uniquely shortest up to sign, because neither horizontal coordinate unit has zero syndrome. Even conditional on these two inputs the selectors give one and zero. These are primitive lattice prefixes (intersection with a rational plane is saturated), with no claim of reducedness.

For an actual output plane $P_A$, on $c=q$ the required probability contribution is the expectation of the indicator of noncapture and $c=q$ times $1-\dim D_A(T')+\dim D_A(T)$, restricted to nonvanishing outputs. On $c>1$, $c\ne q$ the contribution is the mass of those nonvanishing uncaptured outputs themselves. The example shows why inserting the fixed-plane expectations into this decomposition can fail even under ambient uniqueness. Bounding these selected masses for BKZ or slide reduction remains open. The calculation yields no certified decrease in block size.
