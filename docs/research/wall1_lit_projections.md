# Short vectors after selecting a projection

**Research note, 22 November 2026.** I found no theorem giving the requested 99% lower bound for the final retained lattice of the first BKZ pass. The closest arithmetic results describe the successive **orthogonal quotient shapes** of a uniformly counted flag, or the distribution of a uniform primitive residue matrix together with an associated lattice. Neither sampling rule implements the SVP comparisons. There is, however, an exact finite-field conditional law for a prescribed rational projection. It gives a way to insert the missing comparison probabilities into a count at the actual modulus.

At width 506 the retained rank is 505. Write $h(Q)=(\det Q/V_d)^{1/d}$ for rank $d$, where $V_d$ is unit-ball volume. Part 117 reduces the problem to a uniform $400\times105$ initial graph at $q=3329$, followed by 407 adaptive selections with fresh columns and the prescribed lexicographic tie rule. A suffix failure bound of **0.00977696** suffices. For scale, a Haar rank-505 lattice has probability at most $0.954^{505}/(2\zeta(505))<2.350\cdot10^{-11}$ of falling below the requested radius. Even an event-specific multiplicative loss of $4.16\cdot10^8$ from this elementary upper bound would fit the suffix budget. A uniform estimate over every possible prefix cannot give this conclusion at the available determinant cap. ## A fixed projection of a finite q-ary lattice

**Deduction, exact at finite rank.** Use the random-code ensemble first. For prime $q$, let $C$ be uniform among the $k$-planes of $\mathbb F_q^N$, and $L_C$ its inverse image in $\mathbb Z^N$. Fix a rational real subspace $E$ of dimension $r$, put $P=E\cap\mathbb Z^N$, $F=\pi_{E^\perp}\mathbb Z^N$, and $d=N-r$. The sublattice removed is $K=L_C\cap E$. The split exact sequence of free abelian groups with kernel $P$ and quotient $F$ gives a surjection modulo $q$ with kernel $U=P/qP$. Write $s=k-\dim(C\cap U)$. Conditional on any feasible $s$, the following hold.

* The image $S$ of $C$ in $F/qF$ is a uniform $s$-plane. This remains true on prescribing the actual intersection $C\cap U$. Choose an integral basis extending a basis of $P$. In the resulting modular splitting, every invertible map on the quotient lifts to one fixing $U$ pointwise, and these maps act transitively on the possible images.
* The projected lattice is precisely the inverse image of $S$ in $F$. Indeed any lift of an allowed class can be corrected by an element of $qF$, and $qF$ already lies in the projection. Thus $D=\det Q=q^{d-s}\det F$ and $\det F=1/\det P$. The metric on $F$ is inherited from Euclidean projection. An integral basis change used to count classes does not make $F$ cubic.
* Put $B=B^\circ(0,0.954(D/V_d)^{1/d})$. If $B$ contains a nonzero point of $qF$, failure is certain. Otherwise let $J$ be the number of **distinct projective lines** in $F/qF$ represented by points of $F\cap B$ with nonzero residue. Then
  $$
  \Pr(\lambda_1(Q)<0.954h(Q)\mid s)
     \le \min\{1,J(q^s-1)/(q^d-1)\}.
  $$
  Each line belongs to a uniform $s$-plane with the displayed probability. Repeated lifts and multiples carry no additional charge. More generally a set of lines spanning dimension $a\le s$ is included with probability $\prod_{i=0}^{a-1}(q^s-q^i)/(q^d-q^i)$, and with probability zero for $a>s$. Thus finite inclusion–exclusion gives the exact hit probability as well. There are no dimension-dependent asymptotic errors.

**Graph chart and selection.** A uniform $u\times k$ graph matrix, $u+k=N$, is the uniform plane conditioned to project bijectively onto the prescribed $k$ coordinates. The chart probability is $q^{uk}/{N\brack k}_q=\prod_{j=1}^k(1-q^{-j})/(1-q^{-(u+j)})>1-1/(q-1)$. This charges less than a factor 1.00031 at 3329 for an *unconditional* event on the full code. After conditioning on another event one must recompute the chart probability or bound the joint numerator first. This comparison changes no Euclidean lengths.

For the suffix, concatenate the initial matrix and unread columns in their original order to form one $400\times512$ graph. First-pass freezes successively quotient by rational lines, so their total real span $E$ in these coordinates specifies the terminal projection. For each candidate $E$, insert the indicator that the ordered windows actually select this span. Conditional on $E$ **as a fixed test space**, $s$, and $S$, average this indicator and the graph-chart indicator over the remaining lifts of $C$. Multiplying the resulting weight by the explicit bad-image indicator above and summing over candidate spans and $s$ gives the graph failure probability after division by the chart probability. Equivalently one may sum over paths with disjoint lexicographic selection events. This keeps the unexposed extension data available to test competing vectors. The required new estimate is an upper bound on those weights on bad images, including the deterministic $qF$ case. Dropping the weights reintroduces Part 116's obstruction. No estimate of the weighted sum at rank 505 was obtained.

## Exact Haar conditioning and the obstruction to a second moment

**A planted point and a selected point.** Strömbergsson's Proposition 5.1 gives the Palm probability $\nu_p$ for Haar unimodular lattices containing $p\ne0$. Its defining identity integrates $\nu_p(A)$ against $dp$ over a test set to obtain the Haar expectation of the number of marked points there on $A$. This identity is exact in fixed dimension. The all-point measure includes imprimitive marks. For the primitive component the direction-fixed fiber has a Haar determinant-normalized quotient and a uniform torus of lifts. To condition the mark to be globally shortest, restrict the fiber to the event of no nonzero point in the open ball of radius $\|p\|$ and renormalize. The void event already excludes imprimitive marks. The density of a quotient shape under this restriction is proportional to the fraction of its lifts passing the void test. This identifies a quantity to compute in a discrete analogue. A model that merely inserts a short primitive vector omits this fraction. Part 105 has already quantified the one-step Haar comparison. Its input hypothesis fails on subsequent graph windows. [Strömbergsson, §5, especially Prop. 5.1](https://www2.math.uu.se/~ast10761/papers/lprob.pdf) · [One-step selection law](wall1_selection_law.md)

**Intersecting candidate planes.** Kim's Theorem 4 gives an exact negative result complementary to the flag first moment used in Part 115. For Haar unimodular $L$, fixed positive covolume cutoffs, primitive $A,B$ of ranks $d_1,d_2$, and prescribed intersection rank $1\le r<\min(d_1,d_2)$ with $d_1+d_2<n+r$, the expected number of such ordered pairs under both cutoffs is infinite. In particular, two bounded-area primitive planes sharing a line already cause divergence in dimension 506. A Rogers variance estimate for the unweighted candidate-plane count in the final-window sieve therefore has no finite Haar variance. A shortest-line condition or a lower cutoff on the common line changes the integrand and would require a new estimate. The divergence is for Haar averaging. It makes no claim of an infinite variance on a finite graph ensemble. [Kim, Thms. 4–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.10874) · [Part 117, terminal plane sieve](wall1_idea_round2.md)

For congruence information there are exact higher-moment identities for Siegel transforms on continuous spaces of affine lattices and congruence covers. They can count several competitors in prescribed congruence classes under the invariant measure. The base lattice still varies continuously. Conditioning on the specific integral graph and its winning line needs a discrete calculation such as the one above. I did not verify a conditional Rogers theorem for that selector. [Alam, Ghosh and Han, higher moment formulae](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-institute-of-mathematics-of-jussieu/article/abs/higher-moment-formulae-and-limiting-distributions-of-lattice-points/460423CB3FBB8E2947BA4A976A92ABB9)

## Which arithmetic averages see the quotient shape?

For general $L$, $(\pi_{E^\perp}L)^*=L^*\cap E^\perp$ in the quotient metric. Thus a theorem about complementary sections of $\mathbb Z^n$ can test the projected minimum by applying the minimum test **after dualization**. Dualization preserves Haar probability on shapes. Individual primal and dual minima can differ.

* **Uniform primitive flags, height cutoff.** Horesh–Karasik, Thm. 1.1, treats primitive flags ending at $\mathbb Z^n$ with rank increments $(d_1,\ldots,d_\ell)$ and maximum prefix covolume at most $X$. For boundary-controllable domains of successive quotient shapes $B$ and oriented real flags $A$, the count is $c\mu(B)\mu(A)X^m+O_\epsilon(X^{m(1-1/(16n^2)+\epsilon)})$, where $m=2n-d_1-d_\ell$, $c$ is (1.7), and the shape measure is product Haar. Unlike a count of individual prefix shapes this includes the **last projected factor itself**. This generalizes the Schmidt height-ordered single-sublattice setting of Part 107. One could test a suffix block and several earlier quotient shapes jointly, provided the selector weight could be bounded on the resulting domain. At fixed large $n$ the small power saving and unspecified multiplicative constant supply no concrete 1% error. The ambient lattice here is fixed and each qualifying flag receives equal weight. [Horesh–Karasik, Thm. 1.1 and §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.12724) · [Schmidt, 1998](https://geodesic.mathdoc.fr/item/MOMA_1998__125_4_178800/)
* **Uniform primitive vector, exact norm.** For fixed Euclidean dimension $n\ge6$, sample uniformly from primitive integer vectors of squared norm $D$ on nonempty shells. As $D\to\infty$, direction and normalized orthogonal-section shape jointly tend to uniform sphere measure times Haar shape. Aka–Einsiedler–Shapira prove the stronger orthogonal-grid statement. This would give the limiting bad fraction among *all* such vector marks at fixed rank, by dualization (the Haar radius boundary has measure zero by Siegel integration). The theorem provides no numerical shell threshold for ranks 500–1000. Length conditioning alone assigns the wrong weights to marks found by SVP. [Aka–Einsiedler–Shapira, orthogonal grids](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.1272) · [Companion orthogonal-lattices paper in dimension three](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04209)
* **Uniform rational subspace, exact discriminant.** For the standard dot product, Aka–Musso–Wieser Thm. 1.4 applies in particular to $5\le k\le n-k$ (also with ranks exchanged). Take nonempty shells of rational $k$-planes with squared primitive covolume $D$ whose $k$-power-free part tends to infinity. The span and the two integral section shapes jointly equidistribute for product invariant probability. Their theorem also treats smaller $k$ with local hypotheses. At the proposed ranks it is a qualitative fixed-dimension limit, with no finite error term. This answers what a random plane at the *same discriminant* might contribute in a reference count. Neither BKZ's selection frequency on that shell nor the shell error at its realized discriminant is controlled. [Aka–Musso–Wieser, Thm. 1.4 and Remark 1.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.05163)

## Effective q-averages and BKZ models

**A quantitative matrix theorem.** Einsiedler–Mozes–Shah–Shapira's primitive rational point theorem has an effective extension by El-Baz, Lee and Strömbergsson (Thm. 1.2). For $2\le n<d$, $d\ge3$, average over uniform $d\times n$ residue matrices $R$ whose rows generate $(\mathbb Z/q)^n$. Test jointly $R/q$ on the torus and its translated horospherical point as defined by (1.7). The reference law is Lebesgue times the invariant probability on $\Gamma\backslash\Gamma H$, a torus bundle over the rank-$d$ lattice space. For $0<\epsilon<1/2$, write $a=2dn$, $b=(d^2-1)/2$. For functions with bounded derivatives through the smallest integer strictly greater than $\max(a,b)$ the absolute error is
$$
 O_{d,\epsilon}\big(S_{\infty,a+\epsilon}(f)q^{-(n-1)+\epsilon}
 +S_{2,b+\epsilon}(f)q^{-\min(n,d-n)/2+\epsilon}\big).
$$
For the restart matrix dimensions $(d,n)=(400,105)$ the two powers before $\epsilon$ are $-104,-52.5$, but the Sobolev orders are $84000+\epsilon,79999.5+\epsilon$. The probability that the uniform restart matrix violates primitivity is at most $(q^{105}-1)q^{-400}/(q-1)$ by counting kernel lines. Thus primitivity itself costs very little. To use this theorem one would need a smooth majorant of the suffix failure probability as a function of restart data, an estimate of its integral, and bounds on its norms and the implied constant. The limiting homogeneous measure is not a rank-505 Haar graph in its physical metric. In particular the impressive powers of $q$ alone yield no numerical error for the discontinuous SVP selector. [Effective theorem, §§1.1–1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.07408) · [Einsiedler–Mozes–Shah–Shapira](https://people.math.osu.edu/shah.595/primitive-rational.pdf)

**Hecke correspondences.** Clozel–Oh–Ullmo concern averages over a complete double coset. Part 111 already computes the full index-$q$ mean-zero operator bound $2\sqrt q/(q+1)$ in the relevant ranks. Benoist–Oh extend Hecke averaging to translates of closed homogeneous orbits. For example Cor. 1.3 counts points of the Hecke translates of a rational regular semisimple conjugacy class with real-isotropic, rational-anisotropic torus centralizer, in a compact continuity set, asymptotically as degree grows. That corollary gives no numerical remainder. Applying homogeneous-orbit averaging to the missing torsion directions would first require identifying the weighted orbit sampled by the reducer. The surviving mark after step 105 occupies at most $q^{-105}$ of full torsion. Neither an orbit limit nor a Haar operator norm controls the restricted average at a discrete starting state. [Clozel–Oh–Ullmo](https://ullmo.ihes.fr/publications/COU-Invent.pdf) · [Benoist–Oh, §1](https://www.imo.universite-paris-saclay.fr/~yves.benoist/prepubli/05equidistribution.pdf) · [Part 111](wall1_hecke_gap.md)

**Reduced bases and later work.** Laarhoven's 2026 preprint Thm. 1.5 genuinely permits adaptive basis choice. With probability $1-o(1)$ over rank-$n$ Haar input, *every* basis has unpruned enumeration tree size at least $n^{((2-\sqrt2)/4)n+o(n)}$ at the shortest-vector radius. The proof excludes nested chains with jointly small prefix determinants. This suggests using several necessary path inequalities at once, but the observable is total tree size and its asymptotic errors allow a single exceptional retained minimum. Ducas–Loyer explicitly distinguish mean counts of dense sublattices from the probability that one exists. Their reduction model supplies no certified transfer error to the selected terminal quotient. A reduced-basis counting law also weights a lattice by its multiplicity of qualifying bases unless normalized per lattice, and even uniform choice among qualifying bases need not reproduce first-pass output. The Kim–Venkatesh reduced-basis law and its hypotheses are covered in Part 107. I did not verify a theorem of Pulles, Postlethwaite, or a later Kim paper giving a finite-error law for this BKZ observable. [Laarhoven, Thm. 1.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15265) · [Ducas–Loyer, §§2.2–2.3](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/35806/35806.pdf) · [Prior reduced-basis survey](wall1_lit_random_bases.md)

The outstanding estimate is the graph probability of a bad quotient **and** all the comparisons that select its span. Fixed-plane residue counts provide an exact reference at $q=3329$. Flag and shell equidistribution describe different reference measures. None of the checked sources bounds the contribution of the winning spans to the required 0.00977696.
