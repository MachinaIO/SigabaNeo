## The horizontal quotient and its two kinds of planes

Fix the pre-draw history, with old lattice $L$, designated signed shortest $v$, length $s$, and mark group $H$. Put $p=\pi_{v^\perp}$, $N=pL$, and let $u$ be the uniform mark with representative $\tilde u$. The signed old candidate is fixed before this draw. Whether it wins is a test on $u$, including the prescribed tie rule. On a win the output is exactly
$$
 L'= (N,0)+\mathbb Z(p\tilde u,1).
$$
Thus the projection can be forced before the comparison when obtaining a joint upper bound. The equality follows by applying the projection to both generators of the appended window. There are two exact tests for a saturated hit plane $P\subset L'$.

1. **Wholly old.** Such a plane exists if and only if there is a saturated rank-three $J\subset L$ through $v$ with $\det J\le as$. Indeed $N$ is the height-zero kernel in $L'$, and $L\to N$ is onto with kernel $\mathbb Zv$. Inverse image and projection give a bijection on the indicated saturated sublattices. Extending $v$ by lifts of a basis of $P$ gives $\det J=s\det P$. The incidence $v\in J$ matters even if $a_3(L)\le as$.
2. **Mixed.** The height kernel of $P$ is a primitive line $\mathbb Zx\subset N$. Write $t=\|x\|$. For some $j\ge1$ and $n\in N$, a basis of $P$ is $x,w=(n+jp\tilde u,j)$. It passes the area test exactly when
   $$
   t^2\big(j^2+\|\pi_{x^\perp}(n+jp\tilde u)\|^2\big)\le a^2 .
   $$
   For enumeration, require the class of $w$ to be primitive in $L'/\mathbb Zx$, and take $n$ modulo $\mathbb Zx$. These conditions follow by taking the positive generator of the height image of $P$. On the inherited-shortest stratum, $x$ is a shortest vector of $P$, since every shortest vector of $P$ has height zero.

These are the two branches of the primitive append recursion applied **after** forcing the horizontal projection. In the full window the inverse image of either plane is a saturated rank-three lattice through the winner, of volume $s\det P$. In the mixed case it has positive height. For a chosen old rank-two lift $E$ projecting injectively, its area is multiplied by
$\sqrt{1-\|\operatorname{proj}_{\operatorname{span}E}(v/s)\|^2}$.
This is the square root of the determinant of the restricted form $I-vv^\top/s^2$ in an orthonormal basis of the plane. The lift is nonunique, and the first hit only says its old area exceeded $a$. There is no specified gap above $a$.

For example, take the old rank-three basis $v=(28,0,0), x=(0,30,0), y=(\epsilon,15,a/30)$, with $0<\epsilon<1$. The projected binary basis is reduced with minimum 30, so $v$ gives the unique shortest line. Its quotient plane has area exactly $a$. Every old plane through $v$ has area at least $28\cdot30$ when saturated. Every transverse plane projects to a sublattice of the quotient plane, thus has area at least $a$. Equality would require a degree-one projection from a plane orthogonal to $v$, including a lift of $py$ with first coordinate zero. All such first coordinates lie in $\epsilon+28\mathbb Z$. Yet the plane generated by $x,y$ has area $\sqrt{a^2+900\epsilon^2}$, with projection factor tending to one. This local geometry can be used in a horizontal window by adjoining an orthogonal old period $100q\,e_4$ and appending displacement $100e_4$ at height one. All nonzero heights of absolute value at most 28 then have horizontal component of length at least 100. The extra old period cannot supply a hit plane, by intersecting such a plane with the first three coordinates. The example concerns a Euclidean window, without a claim on its restart probability.

## First-hit lengths and the mixed-plane estimate

For either plane, its ternary inverse image has minimum $s$ and determinant at most $as$. The ternary Hermite inequality gives $s^3\le\sqrt2 as$, hence $s\le R=\sqrt{\sqrt2 a}<33.22505$. A shortest vector of $P$ has length at most $T=\sqrt{2a/\sqrt3}=52/\sqrt3$ by binary reduction. Choose an inherited shortest $x$ of $P$, also in the wholly old case. $$
 st>a,\qquad t\ge\sqrt3\,s/2,\qquad
 26<t\le T,\quad 26<s\le R .
 \tag{2}
$$

## Audit

**Verdict — SOUND.** Equation (1) and the membership-conditioned identity (3) hold for the first-hit event specified in the [Part 192 audit](wall1_rank2_tail.md#audit). No correction is required. In particular, a tie involving a fresh shortest vector in any hit plane removes the history from $Z_1$, and a wholly old hit takes priority even when a mixed plane also hits.

**Mixed charges.** At first creation, lifting an inherited shortest vector of length $t$ produces an old plane of area $st>a$. Nearest-integer rounding along $v$ gives $t\ge\sqrt3\,s/2$, hence $t>26$. If a hit contains the designated line of $N$, the two forced old projections preserve the positive height and leave length at most $a/t<T$. Otherwise, after excluding wholly old hits, the independent old pair $x_0,x$ has area greater than $a$, forcing $t^2>a$. Reducing the fresh generator along $x$ then gives squared length below $5a/4$. Only the first case needs a second projection before the mark average, and its line is fixed by the prefix.

A scratch calculation with 100-decimal-digit outward interval arithmetic used $l_{i-1}$ at draw $i$. The two recurrence branches were disjoint at all 406 updates, with 20 period bounds and 386 Hermite bounds. All six table enclosures held. Writing $S_b(h)$ for the indicated rank-$b$ majorant summed through draw $h$, the bounds used in (1) were
$$
\begin{aligned}
9.2997870293\cdot10^{-13}&<S_{504}(406)<9.2997870294\cdot10^{-13},\\
1.2739696044\cdot10^{-11}&<S_{505}(402)<1.2739696045\cdot10^{-11},\\
1.3669674747\cdot10^{-11}&<S_{504}(406)+S_{505}(402)<1.3669674748\cdot10^{-11}.

The other mixed class first exceeds the proposed allowance in this majorant at draw 403. The actual mass on draws 403–406 remains unevaluated.

**Angular counterexamples.** In the first example any transverse old plane of area at most $a$ would have to project with index one and preserve its area. It would therefore contain an orthogonal lift of $py$, precluded by $\epsilon+28\mathbb Z$. The plane generated by $x,y$ is saturated, since $v,x,y$ are an old basis. Its area decreases to exactly $a$ by the factor $a/\sqrt{a^2+900\epsilon^2}$, arbitrarily close to one. The extra old period and the height-one append preserve the horizontal win. In the second example exact rational arithmetic gives the stated height-one minimum, strictly above $900$. The exterior-coordinate test forces $C=0$, $|B|\le26$ and $|qA+26B|\le26$, so $A=0$. The only nonzero solutions are $B=\pm1$. They describe the same saturated plane, with squared area $a^2-4/3$ and unique shortest line $\mathbb Zx$. Both generators survive the projection isometrically. These examples establish local geometric possibilities without assigning either one a probability under the restart law.

**Remaining probability.** The first draw, chosen plane and shortest old lifts select one ordered pair on each residual history. Reverse rounding gives the stated annuli and the old support ends at column $104+i$, immediately before the current draw. For consistent membership the two row equations have rank $r_1$ for each of the 400 independent output coordinates, giving exactly $Q^{-r_1}$. Thus (3) counts each such history once. Independence of the current raw column under membership alone leaves the horizontal win, the mixed-plane test and the continuation in the same conditional event. The two-axis family also passes the annulus check, since its maximum squared length $1{,}107{,}558{,}401$ is already below $405q^2/4$. Membership alone therefore has total mass one on that family.

Independent enumeration over $\mathbb F_3$ gave $1$, $312$ and $5{,}616$ nonempty fibers at input ranks zero, one and two, respectively. Each fiber had size $3^{3-r_1}$. Of the $38{,}437$ compatible next-column tests, $31{,}560$ had one allowed value at a rank drop and $6{,}877$ had three equally frequent values at constant rank. A bound $p_{\rm old}+p_{\rm late}\le7.6330\cdot10^{-11}$ would therefore suffice for the stated $Z_1$ target by (1). Equation (3) gives its exact expression.
