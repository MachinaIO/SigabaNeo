# Can the selected upper-band plane hit be compared with Haar?

**The width-506 event comparison remains open.** Two obstructions can be proved for the proposed argument. Even the normalized first selected length has a singular restart law after discarding any set of histories of probability less than one. Also, both selections in this event use the *same* random window. An exact small graph example shows why the distinction matters for counting. Two prefixes related by reflection give identical unmarked geometry, including all short-structure counts, but their conditional selected-hit probabilities are $0$ and $2/17$.

## Event and comparison measure

Use the marginal restart for plain LWE $(511,512,3329)$, CBD(3), with the no-lookahead first pass and the prescribed signed ambient tie rule. Let $S$ be the history immediately before draw 406, $M$ its retained lattice of rank $d=505$, $D=\det M$, $b=d+1$, $V_j$ the unit ball volume, $g=(D/V_b)^{1/b}$, and $c=477/500$. Draw $U$ uniformly on the finite group $H=K/M$, $K=P_E\mathbb Z^{910}$. Form $W_U=(M,0)+\mathbb Z(\widetilde U,1)$. Let $v$ be the designated shortest line of $W_U$, $x$ the designated shortest line of $L=P_{v^\perp}W_U$, and write $s=|v|$, $t=|x|$, $p=\lambda_1(P_{x^\perp}L)$ for lengths of primitive generators and the transverse minimum. Put $\rho=s/g$ and $h=(D/(sV_d))^{1/d}$.

Write $G=\{32.18553<g<203.888\}$, $a=0.990$, $e=1.097193$. The restart mass $P$ includes $G$, positive absolute height of $v$, and
$$
 a<\rho\le e,\qquad 1<t\le\sqrt{1+c^2h^2},\qquad p<ch\,t^{-1/d}.
$$
The lower cutoff on $g$ follows from the step-405 floor. The determinant-matched Haar probability $p_H(D)$ uses a Haar window of covolume $D$, the two shortest lines, the band and both gates. The Haar experiment has no integral height label. For the averaged comparison set $Q=\mathbb E_S[\mathbf1_G p_H(D)]$. Both $P$ and $Q$ are unconditional masses. Residual (a) is a separate mass $p_a$ sharing the allowance $B=0.00019770868$. ## Why a likelihood bound on good histories fails

**Proposition 1.** Let $T$ be any positive-probability set of restart histories. Even the marginal law of $\rho$ given $T$ is mutually singular with its determinant-matched Haar counterpart. More quantitatively, an inequality
$$
 \Pr_{\rm restart}(S\in T,\ \rho\in A)
 \le C\,\mathbb E_S[\mathbf1_T\Pr_H(\rho\in A\mid D)]+\delta
$$
for all Borel sets $A$ and finite $C$ requires $\delta\ge\Pr(S\in T)$.

*Proof.* The finite graph array and the deterministic tie rule have finitely many possible histories and windows. Let $F_T$ be the finite set of normalized lengths attainable on $T$. At fixed $D$, a normalized Haar minimum in $F_T$ requires a primitive lattice vector on one of the spheres of radii $gF_T$. Each sphere has volume zero. The primitive mean-value formula gives expected count zero on their union, hence hit probability zero. Integrating over $D$ preserves zero. Taking $A=F_T$ proves both claims. The same argument works at a single history. It also applies to any proposed path density from which $\rho$ is measurable. [Primitive mean-value formula in the repository](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/HaarMinimumTails.lean) · [Part 111](wall1_hecke_gap.md)

The same conclusion holds for tests restricted to finite unions of open intervals of unrestricted widths. Take the union of the $1/n$-neighborhoods of the points in $F_T$. Its restart mass on $T$ is constant, while its matched Haar mass decreases to zero by continuity from above. This also shows why merely thickening each atom, without fixing a resolution, cannot justify a uniform comparison.

The quantifier over $A$ matters. For the single event in question one only tests its indicator. Atomicity places no lower bound on $P$, and the all-sets obstruction does not disprove $P\le C Q$. Likewise the arbitrary-selector example in Part 106 and the modified kernel in Part 139 do not evaluate this event for the prescribed reducer. [Part 106](wall1_chain_law.md) · [Part 139](wall1_lyapunov.md)

**Proposition 2.** Given $S$ and the presence of any particular positive-height candidate $v=(y,k)$ in the band, the mark, both projected lattices for the forced first line, and the second designated line are deterministic.

*Proof.* The window contains $v$ exactly when $[y]=kU$. Here $1\le k\le eg<3329$. Multiplication by $k$ is invertible on $H$ since $3329H=0$ and $3329$ is prime. Thus $U=k^{-1}[y]$. Projection along $v$, shortest-line selection with the fixed tie rule, and the second projection now use fixed inputs. The event is measurable before draw 407. Without fixing $v$, a Voronoi section maps the uniform mark bijectively to a representative set. Conditioning on a winning class restricts that set, and conditioning on a particular candidate as above leaves a singleton. [Part 142, Theorem B and audit](wall1_dual_cusp.md) · [Upper-band quotient identity](wall1_upper_band_quotient.md)

There is a second restriction on the proposed factor $2^2$. For a *Haar input*, primitive incidence at a fixed first length has a Haar transverse shape and uniform lift character. Conditional selection weights that shape by $w/\mathbb E w$, where $w$ is the fraction of characters with no shorter lift. To see the range of the estimate, scale the parent to determinant one and write $\ell=\rho V_b^{-1/b}$. In transverse unimodular coordinates put $r=\ell^{b/d}$. For a nonzero unordered pair $\{z,-z\}$ with $|z|<r$, a shorter lift excludes at most $2\sqrt{1-|z|^2/r^2}$ of the character circle. Union bound, ordinary Siegel averaging over pairs, and slicing the unit $b$-ball give
$$
 1-\mathbb E w\le \tfrac12 r^d V_b=\rho^b/2 .
$$
Since $w\le1$, this proves a density bound $1/(1-\rho^b/2)$ where the denominator is positive. It gives $2$ at $\rho=1$ and ceases to give a finite bound at $2^{1/506}\in(1.00137079,1.00137080)$, inside the band. At $\rho=1.05$ the subtracted term exceeds $2.63\cdot10^{10}$. This calculation neither supplies a band-wide Haar bound of two nor puts a law on the finite marks. [Part 105 and audit](wall1_selection_law.md)

## A pair of indistinguishable unmarked histories

**Proposition 3.** In the one-append graph with one output, two old input coordinates and $q=17$, the two prefixes $(1,1)$ and $(-1,-1)$ have the same distribution for every isometry-invariant statistic of the pair (window, old hyperplane). With the prescribed ambient tie rule, their conditional probabilities of the positive-height selected hit with the stated band and both gates are $0$ and $2/17$, respectively.

*Proof.* In coordinates $(z,u_1,u_2,u_3)$ the graph is $z=A_1u_1+A_2u_2+j u_3\pmod {17}$, where $j$ is the uniform final column. The map negating $u_1,u_2$ and fixing $z,u_3$ identifies the windows for the two prefixes at each *same* $j$, intertwining their old hyperplanes and preserving height. All unmarked intrinsic counts thus agree pointwise, including counts of short vectors, planes, and flags at every cutoff.

Here $b=4$, $d=3$, $D=17$. Only squared length $2$ lies in the band. A positive-height vector of this squared length has last coordinate $\pm1$ and exactly one other nonzero coordinate, also $\pm1$. For the two prefixes the congruence therefore requires $j=\pm1$. At either such mark there is no vector of squared length one. Choose the smaller of the two signed tuples lexicographically. On the positive prefix the winner is $(-1,-1,0,0)$ for both marks, of height zero. On the negative prefix the winner is $(-1,0,0,-j)$ for $j=\pm1$. In fact among tied lines only one starting with $-1$ can use the last coordinate, and the old lines starting with $-1$ have a $+1$ in their first or second input coordinate on this prefix.

For these two winning windows one can change coordinate signs to make every short vector a root $e_i-e_j$ up to sign. The congruence for such short points is then that the coordinate sum is zero: its absolute value is less than 17. Project off any root, say $e_1-e_2$. A root sharing one index projects to squared length $3/2$, a disjoint root to $2$. The shortest projected roots join the first pair of coordinates to a third. Projecting off any of them in turn averages those three coordinates, so a root connecting that triple to the fourth projects to squared length $4/3$. All remaining roots project either there up to sign or to zero. This gives $s^2=2$, $t^2=3/2$, $p^2=4/3$. Completeness at these cutoffs follows by rounding lifts along the chosen generators. A first-quotient point of squared length at most $3/2$ lifts with squared length at most $3/2+2/4=2$. A second-quotient point of squared length at most $4/3$ lifts with squared length at most $4/3+(3/2)/4+2/4<3$. Thus the root list suffices. Substituting the lengths and $h=(17/(\sqrt 2 V_3))^{1/3}$ gives both gates strictly. This proves the two probabilities.

To reproduce, take $b=m+3$, $D=q^m$, enumerate all $m\times3$ graph matrices, and list all signed integer points of squared length $1,2,3$ satisfying their congruences. Retain the smaller signed tuple per pair. For every vertical band candidate project the list exactly via $w\mapsto |v|^2w-\langle v,w\rangle v$, carrying the common scale $|v|^2$. Choose the smallest nonzero projected point by squared norm then signed tuple. Repeat projection and test for a nonzero image below the final cutoff. Count a selected hit only when the forced first line is the actual winner. In each row only squared length $s^2=2$ lies in the band. With $h=(D/(\sqrt2 V_{b-1}))^{1/(b-1)}$ and $r^2=1+c^2 h^2$, both $r^2+1/2$ and $c^2h^2+r^2/4+1/2$ are below $4$. Successive nearest-integer lifts therefore certify completeness for the gated counts even on forced lines. The enumeration used integer projections, rational squared lengths and outward interval comparisons for the gates. Its totals agree with the existing quotient note. For each of the two reflected prefixes, six forced candidates pass both gates. Selection removes all six on one prefix and four on the other. These toy counts establish a limitation of reconstructing selected counts from unmarked geometry. They imply no lower bound on the width-506 mass.

## Haar benchmark and the shared allowance

For clarity, the quoted bound $H_*=4.361\cdot10^{-11}$ is an **upper bound** on $p_H(D)$. It is not a measured denominator. Here is a separate derivation using the reported split $u=1.05$. Write $h_0=32.18553(V_b/(eV_d))^{1/d}$, $X=(c^2+h_0^{-2})^{1/2}$. Since $h=g(V_b/(\rho V_d))^{1/d}$, the good band has $h\ge h_0>32.02610$, $h<204$ and $r=(1+c^2 h^2)^{1/2}<204$.

On $a<\rho\le u$, keep all primitive first lines. Their expected number is $(u^b-a^b)/(2\zeta(b))$. Under primitive-line incidence their transverse lattice has Haar shape at covolume $D/s$. The expected count of second lines with $t\le r$ is at most $X^d/2$, since $r/h\le X$. For each marked second line with $1<t\le r$, its Haar transverse lattice has covolume $D/(st)$. The mean number of primitive lines in the strict last ball is
$$
 \frac{V_{d-1}(ch)^{d-1}t^{-(d-1)/d}st}{2\zeta(d-1)D}
 \le Z=\frac{V_{d-1}c^{d-1}204^{1/d}}{2V_d h_0}.
$$
Here $D=sV_d h^d$. These are iterated incidence integrals, each counting unoriented lines. Every selected hit has at least one such flag. The low-strip upper bound is therefore $(u^b-a^b)X^d Z/4$. On $\rho>u$, shortestness forces an empty open ball of radius $ug$. The Haar primitive-line count in it has mean $m=u^b/(2\zeta(b))$ and second factorial moment at most $m^2$. Cauchy–Schwarz gives empty probability at most $1/(1+m)$. [Marked Haar disintegration](wall1_selection_law.md) · [Haar primitive-ball second moment](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/HaarMinimumLower.lean)

At 75-decimal-digit outward interval precision, the first upper bound lies in $(5.64891,5.64892)\cdot10^{-12}$. Replacing $\zeta(b)$ by $1+2^{-b}(1+2/(b-1))$ in the empty-ball bound gives a value in $(3.79528,3.79529)\cdot10^{-11}$. Their sum is less than $4.36018\cdot10^{-11}<H_*$. Use $V_0=1$, $V_1=2$, $V_j=2\pi V_{j-2}/j$ to reproduce these intervals. These estimates are uniform on $G$.

**Budget implication.** If a good-history comparison yields $P\le C Q+\delta$, with $\delta$ a new unconditional exit charge, then $C H_*+\delta+p_a\le B$ suffices. With $p_a=\delta=0$, $B/H_*\in(4533562.94,4533562.95)$. Taking $C=4500000$ leaves exactly $0.00000146368$ for $\delta+p_a$ using the quoted $H_*$. A direct bound $P\le C H_*$ could suffice even if $P\le C Q$ fails. Dividing a bound on $P$ by an upper bound on $Q$ cannot certify the actual probability ratio.

**Remaining conjecture (event-specific).** For the stated finite graph restart, $P+p_a\le B$. A sufficient stronger comparison would bound the history-averaged selected indicator by $C Q+\delta$ with the budget just given. Conjecture R in Part 136 only names an empty-ball statistic and a truncated one-quotient count for its specified window. It does not assert this joint selected-event inequality. Part 117 gives a fresh graph suffix under its coupling, while Parts 108 and 142 specify the conditional mark weights. None specifies the fraction of those marks on which the designated shortest line passes both gates after an adaptively selected prefix. A first moment of the selected indicator is exactly that missing fraction averaged over prefixes. Dropping first selection changes the statistic, and Proposition 3 identifies information an intrinsic unmarked count can lose even with the geometry fixed. The finite-width comparison and the mass of residual (a) both remain unproved. ## Audit

**Verdict — MINOR CAVEATS.** The singularity proof, reflected-prefix probabilities and Haar estimate check out. Proposition 2 should explicitly assume $S\in G$ (or $eg<3329$). Its proof uses this inequality, and the all-history ceiling $g<3337.640$ in Part 159 alone does not imply it. This qualification covers the whole event defining $P$. In the last paragraph, “none specifies the fraction” should read “none bounds the fraction at the required scale.” The finite recursion and Part 162, equation (1), already specify the fraction exactly for each prefix. [Part 159](wall1_plane_hit_rounding.md) · [Part 162 and audit](wall1_upper_band_quotient.md)

**Draw numbering.** Under Parts 108 and 161, the shared mark is drawn at **406**. For a specified positive-height candidate in the event, $kU=[y]$ has the unique solution $U=k^{-1}[y]$. At that mark the signed tie rule fixes whether the candidate wins, the designated minimum in its quotient, and therefore both gates. Draw 407 occurs after all these decisions. Conditioning only on the winning height can leave several marks. [Part 108](wall1_first_tour.md) · [Part 142, Theorem B](wall1_dual_cusp.md) · [Part 161, setting and Proposition 4](wall1_band_plane_hits.md)

**Scope of singularity.** Parts 111 and 138 U1 establish atomicity for lattice shapes. Proposition 1 also checks the point that is needed after taking the scalar minimum. At each fixed determinant, primitive Siegel assigns expected count zero to the spheres at the finitely many attainable normalized lengths, rescaled by $g$. Their union thus has Haar hit probability zero even after averaging over the histories in $T$. Its restart probability conditional on $T$ is one, so the unconditional additive charge for an all-sets comparison is at least $\Pr(T)$. Shrinking open neighborhoods give the same obstruction. An inequality for the single gated event remains open. Part 105 supplies the factor two for a Haar input at $\rho=1$, and its stated estimate loses its positive denominator inside this band. Part 139's modified kernel does not evaluate the prescribed kernel on this event. [Part 105](wall1_selection_law.md) · [Part 111](wall1_hecke_gap.md) · [Part 138, U1 and audit](wall1_reducer_variants.md) · [Part 139](wall1_lyapunov.md)

**Independent toy rerun.** Exact integer projections, signed lexicographic comparisons and interval checks of the gates reproduced both enumeration rows. Each reflected prefix has six forced hits. At $j=1,-1$ the first winner for $(1,1)$ is $(-1,-1,0,0)$ both times. For $(-1,-1)$ it is $(-1,0,0,-j)$, with $(s^2,t^2,p^2)=(2,3/2,4/3)$ both times. All other marks contribute zero selected hits. The reflection preserves the graph and old hyperplane between the two prefixes at each fixed mark. The signed tie rule therefore accounts for the selected probabilities $0$ and $2/17$. Across all designs the forced and selected totals were $24$ and $2$ at $q=17$, and $48$ and $4$ at $q=5$. [Part 162, independent toy enumeration](wall1_upper_band_quotient.md)

**Haar bound and allowance.** The low strip counts unoriented primitive flags and the upper strip uses the empty open parent ball implied by shortestness. With factorial and half-integer factorial ball volumes, an independent outward interval calculation put their summed upper bounds in $(4.36017873984,4.36017873986)\cdot10^{-11}$. This bounds the joint Haar probability uniformly on $G$. Using the quoted $H_*=4.361\cdot10^{-11}$ gives $B-4500000H_*=1.46368\cdot10^{-6}$ exactly. Using the sharper rounded bound $4.36018\cdot10^{-11}$ gives $1.50058\cdot10^{-6}$. Either remainder is shared by $p_a$ and any new exit charge. Part 161's allocation is sufficient for the original target. Neither the event-specific comparison factor nor the residual mass has been established.
