# Independent audit of Parts 391–393

**Verdict:** the three local refinements are valid under the existing fixed-code
lifting and crossing-basis hypotheses, and their current production uses preserve
those hypotheses and the required cover. No counterexample or missing addback
was found. This audit supplies the all-configuration arguments below; the finite
checks alone are not the proof. It does not audit every earlier global counting
lemma, rerun the full cumulant evaluator, establish a selected-history estimate,
or formalize these results in Lean.

## Shared seven-point anchor, four new positions: height 32/5

Write the balanced anchor rows as

    a=(1,1,-1,-1,0,0,0), b=(1,0,0,0,1,-1,-1).

Independent global row signs make the shared entry positive; permutations within
each exclusive triple then give this representative. These operations preserve
balance. Arbitrary coordinate sign changes are not needed for this reduction.
The old Gram matrix is [[4,1],[1,4]]. Exclusive coordinates 1 and 4 give a unit
minor, so the old integer plane is saturated.

Its parallelogram is split along the diagonal joining a and b. Both triangles
have squared side lengths 4,4,6, area squared 15/4, and circumradius squared
(4*4*6)/(16*(15/4))=8/5. Each triangle is covered by the three radius-sqrt(8/5)
balls at its vertices. For example, partition into the three nearest-vertex
Voronoi regions; the maximum distance in each polygon occurs at a vertex, whose
possible distances are bounded by the circumradius (the midpoint distances are
smaller). Translating this triangulation proves covering radius squared at most
8/5. Equality is unnecessary.

The crossing code is generated by a,b and a fundamental row w whose old part is
supported on an independent anchor basis B and is nonzero. Since B is independent,
no nonzero relation of the old anchor can be supported in B. Thus w_old is not
in span_Fq(a,b). Any codeword independent of the old plane has all four new
coordinates nonzero, because those coordinates are a common nonzero scalar
multiple of w_new. Its old part cannot lie in span_Fq(a,b).

This last condition is stronger than merely having four new nonzero coordinates
and must be retained. For a short integral representative c, it also rules out
c_old lying in the *real* old plane: saturation would express it as integer
coefficients of a,b, and reduction modulo q would give the forbidden old part.
Consequently c has a nonzero old perpendicular projection. These are exactly the
crossing-basis requirements documented by `code_ball_bound` and implemented in
the coefficient sum over a>=1 in `other_envelopes`.

In the existing lifted rank-three lattice, take an outside-old generator and
subtract a nearest vector of Za+Zb. The new coordinates and full perpendicular
component are unchanged. If the latter had squared norm <32/5, the reduced
integer row would have norm squared <32/5+8/5=8. Its coordinate sum is a multiple
of q, since the coefficient code is affine. Cauchy–Schwarz gives absolute sum
<sqrt(11*8)<3329; hence its sum is zero. Its squared norm is therefore an even
integer. The four new nonzero coordinates imply norm squared >=4. Equality at
4 would force every old coordinate to vanish, contradicting the old-part fact
above. The only remaining possibility below 8 is norm squared 6. This is excluded
in the complement under consideration. Hence its third squared GS height is at
least 32/5. The argument also handles a nonprimitive initially chosen generator
by applying it to the primitive quotient generator of the saturated lifted
lattice; the older lifting lemma guarantees independent reduction modulo q.

A balanced norm-six row with four nonzero new coordinates has six unit entries;
the only other balanced shape has support three and cannot occur. Choose its two
old positions in 21 ways and its three positive signs among six entries modulo
global sign in 10 ways. This is a bijection onto all 210 possible witness lines.
The determinant is 90-4x^2+2xy-4y^2. Summing the indicator of this expression over
those 21*10 explicitly specified choices gives counts
74:64, 80:46, 84:44, 86:28, 90:28. This finite enumeration is complete because of
the preceding shape theorem. A column exclusive to a, a column exclusive to b,
and a new unit coordinate give a triangular minor of determinant +/-1, even
if c has entries at the old chosen coordinates. Thus the full lattice basis is
saturated and third height equals D/15, rather than just being a lower bound.

For a fixed anchor basis, one line determines at most one normalized crossing
code: express its old class in the quotient by the old relation plane and
normalize one new coordinate. Distinct lines may give the same code; this only
increases the witness bound. Coefficient counting and finite-field saturation
therefore cause no missing multiplicity or missing factor of q.

**Production check.** `wall1_other_short_anchors.weighted_joint` uses the new
bound only for (size,t)=(7,4), which has local size 11 and local rank 8 regardless
of the later full-support rank. It takes the minimum of complete old bounds and

    sum_D count[D] J(D/15) + coefficients * J(32/5).

The complement keeps the full coefficient count; overlap is harmless. The
`ball_bound` implementation keeps beta, Markov prefactor, q^(-8m), all periodic
terms, and exp(-29^2 alpha/2)*theta(alpha/2)^3. Its assertion
15*29^4<3329^2 is the same rank-four-minor cutoff as the old code. No new weighted
metric or changed cutoff is introduced. The old lifting bound is sufficient:
independent short representatives beyond rank three would give a nonzero
four-minor divisible by q with volume at most sqrt(15)*29^2<q. A rank-three
lift has covolume <sqrt(15)*29<q; saturation has index prime to q and remains
inside the code. These are the existing hypotheses needed to extend the old
primitive plane to its lifted basis.

## Shared seven-point anchor, two new positions: conditional D44 deletion

For a balanced norm-four row c nonzero at both new coordinates, precisely two
old coordinates are unit entries. Put x=<a,c>, y=<b,c>. At determinant 44,

    2x^2-xy+2y^2=8,  |x|,|y|<=2.

If both absolute values were <=1 the left side would be <=5. Thus one old row u
has |<u,c>|=2. Its overlap with c consists of exactly those two old positions,
whose relative signs agree. Subtracting sign(<u,c>)*c from u cancels the overlap
and gives another balanced norm-four line on the four exclusive positions.
Their union has six positions. This is a full triangular four/four relation
plane. A pair of exclusive coordinates gives a unit two-minor, so this plane
is saturated and independent modulo q. Its support and sign pattern are in
the existing affine triangular family; there is no merely-real relation that
could disappear upon reduction modulo 3329. The full a,b,c triple has a unit
minor using a-only, b-only and new coordinates as above.

The correct use is a set partition by existence of *any* eligible triangular
anchor. On the no-triangle part, the entire D44 coefficient code is impossible,
so its whole code term, including tail and periodic contributions, may be
removed. It would be incorrect to perform this deletion in an unconditional
other-anchor bound without paying for the triangular event.

**Production check.** `other_envelopes` defaults to `exclude_triangular=False`.
Only when explicitly true is D44 omitted from the t=2 rare sum. The no-new-four
term is retained, and all other branches retain their previous upper bounds.
The two callers passing true are:

* `wall1_larger_triangular_components.actual_parameters`: every candidate using
  `other` also adds `triangular[h,r]`. This triangular bound is itself the
  minimum of a complete old cover and a restricted cover plus its D44 addback.
* `wall1_other_short_anchors.actual_parameters`: its candidate adds the complete
  `triangular_envelopes(s)` result alongside the restricted other bound.

The diagnostic caller uses the default false. Minima with older unconditional
bounds remain valid on the restricted family. The exclusion flows through both
crossing and marked-pair uses of `weighted_joint`, whose crossing geometry is
identical. No missing triangular addback was found.

## Six-support circuit on eleven positions: exclusion of an (8,8) pair

A balanced integral norm-eight vector has unit support eight, shape
(2,1,-1,-1,-1) up to sign/permutation, or shape (2,-2). Girth six excludes the
last two shapes: their nonzero reductions have support five or two. Suppose
independent norm-eight vectors form the short rank-two plane. A nonzero integer
two-minor has absolute value at most 8<3329, so their reductions are independent
and span the rank-two code. Since that code has full support, their supports
cover all eleven positions. Their resulting exclusive unit coordinates also
show saturation.
They overlap at five coordinates and each has three exclusive coordinates.

The eleven nonzero columns of the two-row matrix belong to projective classes
of sizes 3,3,p,5-p, corresponding to slopes infinity,0,+1,-1. In odd
characteristic these are distinct classes. A nonzero coefficient combination
vanishes precisely on one class. A prescribed word of support six must vanish
on five columns, so p=0 or p=5. The inner product is therefore +/-5, and the
appropriate sum or difference has squared norm 16-10=6. It is balanced and
nonzero modulo q. This contradicts the no-six family; it also contradicts
Gauss reduction since 5>8/2.

The remaining Gauss-reduced cases have first squared length 8 and second
squared length >=10, giving second GS squared height >=10-8/4=8; or first
squared length >=10, giving second GS height >=3*10/4=15/2. The complete bound
is therefore the sum of the two complete Nth-power kernels, not a per-coordinate
choice of an independent case.

**Production check.** `wall1_general_two_relations.draw14_bounds` applies the
improvement only when c==6 and union==11, inside the no-six term. It uses the
pairs (8,8) and (10,15/2), with `exp(-98*alpha)*half_sum` and unchanged periodic
terms, and minimizes against the older complete no-six bound. It neither
removes a coefficient family nor changes the one-six/two-six addbacks. The
cutoff remains 14; the rank-three volume test is 14^3<3329 and
sqrt(11)*14<3329 ensures balance of short representatives. Unit exclusives
establish the finite-field independence required by the projective-column
argument. There is no claim of a norm-eight classification in the tail.
