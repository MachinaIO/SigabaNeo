# Averaging the terminal cylinder over the finite mark

In the marginal width-506 restart, **each primitive old line chosen before the terminal draw contributes less than $9.359728\cdot10^{-10}$ to $F_{\rm rem}$**, uniformly in the history. The estimate includes every terminal height and preserves plane multiplicity when summed over old lines. A separate design average bounds the entire strip $7R_*/8\le R<R_*$ by $7.191421\cdot10^{-6}$. Neither estimate controls the sum over all old lines at smaller radii. Section 5 locates a loss in that sum already at plane height one.

## 1. Which average is available?

Condition on the complete history $S$ through selection 406 after the independent $400\times105$ restart graph. Write $L=P_E M\subset E$ for the retained lattice in the old ambient dimension 911, $D=\det L$, $K=P_E\mathbb Z^{911}$, $H=K/L$, $d=505$, $q=3329$, $c=0.954$. Write $V_d$ for the unit ball volume, $G_d=V_d^{-1/d}$, $h=G_dD^{1/d}$, $t=\lambda_1(L)$, $\alpha=\sqrt3/2$ and $R_*=q^{400/912}\sqrt{d/(2\pi)}$. The fresh displacement $U$ is uniform on $H$. The window is $W=(L,0)+\mathbb Z(\tilde U,1)$, with its inherited signed tie rule. On a nonhorizontal win orient $v=(y,k)$ upwards, put $s=\|v\|$ and write $R=R(s)=ch s^{-1/d}$.

Let $Z_{\rm rem}$ count the selected primitive quotient lines on $R<R_*$ and $\alpha<R/s<8$, removing exactly the marked incidences of Part 150, Theorem 4. Set it to zero on all other draws, including horizontal wins. Thus $F_{\rm rem}=\mathbb E_S\mathbb E_U[Z_{\rm rem}\mid S]$ is already a design average. The independent CBD(3) plant enters only in the surrounding reduction. Part 117 transfers a terminal failure probability after the restart estimate. ## 2. Exact averaging perpendicular to a fixed old line

Set
$$
 T=\min\{t,(ch/\alpha)^{d/(d+1)},R_*/\alpha\},\qquad
 u=\min\{R(T),R_*\},\qquad C=Tu .
$$
If $T<1$, the count vanishes. Otherwise every counted winner has $1\le s\le T$ and $sR(s)\le C$. Indeed $s\le t$, $\alpha s<R(s)<R_*$, and $sR(s)$ increases with $s$. Fix an old primitive sign pair $\pm x$, and put $B=\|x\|$, $P=P_{x^\perp\cap E}$, $L_x=PL$, $K_x=PK$, $H_x=K_x/L_x$. Write $p:H\to H_x$ for the induced surjection and $o=|\ker p|$. Then $\det L_x=D/B$, by extending $x$ to a basis. Also $o=[K\cap\mathbb Rx:\mathbb Zx]$, since any class in the kernel has an axial lift after subtracting an element of $L$. In particular $o\in\{1,q\}$.

**Proposition 1 (projected fiber identity).** Let $Z_x$ count the residual witness planes with horizontal kernel $\mathbb Z(x,0)$. Its conditional expectation is
$$
 \frac1{|H_x|}\sum_{1\le j<C/B}
 \sum_{\substack{z\in K_x\\ \|z\|^2<(C/B)^2-j^2}}
 \frac1o\sum_{a\in H:\, jp(a)=z+L_x} f(x,j,z,a).                 \tag{1}
$$
For a draw $a$ in the indicated fiber choose $w\in j\tilde a+L$ with $Pw=z$. Form $M_2=\mathbb Z(x,0)+\mathbb Z(w,j)$. The filter $f$ is one exactly when $M_2$ is saturated in $W(a)$, contains its selected nonhorizontal winner, has area below $sR(s)$, and passes the residual radius, length and marked-incidence tests of section 1. The equality $F_{\rm rem}=\mathbb E_S\sum_x\mathbb E[Z_x\mid S]$ sums over old sign pairs. One may take $f=0$ at $z=0$.

*Proof.* Any saturated witness plane through a nonhorizontal winner has height image $j\mathbb Z$ and primitive horizontal kernel generated by one sign pair. A positive height-$j$ generator is unique modulo that kernel. For fixed $a,x,j$, $Pw$ specifies it modulo $\mathbb Zx$ because two such lifts differ in $L\cap\mathbb Rx$. The plane area is $B\sqrt{j^2+\|z\|^2}$. Conversely the filtered pair generates that saturated plane and its primitive quotient line. If $v=m(x,0)+e(w,j)$, then $e=k/j>0$ and $Py=ez$. Thus $z=0$ gives either Part 150's removed axial incidence or $y=0$, which forces $s=1$ and is outside the residual. The area cap gives $j<C/B\le u\le R_*<315<q$. Multiplication by $j$ permutes $H_x$. Each summand has $o$ possible draws before testing $f$, each of mass $1/(o|H_x|)$. This proves (1), including the signed ties and all witnesses on each draw. [Plane–line correspondence and saturation](wall1_plane_count.md) · [Cyclic extension](wall1_lyapunov.md) · [Height convention and audit](wall1_vertical_multiplicity.md)

Dropping $f$ gives the exact average of the *unfiltered* projected disk count. For a disk $B_r$ at a fixed $j$, it is $\#(K_x\cap B_r)/|H_x|$. A rigorous volume comparison for this quantity uses the covering radius $\mu$ of $K_x$. Its upper bound is $V_{d-1}(r+\mu)^{d-1}/\det L_x$, and its lower bound replaces $r+\mu$ by $(r-\mu)_+$. Indeed the union of the Voronoi cells attached to the counted points lies in the enlarged disk and covers the contracted open disk up to null boundaries. For the filtered count the last factor in (1) is the fraction of the fiber accepted. This fraction depends on both the direction and the transverse point.

## 3. A bound in terms of the retained length spectrum

Let $\theta(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\pi z^2n^2}$, $J_d=e^{d/2}(2\pi/d)^{d/2}/V_d$, $\zeta=u\sqrt{2\pi/d}$, and $\beta=B/T$. Eligible old lines have $1\le\beta<u$. Put
$$
 W(S)=\sum_{x:\,1\le\beta<u}
          \left(\frac{\theta(\beta/\zeta)}{\zeta}\right)^{d-1},
 \qquad A_d=J_dc^d/2.                                               \tag{2}
$$
Here and below a sum over $x$ counts primitive sign pairs once. Use $W=0$ if $T<1$.

**Theorem 2.** On every history,
$$
 \mathbb E[Z_{\rm rem}\mid S]\le\min\{2^{219.843}, A_d W(S)\}.
$$
Any set of at most $m$ primitive old lines chosen from $S$ contributes less than $m\cdot9.359728\cdot10^{-10}$ for $m\ge1$. In particular the $d$ lines selected in any history-dependent choice of independent successive-minimum vectors contribute less than $4.726663\cdot10^{-7}$ in total.

*Proof.* For Gaussian scale $a>0$, drop $f$ and majorize the open ball in $(z,j)$ in (1). Poisson summation gives
$$
 \frac1{|H_x|}\sum_{z\in K_x}e^{-\pi\|z\|^2/a^2}
 =\frac{Ba^{d-1}}{D}\sum_{\xi\in K_x^*}e^{-\pi a^2\|\xi\|^2}
 \le\frac{Ba^{d-1}}{D}\theta(a)^{d-1}.
$$
Indeed $K_x^*=\mathbb Z^{911}\cap E\cap x^\perp$. Projection to a suitable $d-1$ coordinates embeds this group in $\mathbb Z^{d-1}$ and shortens norms. Extending the *Gaussian height sum* to all positive integers gives at most $a/2$ by integral comparison. Only heights $j<q$ were averaged over the mark. With $a=\zeta/\beta$ the resulting per-line bound is
$$
 \frac{B}{2D}e^{d/2}(\zeta/\beta)^d\theta(\zeta/\beta)^{d-1}
 \le A_d\left(\frac{\theta(\beta/\zeta)}{\zeta}\right)^{d-1}.
$$
Here $Tu^d\le c^dD/V_d$ and $\theta(a)=\theta(1/a)/a$. The weight decreases with $\beta$, so its maximum on this range is $\theta(\zeta)^{d-1}$. The audited floor $h>31.9321$ and $T<363$ give $\zeta\ge0.954(31.9321)363^{-1/d}\sqrt{2\pi/d}>3.35854$ (the other radius cap $R_*$ also exceeds this floor in the corresponding units). Section 6 evaluates the result. Sum over lines and apply the uniform cap from Part 150. This also proves the assertion for any preselected set without an independence assumption. ## 4. Two design averages with explicit costs

**Proposition 3 (an exit for excess old short-vector counts).** Let $a_0=q^{400/911}$, $a=5a_0/4$, and define
$$
 X=\frac{D}{a^d}\sum_{x\in L} e^{-\pi\|x\|^2/a^2}.
$$
Then $\Pr(X>2)<2^{-293.276}$ and $\mathbb E[Z_{\rm rem}\mathbf1_{X>2}]<2^{-73.433}$. On $X\le2$ the primitive sign-pair counts at **all** radii obey
$$
 1+2N_L(r)\le (2a^d/D)e^{\pi r^2/a^2},
 \qquad N_L(r)=\#\{x\in L_{\rm prim}/\{\pm1\}:\|x\|<r\}.
$$

*Proof.* For a lattice $A$ write $T_A(a)=\sum_{\xi\in A}e^{-\pi a^2\|\xi\|^2}$. Poisson gives $X=T_{L^*}(a)$. On every history $L^*=M^*\cap E$, so $0\le X-1\le T_{M^*}(a)-1$. For the marginal raw graph with $m=400$ rows and $n-m$ independent columns, sharing one old horizontal dual numerator $p\not\equiv0\pmod q$ gives independent uniform prescribed residues for the $n-m$ vertical numerators. Thus the expected zero-horizontal-residue theta contribution is $\theta(a)^n$, and the rest is at most
$$
 q^{-(n-m)}\theta(a/q)^n=(q^{m/n}/a)^n\theta(q/a)^n.             \tag{3}
$$
This is a first moment over the original columns and applies despite adaptive selection of $E$. With $n=911$, Markov for $X-1$ bounds the exit by $\theta(a)^{911}-1+(4/5)^{911}\theta(q/a)^{911}$. Use the history-uniform Part 150 bound to charge *multiplicity* on the exit. Finally the old zero vector and each sign pair of length below $r$ contribute at least $(1+2N_L(r))e^{-\pi r^2/a^2}$ to the primal Gaussian. This proves all three assertions. The scale is coarse. At the certified lower floor $D=(31.9321/G_d)^d$ the reference factor $a^d/D$ alone would exceed $2^{1472}$. The floor evaluation makes no reachability assertion. Part 147's much finer event $t\le1$ contributes zero here because $s\le t$ and every residual draw has $R>30$. [Unit threshold and its last-used-column proof](wall1_uncovered_lines.md) · [Old graph first moment](wall1_long_lines.md)

**Proposition 4 (upper radius strip).** For the marginal restart,
$$
 \mathbb E[Z_{\rm rem}\mathbf1_{R\ge 7R_*/8}]<7.191421\cdot10^{-6}.
$$

*Proof.* Use the output lattice $Q$ of rank $d$ and determinant $D/s$, and $a_N=q^{400/912}$. Gaussian majorization of both signs of every primitive quotient vector gives $Z_{\rm rem}\le e^{\pi R^2/a_N^2} a_N^d T_{Q^*}(a_N)/(2\det Q)$ on residual draws. Put $v=R/R_*$. Since $V_d R^d=c^d\det Q$, the prefactor equals $A_d\exp[d((v^2-1)/2-\log v)]$. On $7/8\le v<1$ it is largest at $7/8$. Bound $T_{Q^*}$ by the theta series of the full unselected raw dual and average with (3) at $n=912$. The resulting majorant is
$$
 A_d e^{d(((7/8)^2-1)/2-\log(7/8))}
     [\theta(a_N)^{912}+\theta(q/a_N)^{912}].
$$
The raw matrix includes the terminal column. No conditioning on the strip enters (3). [Part 148, Theorem 2](wall1_vertical_multiplicity.md)

## 5. Where the projected theta estimate loses the budget

**Proposition 5 (outer old-line shell, plane height one).** Consider a history with $T\ge0.9h$ and $R(T)<7R_*/8$. In particular $u=R(T)$. Index the projected disk majorants in (1) by $x$ and $j$, drop $f$, include the origin of the transverse lattice, and use the integral-dual bound $T_{K_x^*}(a)\le\theta(a)^{d-1}$. **The resulting upper estimate already exceeds $2^{617.94}$** on the single subfamily
$$
 j=1,\qquad C/8\le B<C.
$$
This holds for arbitrary positive Gaussian scales, even if chosen separately for every line. It holds whenever the stated inequalities on $T,h,R(T)$ hold, including on the good set of Proposition 3. The hypotheses specify a part of history space. Its design probability has not been evaluated here.

*Proof.* For $j=1$ the per-line majorant under discussion is
$$
 \frac BD\exp\!\left(\frac{\pi((C/B)^2-1)}{a^2}\right)
       [a\theta(a)]^{d-1}\ \ge\ \frac BD,                         \tag{4}
$$
since $B<C$ and $a\theta(a)=\theta(1/a)\ge1$. To count how many times this floor is paid, some translate of the open radius-$C/2$ ball contains at least $(C/(2h))^d$ old points, by integrating its translates over a lattice cell. Subtract one of the points to get distinct old vectors of norm strictly below $C$. A primitive old line accounts for at most $2C/t$ nonzero such vectors. Meanwhile the disjoint open balls of radius $t/2$ around the old points of norm below $C/8$ fit in the ball of radius $C/8+t/2$. Thus the number of primitive sign pairs in the shell is at least
$$
 \frac{(0.45u)^d-1}{2u}-\frac{(1+u/4)^d}{2}
 \ \ge\ \frac{(0.45u)^d}{4u}.
$$
The first inequality used $C=Tu$, $T\ge0.9h$ and $t\ge T$. For the second, $30<u<315$ and $(0.45\cdot30)^{-d}+315[(1/30+1/4)/0.45]^d<1/2$. Multiplying by the minimum $C/(8D)$ from (4) gives $(0.45cG_d)^d/32$, using $Tu^d=c^d h^d$. Section 6 encloses its logarithm.

Here $j$ is the positive generator of the **plane's height image**. The winner can have height $k=ej$. Throughout this shell $C/B\le8$, so only $j\le7$ could appear even before selection. There is also a check on what the obstruction means geometrically. If a primitive quotient generator had a horizontal rounded lift, that lift would be $\pm(x,0)$, with $B^2<R(s)^2+s^2/4$. But $R(s)/u=(T/s)^{1/d}\le363^{1/d}$ and $T/u\le1/\alpha$, giving $B<1.17u$. The shell requires $B\ge Tu/8>3.59u$ by the old GH floor. Hence each *actual* shell witness admits a nonhorizontal rounded lift. Part 150's rounded-lift estimate already bounds that actual contribution by $2^{71.640}$. Equation (4) locates the much larger overcount from discarding the accepted-fiber fraction and bounding the entire transverse dual by the coordinate lattice. It gives no lower bound on the actual multiplicity. [Rounded-lift test and proof](wall1_shortened_lines.md)

## Remaining estimate

Choose $d$ old primitive lines from the history (for example the first $d$ by length with deterministic ties). Let $F_{\rm bulk}$ be the exact count (1) averaged after excluding those lines, retaining $X\le2$ and $R<7R_*/8$, with all Part 150 residual tests still in force. Propositions 3–4 and Theorem 2 give
$$
 F_{\rm bulk}\le F_{\rm rem}
 < F_{\rm bulk}+7.191421\cdot10^{-6}
                +505(9.359728\cdot10^{-10})+2^{-73.433}.
$$
Consequently $h_{\rm long}+F_{\rm bulk}\le0.0097661359$ would suffice for Part 150. The exit bound controls all exceptional multiplicity at its stated scale. A budget-level bound on the remaining accepted fibers still needs the joint law of old lines, their transverse lattices, and the selector under the small-radius restriction. Proposition 5 identifies a shell that defeats the unfiltered coordinate-theta majorant even with optimally chosen Gaussian scales. Which shell carries the largest *actual* design-averaged incidence is open.

## Audit

**Verdict — SOUND.** The finite-mark identity, all three moment bounds, and the conditional obstruction in Proposition 5 hold as stated. The final conjecture label needs one attribution clarification. Part 150 proposes $h_{\rm long}+F_{\rm rem}\le0.0097738000$ as a stronger sufficient condition. Its explicitly named conjecture is the Part 147 combined allowance $h_{\rm long}+F_{\rm nh}\le0.0097738441$. Both remain open.

**Finite mark and signed selection.** Fix the complete history and ambient frame as in Parts 108 and 142. The surjection $p$ has cyclic axial kernel of order $o\mid q$. For every height in (1), $1\le j<C/B<q$, so each projected class has exactly $o$ preimages under $jp$. The two normalization factors give each draw its joint mass $1/|H|$. At fixed $x,j,z,a$, any two height-$j$ lifts differ by an element of $L\cap\mathbb Rx=\mathbb Zx$. Thus the saturation and signed-winner tests apply to a unique candidate plane. Conversely its horizontal kernel, height image and projected generator recover $x,j,z$ uniquely. Part 120's bijection then counts every primitive quotient line once, including multiple witnesses on a single draw. At $z=0$ the winner is axially aligned or has $k=s=1$. Part 150's marked exclusion and length gate cover the respective cases.

**Moment bounds.** Equal fibers and $\det L_x=D/B$ give the factor $Ba^{d-1}/D$ after Poisson summation. Only the positive Gaussian height sum is extended beyond the admissible heights. Using $Tu^d\le c^dh^d$ and $B/T\ge1$ bounds one preselected line by $A_d\theta(\zeta)^{d-1}$. For the theta exit, $X-1$ is dominated pointwise by the old raw dual theta sum minus one. The 911-dimensional estimate bounds the probability before application of Part 150's per-history multiplicity cap on $R<R_*$. For the strip, the Gaussian prefactor decreases on $7/8\le R/R_*<1$, and the full 912-dimensional raw dual dominates the selected output dual on each draw. Both raw averages use the independent columns of the marginal restart. Neither needs an independence assumption on the selector.

**Scope of the obstruction.** On the specified stratum, including $z=0$ and using the coordinate-lattice theta majorant costs at least $B/D$ for each old shell line at $j=1$, for every positive scale. Translated-ball averaging counts vectors of length below $C$. Dividing their nonzero count by $2C/t$ and subtracting the inner packing bound leaves at least $(0.45u)^d/(4u)$ primitive sign pairs with $C/8\le B<C$. Thus the relaxed estimate alone exceeds $2^{617.94}$. This rules out a uniform budget-sized bound from that estimate on the stratum. Its design probability and its accepted multiplicity are separate quantities. Indeed a horizontal rounded lift would require $B/u<1.165$, while the shell has $B/u>3.592$, so Part 150 already bounds the actual conditional shell contribution by $2^{71.640}$. Here $j=1$ specifies the plane's height image and allows a winner of height $k>1$.

**Exact reproduction.** Scratch enumeration compared the projected fibers and the direct divisor test against a third enumeration of rounded lifts. The third selected the shortest vector using signed ambient coordinates, used primitive integer minors in a window basis to test saturation, and recovered the plane height and old line from its normal. Rounding gives the exhaustive lift cutoff $\|w\|^2<R^2+s^2/4$. The plane sets agreed on each of the 356 one-draw cases, with all four printed height histograms. For all 81 two-draw continuations the sets again agreed using the inherited ambient coordinates after the first projection. Per first history, the before/after totals were $(4,0),(11,7),(10,4)$ with multiplicities $1,4,4$, giving the design means $88/81$ and $44/81$.

**Numerical reproduction.** Independent 90-decimal-digit outward interval arithmetic passed all seven strict endpoint checks in section 6. Iterating the old determinant recursion 406 times gives $h>31.93213$. The per-line majorant lies in $(9.3597271102,9.3597271103)\cdot10^{-10}$ and the strip majorant in $(7.1914205062,7.1914205063)\cdot10^{-6}$. The base-two log majorants for the exit probability and its multiplicity lie in $(-293.276495,-293.276494)$ and $(-73.433495,-73.433494)$, respectively. The base-two log of the relaxed shell floor lies in $(617.9475961,617.9475962)$. Adding the three outward-rounded charges to $0.0097661359$ gives less than $0.009773799988$. Part 117's coupling cost applies once to the resulting terminal failure probability if the remaining sufficient inequality is proved.
