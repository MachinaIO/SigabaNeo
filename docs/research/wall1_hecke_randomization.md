# What Hecke equidistribution gives for a retained lattice

**Finding.** Even after any fixed finite sequence of prime-index Hecke steps, the supremum over determinant-one starting lattices of the probability of a small terminal plane is exactly **one**, for every positive area cutoff. The same holds for small spacing modulo a designated shortest line. This obstruction applies to sublattices, superlattices, and mixtures of the two. It is a statement about uniformity in the starting point. It leaves open estimates averaged over the specific gated histories in Part 210.

## A uniform pointwise obstruction with exact constants

Fix \(n\ge3\), primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_k\), and a lattice \(L\) of covolume one. For a physical index-\(p\) sublattice \(S\) the shape-space neighbor is \(p^{-1/n}S\). Since \(pL\subset S\), every such neighbor contains \(p^{1-1/n}L\). For a physical index-\(p\) superlattice \(U\) the neighbor is \(p^{1/n}U\), which contains \(p^{1/n}L\). These inclusions hold for every choice at each step.

For a fixed sequence of directions let \(c\) be the product of those scalar factors. Every endpoint contains \(cL\). For arbitrary directions one can use \(C=\prod_i p_i^{1-1/n}\) as an upper bound on the scalar for every path. Take

\[
 L_\delta=\operatorname{diag}(\delta,\delta,
       \delta^{-2/(n-2)},\ldots,\delta^{-2/(n-2)})\,\mathbb Z^n .
\]

Every endpoint has two independent vectors of lengths at most \(C\delta\) spanning a square of area at most \((C\delta)^2\). Saturating their span in the endpoint lattice can only decrease its covolume. For **every** choice of shortest line at least one of the two vectors projects to a nonzero vector of length at most \(C\delta\). Thus for any prescribed spacing \(r>0\) and area \(a>0\), choosing \(0<\delta<\min(r,\sqrt a)/C\) forces both events at every endpoint. This also works at any prescribed initial covolume by overall scaling. The examples need not belong to the reachable gated set. What they exclude is any deduction based solely on rank, covolume, and the finite sequence of indices.

## The explicit spectral factor and the missing pointwise factor

Let \(T_p\) average the normalized index-\(p\) sublattices on \(X_n=\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb Z)\backslash\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb R)\) with Haar probability \(\mu\). For \(n\ge3\), at full level, the Clozel–Oh–Ullmo bound on the mean-zero \(L^2\) operator norm gives

\[
 \|T_p\|_{L^2_0\to L^2_0}\le \rho_p=\frac{2\sqrt p}{p+1}.
\]

Indeed, in their split full-level estimate and its central extension to \(\mathrm{GL}_n\), the Smith type \((p,1,\ldots,1)\) has one nontrivial paired root ratio. Their displayed rank-one spherical function at valuation one is \(p^{-1/2}(1+(p-1)/(p+1))\). The superlattice operator is the adjoint and has the same norm. Thus the explicit upper bound for \(k\) repeated steps is \(\rho_p^k\), with \(\rho_2=2\sqrt2/3\approx0.94280904\). The size of the correspondence does not supply an inverse-square-root-of-degree error estimate. [Clozel–Oh–Ullmo, Theorem 1.1 and remark, §§4.7, 5.1, and Corollary 1.9](https://ullmo.ihes.fr/publications/COU-Invent.pdf)

Here is an event-specific conversion with its constant visible. Let \(T\) be the product of the chosen operators and \(\rho=\prod_i\rho_{p_i}\). Choose a neighborhood \(V\) of the identity in the real group, a starting point \(x\), and a measurable enlargement \(F\) containing \(EV=\{yg:y\in E,g\in V\}\). Write \(v=\mu(xV)>0\) and \(m=\mu(F)\). Then

\[
 T1_E(x)\ \le\ m+\rho\sqrt{(v^{-1}-1)m(1-m)} .
\]

Indeed Hecke correspondences commute with right translation. For each \(z=xg\in xV\), transporting the endpoints with multiplicity gives \(T1_E(x)\le T1_F(z)\). Average this inequality over \(xV\) and center both indicators. The centered averaging density \(1_{xV}/v-1\) has \(L^2\) norm \(\sqrt{v^{-1}-1}\). Cauchy–Schwarz gives the claimed constant. This proof needs only measurable sets. It avoids assigning a Sobolev norm to an indicator. For the area observable an enlargement from \(a\) to \(a e^{2t}\) suffices if every \(g\in V\) has operator norm at most \(e^t\), by the exterior-square norm bound. For the spacing observable a change of shortest line can change the witness, so enlarging 26 merely by a length stretch factor needs a separate justification.

The two numerical inputs for this estimate are the mass of the **enlarged** event and the mass \(v\) of the actual neighborhood in the quotient. Haar mass on the real group counts overlaps if its image wraps around \(x\). For a fixed point and sufficiently small coordinate radius \(t\), an ordinary group-coordinate ball has \(v\sim c_x t^{255024}\) at \(n=505\), since \(\dim\mathrm{SL}_{505}=505^2-1\). The corresponding local power in \(v^{-1/2}\) is \(127512\). These are exponents, not numerical bounds on \(v\) at a chosen radius. They also are not intrinsic lower bounds on the cost of other proofs. The cusp construction above explains why a uniform numerical discrepancy over all \(x\) cannot follow even from an explicit spectral factor. COU Corollary 1.9(2) gives a fixed-point smooth-test asymptotic \(O(p^{-1/2+\epsilon})\). It supplies no numerical error for these gated indicators. [Clozel–Oh–Ullmo, Theorems 1.6–1.7, Corollary 1.9](https://ullmo.ihes.fr/publications/COU-Invent.pdf)

## What the three transitions preserve

* **Sublattice.** The neighbors are hyperplanes of \(L/pL\), so their number is \(d=(p^n-1)/(p-1)\). For a fixed vector \(w\) with nonzero reduction, exactly \((p^{n-1}-1)/(p-1)\) contain it. Its physical membership probability is \((p^{n-1}-1)/(p^n-1)<1/p\). If \(w\in pL\), membership is certain. Conditioning on membership selects a proper subset of the correspondence in the former case. The unconditional spectral estimate gives no identical contraction bound for this conditional kernel.
* **Superlattice.** Every physical neighbor contains every old vector, and its covolume is divided by \(p\). New vectors can be shorter than all old vectors. For example, adjoining \(e_1/p\) to \(\mathbb Z^n\) reduces its minimum from 1 to \(1/p\). A newly selected vector need not have a lift in the original lattice. Normalizing the neighbor to covolume one rescales all its physical lengths by the same factor \(p^{1/n}\) when the initial covolume is one.
* **Unimodular change of basis.** The physical point set is identical. Any statistic of the unmarked lattice, including the least saturated-plane area, is exactly preserved. A basis-dependent tie rule can change the designated shortest line. An ambient rotation also preserves lengths and areas. A general real determinant-one deformation can change lengths and is a different operation.

For a fixed starting point any finite sequence of prime-index steps still has finite support, hence total variation distance exactly one from Haar. Indicator bounds for particular geometric sets are compatible with that fact. The exact entropy of one uniform choice is \(\log_2 d\) bits, equal to \(\log_2(2^{505}-1)\) for \(n=505,p=2\). This measures the randomness of a neighbor choice only. Neither entropy nor spectral contraction measures the cost of a successful reduction.

**Completeness.** The counting identities, cusp obstruction, and \(L^2\)-to-indicator inequality above are paper arguments. They establish why a direct appeal to Hecke equidistribution cannot certify either stated residual. No effective bound on the gated starting-neighborhood volumes or the joint marked law is established here. The two residual ceilings and a success-cost bound for a modified reducer remain open.

## Audit

**Verdict.** All four claims hold with the stated full-level Hecke law and determinant normalization. Many unconditional Hecke steps **do** give a near-Haar area probability uniformly over starting points in any fixed compact set. The cusp example and the compact-set estimate use different orders of quantifiers.

1. **Cusp and gates.** Fix the finite prime list and both cutoffs first. A single choice of \(\delta<\min(1,r/C,\sqrt a/C)\) then works for every path, every choice of directions and every shortest line at its endpoint. The endpoint contains the two vectors \(c\delta e_1,c\delta e_2\) with the scalar \(c\) of that path. Saturation divides their area by an integer, and at least one vector projects nontrivially off any line. The sufficient depth depends on the prime list through \(C\), which grows even for repetitions of one prime. For the physical lattice \(sL_\delta\) at fixed covolume \(s^n\), the primal and dual minima are \(s\delta\) and \(s^{-1}\delta^{2/(n-2)}\). Both tend to zero. Thus a sufficiently deep witness violates the positive dual floor inherited by the retained lattice. Raw-good is a condition on the unprojected graph. The diagonal construction specifies no raw lift or history from which to check it. It supplies no doubly gated counterexample. [Part 205, raw and inherited dual gates](wall1_doubly_gated.md)

2. **Spectral factor.** The remark after COU Theorem 1.1 gives constant one at split full level. Section 4.7 justifies using the rational \(\mathrm{GL}_n\) correspondence modulo real scalars on \(X_n\). Of the 252 paired ratios in §5.1 at \(n=505\), precisely one has valuation one for Smith type \((p,1,\ldots,1)\). The other 251 contribute one. Substituting in the displayed spherical function preceding Theorem 1.1 gives \(2\sqrt p/(p+1)\) as an **upper bound**. Submultiplicativity gives the product bound for the prescribed steps, including adjoints. [Clozel–Oh–Ullmo, Theorem 1.1 and §§4.6–5.1](https://ullmo.ihes.fr/publications/COU-Invent.pdf)

3. **Pointwise conversion and compact starts.** For \(F\supset EV_t\), right equivariance gives the stated one-sided inequality after averaging over \(xV_t\). Its two centered norms are exactly \(\sqrt{1/v-1}\) and \(\sqrt{m(1-m)}\). The quotient map is locally injective since the stabilizer is discrete. A full group-coordinate ball therefore has volume of order \(t^{505^2-1}\) at small radius, giving the stated inverse-square-root exponent \(127512\). On a fixed compact set \(K\), one can choose an injective radius uniformly and hence \(v_K(t)=\inf_{x\in K}\mu(xV_t)>0\). At fixed covolume a fixed positive dual floor puts the normalized shapes in a compact set by Mahler's criterion applied to the duals.

   For any event with Haar-null boundary, take shrinking symmetric neighborhoods \(V_t\) and apply the inequality to the event and its complement. Their enlargements approach their respective Haar masses. Choose \(t\) for the desired enlargement error first, then choose the step count so that \(\rho_2^k/\sqrt{v_K(t)}\) is small, using \(\rho_p\le\rho_2<1\). This proves uniform convergence of the event probability on \(K\). The positive area cutoff has Haar-null boundary: locally only finitely many primitive exterior vectors can meet a bounded area threshold, and each equality of exterior norm to the cutoff is a nonconstant analytic level set in a lattice chart. The same reasoning applies to spacing away from shortest-line ties, using rounded lifts of bounded length. Ties between independent candidate shortest vectors and equalities at the positive spacing cutoff are also Haar-null analytic loci. This argument gives no numerical step count for the stated allowances. It concerns unrestricted walks **starting** in \(K\). Conditioning subsequent steps to pass gates changes the kernel.

4. **Spacing versus area.** If the shortest vector has length \(s\) and the projected witness has length \(h\le26\), its rounded lift has squared length at most \(h^2+s^2/4\) and at least \(s^2\). Hence \(s\le2h/\sqrt3\) and the saturated witness plane has area at most \(sh\le1352/\sqrt3\). At the audited physical floor, substituting the upper spacing endpoint and lower area endpoint gives \(100(1-9.97119357603664\cdot10^{-63}/(2.0005903166\cdot10^{-58}))>99.99501587\). This bounds the **percentage of Haar area hits** missed by spacing at that floor. Transferring a bound to the original marked histories still requires their joint law.
