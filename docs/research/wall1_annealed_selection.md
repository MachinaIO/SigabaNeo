## When a comparison is available

Let $M_i$ be its section supported on the first $105+i$ graph columns, $0\le i\le407$, including all horizontal coordinates. Write $E_i$ for the span frozen through draw $i$, $E_0=0$, and $p_i$ for orthogonal projection off $E_i$. At draw $i$ the window is $p_{i-1}M_i$. Fix a nonzero terminal vector $w$, a lift $z$, and put $K=M_{407}\cap E_{407}$. Its first possible comparison is exactly the least $i\ge1$ such that

$$
                 (z+K)\cap M_i\ne\varnothing .
$$

This is an integer coset test on the unread coordinates. Membership persists through the final draw. At every such draw the selected length $s_i$ is at most the minimum of $\|p_{i-1}x\|$ over this intersection. All candidates here are nonzero after projection since their final image is nonzero. Ties only strengthen the necessary condition if resolved explicitly.

Backward rounding in the full remaining frozen basis gives squared norm at most $\|w\|^2+\sum_{j=i}^{407}s_j^2/4$ in the projected space. The rounded lift need not belong to $M_i$: subtracting a later winner can introduce unread coordinates. Even availability before rounding does not justify using this bound in window $i$. At the last draw rounding always works. If the nearest parallel coefficient of a lift modulo the winner is $u\in[-1/2,1/2]$, then

$$
               \|w\|^2\ge (1-u^2)s_{407}^2\ge 3s_{407}^2/4 .

The triangular lattice attains the last constant. This is the plane through the winner test of [Part 120](wall1_plane_count.md).

## A run for which the lifted flag really does coexist

Write $a=\sqrt3/2$. Consider the final run of winners of physical length strictly below one, of length $k$. Immediately before the run the retained lattice $L$ has minimum at least $a$, by the preceding winner comparison, or at least one by integrality at restart. If $k=0$ the terminal minimum is already at least $a$.

Each winner in the run belongs to the projected old $L$. Indeed appended heights are integral in mutually orthogonal new coordinate axes. Inductively every projection during the run removes a vector in the old real span and leaves these coordinates untouched. A vector of norm below one has all these coordinates zero. The triangular unit heights of the appends then force all the new generator coefficients to vanish, working from latest to earliest. The same argument applies to any terminal witness of norm below one. Thus it and every winner can be lifted *inside* $L$. This also explains why a cutoff of one here refers to physical units.

Choose lifts of the winners and a shortest terminal witness in $L$, in that order. Their Gram–Schmidt lengths are $(t_1,\dots,t_l)=(s_1',\dots,s_k',\|w\|)$, with primes indexing the run and $l=k+1\le408$. At index $i<l$ the projected sublattice generated by the remaining lifts has minimum exactly $t_i$: it contains the winner and lies in its window because all lifts come from the old lattice. Its determinant is the product of the remaining lengths. Blichfeldt's Hermite bound $J_h=[(2/\pi)\Gamma(2+h/2)^{2/h}]^{1/2}$ therefore gives, for $h=l-i+1$,

$$
 t_i\le J_h^{h/(h-1)}(t_{i+1}\cdots t_l)^{1/(h-1)} .
$$

The geometric mean of a tail of length $h$ is at most $J_h^{1/(h-1)}$ times the mean of the next tail. Hence

$$
 a\le t_1\le t_l B_l,\qquad
 B_l=J_l^{l/(l-1)}\prod_{h=2}^{l-1}J_h^{1/(h-1)} .
$$

The script checks all lengths. For own GH write $G_d=V_d^{-1/d}$, $d=505$. The full restart determinant is $q^{400}$. Its section in the frozen span has determinant at least one by integral minors, and is primitive as a section. Thus the terminal determinant is at most $q^{400}$. The bounds above give the stated ratio even using this upper endpoint. The cases of terminal minimum at least one or an empty run automatically pass.

## The finite average and the remaining loss

For each prescribed rational span $E$, partition its Part 118 fiber by intersection $T$, quotient image $S$, and lift map $f:S\to U/T$, where $U$ is the modular kernel of projection. Given $T,S$, the maps have equal mass $q^{-s(407-\dim T)}$, $s=\dim S$, after choosing a splitting. For each map reconstruct the code, check the graph chart, and test its comparisons. The exact probability is the sum of the resulting counts times $\Pr(T,S)q^{-s(407-\dim T)} b_E(S)$, divided by the chart probability, summed over $E,T,S$. Here $b_E$ tests the actual image lattice including its zero-residue vectors. Count a map once for its winning span regardless of the number of bad vectors. In the small-radius calculation the preceding flag inequalities exclude **every** counted map with bad image, so no independence or asymptotic flag-density estimate enters this evaluation. ## Interval reproduction

```python
from mpmath import iv
iv.dps = 50
I = iv.mpf
log, exp = iv.ln, iv.exp
q, d, b = 3329, 505, 506
a = iv.sqrt(3)/2
def G(n):
    return exp(iv.loggamma(1+I(n)/2)/n)/iv.sqrt(iv.pi)
def logJ(n):
    return (log(2/iv.pi)+2*iv.loggamma(2+I(n)/2)/n)/2

tail = I(0)
for l in range(2, 409):
    j = logJ(l)/(l-1)
    floor = a*exp(-l*j-tail)
    assert floor > I('.00093928')
    tail += j
gh_max = G(d)*exp(I(400)*log(q)/d)
assert gh_max < I('3376.985')
assert I('.00093928')/gh_max > I('.00000027814')
c = I('.954')
relative = c*G(d)/G(b)**(I(b)/d)
cut = (relative/a)**(I(d)/b)
assert I('1.096606') < cut < I('1.096607')
assert I('.94986') < relative < I('.94987')
assert iv.sqrt(1-relative**2) < I('.31267')
assert 22304 + 977696 == 1000000  # exact budget in units of 1e-8
```
