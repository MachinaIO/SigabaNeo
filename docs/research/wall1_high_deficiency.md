# High deficiency on a selected, doubly gated flag

**Counterexample to deterministic exclusion.** In the original width-506 marginal restart there are designs passing the radius-250 raw gate and every raw dual floor through draw 407 with the following simultaneous properties.

| Object on the actual signed-shortest flag | Bound |
|---|---:|
| A saturated plane after draw 406 | area at most 65, full-lift deficiency at least 133 |
| The winner at draw 407 | old, length at most √65, frozen-section deficiency at least 134 |
| An inherited line after removing that winner | nonzero point of length at most √65, full-lift deficiency at least 133 |
| Contribution to the conditional score from the two signs of that point at plant height zero | greater than 1.98963 |

Every raw lift of the terminal point has norm greater than 250. Its last graph coordinate is zero. Thus an actual summand in the outstanding high-deficiency score class survives both raw gates and *all* shortest-vector comparisons. The original design probability certified below exceeds \(10^{-20050}\). This is an existence result. The particular column restrictions used in the construction themselves cost less than \(10^{-20043}\) in probability.

There is also a frequency statement about deficiency alone. Outside probability \(10^{-1069}\), **every frozen prefix through draw 407 has deficiency at least 133**, without imposing a hit or a length cutoff. Both statements use the independent uniform plain graph ensemble of Parts 223–239. [Restart and score](wall1_score_rejection.md) · [Section counts](wall1_high_codim_sections.md)

## What deficiency records

Let \(F_i\) denote the frozen rational span after \(i\) draws, let \(S_F=F\cap\mathbb Z^N\), and put \(d(F)=400-\dim\sigma(S_F)\), where \(\sigma(x,y)=x-Ay\bmod q\) and \(q=3329\). Embed older spaces by padding zeros. If \(F\subseteq T\), a basis of \(S_F\) extends to a basis of \(S_T\) by saturation. Hence
\[
 d(F)-(\dim T-\dim F)\le d(T)\le d(F).
\]
Deleting input columns and restricting the *same* syndrome to that coordinate section preserves this inequality. Deleting syndrome rows changes the map and its fixed-space probability. In particular, a subsection of a deficiency-133 lift cannot be a deficiency-132 witness for the same test. This is the monotonicity in Part 226.

The kernel of the next-column mark map on \(\mathbb F_q^{400}\) is exactly \(\sigma(S_F)\). Indeed \(P_F(a,0)\in P_F L\), with \(P_F\) projection off \(F\), precisely if \((a,0)\) differs from a raw graph vector by an integer point of \(F\). Thus a specified next mark has exactly \(q^{400-d(F)}\) raw representatives and conditional probability \(q^{-d(F)}\). For a fixed initial matrix and a full transcript of actual marked displacements, the probability is the product of these conditional probabilities. The physical flag and the indices in that product are determined by the transcript: \(\det(P_F L)=q^{d(F)}/\det S_F\). For a rational section fixed before the design, the different membership bound in Part 223 is \(2q^{-d(k-400+d)}\) at rank \(k\). Counting one mark once already credits the raw representatives that the deterministic projection forgets. A sum over *different* marked transcripts still needs a bound. [Marked transition](wall1_first_tour.md) · [Section determinant](wall1_wholly_old_mass.md)

For the frequency statement, write \(D_i\) for the retained determinant and \(L_i\) for the Part 239 lower bound on \(\log D_i\). Primitivity of each shortest winner gives \(\det(L\cap F_i)=q^{400}/D_i\). At exact deficiency \(d\) the integer height of \(F_i\) is therefore at most \(q^d e^{-L_i}\). Apply the Part 226 chart count to the frozen space *itself*, before any conditioning, using \((N,k,r)=(912,407,142)\) and this cutoff. The base-ten log of the cumulative majorant through 132 lies in \((-1069.02,-1069.01)\). Monotonicity along the actual flag covers all earlier cuts with the same bound. At cut 406 the corresponding interval is \((-2124.49,-2124.48)\) with \(r=141\). This frequency statement concerns frozen spaces. A terminal competitor adds another direction, and a plane adds two.

## The auxiliary distribution

Choose four disjoint groups of seven sample rows, denoting their union of 28 rows by \(B\). Give each group one of the initial inputs. In the order consisting of that input height followed by its seven rows, the residue code is the line generated by
\[
 (1,g,g^2,\ldots,g^7)\pmod q,\qquad g=630,\quad g^8=-1\pmod q .
\]
Each of the four prescribed columns vanishes outside its own seven rows. Choose an ordered pair of distinct visible rows \(e,e'\) uniformly from the other 372 coordinate rows, independently of the graph. All other initial columns and all appended columns through draw 395 are independent uniform full columns.

* On draws 396–400 choose independent columns uniform on \(B\), zero elsewhere.
* On draws 401–403 use visible components \(8e,64e,512e\), in that order. On draws 404–406 do the same with \(e'\). Give each of the six columns independent uniform entries on \(B\).
* Draw 407 is an independent uniform full column.

For fixed visible rows this is exactly the original law conditioned on \(4\cdot400+11\cdot372=5692\) specified entries. The auxiliary probabilities below refer to this sampling procedure including the independent row choice. All gates are imposed by a final union bound.

### Freezing the 32 initial directions

In each rank-eight block the minimum squared length is 1827621. This follows by centering the eight residues for each of the 3328 nonzero input residues and comparing with the period \(q\). One minimizing height is 58. The eight signed cyclic shifts of its centered vector belong to the block since multiplication by \(g\) or \(g^{-1}\) preserves the residue line. They span over \(\mathbb Q\): the shift acts as multiplication by \(X\) in \(\mathbb Q[X]/(X^8+1)\), a field since \(X^8+1=\Phi_{16}(X)\). Hence until the four blocks are exhausted there is a nonzero projected candidate of length at most \(b=\sqrt{1827621}<q\).

For integer ball counts write \(M_n(r)=V_n(r^2+n/4)^{n/2}\). The number of signed integer points in the ball is at most \(2M_n(r)\). Indeed, at least half of each centered unit cube satisfies \(\langle x,u\rangle\le0\) for center \(x\) and offset \(u\), hence is inside the enlarged ball. For a sign-symmetric existence event one representative per pair cancels this factor two. We use \(M_n(r)\) for such unsigned first-moment estimates, and explicitly allow a factor two where counting both signs.

Zero-pad the 133 ordinary input heights available through draw 32. On those heights and the 372 visible rows, any fixed candidate with a nonzero ordinary tuple of total norm at most \(b<q\) costs \(q^{-372}\). The signed-pair bound is \(M_{505}(b)/q^{372}<10^{-102}\). Dropping the 32 block coordinates only decreases the norm along a prefix of block selections. If the ordinary tuple vanishes, a nonzero outside horizontal period costs at least \(q\). Induction now forces the first 32 shortest selections to exhaust the four blocks. The conclusion allows all ties between block vectors.

### Two tests at cut 395

In a comparison process force deletion of the 32 block coordinates, then run the same signed selector. After deletion the remaining graph has 372 rows and 133 ordinary inputs. Its visible entries, including the later ordinary appends through 395, have the independent uniform law *before testing* whether forced and actual deletions agree. The deleted coordinate subspace supplies all 28 syndromes on \(B\). Thus on agreement the frozen deficiency in the full graph equals that in the smaller graph: the integer frozen section is the direct sum of the deleted coordinate grid and the frozen section on the retained coordinates, and reducing the syndrome modulo the 28 deleted rows gives the smaller graph map.

1. The probability that the comparison frozen space at cut 395 has deficiency at most 140 is below \(10^{-2188}\). Use the same height test as above with 372 syndrome rows, \(k=395-32=363\), \(N=505+k=868\), chart split 125 and the determinant recursion initialized at \(372\log q\). In the fixed-space factor the exponent becomes \(-d(k-372+d)\): for \(b_0\) pure horizontal directions there are at most \(2q^{d(372-b_0-d)}\) candidate annihilators, each imposing \(d(k-b_0)\) independent conditions. The chart counts are unchanged. Every nonempty height envelope through 140 grows by at least \(q^{100}\) per step as in Part 226.
2. Except with probability \(<10^{-29}\), the retained lattice at this cut has minimum greater than one. Append a fictitious independent full ordinary column to the comparison process. An old point of length at most one would force a winner of length at most one by its 364th draw. Use the Part 239 stopped-flag test with \(T=364,u=1\). The ambient window still has rank 506; only the initial raw incidence exponent and the initial log determinant change. The floor update \(x\mapsto\max(x-\log q,(505/506)x-\tfrac12\log56.2142)\) is increasing and 1-Lipschitz. Every comparison floor is therefore at most \(28\log q\) below the original floor. Multiplying the original stopped-flag majorant by \(q^{28}\) covers both changes. This fictitious column is used only to bound a marginal probability. It follows that on the passing prefixes the five columns on \(B\) add orthogonal unit axes. Each is the unique shortest line, and removing it returns the same retained lattice. Their syndromes already belong to the frozen image, so the deficiency at cut 400 remains at least 141.

## Why two old directions survive every choice of winners

**Projection lemma.** At a cut, embed the current frozen span in the raw space of the next \(a\) appends by zero padding. Suppose raw graph vectors in that larger space have projections of norm at most \(R\) modulo the current span, and those projections span dimension \(a+h\). After the next \(a\) selections, at least \(h\) independent images of the same vectors have norm at most \(R\). Indeed, projection off the enlarged frozen span contracts norms, and its restriction to the earlier projected space has kernel dimension at most \(a\). The vectors need only belong to the raw graph by the end of the appends. This handles the fact that some of them use the last columns.

The 372 visible row axes project to a space of rank at least 141 modulo the frozen space at cut 395. Indeed, if \(p\) of them span this projection, adjoining them to the frozen space puts all 400 horizontal axes in the enlarged space. The deficiency drops to zero, by at most \(p\). Some set of 141 visible axes is therefore independent after projection. The two prescribed rows project independently with conditional probability at least \(141\cdot140/(372\cdot371)>0.14303\). The five unit deletions preserve this independence.

For each of the two chosen rows, in the order (row, three fresh heights), the following vectors have lifts with additional horizontal coordinates only on \(B\):
\[
 (8,1,0,0),\quad(0,-8,1,0),\quad(0,0,-8,1),\quad(-1,0,4,6).
\]
Their absolute determinant is 3329; the final congruence is \(4\cdot64+6\cdot512=3328\). Each visible length is at most \(\sqrt{65}\). After projection off the cut-400 span the eight vectors are independent, since the two row directions survive independently and the six height coordinates are new. Apply the projection lemma with \((a,h,R)=(6,2,\sqrt{65})\). Consequently **at least two independent images of the displayed vectors** remain after draw 406, both of length at most \(\sqrt{65}\). These are old points for draw 407. This argument uses the full physical projection at each step and allows any six shortest winners, including winners outside the displayed space. The plane they span has saturated area at most 65.

On the last independent column the conditional probability of any positive-height point through \(\sqrt{65}\) is below \(1.02\cdot10^{-162}\), using even Part 239's larger radius \(26/\sqrt3\) and its uniform determinant floor. This bound applies before gate conditioning. The actual last winner is then old and has length at most \(\sqrt{65}\). At least one of the two short points has a nonzero image after this last projection, of the same or smaller length, still of graph height zero. The full inverse image of the plane at 406 adds at most eight directions to the cut-400 space. The frozen space at 407 adds seven, and the full inverse image of the terminal line adds eight. Their respective deficiencies are at least 133, 134 and 133.

## Raw gates and original-law mass

For the raw gate it suffices to check the full graph of dimension 912. A tuple with a nonzero ordinary input costs \(q^{-400}\) for fixed coordinates, giving \(M_{912}(250)/q^{400}\). With only bootstrap inputs the block minimum suffices. Otherwise some coefficient on the eleven special columns is nonzero modulo \(q\); for each such tuple their combined hidden label is uniform on 28 rows. Retain the 28 hidden coordinates and the 15 nonordinary heights. All visible horizontal residues are determined and have at most one representative through 250. The second bound is \(M_{43}(250)/q^{28}<10^{-5.20}\). Both use sign pairs. Zero extension covers all raw prefixes.

For the dual gate at cut \(j\) put \(n=105+j,N=400+n,u=\min(11,\max(0,j-395))\), and \(r=qR_0(j)=(q^n/(408000V_N))^{1/N}-\sqrt N/2\). A short scaled dual vector has residue \((a,-A^\top a)\), with nonzero \(a\) and \(0<r<q/2\). If \(a\) is nonzero on \(B\), the \(n-4\) nonbootstrap inner products are independent uniform, conditional only on the visible row choice. Drop the four bootstrap coordinates determined by \(a\). If \(a\) vanishes on \(B\), drop those 28 zeros and the \(4+u\) determined output coordinates; the \(n-4-u\) ordinary products are independent uniform. The sum over all cuts of
\[
  2M_{N-4}(r)q^{-(n-4)}
  +2M_{N-32-u}(r)q^{-(n-4-u)}
\]
is below \(2.72\cdot10^{-6}\). This allows both signs separately. [Dual floors](wall1_transference.md) · [Raw half-cube estimate](wall1_raw_graph_winner_cap.md)

Subtracting all exceptions from the independent-pair allowance leaves auxiliary probability greater than 0.14. Averaging over the row choice gives the original-law lower bound \(0.14q^{-5692}>10^{-20050}\). Conversely, even the union of the prescribed column patterns over row choices has probability at most \(372\cdot371q^{-5692}<10^{-20043}\).

For the surviving terminal point \(v\), every lift has length greater than 250 by the raw gate. At height zero both signs contribute whenever \(64\le Z\le959.5\), since \(\|v\|^2\le65\). With Part 239's fixed-projector moments and upper cap probability, their contribution is at least
\[
 2\left(\frac{(757.5-64)^2}{2272.5+(757.5-64)^2}-0.00047835048014\right)>1.98963 .
\]
In particular the design rejects the score test. Its late small winner is an old one, but the earlier unit appends are in Part 239's already charged fresh-vector exception. The construction gives neither a score-*accepted* lift failure nor a first wholly old creation in the unpaid \(Z_1\) range. It does exhibit an inherited long-raw-lift summand outside the three Part 239 score charges. Subsections, raw-gate violation and contradiction with global shortestness cannot exclude it pathwise. The \(5.2408\cdot10^{-7}\) remaining **mean**, the \(Z_1\) allowance of more than \(4.0203\cdot10^{-12}\), and the score-accepted prefix probability remain open. At plant heights 2–30 the unpaid score range in Part 239 already starts at deficiency 132.

## Reproduction

Run the embedded interval checker from the existing scripts directory. It imports the audited chart constants, Hermite floors and stopped-flag sum. The new result is a paper proof with arithmetic verification.

~~~sh
(cd docs/research/scripts && awk '/^```python$/{p=1;next} /^```$/{p=0} p' ../wall1_high_deficiency.md | python -B -)
~~~

```python
from math import comb
from mpmath import iv
from wall1_score_rejection import (
    chart_logs, early_flag, log_floors, log_theta, maximum, section_mass,
)

iv.dps = 55
I, log, exp, sqrt = iv.mpf, iv.ln, iv.exp, iv.sqrt
q = 3329
Q = log(q)
V = [I(1), I(2)]
for n in range(2, 913):
    V.append(2 * iv.pi * V[n-2] / n)

def mass(dim, radius_sq, constraints):
    return V[dim] * (I(radius_sq) + I(dim)/4)**(I(dim)/2) / I(q)**constraints

def next_floor(x):
    return maximum(x-Q, I(505)/506*x-log(I("56.2142"))/2)

def show_upper(label, value, cap):
    assert value < I(cap), (label, value, cap)
    print(label, value)

floors = log_floors()
floors.append(next_floor(floors[-1]))
for i, split, lo, hi in [(406, 141, "-2124.49", "-2124.48"),
                         (407, 142, "-1069.02", "-1069.01")]:
    value = iv.log10(section_mass(505+i, i, -floors[i], 132, split))
    assert I(lo) < value < I(hi)
    print("log10 frozen-section upper bound", i, value)

# Smaller uniform graph following the four coordinate-block deletions.
k, rows, split, cutoff = 363, 372, 125, 140
N = 505+k
small_floor = rows*Q
for _ in range(k):
    small_floor = next_floor(small_floor)
height = cutoff*Q-small_floor
assert height > 0 and max(k, N-k)-(k-rows+2*cutoff-1) >= 100
chart_high, chart_low = chart_logs(N, k, split)
terms = [v+(max(k,N-k)+2*j+I(".02"))*height for j,v in enumerate(chart_low)]
low = log(split) + I([max(v.a for v in terms), max(v.b for v in terms)])
high = chart_high + (N+I(".01"))*height
section = (4*comb(N,k)*exp(maximum(high,low)-cutoff*(k-rows+cutoff)*Q)
           / (1-I(q)**(-100)))
show_upper("reduced prefix deficiency through 140", section, "1e-2188")
old_unit = I(q)**28 * early_flag(364, I(1), floors)
show_upper("old unit test", old_unit, "1e-29")

powers = [pow(630, j, q) for j in range(8)]
assert pow(630, 8, q) == q-1
def norm_sq(t):
    return sum(((t*a+q//2) % q-q//2)**2 for a in powers)
bootstrap_sq = min(q*q, min(norm_sq(t) for t in range(1,q)))
assert bootstrap_sq == norm_sq(58) == 1827621
bootstrap_exit = mass(505, bootstrap_sq, 372)
show_upper("bootstrap comparison", bootstrap_exit, "1e-102")

# Check the visible four-dimensional lattice basis by integer elimination
# along the last three (unit triangular) height coordinates.
vectors = [(8,1,0,0),(0,-8,1,0),(0,0,-8,1),(-1,0,4,6)]
assert all((x-8*a-64*b-512*c) % q == 0 for x,a,b,c in vectors)
assert max(sum(x*x for x in v) for v in vectors) == 65
last = list(vectors[-1])
for i in (2,1,0):
    coefficient = last[i+1]
    last = [x-coefficient*y for x,y in zip(last, vectors[i])]
assert last == [-q,0,0,0]

raw_ordinary = mass(912, 250**2, 400)
raw_special = mass(43, 250**2, 28)
show_upper("special-only raw exit", iv.log10(raw_special), "-5.20")
dual = I(0)
for j in range(408):
    n = 105+j
    N = 400+n
    used = min(11, max(0, j-395))
    radius = exp((n*Q-log(408000*V[N]))/N)-sqrt(N)/2
    assert 0 < radius < I(q)/2
    dual += 2*mass(N-4, radius**2, n-4)
    dual += 2*mass(N-32-used, radius**2, n-4-used)
show_upper("all dual exits", dual, "2.72e-6")

width = (26/sqrt(3))*sqrt(2*iv.pi/506)
fresh = exp(253+506*log(width)+505*log_theta(width)-floors[406])/2
show_upper("fresh last-draw exception", fresh, "1.02e-162")
pair = I(141*140)/(372*371)
auxiliary = pair - (section+old_unit+bootstrap_exit+raw_ordinary+raw_special+dual+fresh)
assert auxiliary > I(".14")
exponent = 4*400+11*372
assert exponent == 5692
assert I(".14")/I(q)**exponent > I("1e-20050")
assert I(372*371)/I(q)**exponent < I("1e-20043")
delta = I("757.5")-64
score = 2*(delta**2/(I("2272.5")+delta**2)-I(".00047835048014"))
assert score > I("1.98963")
print("auxiliary probability lower bound", auxiliary)
print("conditional signed score lower bound", score)
```

## Audit

**Verdict.** All four claims hold for the unconditioned width-506 marginal restart with the signed forward selector. The embedded interval verifier passed every assertion. No numerical correction is needed.

**Frozen prefixes.** At exact deficiency \(d\), the section identity gives \(h(F_i)=q^d/D_i\). The deterministic floor for \(D_i\) therefore supplies a height list before the matrix is drawn, as required by the fixed-space probability. As a separate arithmetic check, summing the chart envelopes directly over the 23 nonempty exact deficiencies at each cut gave base-ten logarithms of \(-2124.487\) at 406 and \(-1069.017\) at 407, rounded to three decimals. Zero padding preserves the earlier syndrome image, which is contained in the later image. Thus any earlier deficiency at most 132 forces that event at 407. The single cut-407 estimate proves the simultaneous probability \(>1-10^{-1069}\).

**Selected plane and line.** The forced deletion exposes an unconditioned \(372\times133\) visible initial graph. Charging disagreement with the first 32 actual selections allows the reduced-prefix estimates to be used before conditioning on agreement. At cut 395 the passing prefix has deficiency at least 141 and minimum greater than one. The five unit selections preserve that deficiency. For an independent visible row pair, the determinant \(3329\) in each four-coordinate block and the six fresh heights give eight independent projected directions at cut 400 in the enlarged raw space. Six actual selections remove at most six of them in rank. Two independent old images of norm at most \(\sqrt{65}\) remain at cut 406, giving saturated area at most 65. The last fresh-point exception has conditional probability below \(2.504\cdot10^{-165}\). Off that exception, the winner is old, and removing its line leaves at least one nonzero old image. Starting at cut 400, the rank increases for the plane lift, the final frozen space and the surviving line lift are respectively eight, seven and eight. Their deficiencies are therefore at least 133, 134 and 133.

**Gates and probability.** The raw test at dimension 912 covers all prefixes by zero extension. In particular every raw lift of the surviving nonzero point has norm greater than 250. In the dual test the free inner products come from the independent hidden entries when the dual row tuple is nonzero on \(B\), and from the ordinary columns when it vanishes on \(B\). The sum over all 408 cuts is below \(2.711\cdot10^{-6}\). Subtracting all exceptions leaves auxiliary probability greater than \(0.1430220\). With the four bootstrap columns fixed, averaging over row pairs gives the lower certificate \(0.14q^{-5692}\). Taking the union over the prescribed patterns gives the upper certificate \(372\cdot371q^{-5692}\). Their base-ten logarithms are \(-20049.8640\) and \(-20043.8702\), respectively, rounded to four decimals. The bracket \(10^{-20050}<\Pr(E)<10^{-20043}\) applies to the successful construction with its column restrictions. The lower endpoint also applies to the larger event defined solely by the geometric and gate properties.

**Score and open bounds.** On each successful design the two signs count at plant height zero throughout \(64\le Z\le959.5\). The fixed-projector moment and cap bounds give their conditional contribution \(>1.98963754\), so the design rejects the score test. This proves that both gates and global shortest selection can coexist with a high-deficiency, long-raw-lift score term. The earlier fresh unit winners already meet Part 239's charged fresh-vector exception. The probability of lift rejection on score-accepted designs, the remaining mean-score allowance \(5.2408\cdot10^{-7}\), and the unpaid \(Z_1\) mass against the available balance \(>4.0203\cdot10^{-12}\) all remain open.
