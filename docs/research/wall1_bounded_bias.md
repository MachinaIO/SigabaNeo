# How large can one selection bias be?

For the marginal width-506 restart, the sharp history-uniform factor for conditioning on a named winner is **$3329^{400}$** if the observables include counts of saturated planes through the actual winner at fixed area cutoffs. One such plane count attains the factor at draw 406 or 407 even when the only information revealed is that the winner has nonzero height. Its exceptional history has explicitly computable, very small probability. For the narrower comparison that conditions first on a *fixed candidate being present*, the denominator changes. The example incurs its entire cost at this first conditioning.

Horizontal depth and the number of selections that affect a terminal count are different. Two reachable histories below have identical subsequent raw columns and exclusively vertical subsequent wins, but different plane counts. A suffix change-of-measure inequality does hold with an explicit hypothesis on its reference kernels. Its reference expectation still integrates the selected **block-start** state. The known counts do not certify either requested allowance.

**Lemma 1 (finite-mark conditioning).** Fix the incoming history $s$, including the lattice $L$, finite subgroup $H$, and frame for signed ties. For a nonempty subset $A\subseteq H$ of marks giving a specified selection outcome, write $p=|A|/|H|$. For any nonnegative count $N$ defined on every draw, the sharp all-observable inequality is
$$
 \mathbb E[N\mid s,A]\le p^{-1}\mathbb E[N\mid s],
 \qquad p^{-1}\le |H|\le Q:=3329^{400}.
$$
The same inequality holds for a future count if its conditional continuation given the current draw is the same in both expectations. When the reference conditions on candidate incidence $I\supseteq A$, the sharp all-observable factor is instead $|I|/|A|$. A zero-probability selection has no such conditional law.

*Proof.* Under Part 108 the new raw residue maps onto $H$ with equal fibers. The conditional density on marks is $\mathbf1_A/p$, so multiplying by $N$ and summing proves the inequality. Equality holds for $N=\mathbf1_A$, or for any nonzero nonnegative count supported on $A$. For a future count first take its mean over continuations. Restrict the uniform sums to $I$ to obtain the incidence version. The domain of the raw residue map has $Q$ elements. ∎

In particular, for a fixed positive-height candidate $v=(y,k)$ with $q\nmid k$, incidence requires $kU=[y]$ in the old torus. Multiplication by $k$ is bijective on the mark since its exponent divides the prime $q$. Thus incidence, when possible, already identifies the displacement. If this candidate wins, its selection adds a factor of one *within incidence*. A horizontal primitive candidate is present on every draw, and its signed winner test can still bias the mark within incidence. Part 112 gives $4/(9/4)=16/9$ for its common horizontal candidate. Part 156's $3.5330\ldots/(9/4)$ uses the Gibbs oracle instead. Part 105's factor two compares a transverse shape to Haar for a Haar input at the unit-volume radius. None of these numbers bounds all named selections from the uniform finite-mark law. ## A plane count attaining the sharp constant at width 506

**Proposition 2.** At either suffix draw $j=406,407$, there is an incoming raw history of probability $Q^{-(105+j-1)}$ and a nonzero-height win of conditional probability $1/Q$ such that the expected number of saturated planes through the winner of area strictly below $3/2$ increases from $105/Q$ to $105$. This area is a diagnostic cutoff for the uniform claim. The terminal residuals use their own cutoffs and gates.

*Proof.* Write $m=400$, $n=105$, $q=3329$. Prescribe the initial graph with columns $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ in $\mathbb F_q^m$. Its physical lattice is
$$
 L=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb Z^m\oplus\mathbb Z^n:\ x\equiv (y,0)\pmod q\}.
$$
Its shortest squared norm is two. Its only pairs of squared norm two are the $n$ graph-column pairs. With one nonzero component of $y$ of size one, $x$ must be the matching signed unit vector to attain squared norm two. With two nonzero components of $y$, the required $x=0$ violates the congruence, and with $y=0$ a nonzero $x$ has length at least $q$. The congruence also excludes unit vectors. The horizontal classes give a mark of order $Q$, since $L\cap(\mathbb Z^m,0)=(q\mathbb Z^m,0)$.

Prescribe zero for the first $j-1$ suffix columns. Each appends an orthogonal unit axis, the unique shortest line, and projecting it away returns $L$ and its full original mark. Each specified raw column, including at initialization, costs exactly $1/Q$ independently. On draw $j$, the pure new axis wins exactly at zero displacement. At any other displacement the old lattice has no unit vector, and a unit vector of nonzero height would have to be the pure new axis. All coordinates here are integral, so the shortest squared norm on a nonzero draw is at least two.

In fact the nonzero-height winner event is precisely the zero mark. At nonzero displacement the old vector $(-e_1,-e_1,0)$ attains squared norm two, with the entries denoting the output, old input and fresh coordinates. A nonhorizontal tie has fresh coordinate $\pm1$ and exactly one signed unit among all old coordinates. If its first output coordinate exceeds $-1$, the old vector precedes it. If that coordinate is $-1$, all its other old coordinates vanish, whereas the first old input coordinate of the displayed old vector is $-1$. The old vector again precedes it. Previously frozen zero coordinates do not affect these comparisons. Thus the same posterior is obtained by revealing only horizontal versus nonhorizontal.

In any rank-two lattice with shortest length $a$, extend a shortest vector to a basis and round its partner along the first vector. The rounded partner has length at least $a$ and parallel component at most $a/2$. Hence the area is at least $\sqrt3\,a^2/2$. At nonzero displacement this gives area at least $\sqrt3>3/2$ for every plane. At zero the window is $L\oplus\mathbb Z e_{\rm new}$. Saturated planes through the winning axis correspond bijectively to primitive unoriented lines in $L$, and their areas are precisely the lengths of the primitive generators. Exactly the $n$ graph-column lines are shorter than $3/2$, by the integral squared norms. Thus the count is $105\,\mathbf1_{\{U=0\}}$ even though its definition tests only the winner and plane area. Averaging proves the claim. The joint raw-history-and-winner probabilities are $Q^{-511}$ and $Q^{-512}$, respectively. In particular this one prescribed draw-407 history contributes only $105Q^{-512}$ to the design-averaged diagnostic count. ∎

The same history gives a short-vector example even before quotienting. The sign-pair count in the window at squared norm below $3/2$ is $\mathbf1_{\{U=0\}}$. For the fixed candidate $e_{\rm new}$ viewed *under incidence*, the reference has just one mark, as in Lemma 1. Thus Proposition 2 gives a sharp obstruction to forgetting the named winner from the full draw law, and leaves room for much smaller constants for restricted events or for common-candidate quotient counts. In particular its exceptional winner has length one and does not witness the near-GH vertical residual or the $F_{\rm rem}$ length band.

## What depth truncation proves

**Lemma 3 (suffix comparison).** Let $P$ be a selected path law with block-start marginal $\mu$. Let $R$ start from the same $\mu$ and use specified reference kernels for the last $D$ draws on a common history space. Retain enough labels to evaluate the same terminal function $Z\ge0$ and the same shallow event $G$ under both laws. Suppose that on paths in $G$ with positive $P$-mass all corresponding reference transitions have positive mass, and that the product of the actual-to-reference conditional transition ratios there is at most $C^D$. Then
$$
 \mathbb E_P Z\le C^D\,\mathbb E_{s\sim\mu}\mathbb E_R[Z\mathbf1_G\mid s]
                    +\mathbb E_P[Z\mathbf1_{G^c}].
$$
Pointwise domination by $C$ on each transition along a shallow path is sufficient. For an indicator the last term is at most $P(G^c)$. For a plane multiplicity the exit is a *weighted* expectation.

*Proof.* Factor each shallow path mass into its block-start mass and its $D$ conditional transition probabilities. The initial mass cancels in the ratio, and the assumed bound applies to the product. Multiply by the path's terminal count and sum. Add the exact deep contribution. For $0\le Z\le1$ that contribution is bounded by the exit probability. ∎

Here is one explicit choice of reference. At each state draw two independent uniform marks $u,u'$ and let $v$ be the signed winner computed using $u'$. Use $u$ for the actual window. If $v$ is primitive in that window, project it out and carry the resulting lattice, mark and frame forward. Otherwise enter an absorbing failure state with zero terminal count. Record $(u,v)$, integrating out $u'$. Writing $p_s(v)$ for the fraction of marks whose winner is $v$, the reference mass of a consistent transition $(u,v(u))$ is $p_s(v(u))/|H|$, against $1/|H|$ in the selected process. Every actual transition is therefore covered and its ratio is at most $Q$. Declare $G$ false on inconsistent reference paths, or extend its terminal test nonnegatively on them for an upper bound. Lemma 3 now gives a concrete $Q^D$ comparison. Even this reference needs the incoming state's winner frequencies. Its block-start distribution is the original selected marginal.

A one-step estimate for a single count cannot automatically be iterated. After integrating the final transition, the next test function is the conditional expectation of the entire remaining filtered count. Either kernel domination or estimates for these specific continuation functions are needed. Also, choosing a separate posterior for each possible winner and summing it without that winner's probability changes the path law.

For Part 122 let $d_h$ count horizontal full preimages of the designated plane ending at draw 406. If $G=\{d_h<D\}$, a depth $\ell<D$ requires $\ell+1\le D$ draws including the first nonhorizontal pullback. For $1\le D\le406$ the bounded-charge exit is at most
$$
 \mathbb E_{S_{406-D}}\prod_{i=1}^D(1-p_i(S_{406-D})).
$$
Indeed all $D$ horizontal preimages force the successive old winners calculated at the block start. The $i$-th fresh column alone with the projected starting lattice gives a strictly shorter competitor on a fraction $p_i$ of its marks. Those prescribed tests use independent raw columns. Avoiding all of them is necessary. This bounds the exit for this designated plane. Draw 407 lies outside the depth definition, and this exit alone does not bound its $F_{\rm rem}$ multiplicity. On the zero graph followed by 303 zero columns, an event of probability $Q^{-408}$, the 105 old unit axes force depth at least 103 regardless of the next 103 marks. Thus even the unweighted exit can equal one conditionally on a reachable start. Its average is a separate question. ## Verification

It enumerated integer vectors of squared norm at most five satisfying the graph congruence and chose the winner by squared norm and signed ambient coordinates. ## Audit

**Verdict — SOUND.** The sharp factor is attained for the fixed area cutoff in the marginal width-506 restart. No mathematical or numerical correction is needed.

**Sharpness and the two probabilities.** Part 108 gives equal fibers onto the incoming mark group, so conditioning on a nonempty set of marks costs at most its reciprocal probability, at most $|H|\le Q$. At draw 407 the prescribed graph and preceding zero columns fix $105+406=511$ independent raw columns. Their probability is $Q^{-511}$. At this history the mark map is injective. At zero the signed winner is $-e_{\rm new}$. On every nonzero mark a fresh competitor tied at squared norm two has only one nonzero old coordinate. In signed ambient order it loses to $(-e_1,-e_1,0)$, including when its first output coordinate is $-1$. Thus revealing nonzero height selects exactly one mark, of conditional probability $1/Q$ and joint probability $Q^{-512}$.

On that mark, projection along the unit winner identifies saturated incident planes of area below $3/2$ with the 105 primitive unoriented old lines of squared norm two. Every plane on a nonzero mark has area at least $\sqrt3$. Hence the conditional means before and after the height reveal are exactly $105/Q$ and $105$. This also attains the bound for a named signed winner. Under the incidence reference for the fresh axis, the zero mark already has probability one.

**Depth and change of measure.** On the common all-zero suffix, both starting graphs repeatedly remove the fresh unit axis. The full inverse image through draw 406 contains that vertical kernel, giving Part 122 depth zero for either graph. Projection leaves the respective old graphs intact. The doubled graph has no primitive line of squared norm at most two, giving zero planes where the unit graph gives 105. For Lemma 3, a consistent labeled transition has selected-to-reference mass ratio $1/p_s(v)$ in the independent-mark reference. Multiplication over the suffix proves the stated comparison. Its shallow expectation still averages over the selected block-start law. The exit for an unbounded plane count retains its multiplicity weight. The numerical limits on the reference means are sufficient thresholds for this comparison to certify each budget.

**Terminal scope.** The area $3/2$ is diagnostic. The exhibited length-one winner misses Part 151's near-GH vertical band and Part 150's residual length band. The exceptional raw history contributes $105Q^{-512}$ to the draw-407 diagnostic mean. The two selected-history allowances remain open. Part 117's coupling error applies once to a terminal event probability. Parts 105, 112 and 156 use the distinct reference laws identified above.

**Independent reproduction.** Scratch code enumerated all 25 next columns for each of four toy graphs. It checked saturation by the gcd of the rank-two minors in a window lattice basis and tested area with the physical Gram determinant. For one and two initial columns the unit-graph totals were respectively one and two, all on the zero mark, which was also the sole vertical win. Both doubled-graph totals were zero. Enumeration of Part 112's eight marks gave 18 quotient pairs in total and four on its sole accepting mark, yielding $9/4$ and $4$. At 90-decimal-digit outward-rounded precision, all four printed enclosures passed strict endpoint tests, and both half-budget values agreed as exact rationals.
