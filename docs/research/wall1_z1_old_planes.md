# How close must an old plane be before a horizontal first hit?

Let $a=1352/\sqrt3$, let $M_t$ be the retained lattice after draw $t$, and let $a_2(M)$ be its least saturated plane area. Write $\tau_X=\min\{t\ge0:a_2(M_t)\le X\}$, with value $\infty$ if there is no hit. Use Part 194's $p_{\rm old}$, including the terminal hit, the good event $G$, and the exclusion of a fresh shortest vector in **any** hit plane. A wholly old hit takes priority over a simultaneous mixed hit. [Part 194 audit](wall1_z1_horizontal.md#audit)

**Result.** A wholly old first hit at draw $i$ forces
$$
     a<a_2(M_{i-1})\le ca,\qquad c=\sqrt{3/2},\qquad ca=676\sqrt2.
     \tag{1}
$$
The factor is sharp for Euclidean horizontal windows. At draw 154, inserting this cutoff into the published all-origins flag bound gives a single summand exceeding $10^7$. The conjectured allocation $p_{\rm old}+p_{\rm late}\le7.6330\cdot10^{-11}$ remains open for the restart.

## A sharp pre-step interval

Fix the incoming lattice $M$, its designated shortest vector $v$ of length $s$, and the projection $p$ off $v$. At a horizontal win the old part of the output is $pM$. For a wholly old saturated hit plane $P$, put $J=(p|_M)^{-1}(P)$. It is saturated of rank three in $M$, $\det J=s\det P$, and $\lambda_1(J)=s$. Work in its intrinsic three-dimensional span. Every saturated plane of $J$ is the kernel in $J$ of a primitive $z\in J^*$, unique up to sign. Adjacent parallel lattice layers have spacing $1/\|z\|$, so the kernel has area $\det J\,\|z\|$. A shortest dual vector is primitive. Consequently
$$
 \frac{a_2(J)}{\det P}=s\lambda_1(J^*)
    =\lambda_1(J)\lambda_1(J^*)\le\gamma'_3=\sqrt{3/2}.
 \tag{2}
$$
The inequality is the three-dimensional Bergé–Martinet theorem. A saturated plane of $J$ stays saturated in $M$ since $J$ is saturated there. Thus $a_2(M)\le a_2(J)$, and $\tau_a=i$ supplies the strict lower endpoint of (1). The small parent plane furnished by (2) may contain $v$ and project to a line. [Bergé–Martinet constant, definition (3.1) and Example 3.3](https://intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/cag/2004/0012/0003/CAG-2004-0012-0003-a008.pdf) · [Part 194, old triple correspondence](wall1_z1_horizontal.md#the-horizontal-quotient-and-its-two-kinds-of-planes)

For sharpness take $J=hD_3$, where $D_3=\{x\in\mathbb Z^3:\sum x_j\in2\mathbb Z\}$ and $h^2=a/\sqrt2$. Before scaling, $D_3$ has determinant 2 and minimum $\sqrt2$. Its dual is $\mathbb Z^3\cup((1/2,1/2,1/2)+\mathbb Z^3)$ with minimum $\sqrt3/2$. Hence $a_2(J)=h^2\sqrt3=ca$. Projecting off **any** shortest line leaves a plane of area $2h^3/(h\sqrt2)=a$. For the head $(1,1,0)$, extend by $(1,0,1),(0,1,1)$. These three vectors have absolute determinant 2. The unscaled projected Gram matrix has diagonal $3/2$ and off-diagonal $1/2$. Its determinant is 2. An exact rational scratch check verified the matrix and enumerated all twelve unscaled signed shortest vectors.

This example admits an append with a uniform mark in a group of order $q=3329$. Use $M=J\oplus100q\mathbb Ze_4$ and marks generated by $100e_4$ modulo $M$. Every parent plane extending out of $J$ has a kernel line in $J$ and area at least $100q\,s$. For each nonzero mark and each nonzero height $|k|\le s<34$, the fourth coordinate has absolute value at least 100 since $q$ is prime. Larger heights already exceed $s$. Thus all $q-1$ nonzero marks select an old shortest line and give the wholly old hit. At the zero mark the new unit axis wins. The horizontal comparison can therefore have conditional probability $(q-1)/q$ even at the sharp endpoint. This example tests a single transition. Its frequency among reachable restart prefixes, including $G$, is unspecified.

## Audit

**Verdict — SOUND.** The interval in (1) holds with both endpoints as stated. The scaled face-centered cubic lattice attains the upper endpoint for a Euclidean horizontal transition. No correction is required.

For the wholly old plane $P$, the quotient $M/J$ is isomorphic to $pM/P$ and is torsion-free. A primitive dual normal of $J$ maps $J$ onto $\mathbb Z$, so its kernel has area $\det J$ times the normal length. Minimizing and using $\det J=s\det P$ gives (2), and saturation passes from that kernel through $J$ to $M$. The strict lower bound uses survival through draw $i-1$. Bangert and Katz use the product of the two shortest **lengths** in (3.1). Their Example 3.3 gives $\sqrt{3/2}$ and identifies the face-centered cubic extremizer, citing Bergé and Martinet, Proposition 2.13(iii). [Bangert and Katz, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0304494#page=7)

For $hD_3$ the primitive normal $(1,1,1)/(2h)$ has minimum dual length. Its kernel has area $h^2\sqrt3$, while the quotient by any shortest line has area $h^2\sqrt2$. In the appended example the fourth and fresh coordinates both survive horizontal projection. For a nonzero mark and $0<|k|\le s$, the fourth coordinate has magnitude at least 100. For $|k|>s$, the fresh coordinate alone exceeds $s$. Thus even **after** projection every fresh vector is longer than $s$. Here $s^2=\sqrt2\,a>2a/\sqrt3$, and every hit plane has a shortest length at most $\sqrt{2a/\sqrt3}$. This verifies Part 194's fresh-shortest exclusion in every simultaneous hit plane for all $q-1$ nonzero marks. The example establishes sharpness for the stated class of windows. Its probability of occurrence in the restart remains unknown.

An independent scratch calculation used 90-decimal-digit outward intervals, factorial and half-integer factorial formulas for the Blichfeldt caps, and 37 determinant updates. It gave $3191.76781835<T'b_{155}<3191.76781836$ and $6.37707164<z<6.37707165$. At $(i,j)=(38,0)$ the prefix-cap sums cancel and $\pi w^2/z^2=253$. With the theta factor replaced by one, the term lies in $(10038946.28602,10038946.28604)$. The full positive summand therefore exceeds $10^7$. This comparison concerns the numerical upper bound in Part 192.

The signed horizontal candidate and the incidence $I_t$ are fixed on the incoming framed history. Averaging the horizontal win indicator over the next uniform mark gives exactly $w_t$ there. In Part 194's formula the remaining continuation and exclusion weight is at most one on surviving prefixes and vanishes on prefixes that already failed a good-event test. This gives the stated weighted upper bound. For (3), each old first hit supplies a single predecessor in the indicated time interval, so the event containment needs no multiplier. Parts 192–195 leave the surviving-prefix incidence and the late mixed mass without estimates at the requested combined scale.
