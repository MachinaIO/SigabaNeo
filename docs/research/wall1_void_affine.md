# Ball avoidance on a fixed-profile affine fiber

A uniform shift gives an exact second moment on each prefix torus. The fixed-profile average of that moment reduces to a finite sum of radial integrals. Counts at distinct last indices are uncorrelated, even when the corresponding hit events are dependent. Together these facts give the explicit window exponent (5) below. No power-law lower bound on this exponent in the proposed large-mean regime is established here.

## Coverage bounds for one lattice

Let $D$ have rank $d$ in Euclidean $d$-space, determinant $\Delta$, and successive Euclidean minima $\lambda_i$. Let $C_D(r)$ be the Haar probability of the image of the open ball in the torus. Write $v=|B_r|/\Delta$. For $r>0$,

$$ C_D(r)\ge \max\left\{\frac{|B_{\min(r,\lambda_1/2)}|}{\Delta},\;\min\left(1,\frac{2r}{\sqrt{\sum_i\lambda_i^2}}\right)^d\right\}. \tag{1} $$

The smaller ball injects up to null boundaries. For the second bound choose independent vectors attaining the successive minima in order. Nearest-plane rounding in their sublattice gives covering radius at most half the root sum of their squared Gram–Schmidt lengths, each bounded by the corresponding minimum. The Voronoi cell of $D$ is contained in the resulting covering ball of radius $H$. Scaling the cell by $\min(1,r/H)$ keeps it inside the cell and the test ball, up to boundaries. Conversely any nonzero dual vector $u$ sends the uniform shift to a uniform circle phase by the character $x\mapsto u\cdot x\bmod1$. Hence $C_D(r)\le\min(1,2r\lambda_1(D^*))$. Already $D=L\mathbb Z\oplus L^{-1}\mathbb Z$ gives coverage tending to zero for any prescribed positive $v$ as $L\to\infty$. This counterexample concerns one affine fiber. It does not give a counterexample for a surviving geometric profile.

Define $h_{d,r}(t)=|B_r\cap(B_r+t e_1)|$, zero at $t\ge2r$. If $X$ counts ball points of a uniform translate, unfolding the shift once per ordered difference gives

$$ \mathbb EX=v,\qquad \mathbb EX^2=\Delta^{-1}\sum_{z\in D}h_{d,r}(|z|),\qquad C_D(r)\ge v^2/\mathbb EX^2. \tag{2} $$

The last inequality is Cauchy–Schwarz against the hit indicator. Equivalently the fixed-fiber variance is $\Delta^{-2}\sum_{0\ne u\in D^*}|\widehat{1_{B_r}}(u)|^2$ by torus Parseval with Fourier phase $2\pi u\cdot x$. The overlap form has compact support.

## Averaging over the actual triangular histories

Use upper triangular columns, $R=g_0$, $D_j$ generated by the first $j$ columns, $\Delta_j=\prod_{i<j}g_i$, and $\Delta_0=1$. For each $1\le j<w$ and $1\le m<R/g_j$ let $r=\sqrt{R^2-m^2g_j^2}$ and $X_{jm}$ count all lifts with last coefficient $m$. Given the history before column $j$, the new horizontal part $x$ is uniform on the centered GS rectangle, a fundamental region by backward reduction. Multiplication by $m$ preserves Haar on the quotient. Thus (2) applies conditionally and its first moment $v_{jm}=|B_r^j|/\Delta_j$ is deterministic.

**Exact averaged variance.** For $h=h_{j,r}$ set

$$ A_{jm}=\frac1{\Delta_j}\left[h(0)+2\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\frac1{\Delta_k}\sum_{1\le l<2r/g_k}\int_{\mathbb R^k} h\!\left(\sqrt{|y|^2+l^2g_k^2}\right)dy\right]-v_{jm}^2. \tag{3} $$

Then $A_{jm}=\operatorname{Var} X_{jm}$ under the joint law of the prefix and the fresh column. Indeed, classify each nonzero difference $z\in D_j$ by last index $k$ and unsigned last coefficient $l$. Its horizontal lifts are $lx_k+D_k$. Integrating their radial weight unfolds to Lebesgue measure divided by $\Delta_k$, also at $k=0$ with a point integral. Both signs contribute equally. Inserting this identity into (2) proves (3). One-dimensional quadrature suffices for each radial integral, with $h_{j,r}(t)=2\kappa_{j-1}\int_{t/2}^r(r^2-s^2)^{(j-1)/2}ds$ on the support, where $\kappa_i$ is the unit ball volume. Thus a single endpoint and multiplicity alone give void probability at most $A_{jm}/(A_{jm}+v_{jm}^2)$ after averaging.

A quadrature-free upper bound follows from monotonicity. Write $I_k=\int_{\mathbb R^k}h(|y|)dy$ with $I_0=h(0)$. For each $k$, twice the positive grid sum in (3) is bounded by $I_{k+1}/g_k$, by upper bounding each grid value with the average on its preceding interval. The last term then cancels $v_{jm}^2$ exactly, since $I_j=|B_r^j|^2$. Hence
$$
 A_{jm}\le \Delta_j^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}I_k/\Delta_k,\qquad
 I_k=\kappa_k^2\,\pi^{(j-k)/2}\frac{\Gamma(k+1)}{\Gamma(1+(j+k)/2)}r^{j+k}.
$$
Indeed $I_k$ is the integral of the squared $k$-dimensional section volume over the orthogonal $(j-k)$-ball, which gives the displayed beta integral. This version of (4)–(5) uses only positive terms and avoids subtracting large nearly equal moments.

Let $N_j=\sum_m X_{jm}$ and $E=\sum_{jm}v_{jm}$. The centered $N_j$ are martingale differences under the column filtration, since their conditional means are constant. Within each column use the triangle inequality in centered $L^2$. This gives

$$ \operatorname{Var} N\le S(g)=\sum_j\left(\sum_m\sqrt{A_{jm}}\right)^2. \tag{4} $$

For $E>0$, Cauchy–Schwarz now gives the profile-dependent exponent

$$ \Pr(N=0)\le e^{-F(g)},\qquad F(g)=\log(1+E^2/S(g)). \tag{5} $$

Zero variance gives an infinite exponent. Any deterministic subset of the pairs $(j,m)$ also gives (5) with its own mean and variance bound, so one can maximize over subsets. For the one-parameter geometric family $g_i/R=e^{-ai}$, (5) is an explicit implicit-parameter $f_w(E)=F(g(a(E)))$: the exact mean is continuous and strictly increasing for $a>0$ by the section formula (already the $j=m=1$ term increases strictly), starts at zero and is unbounded for $w\ge2$. For arbitrary profiles (5) retains information beyond $E$.

## Conditioning and the strength of the exponent

On a surviving prefix one knows $\lambda_1(D_j)=R$ since the head vector attains $R$. Consequently (1) gives the deterministic conditional hit bound $q_j=|B^j_{\min(r_{j1},R/2)}|/\Delta_j$ when $g_j<R$, and zero otherwise. A value exceeding one excludes prefix survival. Iteration gives exponent $\sum_j-\log(1-q_j)$ on feasible prefixes, also allowing the common-box improvements of [Part 68](wall1_covering_fraction.md). Formula (3) uses the *unconditional* prefix distribution. Multiplying its marginal void bounds across columns would require a bound after survival conditioning. Orthogonality in (4) avoids that step. For disjoint windows the underlying triangular entries are disjoint and the exponents add.

Even a variance estimate $S=E$ would yield only $\log(1+E)$ in (5). At a benchmark $E=e^{130}$ the requested $E^{0.11}$ exponent is $e^{14.3}$, whereas the variance exponent in that scenario is below 131. To certify a given exponent $b$ by (5) requires $S\le E^2/(e^b-1)$. This is a diagnostic, not an estimate of the actual variance. No evaluation or uniform inequality here certifies the requested suppression on the surviving domain. The twelve previously excluded boxes already have infinite exponent by [the packing certificate](wall1_dense_packing_ld.md). Outside that cut, a stronger route would need control of the uncovered torus measure on surviving histories, or another estimate sensitive to the zero-count tail beyond the variance.

## Which literature averages apply

* Strömbergsson's convex-set estimates concern the invariant probability on unimodular linear lattices and also a fiber conditioned to contain a prescribed point. In particular translating the test set in this theorem still averages the lattice shape. Fixing all GS lengths specifies a different measure. [Paper, introduction and Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.3805)
* Strömbergsson and Venkatesh explicitly count points in a translate and then average over a Hecke orbit. Their Theorem 1 gives the limiting count law for affine lines modulo a prime in a scaled box, with an error as the prime grows. The additional average over linear lattices is visible in the proof of Theorem 2. The present profile has no such prime limit. [Paper, Sections 1 and 3–5](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/83230)
* Marklof and Strömbergsson turn collision-free trajectories into lattice avoidance in thin cylinders and obtain limiting laws by equidistribution as scatterer radius tends to zero. Random direction and rescaling are part of the limit theorem. A fresh torus shift at one diagonal supplies neither operation. [Paper, Sections 1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0706.4395)
* El-Baz, Marklof and Vinogradov prove Poissonian limiting two-point correlations of planar directions under their hypotheses while the corresponding gaps have a heavy tail. Even in that limit, pair correlation alone does not specify a void law. [Paper, introduction and Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.0028)
