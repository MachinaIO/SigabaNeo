# How much replanting can a stable prefix absorb?

Fix the uniform design matrix A and write x=(e,s) in N=m+n horizontal coordinates. As in [the planted model](usvp_planted.md), the lattice is generated by its horizontal lattice H and the mark (x,1). Two different perturbations matter here. Changing the metric with the plant held fixed explores a reduction decision region. To use the independent-coordinate estimate of [Part 9](wall1_planted_prob.md), one needs the mass of that region under **replanting**. The following gives an explicit conversion and a probability bound for horizontal prefixes.

## A radius from a reduction transcript

Use abstract coordinates H direct-sum Z, mapped to the embedding by (h,t) ↦ (h+tx,t). Compare two metrics G,G' in the same coordinates using the least r≥0 such that exp(−r)G ≤ G' ≤ exp(r)G as quadratic forms. At each query in a finite schedule of exact projected SVP selections, let a be the squared minimum and b the least squared length on a different line (infinity in rank one). Assume unique lines and deterministic metric-independent basis completions from their coefficient vectors. Then every perturbation of distance strictly below

    R = min over queries of (1/2) log(b/a)

preserves the coefficient transcript. Indeed, minimizing the squared norm over real lifts modulo a common prefix preserves both form inequalities. The winning primitive class then has squared length at most exp(r)a and each other line at least exp(−r)b. Induct on the queries, using the common completion to identify the next block. This certifies a lower bound on the stability radius, not its exact value. Metric-dependent stopping, size reduction or other branch decisions require their own margins if they affect subsequent queries. Full BKZ reducedness alone specifies no selector.

Changing x to y is exactly the shear (z,t) ↦ (z+t(y−x),t) in physical coordinates. Its extreme singular values are exp(±asinh(||y−x||/2)), since its nonidentity two-dimensional shear has determinant one and squared Frobenius norm 2+||y−x||² (the zero displacement is immediate). Thus the form distance is exactly 2 asinh(||y−x||/2). A transcript with certificate R persists on the open replanting ball of radius 2 sinh(R/2). A horizontal prefix on this ball is literally the same physical subspace because the shear fixes H pointwise. For a tilted prefix only its abstract coefficients persist.

## Averaging with the correct measure

Conditional on A let X,Y be independent plants of the same law μ. Fix any reduction coins independently in advance, and let W(x) be the selected physical prefix. Suppose that at every x of a good set S the prefix is horizontal and constant throughout a replanting ball B(x) of μ-mass at least α>0. Suppose a bad event F(y,w) has μ-probability at most p for every deterministic horizontal candidate w. Then

    Pr[X in S and F(X,W(X)) | A, coins] ≤ min(1,p/α).

Indeed on the indicated event, drawing Y in B(X) gives W(Y)=W(X). Hence α times the left side is at most the probability that W(Y) is horizontal and F(X,W(Y)) holds. Conditioning on Y bounds this by p. Add the mass outside S to bound total failure when that mass is known. The same proof uses the whole fiber of W in place of a ball. Thus improved geometric estimates can help only to the extent that they certify probability mass under the appropriate input law.

For iid CBD2 coordinates the largest atom of one coordinate is 6/16. Uniformly in the center x, the number of mismatching coordinates of Y stochastically dominates Binomial(N,5/8), by coupling the independent mismatch indicators with independent uniforms. Each mismatch costs at least one unit of squared displacement. A ball of squared radius at most N/4 consequently has mass at most exp(−2N(5/8−1/4)²)=exp(−9N/32) by Hoeffding's inequality. For example, any radius obtained solely from the transcript certificate with R ≤ 2 asinh(√N/4) falls in this range. An inverse-polynomial fixed-prefix deviation bound cannot pay that exponential loss. This calculation concerns the certified ball; the actual prefix fiber can be larger or disconnected.

## A stable failure in three dimensions

Take m=n=1, q=17, A=3, s=0 and e=1. The lattice is x₁+3x₂≡t modulo 17 and u=(1,0,1). Every vector of squared length at most two has entries in {−1,0,1} and at most two nonzero entries. Its syndrome has absolute value at most four, and can vanish for a nonzero vector only at ±u. Thus every other line has squared minimum at least three. Start a basis with u (primitive by its last coordinate), and choose the other two vectors by lifting a Gauss-reduced basis of the quotient. This is fully BKZ-2 reduced in the shortest-in-every-projected-block convention: u is globally shortest and the second projected basis vector realizes the quotient minimum. The terminal rank-two block kills u. The first line remains the unique global shortest line for every metric at distance less than (log(3/2))/2 by the same comparison. In particular a positive-radius region can consist entirely of failures of terminal nonvanishing even in the plain embedding with a unique ambient shortest line. This example describes a fully reduced basis, without claiming that the canonical slide loop returns it. Finding u in the prefix could of course already recover it by a different criterion.

The remaining estimates are the probability and fiber mass of horizontal stable prefixes, and a fixed-prefix bound for competing quotient lines. Part 9 supplies a length estimate under iid planting and primitivity for horizontal prefixes. Its length estimate does not control a competitor. In the registered fixed-(e,s) statement the randomness is instead over A: replanting does not sample that probability space. A transfer there needs a coupling varying A and a fixed-prefix estimate under that law. The radius and averaging inequalities above yield no certified block-size improvement for that statement.
