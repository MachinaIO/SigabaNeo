## Exact criterion for losing the first plane

Fix a saturated hit plane $P$ at its first-hit draw, with the wholly old priority and tie rule of Part 194. At each subsequent append regard the current plane as horizontal in the new window. Project it off the selected line, saturating its image whenever the image still has rank two. Stop tracing when the rank drops.

**Lemma.** At a given step let $v$ be the incoming lattice's designated shortest vector. The trace dies exactly when $v\in P$ and the horizontal candidate wins. Otherwise the trace remains a saturated hit plane. In particular, if $\lambda_1(M)<\lambda_1(P)$ the trace survives this step for every mark.

Indeed, a rank drop requires the selected line to lie in the real span of $P$. This excludes every nonzero fresh height. For the horizontal candidate, saturation gives $M\cap\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}P=P$. If the rank stays two, orthogonal projection contracts exterior area, and saturating the discrete image can only decrease its area further. At each step saturation leaves the traced real span unchanged. The strict shortest-length test follows immediately. Equality requires the stipulated tie rule.

Consequently if $B$ denotes the specified gated first hit and its exclusions, with just the terminal test removed, and $S$ denotes survival of its chosen plane to draw 406, there is an exact disjoint decomposition
$$
 \Pr(D)=\Pr(B\cap S)+\Pr(B\cap S^c\cap A_{406}).
$$
The second term requires a terminal plane distinct from the rank-two trace. A single terminal kernel applied to a surviving trace accounts for the first term only. The one-step death probability on an incoming history with a live plane is exactly $\mathbf1_{v\in P}$ times the horizontal win fraction of that history. This statement uses the original fresh-mark law before conditioning on future gates or on $A_{406}$. All the draws here belong to the first tour. ## Two horizontal wins and a replacement plane

Use orthonormal axes and put $q=3329$, $x=28$, $y=111/4$, $c=139/5$. The initial old lattice is the orthogonal sum of
$$
 J=\langle 29e_1,\ (29/2)e_1+xe_2,\ (29/2)e_1+ye_3\rangle_{\mathbb Z},
 \qquad 100q\mathbb Ze_4,\qquad cq\mathbb Ze_5 .
$$
Use the subgroup generated by the classes of $100e_4$ and $ce_5$, isomorphic to $(\mathbb Z/q)^2$. Both appends have height one. Choose $100e_4$ on the first draw and the pushed-forward class of $ce_5$ on the second. Each prescribed mark belongs to the actual group at its step. Orthogonal sides of period $100q$ can pad the incoming rank from 5 to 505. This gives width 506 for the two comparisons.

1. **Parent miss.** Project $J$ along $e_1$ to the rectangle with sides $x,y$. Its only nonzero points of length at most 29 are the signed unit multiples of these sides. Every lift of any such point pays at least $(29/2)^2$ in additional squared length. The unique shortest line upstairs has primitive generator $29e_1$. A plane projecting to a line has area at least $29y=804.75$. A plane projecting with index at least two has area at least $2xy$. For index one, use lifts of the rectangular basis. Their first components belong to $29/2+29\mathbb Z$, so their squared area is at least $(xy)^2+(29/2)^2(x^2+y^2)$. Each bound exceeds the cutoff in the corresponding units. For the orthogonal auxiliary directions, a Gauss-reduced basis of any hit plane with minimum at least $y$ has both lengths at most $2a/(\sqrt3\,y)<33$, so those directions cannot enter a hit.
2. **First horizontal win.** At any nonzero height $|h|\le29$ the centered fourth coordinate has magnitude $100|h|$. Thus $29e_1$ wins. Its quotient contains the rectangle $P=\langle xe_2,ye_3\rangle$ of area $777<a$. The first auxiliary block is now generated by $100q e_4$ and $100e_4+e_6$. Its minimum is at least 100 by checking heights of magnitude below 100. The other auxiliary periods exceed 100 as well. The reduced-basis bound above shows that $P$ is the unique saturated hit plane. Every vector of $P$ is old. Its unique shortest line $\mathbb R e_3$ is also the unique shortest line of the retained lattice.
3. **Second horizontal win.** At any nonzero height $|h|\le28$, the centered fifth displacement has magnitude $c|h|$. The shortest fresh length is $w=\sqrt{1+c^2}$ with $y<w<x$. The line $\mathbb R e_3$ wins and $P$ projects to a line. The output nevertheless contains the plane generated by $xe_2$ and $ce_5+e_7$. These are part of a basis of the output, so the plane is saturated. Its area is $xw\in(778.9034,778.9035)$, below $a$. After projecting the terminal lattice off its own unique shortest line, generated by $ce_5+e_7$, the remaining period of that block is $cq/w>28$. The other factors have minimum at least 28 with equality on $xe_2$. Thus terminal internal spacing is **28**, above 26.

There is also a conditional frequency statement for *loss of the trace*. After the specified first mark the two original mark generators still have order $q$ and are independent. In particular, an old multiple of $100e_4$ in the new lattice needs zero sixth coordinate, so it must be a multiple of $100q e_4$. On any second mark with nonzero fifth coefficient modulo $q$, every fresh layer with $0<|h|\le y$ pays at least $c>y$ in that coordinate. Hence the unique old shortest line wins for at least $(q-1)/q$ of the second marks. The replacement-plane assertion above was proved for one specified second mark. These two frequency statements have different numerators.

The sharp Hermite inequalities in ranks five and 506 apply throughout this example. Its first winner has length 29, its first plane has minimum 27.75, and the next winner again has length 27.75. Thus the sharp caps and the first-hit length intervals alone permit immediate loss of the trace. A split on $\lambda_1(M)<\lambda_1(P)$ certifies survival on that branch. On the equality branch, shortest-line incidence can have conditional probability one. The example gives no estimate of the mass of that branch on eligible restart histories and does not impose the raw or dual gates.
