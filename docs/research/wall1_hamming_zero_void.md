# A cubic-grid, determinant-consistent zero-void transition

This example rules out universal zero-preserving multiplicative transport
based only on the q-ary append/project structure, shortest selection and
determinant normalization. It uses q=2 and width16, not the production
q=3329,width506 ensemble. It does not give a production failure bound.

Let H be the4-by15 binary matrix whose columns are every nonzero vector
of F_2^4, with the four coordinate vectors first. Put

    K=Z^15, M={x in Z^15:H x=0 mod2}.

This is a legitimate systematic q-ary raw initial state: M has4 output
and11 input coordinates, K is generated by unit coordinate vectors, and
det M=16. Each of the16 marks has a representative of squared norm at
most1. The nonzero marks have minimum exactly1.

Use the actual appended dimension n=16 in the scale and radius law:

    g=(16/V_16)^(1/16), L^2=(1.1g)^2,
    R^2=L^2-1,   b_u^2=L^2*(s_u^2)^(-1/16)-1.

Since V_16=pi^8/8!, L^2=(121/100)*(16*8!)^(1/8)/pi.
Rational bounds157/50<pi<22/7 certify

    L^2>2,    1/2<L^2*2^(-1/16)-1<1.

In particular incoming void P=0. Numerically R^2 is about1.05040929;
the numerical values are not used in the exact comparisons.

## Actual shortest winners and complete outgoing count

Append a uniform mark u in F_2^4, giving

    W_u={(x,j) in Z^16:H x=j u mod2}.

For u!=0, a column e_a with H e_a=u gives the graph vector(e_a,1)
of squared norm2. No unit vector belongs to W_u: horizontal columns are
nonzero, and the vertical unit has mark u. Thus every shortest winner has
squared norm2. Distinct columns exclude horizontal norm2 vectors, so
every such winner uses exactly the matching coordinate and the vertical
coordinate. Signs and the prescribed lexicographic tie choice do not
change the count below.

Let Q project off that winner w. Any projected hit has an integer lift
z whose parallel coordinate lies in a half-open interval of length||w||,
by subtracting an integer multiple of primitive w. Its squared norm is
at most b_u^2+||w||^2/4<3/2. Since squared norms of integer vectors are
integers, every possible hit therefore has a lift equal to zero or a
signed unit vector. This proves completeness of the finite count.

Zero has mark0. The matching horizontal unit and vertical unit project
to squared norm1/2 and both have mark u. All other horizontal units
project to squared norm1 and are missed. Thus exactly marks0 and u are
covered, and P'_u=14/16=7/8. Uniform old marks push forward uniformly
under projection, so counting these pulled-back marks is legitimate.

For u=0 the unique shortest line is vertical, of length1; projection
preserves M, the radius and full coverage. Consequently for delta=.280,
and both k=1 and k=24,

    E min(1,(P'_U/delta)^k)=15/16, while Phi_k(P)=0.

No finite multiplicative constant can bound this transition by C*Phi_k(P).
This state has positive probability as the specified systematic initial
matrix under the corresponding uniform binary ensemble. It is not claimed
to occur in the production draw14 continuation, whose dimension, modulus,
rank and state law are different. The previous low-dimensional example's
artificial506 exponent and long unmarked-axis issues are absent here.

Run scripts/wall1_hamming_zero_void.py. It certifies the radius comparisons
with rational arithmetic, checks all16 shortest-winner branches by a
complete norm-at-most2 enumeration, and counts every outgoing hit using
the proven lift cutoff. An independent mathematical audit confirmed the
construction and its scope. A production multiplicative proof must use
additional properties of its particular ensemble, or charge exceptional
transitions rather than infer zero preservation from these generic facts.

## Odd-prime versions and the limit of this construction

The failure is not specific to characteristic two. For q odd and rank r,
choose one column from each pair {v,-v} of nonzero vectors in F_q^r.
There are m=(q^r-1)/2 columns; their signed units cover every mark, and
they contain a signed coordinate basis, permitting systematic form by
column permutation and sign changes. K=Z^m and det M=q^r as before.
No two columns agree up to sign, so no horizontal integer vector of squared
norm1 or2 lies in M. Every nonzero fresh mark again has an actual shortest
vector of squared norm2 with a single horizontal unit and a vertical unit.

Two further parameter choices satisfy the same strict radius conditions:

- q=3,r=3,m=13,n=14: V_14=pi^7/7!. Incoming void0; outgoing void8/9
  on each of the26 nonzero branches. Expected capped potential is26/27.
- q=5,r=2,m=12,n=13: V_13=128*pi^6/135135. Incoming void0; outgoing
  void22/25 on each of the24 nonzero branches. Expected capped potential
  is24/25.

The centered-lift cutoff still restricts every hit to zero or a signed
unit. Exactly marks0,U,-U are covered. The script certifies2^n<(L^2)^n
<2^(n+1) with rational bounds on pi, which implies the required upper
radius bound; (4/3)^n>2 also gives the lower bound1/2. All52 additional
branches pass exact enumeration, and an independent audit confirms both
constructions. These examples use their own dimension-dependent exponent.

For q=3329 this particular construction cannot fit width506. Even rank1
needs1664 horizontal coordinates, whereas only505 are available. More
generally, the signed coordinate units in Z^505 can represent at most1010
nonzero marks, fewer than3328. This excludes transferring this unit-cover
construction; it does not exclude other counterexamples or prove the
production transport bound. Projected grids may have additional short
vectors, so the coordinate-unit count is not a general bound for P Z^N.
