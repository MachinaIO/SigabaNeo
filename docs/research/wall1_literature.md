# Average-case reduction and the marked terminal block

A search of 2015–November 2026 papers and preprints located a theorem controlling adaptive HKZ prefixes simultaneously on Haar-random lattices. None of the sources reviewed bounds either failure probability for the **selected slide prefix** in Wall 1. There is also a quantifier obstruction for fully BKZ-reduced bases. If the plant is the unique ambient shortest pair, there always exists a fully reduced basis that places it first. Every proper terminal projection of that basis kills the plant.

## The closest profile theorem

Laarhoven's October 2026 preprint, Proposition 3, considers Haar probability on determinant-one lattices of dimension $d$. Write $P_k$ for the log covolume of the first $k$ basis vectors. With probability $1-\exp(-\Omega(d))$, simultaneously over all quasi-HKZ bases and all prefix ranks, $P_k/(d\log d)$ differs by $o(1)$ from $-(1-k/d)\log(1-k/d)/2$, interpreted continuously at the endpoint. In particular, the log covolume at half rank has leading term $(\log 2)d\log d/4$. Section 4 bounds an exponential moment by summing over admissible primitive first vectors and then their quotients. This handles adaptive shortest-vector selection directly. Theorem 1.1 deduces the exponent $1/(2e)$ for the unpruned enumeration tree at the shortest-vector radius. These are preprint results, reported here without an independent audit of the proofs. [Laarhoven, §§2.1, 4.2–4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15265v1)

Quasi-HKZ requires the projected tail after the first vector to be HKZ-reduced. Finite-width BKZ only requires a shortest vector in each projected window. Even if the profile theorem were transferred to that weaker reduction and to the discrete planted ensemble, its observable would still be a prefix determinant. Neither the divisibility of the projected mark nor its length relative to the shortest competing line occurs in the theorem. Its asymptotic error also supplies no numerical confidence bound at a specified finite rank.

## A fully reduced basis can already contain the answer in its prefix

Suppose $u$ generates the unique shortest line and is primitive. Choose $u$ as the first vector of an HKZ basis by recursively choosing a shortest primitive vector in the remaining quotient and size-reducing the lifts. At each step the shortest vector of the whole remaining quotient also belongs to the block starting at that step. It therefore attains the minimum of that block. The basis is fully BKZ-reduced at every width. For every width $\beta<d$, the prefix of rank $d-\beta$ contains $u$, hence its terminal projection is zero. Consequently, conditional on ambient unique shortness, the probability of terminal success **for every fully reduced basis** is exactly zero. The construction works instance by instance and leaves the output law of a specified algorithm open.

For an integral example in the stated embedding take $m=n=1$, $q=3$, $A=(1)$ and $e=s=0$. The lattice consists of $(x,y,t)$ with $x+y=0\pmod3$, and $u=(0,0,1)$. Its horizontal lattice has minimum $\sqrt2$ since it contains $(1,-1,0)$ and neither horizontal unit vector. The ordered basis
$$
(u,\ (1,-1,0),\ (1,2,0))
$$
has determinant of absolute value 3, the index of the congruence kernel. Its first vector is ambient shortest, its second is shortest in the horizontal quotient, and the only nonzero size-reduction coefficient is $-1/2$. It is HKZ and fully BKZ-2 reduced. The terminal plane projects the plant to zero, even though the ambient shortest pair has already been recovered. This concerns the terminal criterion specifically.

## What the selected-output theorem still requires

Fix $e,s$ in the prescribed shell, draw $A$ uniformly, and let $P$ project off the actual prefix of the specified slide output. Write $Q=P(L)$. Define $F_1$ by $Pu=0$ or $Pu$ nonprimitive in $Q$. On $F_1^c$ define $F_2$ by the existence of $v\in Q\setminus\mathbb R Pu$ with $\|v\|\leq\|Pu\|$. Failure is the disjoint union $F_1\sqcup F_2$. Indeed, for a primitive nonzero mark the only vectors on its line of length at most its own are zero and its two signs. The weak inequality in $F_2$ counts ties. The registered 99% instance requires $\Pr_A(F_1)+\Pr_A(F_2)\leq0.01$ for the specified inputs. No choice of basis is available after sampling in that formulation. The literature above supplies neither summand and hence no smaller certified block size. The existing Part 9 obstruction already rules out discarding the selector and testing all determinant-admissible primitive prefixes at its stated parameters. ## Successive shortest-vector projections and the Part 246 void bound

Part 246 asks for $\Pr(\max_{2\le i\le404} b_i>0.273)\le 3.64\cdot10^{-12}$ under the 400-row marginal at $q=3329$. At draw $i$, $M_i\subseteq K_i$ are the retained rank-505 lattice and the projected integer grid. The fraction $b_i$ counts classes in $K_i/M_i$ whose distance from $M_i$ exceeds $r_i=\sqrt{(1.10g_i)^2S_i^{-2/506}-2}$, with $g_i$ the incoming window scale and $S_i$ its winner-length cap. The fresh class is uniform given the history. The history determines both lattices and the radius. I found no theorem proving this exit bound. Part 230 bounds the next miss in terms of the current profile. Parts 243–245 give independence on specified coordinate sections and identify directions their tests miss. Part 247 gives a positive-probability family with $b_2>0.2974$, so exceptional histories already occur at the first draw in the maximum. [Proof gap, Parts 230 and 243–247](../PROOF_GAP.md) · [Radius and draw-2 example](wall1_void_exit.md)

1. **Successive shortest projections of a Haar lattice.** Laarhoven’s 2026 preprint, Proposition 1 and Lemma 4, treats global HKZ reduction of a single Haar lattice. The lemma bounds an exponential moment of the cumulative shortfall of the normalized shortest lengths, taking the maximum over all shortest-vector tie choices. Its proof bounds that maximum by a sum over primitive vector pairs in a ball. The normalized quotient has Haar measure inside this averaging formula. This gives a way to account for adaptive choices without assuming fresh Haar samples after each comparison. Part 246 would require an analogous estimate for the *pair* consisting of the projected grid and its sublattice, with a fresh column appended at each step. The HKZ moment controls lengths in decreasing rank. It gives no estimate for the fraction of missed cosets at a fixed window width. [Laarhoven, Proposition 1 and §4.2, especially (4.7)–(4.8)](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15265v1)

2. **Shapes of successive orthogonal quotients.** Horesh and Karasik, Theorems 1.1–1.2, count primitive flags in $\mathbb Z^N$ by height with restrictions on their directions and quotient shapes. As the height cutoff grows, the successive quotient shapes have the product invariant distribution for the stated regular test sets. The final factor can describe the shape of the projected integer grid for a flag counted this way. Applying it here would require weights for the graph and the shortest-vector comparisons selecting each flag. Even the grid shape alone leaves the placement of $M_i$ inside $K_i$ undetermined. [Horesh–Karasik, *Counting flags of primitive lattices*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.12724)

3. **Uncovered fraction for a Haar lattice.** Rogers’ estimate, as stated by Ordentlich, Regev and Weiss in Theorem 2.3 and Corollary 2.4, is the closest match to the observable. For a fixed Borel set of volume $V\le\eta_d=\tfrac d4\log(27/16)-3\log d$ and a Haar unimodular lattice $L$, the probability that the uncovered fraction of $\mathbb R^d/L$ exceeds $\rho$ is at most $(e^{-V}+c_{\rm Rog}e^{-\eta_d})/\rho$. At $d=505$, $\eta_d=47.3864\ldots$. The theorem measures translates by continuous torus volume. A discrete comparison is possible if the grid has covering radius $u<r_i$: the equal-volume Voronoi cells surrounding missed grid classes lie in the continuous void at radius $r_i-u$, by the triangle inequality. Using Rogers after this radius loss would still require control of the selected lattice law, the normalized test volume and the error constant. [Ordentlich–Regev–Weiss, *New bounds on the density of lattice coverings*, §2.2](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~barakw/papers/ORW_for_arxiv.pdf)

4. **Full covering of finite-field cosets.** Corollary 2.9(ii) of the same paper works at finite $q$. If a fixed $A\subseteq\mathbb F_q^h$ has density at least $1-\delta e^{-2h/q}$, a uniform two-dimensional subspace has every translate meeting $A$ with probability at least $1-\delta$. Two fresh independent uniform marks span such a plane conditional on linear independence. This can test a section where the hit set is fixed before revealing both marks. Geometric applicability requires controlling the lengths of the representatives used for the intersections. An arbitrary pair of coefficients in the plane can cost more squared height than the radius allows. Part 230 certifies hits only at admissible offsets. [Ordentlich–Regev–Weiss, Corollary 2.9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.00340) · [Two-mark inequality (2)](wall1_inductive_typicality.md)

5. **Random Construction A at a specified modulus.** Ordentlich, Regev and Weiss, Corollary 1.5, give a finite-dimensional smooth covering result for a Euclidean ball and a random Construction-A lattice. On the good event, every translate has a positive count when the relative error $\varepsilon<1$, so every finite subgroup of marks is covered as well. The corollary prescribes the code dimension and ball volume, and requires $p\ge1024d^2/(\varepsilon\delta)^2$ for failure probability $\delta$. At $d=505$ even $1024d^2$ exceeds $2.61\cdot10^8$. Thus $p=3329$ falls outside the theorem before accounting for selection. [Ordentlich–Regev–Weiss, *Bounds on the density of smooth lattice coverings*, Corollary 1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.04644) · [Journal version](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11854-025-0367-2)

6. **Rigorous block-reduction output.** Hanrot, Pujol and Stehlé, Theorem 1, give a deterministic bound on the first returned vector of their modified BKZ algorithm after a prescribed polynomial number of block-oracle calls. Their Gram–Schmidt analysis can supply length bounds when its algorithm and stopping rule apply. The exit event concerns intermediate lattices in a single forward pass and counts distinct cosets outside a ball. The output theorem supplies neither their distribution nor such a count. [Hanrot–Pujol–Stehlé, *Analyzing Blockwise Lattice Algorithms using Dynamical Systems*, Theorem 1](https://www.xpujol.net/publications/bkz.pdf)

For scale, splitting the allowance equally over the 403 possible first exits gives $3.64\cdot10^{-12}/403\approx9.03226\cdot10^{-15}$ per draw. It suffices to bound the **joint** mass of reaching each draw without an earlier exit and finding $b_i>0.273$ there. This formulation permits the rare histories of Part 247 and shows where a comparison theorem would need a finite error estimate.
