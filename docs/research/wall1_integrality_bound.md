# Frozen Gram determinants and short quotient tests

**Partial bound.** In the width-506 restart, outside a set of joint probability below $10^{-748}$, the primitive integer section of the frozen space has squared covolume at least $2^{99}$ at every draw from 395 through 406. The terminal mark-group rank is then at least 115. The exceptional set contributes less than $10^{-464}$ to the eleven-draw single-ball mean, less than $10^{-93}$ to the line-plus-plane mean, and less than $10^{-748}$ to the horizontal terminal-area probability. These estimates include histories passing both gates.

On the complementary set, a direct Gaussian count that keeps the **entire mod-3329 dual residue code on a coordinate chart**, the dual minimum gate and the determinant, but drops the remaining integral lifting constraints, gives a line-count majorant **larger than $10^{30}$** at every eligible vertical endpoint and every Gaussian scale. This is a lower bound on a particular upper estimate. The actual test mean on this set is unknown. The terminal-area comparison remains open as well. ## Which Gram determinant is integral?

After $f$ freezes write $N=505+f$, $\sigma=[I_{400}\ A]$ over $\mathbb F_q$, $q=3329$, and $L=\ker(\sigma:\mathbb Z^N\to\mathbb F_q^{400})$. For a rational subspace $F$, let $S_F=F\cap\mathbb Z^N$, $h_F=\det S_F$, $p_F=h_F^2$, and $d_F=400-\dim\sigma(S_F)$. Use $P$ for projection orthogonal to $F$. The identities below also apply when additional lines or planes are projected out in a fixed raw space.

$$
 K=P\mathbb Z^N,\qquad qK\subseteq M=PL\subseteq K,\qquad
 [K:M]=q^{d_F},\qquad \det M=q^{d_F}/h_F.                 \tag{1}
$$

Indeed, projecting identifies the quotient with the syndrome space modulo $\sigma(S_F)$. Completing a basis of the primitive section to a unimodular basis gives $\det K=1/h_F$. The integer $p_F$ is the sum of squares of the maximal minors of a basis matrix of $S_F$. The projected periods $qPe_j$ span the retained real space. Together they generate $qK$, which can have proper index in $M$. [First-tour law](wall1_first_tour.md) · [Projected section formula](wall1_hybrid_tail.md)

If $U\supset F$ is rational, the saturated lattice $M\cap(U\cap F^\perp)$ has determinant $c$ satisfying
$$
               c^2=q^{2(d_F-d_U)}\,p_U/p_F.                 \tag{2}
$$
Projecting off this section leaves the projection of $L$ orthogonal to $U$, so (2) follows by dividing the two determinants in (1). The exponent difference is between zero and $\dim U-\dim F$ because $S_F$ is primitive in $S_U$. Thus $p_F$ is a **history-dependent common denominator** for primal squared lengths and squared areas. An individual fraction in (2) can reduce. For example, in the width-two graph $x\equiv 2y\pmod{3329}$, the unique shortest line is generated by $(2,1)$. At heights of magnitude one and two the centered displacements are twice the height, and all larger heights exceed $\sqrt5$. The quotient has squared primitive length $3329^2/5$. Even multiplying it by $3329^2$ leaves a denominator of five. This example verifies the units in (2).

## A high-probability lower bound on the frozen Gram determinant

**Coordinate-minor lemma.** Suppose every $f$-coordinate restriction of $\sigma$ has rank at least $s_f$. For every rational $f$-space $F$, simultaneously,
$$
                   p_F\ge 2^{\max(0,s_f+d_F-400)}.           \tag{3}
$$
To see this, take an $N$-by-$f$ integer basis matrix $X$ of $S_F$. Primitivity gives a set $I$ of $f$ rows independent modulo $q$. View $\bar X$ over the field as a graph over these coordinates. If its complementary row block has rank $u$, the ranks of $\sigma\bar X$ and $\sigma_I\bar X_I$ differ by at most $u$. Hence $u\ge s_f-(400-d_F)$. Choose $u$ independent complementary rows. Every subset of them can be completed to a basis using only rows of $I$. The resulting $2^u$ distinct minors are nonzero modulo $q$, so each contributes at least one to the integer sum $p_F$.

Take $k_f=\min(f,400)-17$, $s_f=k_f+1$. For a fixed set of $f$ coordinates, let $b$ be the number of horizontal coordinates. If $b>k_f$, rank at most $k_f$ is impossible. Otherwise, choose a $k_f$-space containing those $b$ independent columns. There are at most $2q^{(k_f-b)(400-k_f)}$ choices by the finite Grassmannian product formula. Its correction factor is bounded by $\prod_{j\ge1}(1-q^{-j})^{-1}\le(1-1/(q-1))^{-1}<2$. Each of the remaining $f-b$ independent uniform columns lands in the chosen space with probability $q^{k_f-400}$. Therefore the probability of any failed coordinate-rank condition across the twelve prefixes is at most
$$
      U=2\sum_{f=395}^{406}\binom{505+f}{f}
                   q^{-(400-k_f)(f-k_f)} <10^{-748}.         \tag{4}
$$
Call this exception $\mathcal X$. The union is taken in the original random graph before conditioning. It permits adaptive choices of all coordinate sets and frozen spaces. An estimate conditional on passing an event of small probability would require its denominator.

Let $L_f$ be the Part 193 lower bound on the log retained determinant. By (1) and (3), off $\mathcal X$ the actual integer $d=d_F$ must satisfy
$$
       d\log q\ \ge\ L_f+\tfrac12\max(0,s_f+d-400)\log2 .
$$
The smallest permitted integers and the resulting lower exponents of $p_F$ are as follows. [Determinant recurrence](wall1_z2_vertical.md#audit)

**Effect on the two means.** Let $s>30.5$ be the vertical winner, $r$ the shortest retained length, $Q$ its line quotient, $a=1352/\sqrt3$, $B=52/\sqrt3$, and $\alpha=\sqrt3/2$. Rounding a lift along a shortest line gives a quotient minimum at least $\alpha$ times that line's length. Applying it twice gives $\lambda_1(Q)\ge\alpha r\ge\alpha^2s>22.875=\ell$. Packing disjoint balls of radius $\ell/2$ bounds the number of points through $t$ by $(1+2t/\ell)^{504}$. This also bounds the unoriented primitive count. Each saturated plane of area at most $a$ has a Gauss-reduced basis with both lengths at most $2a/(\sqrt3\,\ell)$, since the first length is at least $\ell$ and the sine of the angle is at least $\sqrt3/2$. Distinct planes have disjoint sets of basis pairs. Hence the single-ball count is below $10^{282}$ and the plane count is below $10^{654}$. The smaller ball has $a/r<B$ on the band.

Multiplying the explicit packing expressions by $11U$ gives the two exceptional mean bounds in the opening paragraph. Restricting to first hits, the two gates, or a terminal hit can only decrease these nonnegative joint integrals. The horizontal terminal residual uses an indicator, so its contribution on $\mathcal X$ is bounded by $U$, including a terminal hit after loss of the original plane. This also bounds the corresponding contributions from violating the height or group-rank thresholds in the table.

## Quantifying the loss from counting the scaled integral dual by residues

Use $T$ for the frozen span enlarged by the designated shortest line, $m=504$, and $\Delta=\det Q$. On an eligible endpoint off $\mathcal X$, (2) gives
$$
       h_T\ge (r/q)h_F>6.31\cdot10^{12}.
$$
In fact the exponent drop in (2) is at most one. Also
$$
    \Delta=\frac{V_{506}g^{506}}{sr}
       <\frac{V_{506}(R/0.945)^{506}}{30.5^2\alpha}
       <10^{405.453},\qquad R=2^{1/4}\sqrt a .
$$
Here $g$ is the incoming GH length and $s\le R$. The dual gate gives $\lambda_1(Q^*)\ge\rho=R_0(f)$, since each quotient dual is a section of the raw dual. Its stated floor satisfies $0<\rho<0.201$ on these draws. [Dual gate](wall1_doubly_gated.md)

Choose $m$ coordinate rows whose restriction to the primitive section $K^*=T^\perp\cap\mathbb Z^N$ has full rank modulo $q$, with $K=P_{T^\perp}\mathbb Z^N$. Denote this coordinate map by $J$. We have $qK^*\subseteq qQ^*\subseteq K^*$. Reduction of $J(qQ^*)$ gives a code $C\subseteq\mathbb F_q^m$ of dimension $d_T$, since $[Q^*:K^*]=[K:Q]=q^{d_T}$ and the chosen rows preserve rank modulo $q$. The lattice
$$
         Y_C=\{z/q:z\in\mathbb Z^m,\ z\bmod q\in C\}
$$
contains $JQ^*$ and has determinant $q^{-d_T}$. It retains every residue constraint on these coordinates. It fills in all integer lifts and uses only the norm of the chosen coordinates and the gate floor.

There are two exact factors behind this relaxation. Write $b=\det(JK^*)$, the absolute integer minor of the chosen rows in a basis of $K^*$. The index $[Y_C:JQ^*]=b$ follows by dividing both coordinate covolumes, using $[JQ^*:JK^*]=q^{d_T}$. The volume contraction of $J$ on the retained space is $b/h_T$, since $\det K^*=h_T$ by (1) and duality. The lift index times the reciprocal volume contraction is exactly $h_T$ for every admissible choice of rows.

For a Gaussian scale $w>0$, the resulting ball-count upper bound for primitive lines is
$$
 \mathcal B_C(t,w)=\tfrac12 e^{\pi t^2/w^2}
       \left[\frac{w^m}{\Delta}U_C(w)-1\right],\qquad
 U_C(w)=1+\sum_{z\in Y_C\setminus\{0\}}
       e^{-\pi w^2\max(\rho^2,\|z\|^2)}.                     \tag{5}
$$
Indeed Poisson summation on $Q$ gives its Gaussian mass as $w^m/\Delta$ times the dual mass at inverse scale. Injecting $Q^*$ by $J$ and shortening each squared norm to the displayed maximum bounds that dual mass above. Subtract the primal origin and divide by two for signs. [Gaussian Poisson formula, Lemma 13 and Example 5](https://cims.nyu.edu/~regev/teaching/lattices_fall_2004/ln/FourierTransform.pdf)

**Loss for this method.** Applying Poisson in the opposite direction to $Y_C$, keeping its dual origin, gives
$$
   \frac{w^m}{\Delta}U_C(w)
    \ \ge\ \max\left(\frac{w^m}{\Delta},\
              e^{-\pi w^2\rho^2}\frac{q^{d_T}}{\Delta}\right)
    =\max\left(\frac{w^m}{\Delta},\ e^{-\pi w^2\rho^2} h_T\right).       \tag{6}
$$
The second term uses $\max(x,y)\le x+y$, including the harmless origin inequality. This lower estimate holds for the *exact* code in (5) for each eligible mark. For $w\le6.7$, substitute $h_T>6.31\cdot10^{12}$ and $\rho<0.201$ in the second branch. For $w\ge6.7$, use the first branch. At either vertical radius $t=B$ or $t=a/r$, one has $t\ge26$. The function $e^{\pi 26^2/w^2}w^{504}$ increases above 6.7. So does $e^{\pi 26^2/w^2}(w^{504}/\Delta-1)$ there. Evaluating the two lower bounds at 6.7 gives $\mathcal B_C(t,w)>10^{30}$ for every scale. Even the line term alone incurs this loss.

For a terminal area hit in rank 505, binary reduction gives a line through $B$. Applying (5) to the retained lattice itself uses $m=505, T=F$. In the terminal determinant range $\det M\le10^{406}$, which includes the Part 192 floor, (6) with $h_F\ge2^{99/2}$ and the same split gives $\mathcal B_C(B,w)>10^{38}$ off $\mathcal X$. This is a loss of the single-line cover of terminal area in this determinant range. It does not evaluate the rank-two terminal probability elsewhere.

## What remains in the finite law

For each fixed prefix one can sum the true test, or (5), over the fresh marks and divide by $|H|$ **before selection**. Each summand carries its band indicator and its gate or continuation weight in $[0,1]$. The bounds above pay for the small-height or low-rank exception in that joint sum. On regular prefixes (5) would still require an independent estimate of eligible-mark mass. Knowing the modulus, residue code and group size alone in this calculation loses the integer lifting index and the norm contributions of the discarded coordinates. A count using the entire embedded dual Gram form could retain both.

There is a separate absolute bound on part of the regular vertical sector. The Part 193 projected-grid estimate bounds the conditional expected number of *fresh positive-height candidates* of length at most $R$ by $1.422743743939(R/g)^{506}$ per incoming prefix. A vertical hit requires such a candidate. Summing eleven draws, all residual hits with incoming $g\ge35.15$ cost less than $6.575\cdot10^{-12}$; the band ends at $R/0.945<35.159$. This uses the fresh-mark law before either selection or future gates. It bounds hit probability; the two Part 209 means count multiplicities. Paying for this slice leaves more than $5.8275\cdot10^{-11}$ of the vertical budget for the smaller incoming scales. [Positive-height grid estimate](wall1_z2_vertical.md#charging-the-parts-removed-in-1)

For the event indicators on the unresolved prefixes the unit bound still exceeds $6.485\cdot10^{-11}$ and $7.1275\cdot10^{-11}$ by factors above $1.5420\cdot10^{10}$ and $1.4030\cdot10^{10}$, respectively. Neither the permitted vertical factors 2094.97 and 6,947,681 nor the terminal-area factor $3329^{13.4958}$ is established. The lower bounds on (5) assign no positive probability to a hit and furnish no reachable doubly gated counterexample. ```python

hF = iv.sqrt(I(2)**99)

## Audit

**Verdict.** All four claims hold for the unconditioned width-506 restart with the Part 193 and Part 209 event restrictions. The high-$g$ result bounds joint hit probability. The $10^{30}$ result bounds expression (5) from below. No numerical correction is needed.

**Frozen section and terminal group.** For a fixed coordinate set, the identity columns span a fixed $b$-space and the graph columns are independent uniform vectors. A union over containing $k_f$-spaces, coordinate sets and raw prefixes therefore applies simultaneously to the selected frozen spaces. Primitivity makes $S_F/qS_F$ inject into the ambient residue space, and the $2^u$ minors in (3) are distinct because their sets of complementary rows differ. An exact-rational evaluation gives $-749.441<\log_{10}U<-749.440$. Independently iterating the outgoing covolume floor as $D_f^*=\max(D_{f-1}^*/q,(D_{f-1}^*)^{505/506}/\sqrt{56.2142})$ from $D_0^*=q^{400}$ and comparing it with $q^d/2^{\max(0,s_f+d-400)/2}$ reproduces both rows of the table. Every raw syndrome has a horizontal representative, so the projected horizontal marks generate the terminal group $K/M$. Its dimension over $\mathbb F_q$ is $d_F$, giving at least 115 after draw 406.

**Exceptional contributions.** Two successive shortest-line projections give the packing separation $\alpha^2s>22.875$. Gauss reduction represents each saturated plane by an ordered pair within the stated length bound, and a given pair spans only one plane. Using the exact $U$, the base-ten logarithms of the eleven-draw packing bounds on the exceptional single-ball and combined means lie in $(-466.515,-466.514)$ and $(-95.342,-95.341)$, respectively. The horizontal terminal indicator costs at most $U$, including paths with a replacement plane. These are joint bounds even when a gate uses later columns.

**Gaussian estimate.** Part 209 restricts the eligible designated line to $r\le B$ even when the current plane outcome is left untested. This gives $a/r\ge a/B=26$ at the smaller radius. The coordinate chart injects $K^*/qK^*$ modulo $q$, so the residue image has dimension $d_T$. Its full lift has determinant $q^{-d_T}$, and $q^{d_T}/\Delta=h_T$. These identities justify the Poisson lower bound in (6) for each chart separately. At $w=6.7$ and $t=26$ the conservative lower expressions in the two branches have base-ten logarithms greater than $30.570$ and $31.133$, respectively. Below the split the exponential prefactor and the positive second-branch bracket both increase as $w$ decreases. Above the split, $504w^2>2\pi\,26^2$ makes the first-branch product increase, including the subtracted origin. The conclusion holds at every scale on each eligible endpoint outside $\mathcal X$ with the dual gate. It gives no lower bound on an actual count, mark mass, or hit probability. [Part 209, Lemma 2 and test restrictions](wall1_zrem_flag_moment.md#reference-measures-and-the-size-of-the-missing-comparison)

**Vertical hits at $g\ge35.15$.** Fix the raw history immediately before a draw $i\in\{396,\ldots,406\}$. Its incoming determinant $D=V_{506}g^{506}$ is fixed. The unread column induces a uniform mark in $H=K/M$. Every positive-height point $(y,k)$ of $K\times\mathbb Z$ in the radius-$R$ ball has $1\le k\le33<q$, so $kU=[y]$ singles out exactly one mark. The expected number of such candidates is consequently their grid count divided by $|H|$. This rank-506 grid is the projection of the cubic grid onto $F^\perp\times\mathbb R$ in the used coordinates plus the fresh height coordinate. Its covolume is $D/|H|$, and its positive-height candidates are disjoint from their negatives. These are the hypotheses of the projected-ball estimate. The theta factor in every radial bin decreases with physical radius. An independent outward interval sum of all 4096 bins gave constants in $(1.42274374393791,1.42274374393793)$ at $B$ and $(1.42274374393700,1.42274374393702)$ at $R$.

A residual vertical hit supplies at least one such candidate because its selected length is at most $R$. Dropping selection, prior survival and the gate and terminal restrictions therefore bounds the joint contribution on any prefix with $g\ge35.15$ by $1.422743743939(R/35.15)^{506}$. Integrating over the original prefix law and summing eleven draws gives a bound in $(6.57448019427,6.57448019428)\cdot10^{-12}$. This argument also covers prefixes in $\mathcal X$ and uses no independence across draws. The remaining allowance exceeds $5.82755\cdot10^{-11}$. Bounding the smaller-$g$ hits and the Part 209 multiplicity means remains open. [Part 193, projected-grid charge](wall1_z2_vertical.md#charging-the-parts-removed-in-1)
