# Five joint correction bounds for the sixth-moment route

Part 475 supplies a candidate set with exact nonzero-mark mean mu=750000
through the full stated freeze range. This note specializes the existing
factorial-polynomial transfer to its sixth central moment. It isolates five
one-sided joint corrections, without dividing by a rare selection probability.
The proposed correction bounds are not yet proved for production histories.

## A finite polynomial identity

For j>=0 define the centered factorial polynomial

    H_j(z;mu)=sum_{l=0}^j binom(j,l)(-mu)^(j-l)(z)_l.

Its generating function is exp(-mu*t)*(1+t)^z. Let E denote the old-history
gate of Part 475 together with U!=0, put a=Pr(E), and set

    C_j=E[1_E H_j(Z;mu)].

The exact conditional mean already established gives C_0=a and C_1=0.
This remains true after arbitrary old-history restrictions, with the same
candidate normalization. Conditioning further on the next selected winner
would generally invalidate that mean identity.

The following identity holds pointwise:

    (z-mu)^6=sum_{j=0}^6 w_j(mu) H_j(z;mu),

where

    w_0=15mu^3+25mu^2+mu,
    w_1=105mu^2+56mu+1,
    w_2=45mu^2+180mu+31,
    w_3=110mu+90,
    w_4=15mu+65,
    w_5=15,  w_6=1.

It can be derived from the finite difference transfer used in
[the factorial-cumulant calculation](wall1_factorial_cumulants.md):
w_j=E_Poisson(mu)[Delta^j(X-mu)^6]/j!. This is an algebraic way to obtain
the coefficients, not an assumption that the actual Z is Poisson.
All coefficients are nonnegative. Therefore the actual joint sixth moment is

    T=a*(15mu^3+25mu^2+mu)
      +(45mu^2+180mu+31)C_2+(110mu+90)C_3
      +(15mu+65)C_4+15C_5+C_6.                          (1)

In particular negative centered-factorial corrections remain favorable.
Bounding their absolute values is sufficient but unnecessary.

## Explicit sufficient targets

One sufficient set of ONE-SIDED joint bounds is

    C_2 <= 4a*mu,
    C_3 <= a*mu^2,
    C_4 <= a*(48mu^2+mu),
    C_5 <= a*(40mu^2+mu),
    C_6 <= a*(960mu^3+70mu^2+mu).                        (2)

Substitution gives exactly

    T <= a*(1985mu^3+4640mu^2+206mu)
       <1986a*mu^3 <2000a*mu^3,          mu=750000, a>0.

For a=0 all terms vanish. If the stronger C_3<=a*mu is available, the
corresponding expression is a*(1875mu^3+4660mu^2+296mu)<1876a*mu^3.
A bound on the single weighted combination in (1) can replace all five
separate targets. Neither form introduces a lower bound on a.

For comparison, when a>0 sufficient conditions on the factorial cumulants
of the actual conditional law Z|E are

    |kappa_f,2|<=4mu,    |kappa_f,j|<=mu for j=3,...,6.

The centered factorial moments divided by a are generated by
exp(sum_{j>=2} kappa_f,j*t^j/j!), because the first cumulant is exactly mu.
For example C_4/a=kappa_f,4+3*kappa_f,2^2 and
C_6/a=kappa_f,6+15*kappa_f,4*kappa_f,2+10*kappa_f,3^2+15*kappa_f,2^3.
These imply the stronger bounds above. They are alternative unproved
conditions, not properties inferred from the first moment or minimum gate.

With (2) at each of 390 transitions, Part 475's implication would give
390*2000/mu^3=1.848888...e-12. This arithmetic is verified exactly.
It is a conditional long-winner bound, not a proved production probability
and not automatically a bound for the capped empty-coset exit event.

## The minimum gate uses quotient exclusions

There are two distinct old-state exclusion lists in a fixed proposed flag:

- Full graph membership exclusions encode the chronological winner tests.
- Quotient-zero exclusions encode the absence of nonzero projected points
  of norm strictly less than c in M, hence lambda_1(M)>=c.

The second condition is label zero in K/M, not full syndrome zero. The
finite short-point list can be represented by fixed raw quotient equations;
the actual full and quotient event families must not be interchanged.
For full forbidden list B_F, quotient forbidden list B_Q and additional
positive quotient constraints C, the exact old gated incidence is

    Q_E(C)=sum_{I subset B_F, J subset B_Q}(-1)^(|I|+|J|)
                    P(I; J union C),                    (3)

with the base fixed-image/kernel stratum retained in P. The one-fresh-mark
elimination of Part 472 then expresses all factorial moments through order
six using these old incidences. Combining them linearly gives the C_j in (1).

A complete grouped expansion keeps a pair (full span, quotient span) as its
state, retaining signed integer coefficients. A tagged forbidden vector
extends only its own span. Discard a state only when its joint event has
zero probability, or its coefficient cancels exactly. No division by a
selected or surviving mass is performed.

The individual H_j are signed polynomials. Even Bonferroni truncation is
therefore NOT automatically an upper bound for C_j. To use the existing
nonnegative-observable truncation argument, retain (Z-mu)^6 as a whole,
or prove suitable one-sided bounds for the signed combinations separately.
The positivity of the w_j does not change this requirement.

## Verification and remaining work

`scripts/wall1_sixth_centered_factorial.py` derives all coefficients by exact
integer polynomial arithmetic, and verifies 130 pointwise identities, 20
arbitrary selected-law transfers, and 15 exact-mean cases. It also checks
both sufficient-cap substitutions and the conditional budget comparison.
Independent mathematical review approved the identity, joint normalization,
the stronger-cap substitution and the factorial-cumulant alternative. The
relaxed C_3 cap is separately verified by exact coefficient substitution.

`scripts/wall1_mixed_exclusion_gates.py` checks all prefixes of a tagged
full/quotient exclusion example, 160 additional joint incidences and 36
pointwise expansions. Full-only A!=0 has mass 8/9, while quotient-only
A_Q!=0 has mass 2/3; replacing one by the other is detected. The combined
example has mass 5/9. This is a finite algebraic fixture, not the production
old-minimum geometry. The parent reran both checkers.

The next missing quantitative result is an actual reached-history upper
bound for the weighted correction in (1), or sufficient separate bounds
such as (2), with the complete gates in (3). The formulas and finite tests
do not evaluate those production sums. The full BKZ proof remains open.
