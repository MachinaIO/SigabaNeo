# Wall 1 literature update, 22 November 2026

**Finding.** I found a rigorous bulk Gram–Schmidt profile for Haar quasi-HKZ reduction, an average-case all-coordinate bound for slide reduction on a random knapsack construction, and effective equidistribution for the precise Smith type of the unplanted q-ary lattice. None gives a 99% lower bound for the minimum of the quotient selected by width-406 BKZ or slide at rank 1023. The closest result about actual finite-width outputs is Joux–Węgrzycki, Appendix A. Its proof identifies exactly where the input ensemble enters.

## Which probability needs bounding?

Write $d=1023$, $m=511$, $q=3329$, $D=q^m$, $g_i=\|b_i^*\|$ in forward order, and $V_j=\prod_{i\le j}g_i$. After projecting off the first $k$ rows, the terminal lattice $T$ has determinant $D/V_k$. If the entire remaining block is SVP-reduced at its first row, $g_{k+1}=\lambda_1(T)$. In general $g_{k+1}$ is an **upper** bound on that minimum. Even a lower bound on the terminal geometric mean $(D/V_k)^{1/(d-k)}$ leaves room for a short direction.

Part 47 reduces the decoupled variant to a terminal minimum on the unplanted lattice and a sum of prefix Babai failure bounds. The ordinary planted reducer has a different output law and a capture-or-terminal success criterion. Part 49 already certifies prefix lifting at about width 915 and improves the width-1000 terminal radius to 80.21 without improving the crossing width. The dimension-647, 99% certificate uses puncturing and an exact SVP call. None of those numbers is an observation about a typical BKZ profile. ## Reduction outputs

**Laarhoven, October 2026.** Proposition 3, equation (4.10), gives more than the uniform prefix estimate in the earlier survey. For Haar probability on unimodular lattices, with probability $1-e^{-\Omega(d)}$, simultaneously for every quasi-HKZ basis, for fixed $0<\varepsilon<1$ and $x_i=(i-1)/d$,
$$
\frac1d\sum_{x_i\le1-\varepsilon}
\left|\frac{\log g_i}{\log d}-\tfrac12(1+\log(1-x_i))\right|=o(1).
$$
Quasi-HKZ includes size reduction, an HKZ projected tail, and the first adjacent condition in (2.4). On this event only $o(d)$ bulk indices can miss the curve by any fixed positive tolerance in these normalized units. This gives convergence for a uniformly sampled bulk index. A prescribed index, including $d-\beta+1$ with $\beta/d$ bounded away from zero, could always be exceptional. Equation (4.9) does give the geometric mean on any interval of macroscopic length by differencing its endpoints. Differencing adjacent endpoints loses the precision. Section 4.2 sums a nonnegative exponential moment over all eligible primitive vectors before disintegrating the Haar integral into quotients. It does **not** assume that a shortest-vector-selected quotient is independently Haar. Formalization could start with the deterministic bulk-index corollary. The probabilistic input would require the primitive-vector integration formula and an effective analogue for the actual block selector. [Proposition 3 and proof](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15265v1)

**The September papers address different random objects.** For the thin shell specified by Laarhoven at $R=cR^*$, fixed $c>1$, Lemma 4.10 gives shell population asymptotic to shell volume with probability tending to one on Haar input. The attached Lean work assumes the stated classical integration formulas and probability model. Doulgerakis–Laarhoven obtain exact SVP on Haar input with joint success $1-o(1)$ in time $2^{0.2924\ldots d+o(d)}$ and space $2^{0.2075\ldots d+o(d)}$ using their modified sieve. These results concern a complete ambient shell and a particular randomized SVP algorithm, respectively. Applying either inside a BKZ tour requires a theorem about the block presented to that call. I found no later preprint through the search date that makes this transfer. [Shell theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13635v1) · [Random-lattice sieve](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37482)

**Joux–Węgrzycki, STACS 2026.** Appendix A, Lemma 10, proves a simultaneous bound on every GS length of the basis produced by the Gama–Nguyen sliding algorithm on the paper's modular subset-sum lattice (3). For fixed reduction parameter $Y>1$ and the associated block width $w$, with probability at least $1-2^{-\Omega(n\log n)}$,
$$
\max_i g_i\le C_Y Y^{(n-1)/2}\det(L)^{1/n}.
$$
Theorem 3 draws the independent weights uniformly up to $\lceil\sqrt\Gamma\rceil$, where $\Gamma=Y^{n^2}2^{\Theta(n\log n)}$. A single block reduction suffices to construct a tester for all targets. The proof uses a lattice-invariant primal and dual short-vector exclusion (Claim 7), giving $g_n\le\det(L)^{1/n}\le g_1$, then a deterministic decline bound $g_i\ge\gamma_{\rm LLL}^{-2w}Y^{j-i}g_j$ for $j<i$ (Claim 12). Thus there is a lower envelope from $j=1$, but the displayed all-coordinate average-case theorem is an **upper** envelope. The modulus regime, lattice construction, and fixed-width constants prevent numerical substitution here. For formalization, derive the decline estimate directly by chaining the aligned block-start inequalities (5) and using LLL for the two end pieces. The displayed intermediate exponents in the online proof of Claim 12 disagree in sign with its statement. The factor $\gamma_{\rm LLL}^{-2w}$ also makes the distinction between a fixed and a large concrete width important. Parts 48–49 already exploit primal/dual endpoint propagation on the target ensemble. [Theorems 1 and 3, Claims 7 and 11–12, Lemma 10](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol364-stacs2026/html/LIPIcs.STACS.2026.57/LIPIcs.STACS.2026.57.html)

**Li–Nguyen and Hanrot–Pujol–Stehlé.** Li–Nguyen, Theorem 2, bounds every normalized prefix determinant $V_i/D^{i/d}$ after a specified number of GBKZ tours from a $3/4$-LLL basis. One specialization uses exact oracles and relaxation factors $\delta=\eta=1$ with $d>\beta\ge2$ satisfying the theorem's Hermite-constant constraint. For fixed accuracy the tour bound is $O((d/\beta)^2\log d)$. If $U_i$ is their explicit upper bound, $g_d\ge D^{1/d}/U_{d-1}$ and the last $d-i$ lengths have geometric mean at least $D^{1/d}U_i^{-1/(d-i)}$. Two upper bounds for consecutive prefixes do not bound their ratio from below. Theorem 4 of the same paper proves $|\lambda_1/h(d)-1|\le(\log\log d)/d$ with probability $1-o(1)$ for a fresh unit-volume random lattice, where the radius $h(d)$ encloses volume one. Theorem 4 does not supply the oracle-input distribution required to combine those statements inside a tour. HPS obtains a first-vector Hermite bound after polynomially many exact block calls for its BKZ variant. Their affine inequalities and Li–Nguyen's Rankin-profile inequalities are candidates for finite-dimensional formalization given the specified update semantics. Neither paper proves stochastic convergence of the actual GS profile to a straight line. [Li–Nguyen, Theorems 2 and 4](https://pure.royalholloway.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/39276060/BKZ2020.10.10.pdf) · [Hanrot–Pujol–Stehlé](https://perso.ens-lyon.fr/damien.stehle/downloads/bkz.pdf)

## Primal uSVP success

**A rigorous decoding theorem.** Luzzi–Stehlé–Ling, Theorem 2 and Lemma 2, consider a base lattice $M$, target at distance $r$ from it, embedding height $t$, and an oracle for $\gamma$-uSVP. For $0<t<\lambda_1(M)/\gamma$, correct decoding is guaranteed if
$$r^2<t\lambda_1(M)/\gamma-t^2.$$
Choosing $t=\lambda_1(M)/(2\gamma)$ gives radius $\lambda_1(M)/(2\gamma)$. For the Part 47 quotient, projection off horizontal rows preserves embedding height. With $M$ the projected horizontal lattice, $t=1$ and $\gamma=1$, the condition becomes $\lambda_1(M)>1+r^2$. Using the actual projected error norm in place of distance suffices for the specified lift: the same strict inequality places it within the unique decoding radius. This recovers the quadratic threshold already identified by the project. The lemma is a good finite-dimensional formalization target even with general $t,\gamma$. For an ambient approximation algorithm a separate simple certificate is $\lambda_2>\gamma\lambda_1$: any primitive output of length at most $\gamma\lambda_1$ is then on the unique shortest line and equals one of its two generators. Neither argument proves the gap or determines a concrete BKZ width. [Decoding by Embedding, §IV-A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.2936) · [Part 47](wall1_decoupled.md)

**Estimator lineage.** Alkim–Ducas–Pöppelmann–Schwabe compare a modeled projection of the planted vector (root-mean-square length about $\sqrt{\beta/d}\|u\|$ for an independent uniform plane) with a modeled block-head GS length from GSA. Even for an independent plane, this RMS value is not a 99% bound. Dachman-Soled–Ducas–Gong–Rossi estimate progressive-BKZ success, and Postlethwaite–Virdia extend and test this approach for BKZ 2.0. De Perthuis–Trenkić (PQCrypto 2026) identify resampling of the mark length across attempts as a source of error, condition on a single length, then integrate over its law. They validate the resulting estimates experimentally. Conditioning this way is compatible with the repository's fixed-shell question but leaves the distribution of the selected plane conditional on that mark open. Formalizing the estimator arithmetic or an independent-plane beta law would certify only the corresponding model. For the planted reducer the required failure bound still excludes early capture and includes both divisibility and off-line competitors, including ties. [NewHope, primal estimate](https://tpoeppelmann.de/documents/newhope_11_12_2017.pdf) · [DDGR](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/292) · [Postlethwaite–Virdia](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/1308) · [De Perthuis–Trenkić](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/36737)

## Effective q-ary equidistribution

**El-Baz–Lee–Strömbergsson, revised 2023, Proposition 4.2.** For prime $q$, a uniformly random codimension-$m$ subspace of $\mathbb F_q^d$ lifts to a lattice of Smith type $(1^{d-m},q^m)$, where superscripts here denote multiplicities. After scaling lengths by $q^{-m/d}$, these lattices are exactly the equally weighted Hecke points for $q^{-m/d}\operatorname{diag}(I_{d-m},qI_m)$. This identifies the relevant family even though the prime-index Goldstein–Mayer example has only one nontrivial invariant factor. For $d\ge3$, $0<m<d$, $\varepsilon>0$, $\kappa=(d^2-1)/2$, and $f\in C_b^{\lceil\kappa+\varepsilon\rceil}$ on the space of unimodular lattices, their result implies
$$
|\mathbb E_{q,m} f-\mathbb E_{\rm Haar} f|
\le C_{d,\varepsilon} S_{2,\kappa+\varepsilon}(f)\,
 q^{-\min(m,d-m)/2+\varepsilon}.
$$
At our dimensions the exponent before $\varepsilon$ is $-255.5$, but the Sobolev order exceeds **523264**. The constant and the norm of a smooth majorant for an algorithmic failure event have not been bounded here. The discrete orbit and Haar measure have total variation distance one. An indicator of the orbit itself demonstrates why the rate cannot hold for every bounded test with a uniform constant. Moreover, the canonical q-ary input basis and tie rules have to be represented in any extension of the selector to Haar input. Proving the elementary Hecke-orbit identification is feasible independently of formalizing the automorphic spectral estimate. [Lemmas 2.2 and 4.1, Proposition 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.07408)

**Finite adjustment for the systematic matrix.** The graph of uniform $A$ is uniform among $(d-m)$-planes transverse to the fixed $m$-coordinate plane $E$. Every transverse plane is the graph of exactly one map. For a uniform Grassmannian plane $K$, the chance that a fixed line lies in $K$ is $(q^{d-m}-1)/(q^d-1)$. A union bound over the $(q^m-1)/(q-1)$ lines of $E$ gives
$$
\Pr[K\cap E\ne0]\le
\frac{(q^m-1)(q^{d-m}-1)}{(q-1)(q^d-1)}
<\frac1{q-1}=\frac1{3328}.
$$
This is also an upper bound on total variation between the full **finite** Hecke ensemble and the systematic graph law. For a bad event of unconditioned probability $p$, conditioning gives the sharper upper bound $p/(1-1/3328)$ when useful. This deduction applies to the unplanted lattice and to any common selector defined on both finite ensembles. It does not pay for replacing either ensemble by Haar or for introducing the planted column.

## Concrete next steps

1. **Formalize the finite conditioning lemma first.** Specify the coordinate split and the selector on transverse inputs. Extend it arbitrarily elsewhere. A full-orbit failure bound of $0.01(1-1/3328)$ would suffice for a 1% systematic-graph budget. This step has no analytic or asymptotic hypotheses.
2. **Target one actual transition on the null lattice.** Fix the initial canonical basis, oracle, tie rule, visited window, and prefix rank. Sum an indicator of a bad projected extension over admissible primitive candidates, retaining the condition that the oracle selected that candidate. For a fixed integer tuple its membership in the null lattice is a finite modular rank calculation. The first open estimate is the joint probability of membership and winning the window comparison. Dropping the comparison returns the all-prefix first moments already excluded by Parts 41–43. A one-window bound, checked against exact enumeration at small rank, would test the proposed analogue of Laarhoven's moment recursion before attempting a tour.
3. **Use the Hecke estimate only for an observable with a quantified approximation error.** A first test is a smooth function of a short-vector count with a cusp cutoff. Record the cutoff error, Sobolev norm, and dimension-dependent constant before drawing a finite-rank conclusion. A transfer for that test would still require a separate argument connecting it to the terminal minimum. For a deterministic baseline, specialize Li–Nguyen's prefix bounds to the actual BKZ schedule and compare the induced last-coordinate and suffix-product bounds with Part 49. Only an improvement for the first GS height of an SVP-reduced terminal block, or a direct minimum bound there, addresses the current bottleneck.
