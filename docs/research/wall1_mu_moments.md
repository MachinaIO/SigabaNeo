# Factorial moments in a fixed triangular chart

The fixed-profile moment formula has an integer row lattice at each depth. Its high-order terms can be exponentially larger than Poisson moments even on the geometric profiles of Part 70. At rank 1023 the mean on each of the twelve Part P witness boxes is large enough for a very weak exponential void theorem to eliminate that box. Proving such a void theorem remains open.

## Exact unfolding

Use the upper triangular convention with physical row coordinates
$g_i(x_i+\sum_{j>i}\mu_{ij}x_j)$, independent uniform centered coefficients, and $R=g_0$. Represent each sign pair by a positive last nonzero coefficient. For an ordered tuple of $k$ vectors write its integer coefficient rows as $a_i\in\mathbb Z^k$. Starting at the bottom with $L_w=0$, make the following choices and integrations for $i=w-1,\ldots,0$:

1. Sum over $a_i\in\mathbb Z^k/L_{i+1}$ using one representative per coset. Set $L_i=L_{i+1}+\mathbb Za_i$.
2. Integrate the unscaled real row $y_i$ on $a_i+\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}L_{i+1}$ with Euclidean measure divided by the covolume of $L_{i+1}$ in its span (point mass one at rank zero).
3. At the end retain tuples of distinct sign representatives and integrate the product over columns $s$ of $\mathbf1\{\sum_i g_i^2 y_{is}^2<R^2\}$.

**Theorem.** The resulting sum of integrals equals $\mathbb E(N)_k$ at every order. The sign and equality tests use the integer rows of the chosen representatives.

**Proof.** Expand the ordered factorial count before averaging. For fixed integer rows, the real rows are independent translations of sums of uniform segments in the directions $a_j$, $j>i$. Unfold the *top* integer row sum first. Grouping its translates by the lattice generated by lower rows periodizes the segment distribution. Modulo this lattice the segments produce normalized Haar by the surjective homomorphism from the coefficient torus onto the span modulo its lattice. Summing the period translates therefore gives the measure in step 2. Shifting a row by integer combinations of lower rows preserves its generated suffix lattice. Such triangular row operations also preserve last nonzero entries, zero columns and equality between columns. Thus the already unfolded top kernel is invariant under these shifts of the next integer row (its cosets and real integration domains are unchanged). Induction unfolds all rows and yields the displayed bottom-up enumeration. Tonelli applies to positive terms. For fixed positive $g$ the original count is uniformly bounded by backward substitution from $|x_i|<R/g_i+\sum_{j>i}|x_j|/2$ (a weak inequality suffices).

At full suffix rank the cosets tile $\mathbb Z^k/L$, all affine planes are $\mathbb R^k$, and their number cancels the covolume. Above such a suffix the row measure is product Lebesgue. At deficient rank the affine planes, covolumes and successive cosets are the diagrams. They include repeated last indices, even for tuples of full *final* rank. This uses the torus unfolding of [Part 46](wall1_haar_prefix_law.md). The quotient-Haar theorem in [Stage 3](../PROOF_GAP.md#stage-3-sieving-assumption-b) integrates over the diagonal as well and has order range $k<w$.

An explicit product contribution comes from prescribed distinct last indices $j_s$ and positive last entries $m_s$. Its value is the product of
$V_j(R^2-m^2g_j^2)_+^{j/2}/\prod_{i<j}g_i$ for $j\ge1$ (index zero gives no short pair). Indeed, conditional on earlier columns the new column gives a uniform translate of the earlier lattice, also after multiplication by $m$. Its expected lift count is section volume divided by determinant, independent of the earlier columns. Integrate in decreasing order of the prescribed endpoints. This argument concerns counts summed over lifts.

## High-order obstruction in the actual coefficient family

Normalize $R=1$, put $g_j=e^{-aj}$ with $a=-2\log V_w/[w(w-1)]+\varepsilon$, and constrain every coefficient in the last $h$ columns to have magnitude less than $\eta=1/(100w)$. This event has probability at least $(2\eta)^{wh}$. Every sign vector supported with entries $\pm1$ on exactly these columns has squared norm at most
$$w(h\eta)^2+h e^{-2a(w-h)}(1+h\eta)^2.$$
Consequently when this is below one there are at least $B=2^{h-1}$ distinct primitive pairs there, and for $k<B$
$$\log\frac{\mathbb E(N)_k}{E^k}\ge wh\log(2\eta)+k\log((B-k)/E).$$

At $w=406$, $\varepsilon=.0002$, $h=50$, $k=20000$, the squared norm bound is below $.162$, $6919491<E<6919492$, and the log moment ratio exceeds **162944**. All these vectors even have the same last index. Asymptotically choose $\varepsilon=8\log w/[w(w-1)]$, $h=\lfloor w/4\rfloor$, $k=w^3$. Part 70 gives $E\sim w^4/2$ and $aw=\log w+O(1)$. The second squared-norm term is $O(w^{-1/2})$. For this asymptotic example **tighten** $\eta$ to $1/(100w^2)$, so the first term is $O(1/w)$. The same proof gives log ratio $\Omega(w^4)-O(w^2\log w)$. Hence no absolute $C,c>0$ give $|\delta_k|\le Ck^2/E$ through $k\le cE$ throughout this small-excess family. This obstruction uses unconditioned coefficients. It concerns high counts and leaves the lower tail undetermined. [Part 70](wall1_window_asymptotics.md)

## Void inference

Bonferroni gives an **even** upper truncation and an odd lower truncation. Even with exact Poisson moments the even remainder is bounded by $E^{m+1}/(m+1)!$ at truncation $m$ by Taylor's theorem. For instance an even $m\ge4E$ makes that bound $o(e^{-E})$ as $E\to\infty$. Relative moment errors enter weighted by $E^k/k!$, whose full sum is $e^E$. Two-sided polynomial accuracy alone cannot justify the cancellation. More explicitly, the zero-inflated Poisson example in Part 60 with inflation parameter $E^{-2}$ has moment ratios $(1+E^{-2})^{k-1}=1+O(k/E^2)$ for $1\le k\le E$, yet void probability at least $1/(1+E^2)$. [Part 60](wall1_poisson_smallball.md)

Independent rows give a valid but expensive dependency graph on fixed vector tests. Every potentially short integer vector has a nonzero suffix below row zero, so the shared-row graph is complete. Its Chen–Stein $b_1$ (including self-neighbors) is $E^2$. Even the usual factor $\min(1,1/E)$ leaves an additive error budget of order $E$ for large means. Joint increasing events are also unjustified. For $g=(1,.49)$ the tests for $(0,2)$ and $(-1,2)$ each have positive probability but their intervals $|2\mu|<\sqrt{1-.98^2}$ and $|2\mu-1|<\sqrt{1-.98^2}$ are disjoint. No common increasing product-space realization preserves even this pair law, by positive association. This example addresses the unrestricted construction. No alternative monotone encoding or small dependency sum for the target profiles is established here. [Riordan and Warnke](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.1024)

## Applying the mean to the surviving boxes

For each box, select one full-width window by maximizing a certified lower bound for the single $j=b-1,m=1$ summand. Writing $x_i=\log g_i$, $d=b-1$, its log is
$$\log V_d+d x_i-\sum_{l=i}^{i+d-1}x_l+(d/2)\log(1-e^{2(x_{i+d}-x_i)}).$$
Only positive radii contribute. Recentring cancels and each log difference moves by at most $2\cdot10^{-6}$. Each entry below holds throughout the corresponding box at **each** of $.25,.5,.85$ times GSA.

| Width | Lower bound on $\log E$ in the selected window | Largest P upper bound on log unweighted intensity at these radii |
|---:|---:|---:|
|406|129|842097|
|506|159|607041|
|647|200|405920|
|800|245|266451|

If a uniform void exponent $cE$ held on these windows, even $c=10^{-49}$ would suppress each individual box below intensity $.01$, since $10^{-49}e^{129}>842097+\log100$. This comparison uses the **unweighted upper** integral for the prefactor. The joint acceptance is at most the unconditional single-window acceptance, so no multiplication by a correlated width-three weight is required. P's $229742$–$828813$ nats are lower bounds on the relaxed log integral. Eliminating its witness boxes would still require an upper estimate on the complement to certify a radius. The actual exponential penalty on the boxes and the full rank-1023 integral both remain unproved. [Part P](wall1_surviving_mass.md)

## Interval certificate

Run the following from the repository root with Python, NumPy and mpmath. The second calculation reuses P's integer recurrence and interval cap checks verbatim.

I.dps=40
w,h,k=406,50,20000
def logV(d):
    return d*I.ln(I.pi)/2-I.loggamma(1+I.mpf(d)/2)
a=-2*logV(w)/(w*(w-1))+I.mpf('.0002')
mean=I.mpf(0)
for j in range(1,w):
    stop=int(float(I.exp(a*j).b))+2
    assert stop>I.exp(a*j)
    for m in range(1,stop):
        t=1-m*m*I.exp(-2*a*j)

            mean+=I.exp(logV(j)+a*j*(j-1)/2)*t**(I.mpf(j)/2)
        else: assert t<0
eta=1/(I.mpf(100)*w)

print(mean,norm2,logratio)
assert 6919491<mean and mean<6919492 and norm2<I.mpf(".162") and logratio>162944
