# Which sublattice does two-block slide reduction select?

For the standard \(qI\)-first basis of the uniform graph lattice \(L_0(A)\), the exact, strict-improvement version of slide reduction has a **sharp lower support endpoint**. In physical units,
\[
 \Pr[\det Q_{\rm out}=1]=3329^{-511\cdot512}.
\]
This probability is the same for every choice among exact oracle ties. The endpoint is far too rare to settle a one-percent quantile. There is also a second monotone quantity besides the published prefix potential. Both prefix and extension covolumes decrease weakly, yet even the entire prefix-potential history can leave the terminal extension covolume undetermined.

There is a separate dimension issue. The S2 statistic measures a rank-512 **horizontal** quotient. Part 47 adds one independent height direction to that quotient. Its final SVP with the same prefix has rank **513**.

## 1. The intrinsic steps and the two covolumes

Write \(N=1023,\ r=511,\ b=512=r+1\), with \(N=r+b\). In Algorithm 2 of ALNS20 these are respectively \(n,q,k\). Below \(q=3329\) denotes the modulus. A prefix \(P\) is a saturated rank-\(r\) sublattice of \(L=L_0\). Write \(\pi_P\) for orthogonal projection off its real span and \(\det\) for Euclidean covolume in the span. Work in physical units until explicitly normalizing. The paper takes positive slack. Here the oracle is exact and the dual update is accepted precisely on **strict** improvement, i.e. the zero-slack interpretation of line 7. The statements concern the three exact minimum conditions of S2. They do not require a particular size-reduced completion of a flag. [ALNS20, Algorithm 2 and Theorem 3.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.03724#page=12) · [Part 138 and its audit](wall1_reducer_variants.md)

**Proposition 1.** In each round let \(a\) be the chosen shortest vector in \(P\). Then the primal suffix call selects a minimum-covolume saturated extension \(Q\supset P\) of rank \(r+1\). The dual call proposes a minimum-covolume saturated hyperplane \(P'\subset Q\) containing \(a\). The algorithm accepts just when \(\det P'<\det P\). At successive suffix calls,
\[
 \det P_{t+1}<\det P_t,\qquad \det Q_{t+1}\leq\det Q_t.
\]
For integral \(L\), the process terminates after at most \((\det P_0)^2-1\) accepted dual changes.

*Proof.* A primitive line in \(\pi_P L\) specifies an extension by taking its inverse image in \(L\). Its covolume is \(\det P\) times the length of the primitive generator. This gives the first minimization. The hyperplanes in the second are exactly kernels in \(Q\) of primitive \(x\in Q^*\) perpendicular to \(a\), up to sign. This set of covectors is the primitive part of \((\pi_{\mathbb Za}Q)^*\). The distance between adjacent level sets of \(x\) is \(1/\|x\|\), so each kernel has covolume \(\det Q\,\|x\|\). The current \(P\) is eligible. Maximizing the new last Gram–Schmidt length is equivalent to minimizing this covolume. On acceptance \(Q_t\) still contains \(P_{t+1}\) as a saturated hyperplane, so it competes in the next suffix minimization. Finally, the squared prefix covolume is the positive integer Gram determinant. Each acceptance lowers it by at least one. This proves finite termination. The polynomial oracle-call bound in ALNS20 uses positive slack. ∎

For the \(qI\)-first basis, \(P_0=q\mathbb Z^{511}\) in the sample coordinates and \(\pi_{P_0}L=\mathbb Z^{512}\) in the other coordinates. Thus \(\det Q_0=q^{511}\) for **every** design \(A\). Monotonicity gives an upper bound on the terminal determinant. A lower bound would require controlling the cumulative decrease.

For a fixed integral \(L\), the reachable flags form a finite set at round boundaries: the exterior coordinates of \(P\) and \(Q\) are integral and their squared norms are bounded by Proposition 1. There are finitely many such exterior vectors, and for a saturated sublattice its real span determines it inside \(L\). After fixing a rule for resolving ties, each terminal probability is the sum of the probabilities of the paths ending there. For a rule depending only on the current flag, the weight of a path is the product of its transition probabilities. Strict prefix descent bounds the path length even for rules depending on the whole history. This is an exact finite description. Its evaluation at the stated quantile remains open.

**Randomized starts with the same prefix.** Suppose each oracle tie is resolved uniformly among the shortest unoriented lines of its intrinsic lattice. Conditional on fixed \(A\), *every* random choice of initial basis with prefix lattice \(P_0\) gives the same terminal law as the original basis. Indeed, couple the initial prefix winners, then the shortest lines of the identical projected lattices, then the shortest dual lines of the identical marked extensions. Proposition 1 makes the subsequent prefixes and the stopping decisions identical. Induction proves equality of the terminal sublattices on the coupling. This covers arbitrary changes of basis within the prefix and changes of lifts in the suffix. A distribution on genuinely different initial prefix lattices gives the corresponding mixture of conditional laws. Basis-dependent choices, such as lexicographic order on coefficient vectors, need their own transition probabilities.

## 2. A sharp support obstruction on the actual ensemble

**Proposition 2.** Let \(A\) be uniform in \(\mathbb F_q^{511\times512}\), with the start and update convention of §1. Then \(1\leq\det Q_{\rm out}\leq q^{511}\). Equality on the left occurs exactly when \(A=0\). In the determinant-one scaling \(\widehat L=(\det L)^{-1/N}L\), the smallest attainable value of \(\log\det\widehat Q_{\rm out}\) is
\[
 -\frac{512\cdot511}{1023}\log q
\]
with the probability stated above.

*Proof.* For an integer basis matrix of \(Q\), Cauchy–Binet expresses \((\det Q)^2\) as a sum of squares of maximal minors. Equality to one means that exactly one such minor is nonzero and it is \(\pm1\). Every row outside that minor vanishes: its coefficients in the selected row basis are ratios of replacement minors to the nonzero minor. Hence \(Q\) is a full coordinate sublattice on 512 axes. The graph congruence \(x+Ay=0\bmod q\) excludes every sample unit vector. The only possible choice is all 512 secret-coordinate axes, and these belong to \(L\) precisely for \(A=0\).

It remains to prove that the algorithm actually reaches that extension. For \(A=0\) the lattice is an orthogonal product with 511 axes of length \(q\) and 512 of length one. Initially \(P\) consists of long axes. Inductively \(P\) is a coordinate sublattice in this orthogonal basis. Its shortest vector is a signed shortest axis of \(P\). At least one unit axis is missing from \(P\), so \(Q\) adds one such axis. After projecting off \(a\), the shortest dual vector is the reciprocal of a longest remaining axis in \(Q\). If \(P\) contains a long axis, one is available for removal: when all of \(P\) is long, \(r\ge2\), and otherwise \(a\) is short. The accepted change replaces one long axis by one unit axis. When \(P\) consists only of unit axes, \(Q\) contains all 512, and the dual step cannot improve. This argument covers all ties. The probability of \(A=0\) is \(q^{-511\cdot512}\). Scaling 512-dimensional volume gives the displayed endpoint. ∎

In particular, even from the prescribed start there is no pathwise lower bound at either positive log-determinant threshold in Part 138. Proposition 2 determines one atom and leaves the mass *above* that atom unresolved.

## 3. Identical prefix-potential histories, different terminal determinants

**Proposition 3.** Even conditional on one integral lattice, one starting basis, and the sequence of squared prefix covolumes, the exact block specifications do not determine the law of \(\det Q_{\rm out}\).

*Proof by example.* Let \(e_0,\ldots,e_4\) be orthonormal, take \(r=2,\ b=3\), and start with the ordered basis
\[
 (e_0,\ 10(e_1+e_2),\ 10e_1,\ 5e_1+9e_3,\ 20e_4).
\]
The primal prefix winner is \(e_0\). The first suffix winner is the line of the projected \(10e_1\), of squared length \(50\). Indeed the other two generators have respective independent perpendicular components of lengths 9 and 20. The extension is \(Q=\mathbb Ze_0+10\mathbb Ze_1+10\mathbb Ze_2\). The dual block is a square, so its two shortest unoriented dual lines give two allowed new prefixes of squared covolume 100, down from 200.

* If the retained square axis is \(10e_1\), the suffix has orthogonal lengths \(10,9,20\) and picks the line of \(9e_3\). In the new extension, the plane perpendicular to \(e_0\) has basis \(10e_1,5e_1+9e_3\). Its minimum is 10: coefficients with second entry \(\pm1\) have squared norm at least \(25+81=106\), and entries of magnitude at least two already give vertical length 18. In this rank-two plane the current line is a shortest primitive hyperplane, so the dual step stops. The terminal covolume is \(10\cdot9=90\).
* If the retained axis is \(10e_2\), the suffix is that same plane orthogonally summed with the length-20 axis. Its shortest line is \(10e_1\). The extension again contains the square, and the dual step stops at equality. The terminal covolume is \(10\cdot10=100\).

Thus a fair choice at the initial dual tie assigns half the terminal mass to each determinant. A deterministic preference can put all the mass at either one. The potential history \(200,100\) and the number of accepted changes are identical. ∎

This example concerns integral slide dynamics. It does not assign a frequency to ambiguous histories of the target graph ensemble. The computations were checked in scratch arithmetic by evaluating the three quotient quadratic forms on integer triples with coefficients in \([-3,3]\); the length bounds in the proof establish the global minima.

## 4. What a first moment with the twin conditions counts

Let \(\Delta_{r+1}(L)\) be the smallest covolume of a saturated rank-\((r+1)\) sublattice of \(L\). Let \(C_T(L)\) count distinct such sublattices of covolume at most \(T\) that admit a basis flag satisfying the three S2 minimum conditions. Count each sublattice once.

**Proposition 4.** For each integral \(L\) in the two-block setting,
\[
 \{C_T(L)>0\}=\{\Delta_{r+1}(L)\le T\}.
\]
In particular, even a perfect test for existence of a passing sublattice below a cumulative cutoff measures a Rankin minimum. The probability of a visit from the specified start is an additional quantity.

*Proof.* Bounded exterior coordinates give finiteness below any fixed cutoff and attainment of the minimum. In a minimizer \(Q\), choose a shortest \(a\in Q\) and a minimum-covolume saturated hyperplane \(P\subset Q\) containing \(a\). Then \(a\) is shortest in \(P\), and the dual condition follows from Proposition 1. If there were a shorter suffix line than \(Q/P\) in \(\pi_P L\), its primitive extension would have smaller covolume than \(Q\). Thus the suffix condition also holds. A basis adapted to the primitive flag \(\mathbb Za\subset P\subset Q\subset L\) realizes the three tests. The reverse implication holds by definition. ∎

**Correction to the side condition in S2(c).** The preceding proof establishes that a densest \(Q\) passes. It proves an inequality *before* the quotient is projected off \(Q/P\). The stronger sufficient side condition in S2(b), \(\lambda_1(\pi_Q L)\ge\det Q/\det P\), can fail even at a minimizer.

For an integral example take the orthogonal sum \(L=\mathbb Z^2\perp A_2\perp 3\mathbb Z\), where \(A_2\) has generators \(u=(1,1,0),v=(1,0,1)\). Take \(P=\mathbb Z^2\) and \(Q=P+\mathbb Zu\). Then \(\det Q=\sqrt2\) is minimal in rank three. A smaller determinant would be one by integrality of the squared covolume, requiring three coordinate unit vectors by the minors argument of Proposition 2, while \(L\) has only two independent units. With \(a\) a coordinate unit in \(P\), the dual minimum after removing \(a\) is \(1/\sqrt2\), and the suffix minimum is \(\sqrt2\) since \(A_2\) has squared norm form \(2(i^2+ij+j^2)\). Yet after removing \(Q\) the projected \(v\) has squared norm \(2-1/2=3/2<2\). The side condition therefore cannot be used to discard candidates in a first-moment upper bound for all passing \(Q\).

More explicitly, let \(p_A(Q)\) be the conditional terminal mass of \(Q\), including any start or oracle randomness in the specified rule. For any window \(I\), the exact probability is \(\mathbb E_A\sum_Q p_A(Q)\mathbf1_{\det Q\in I}\). Dropping the weights and counting passing candidates gives a valid upper bound. A small first moment would suffice, including for a window; a large first moment supplies no lower bound on selected mass. Proposition 4 applies to cumulative cutoffs, not arbitrary windows. Parts 40 and 92 give finite-field incidence information. Such information alone does not specify the weights. [Parts 40 and 92](../PROOF_GAP.md)

## 5. Recorded thresholds and remaining gap

In determinant-one units, the audited S2 implication is \(\log\det\widehat Q\ge954.34\Rightarrow\rho\ge0.954\). It is sufficient. The cutoff near \(862.75\) assumes typical primal and dual Hermite ratios **[heuristic]**. The estimate \(\rho\approx0.28\) at the densest extension also uses a heuristic for its covolume and shape. Proposition 4 supplies the passing flag there, without assigning that estimate to the actual ensemble. The repaired Theorem B is a feasibility statement for block-profile inequalities through width 566. It assigns no probabilities to terminal flags. [Part 138, Audit](wall1_reducer_variants.md#audit)

For Part 47 let \(W=\operatorname{span}P\) and \(u=(e,s,1)\). Height vanishes on \(W\); the projected lattice splits as an abelian group into \(\pi_P L_0\oplus\mathbb Z\pi_P u\), since \(\pi_P u\) still has height one. Its rank is \(1023-511+1=513\). Consequently an oracle ceiling of 512 for the *whole procedure* is incompatible with applying Part 47's final SVP to this same \(P\). A ceiling of 512 for the *null reduction* is consistent. The recorded own-GH target is a horizontal statistic and its transfer to a CBD recovery claim in this setting is unproved. [Part 47, projected lattice](wall1_decoupled.md) · [Part 138, Audit](wall1_reducer_variants.md#audit)

**Open selection question.** For a specified tie rule and the standard start, how much mass does the joint law of \((\det\widehat Q,\|\widehat b_1\|,\eta_*)\) assign to the event \(\rho<0.954\)? No one-percent upper bound or conjectured numerical probability follows from the results here. Parts 86–87, 96 and 98 give predicate or Hermite bounds. Parts 105–112 distinguish one-step estimates from a history-averaged law. Parts 139 and 142 concern the first-tour append process and do not supply a transition law on slide flags. Randomizing a tie gives a computable law in Proposition 3. At the recorded dimensions the rigorous information here is the finite path description, the two monotonicities, and the exact bottom atom. ## Audit

**Verdict — MINOR CAVEATS.** Propositions 1–4 hold under the stated exact-oracle, strict-improvement convention. One sentence after Proposition 2 needs a probability clarification. The note correctly identifies two issues in Part 138.

1. **Endpoint and its probability.** A unit maximal minor and vanishing replacement minors force every row outside the chosen axes to vanish. The unit minor then gives the entire coordinate lattice on those axes. Every sample unit fails the graph congruence, so the 512 axes have to be all the secret axes. Their membership is equivalent to every column of \(A\) vanishing. Conversely, at \(A=0\) each accepted round removes exactly one long axis, irrespective of all ties, until the extension consists of the 512 unit axes. Thus the physical endpoint is 1 with mass \(p=3329^{-511\cdot512}\) under the stated uniform matrix law. Scaling each vector by \(q^{-511/1023}\) gives the claimed log endpoint. The sentence “leaves the mass above that atom unresolved” should read “the total mass above the atom is \(1-p\); its distribution among larger covolumes is unresolved.”
2. **Monotonicity and ties.** The suffix minimizes the extension covolume at fixed prefix. The dual call minimizes the hyperplane covolume at fixed extension and marked vector. After an accepted change the old extension is an eligible competitor at the new prefix. This proves strict prefix descent and weak extension descent, including extension equality on an accepted round. The squared prefix covolume is a positive integer, giving the stated termination bound. For uniform ties conditional on each intrinsic oracle input, the coupling in §1 uses the same finite set of lines at each step. The paper's polynomial bound has a separate positive-slack hypothesis. [ALNS20, Algorithm 2 and Theorem 3.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.03724#page=12)
3. **Independent rank-five rerun.** A scratch script formed the projected Gram matrices from the given basis, inverted them exactly for the dual calls, and enumerated every possible shortest line. For Gram matrix \(G\) and squared trial radius \(R=\min_i G_{ii}\), Cauchy–Schwarz bounds each coefficient by \(z_i^2\le R(G^{-1})_{ii}\), so the enumeration is exhaustive. It found initial suffix squared minimum 50 and exactly two unoriented dual winners of squared norm \(1/100\). Retaining \(10e_1\) gives suffix and dual squared minima \(81,1/81\), while retaining \(10e_2\) gives \(100,1/100\). Both dual calls stop at equality. The terminal covolumes are respectively 90 and 100, with the same squared prefix history \(200,100\).
4. **Densest extension.** For the minimizing \(Q\) and chosen \(P\), any shorter line after projecting off \(P\) would give a smaller saturated extension. This proves the third minimum condition and the cumulative-cutoff equivalence. In the stated orthogonal sum, rank-three squared covolume 1 is impossible because only two coordinate units are available. The proposed \(Q\) attains squared covolume 2. Exact arithmetic gives \(\lambda_1(\pi_P L)^2=2\), the marked dual squared minimum \(1/2\), and \(\lambda_1(\pi_Q L)^2=3/2\). Thus S2(c)'s passing-flag conclusion survives, while its claim that S2(b)'s side condition is automatic is false. The earlier audit's blanket approval of S2(c) needs this qualification. The first-moment upper bound in §4 follows from each conditional selection weight being at most one.
5. **Oracle ranks.** Part 47 defines its horizontal projector to have rank \(\beta-1\). At this prefix the null quotient has rank \(1023-511=512\). Height induces a surjection of the full projected lattice onto \(\mathbb Z\), with kernel the horizontal quotient and a section generated by the projected height-one vector. Hence Part 47's final SVP has rank 513. Part 138's “oracle rank is 512” is accurate for its null reducer alone. Applied to the combined procedure advertised in its §6, that cost omits a dimension. The Lean declarations agree with Part 47. In `AttackLattice.lean`, `finrank_perp` uses \(J+d=N\) in the horizontal space and `canonical_proj_snoc` preserves height. In `DecoupledDecoder.lean`, `decode` passes the image of the full input lattice to the SVP oracle. This was a source inspection. [Part 47](wall1_decoupled.md) · [AttackLattice.lean](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/AttackLattice.lean) · [DecoupledDecoder.lean](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/DecoupledDecoder.lean)

Substitution into S2(a) also reproduces the sufficient cutoff \(954.33968172\ldots\), rounded upward to 954.34, and the typical-shape cutoff \(862.74686368\ldots\). At the stated heuristic log covolume 547.87, the same typical-shape substitution gives \(\rho=0.27918568\ldots\). These evaluations leave the selected lower-tail probability open.
