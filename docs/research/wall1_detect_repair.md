# What the width-506 spacing event certifies

There are two obstructions to the proposed conclusion. A decoder cannot recover the secret on every design in the spacing event, even with unlimited computation and the full observation. Also, bounding the frequency of this event would settle only one necessary condition in the proposed first-moment proof. A distributional guarantee for a changed decoder remains open.

## From a short plane to score rejection

Use the repository convention of 511 available sample rows and 512 secret coordinates, with modulus 3329 and product CBD(3). The concrete terminal theorem uses the **marginal restart** with 400 uniform rows and 512 columns. After draw 406 its retained lattice $M$ has rank 505. Let $x$ be its designated signed shortest vector. The following events concern the design alone.

| Event | Predicate |
|---|---|
| $E=J$ | The rank-504 projection of $M$ orthogonal to $x$ has a nonzero point of length at most 26. Equivalently a saturated plane through $x$ has internal spacing at most 26 modulo its line. |
| $F$ | After the actual last column and its selected winner, the final rank-505 horizontal lattice $H$ has minimum at most 26. |
| $A_{406}$ | $M$ contains a saturated plane of area at most $1352/\sqrt3$. The plane may omit $x$. |

Here $E\subseteq A_{406}$. The concrete Lean comparison gives $\Pr(F\mathbin\triangle E)<5.244\times10^{-9}$, including ties. The same discrepancy bound holds conditional on each 511-column history, over the fresh last column. Thus a history in $E$ predicts $F$ with high conditional probability. The two events need not agree on each last column. [Concrete event definitions and matrix discrepancy](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/ConcreteTerminal506.lean) · [Area comparison](wall1_plane_comparison.md)

For a fixed terminal block let $X$ be an independent CBD plant, $P$ its horizontal projector, and $Z=\|PX\|^2$. The squared length of the projected planted vector, including its height coordinate, is $1+Z$. On $F$, choose a nonzero horizontal $v$ of norm at most 26. Whenever $675\le Z\le959.5$, both signs of $v$ compete at height zero. Equality already violates signed-plant uniqueness. At rank 505 the conditional mean of $Z$ is 757.5 and its variance is at most 2272.5. Cantelli and the cap tail give

$$
 \Pr_X(675\le Z\le959.5)
 \ge \frac{82.5^2}{2272.5+82.5^2}-0.00047835048014
 >0.74921186.
$$

Consequently on **every** such $F$ block the Part 181 score exceeds $2(0.74921186)=1.49842372$, far above the acceptance threshold $0.00012938$. The factor two counts points in the score. The lower bound on the probability of failed uniqueness counts the event once. A score-testing procedure rejects this block before a lift. For a procedure that actually runs shortest-vector decoding on it, failed uniqueness alone says nothing definitive about which vector is returned in a tie or which secret a later rule outputs. [Part 182 derivation](wall1_restart_passes_test.md) · [Exact capped score](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/FixedBlockScore.lean)

The earlier `smoothError` certificate requires a valid horizontal minimum floor $L>\sqrt{960.5}$ at this cap. On $F$ no such floor exists, and the formula returns the trivial bound 1 for every valid floor. Part 171 separately found a determinant contradiction for the old *uniform terminal Gram–Schmidt floor* test. The score test uses no such floor. On a score-accepted block Lean bounds terminal failure by $0.000607731$. The prefix Babai allowance is a separate sum over the actual prefix lengths. ## What is observable

The penultimate history determines $E$ once the tie convention is fixed. A supplied plane through the selected line can witness a hit via its internal spacing. The retained basis alone does not designate a second generator for such a plane. Ruling out all witnesses is a rank-504 quotient-minimum question. Two-dimensional reduction of a supplied plane checks only that plane. The certificate establishes neither a cheap complete detector nor a cost bound for locating a witness. The terminal basis determines $F$ and the model score. Lean defines the score noncomputably as an expectation over the plant law. Score rejection, if evaluated, is observable. The realized plant energy and correctness of a returned secret are separate quantities. ## A reachable obstruction to deterministic repair

Consider the all-zero design. In the concrete 400-row restart, immediately before the first draw the lattice is isometric to $3329\mathbb Z^{400}\oplus\mathbb Z^{105}$. Each zero-column append adds one orthogonal unit axis. The shortest lines in the enlarged lattice are exactly the unit coordinate lines, and removing any one restores the same isometry class. Induction through draw 406 proves that projecting the retained lattice off its designated shortest line still leaves 104 unit axes. In particular $E$ holds with internal spacing 1. The same argument on the actual last column gives $F$. This reasoning is independent of ties. [Concrete start, append, and freeze recursion](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/TerminalRestartPath.lean)

Take all 511 available sample rows to be zero as well. Then the public right-hand side is exactly the error and is independent of the secret. Conditional on these rows and **every** observed right-hand side in the support, any single-answer algorithm, including a randomized one, has success probability at most the largest prior atom,

$$
 \max_s \Pr(S=s)=(20/64)^{512}=2^{-859.172815\ldots}.
$$

Guessing the all-zero secret attains this bound. Evaluating a proposed secret against all the rows gives the same residual for every candidate. This example rules out deterministic recovery on every $E$ design and any useful uniform success lower bound there. Its probability under the uniform 400-row law is only $3329^{-400\cdot512}$. It fails the raw-good gate and supplies no lower bound of practical size for the residual events. A distributional repair could discard examples of this kind.

## What the terminal theorem leaves open

`ControlledConcreteBounds` gives the earlier conditional result. The subsequent `terminal_bound_unconditional` discharges the geometric hypotheses and proves

$$
 \Pr(A_{406})<\Pr(Z_1)+\Pr(Z_2)+4.652\times10^{-12}.
$$

Both residuals require the good gates and a terminal area hit. They classify the *first* hit by selection history. $Z_1$ uses a horizontal win from draw 155 onward with no fresh shortest vector of the hit plane. $Z_2$ uses a positive-height win from draw 395 onward in the specified winner-length band. They are disjoint. A plane can disappear between the first and terminal hits. No term in this theorem concerns a secret, a candidate list, or the output of a decoder. [Residual predicates](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/ControlledPlaneTail.lean) · [Conditional theorem](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/ControlledConcreteBounds.lean) · [Unconditional theorem](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/ControlledTerminalUnconditional.lean)

The $4\times10^{-7}$ target comes from trying to prove score rejection at most 0.005 by Markov. A sufficient mean-score bound for that argument is $0.005(0.00012938)=6.469\times10^{-7}$. The lower bound on the score on $F$ then forces $\Pr(F)<4.318\times10^{-7}$. This implication runs **from the proposed score estimate to the spacing tail**. Small spacing-tail probability gives no upper bound on the score on $F^c$. Even at height zero the cap allows competitors up to $\sqrt{960.5}\approx30.992$. Heights 1 through 30 and the prefix lift also remain. [Part 182](wall1_restart_passes_test.md)

For a changed procedure with a design-dependent trigger $T$, its error is the sum of two joint masses, the ordinary-branch error on $T^c$ and the changed-branch error on $T$. An expected extra-cost claim also needs the joint expectation of the trigger indicator times branch cost. Existence of one horizontal plane bounds neither candidate multiplicity nor identification of the planted secret. A change of final block or block alignment changes the projector, score, and prefix used by the theorem. The rejection product in `restartScore_lwe` requires disjoint row indices and uses the first accepted block's *own* score and prefix. Two disjoint 400-row blocks require 800 rows, exceeding the 511-row experiment. Reusing rows provides no such product bound. [Restart theorem and hypotheses](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/FixedBlockScoreRestart.lean)

Thus substituting a repair-failure probability for $\Pr(Z_1)+\Pr(Z_2)$ in the geometric inequality would change its meaning. The cited estimates give neither of the required decoder error bounds for that substitution, nor an expected extra cost. The deterministic obstruction above leaves the average-case question open.

## Audit

**Verdict.** Proving $\Pr(E)\le 4\times10^{-7}$ would settle the Part 184 spacing target. The width-506 success claim would remain open. Two distinctions matter. The proposed mean-score bound forces $\Pr(F)<4.317204\times10^{-7}$. Part 184 chose the smaller, round target for $E$ so that transferring it back to $F$ would fit within that ceiling. Also, meeting both Part 224 residual allowances is a **sufficient, strictly stronger numerical route** to the spacing target. The cited inclusions and upper bounds give no converse. [Part 182 audit](wall1_restart_passes_test.md#audit) · [Part 224 ledger](wall1_budget_ledger.md)

**Checks on the obstruction.** A zero column adds an orthogonal unit axis. Freezing any shortest line deletes one such axis, so the retained lattice has 105 unit axes after draw 406 and its quotient by the designated line has 104. Its minimum is exactly 1. The raw graph contains $(0,e_j)$ of squared length 1, witnessing `rawBad` at radius 250 and excluding both gated residuals. If **all 511 rows** vanish, conditioning on any supported right-hand side leaves the secret prior unchanged. Its largest atom is $(\binom{6}{3}/64)^{512}=(5/16)^{512}$, attained by guessing zero even after conditioning. The marginal zero design has mass $3329^{-400\cdot512}$, whereas the full zero design has mass $3329^{-511\cdot512}$ under independent uniform rows. [Restart recursion](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/TerminalRestartPath.lean) · [Raw membership](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/ControlledRawBall.lean) · [Good gate](../../SigabaNeo/Lib/Lattices/Research/Wall1/ControlledPlaneTail.lean)

Part 175's zero-design example prescribes a different ordered basis. It places all 512 secret coordinate units in the prefix and leaves $3329\mathbb Z^{505}$ in the terminal block. Its terminal success bound is compatible with the posterior bound above because recovering the secret still requires the prefix lift. [Part 175 physical control](wall1_smoothing_uniqueness.md)

On each $F$ block, the Cantelli lower bound minus the cap charge exceeds $0.74921186017$. Both signs count at height zero on that event, so the exact score exceeds $1.49842372$ and the block always fails the $0.00012938$ score test. The discrepancy $<5.244\times10^{-9}$ applies **between $E$ and $F$**, conditional on every history and also under the matrix law. Other causes of rejection can occur outside $F$. The cited results give a way to check a supplied spacing witness and give no complexity bound for a complete detector.
