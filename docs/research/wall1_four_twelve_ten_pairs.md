## All short rows and the missing case

Let C be the first four positions; every non-C code direction has all eight
new coordinates nonzero. Its old part cannot vanish: otherwise combining it
with the C direction gives old support either zero or all four positions,
contradicting a prescribed proper nonempty second-support overlap. Thus an
outside row has support at least nine.

Balanced norm-six rows have support three or six. Support three violates
girth4, while support six fits neither C nor the outside direction. Balanced
norm-eight rows have support two, five or eight. Support two violates girth4;
support five does not fit C or the outside direction; a unit-eight row on
all eight new positions would have zero old part and was just excluded.
Therefore the no-four family has first squared minimum at least ten.
In particular the production fallback term K(8,10)^N covers an empty case
and can be removed from its complete (8,10)/(10,10)/(12,12) split.

## Every norm-ten pair is inside/outside

A balanced norm-ten row of support at least four has either support four,
seven or ten (the remaining square partition 9+1 has support two). Its
inside-C form must be a permutation of (2,-2,1,-1), up to sign. There are
12 unoriented inside lines. An outside row must have support at least nine,
so it has ten unit entries, covering eight new and two old positions.
There are choose(4,2)*choose(10,5)/2=756 such lines.

Two independent outside norm-ten rows have proportional restrictions to the
eight new coordinates. Unit entries make the proportion +/-1 over q=3329.
After adjusting signs their difference is a nonzero old-supported row with
at most four nonzeros. If it has at most three, it contradicts girth4. If
it has four, the two old supports must be disjoint, so all four entries of
the difference are units and its squared norm is four. This contradicts the
no-four assumption. Thus two outside norm-ten lines cannot coexist.
Two independent inside lines are likewise impossible: modular proportionality
on C, together with the integer minor bound <q, forces real proportionality.
Consequently every two-norm-ten plane is generated by one of the 12 inside
lines and one of the 756 outside lines, at most 9072 witnesses.

Let a be inside and b outside. At most two old entries contribute to their
scalar product, each with magnitude at most two, so |<a,b>|<=4 and the Gram
determinant is at least 100-16=84. The basis is Gauss reduced and has squared
GS lengths 10 and at least 42/5. Select an old position where a is a unit
and any new position: a vanishes at the new position while b is a unit there.
Their minor is +/-1, regardless of b at the old position. This proves
saturation of the integer plane and modular independence.

Each pair fixes one modular code and at most one normalized coefficient
chart for a given prescribed proper second support. Thus 9072 is a safe
witness bound for each overlap; it need not equal the distinct chart count.
The script independently enumerates every pair and verifies all unit minors.

## Effective refinement: norm-four / norm-twelve pairs

Retain the EXISTING overlap-three norm-ten term 546/Q*K4(4,10)^N,
where Q=q^11 and K4 retains the existing cutoff40 one-four tail. The old
complement uses squared GS lengths(4,11). It is this complement that dominates
the selected bound, not the no-four family.

A norm-twelve crossing row has at least nine nonzero coordinates. The only
possible shapes are:

- twelve unit entries, supported on all twelve positions, with six signs of
  each kind:462 unoriented lines;
- one entry of magnitude2 and eight unit entries, supported on all eight
  new positions and one old position. Orient the magnitude2 entry positively;
  exactly three unit signs are positive and five negative. There are
  4*9*choose(8,3)=2016 lines.

Other square partitions of twelve have support at most eight and cannot
occur. There are three old norm-four lines a. Hence there are at most
3*(462+2016)=7434 pair witnesses.

In the unit-twelve case, let r_i=b_i/a_i be the four old sign ratios.
For the prescribed overlap three, cancelling position3 must leave the
other three positions nonzero. The ratio at position3 must therefore be
unique and the other three equal. It follows that |<a,b>|=2, so the Gram
determinant is44. The all-equal ratios would give old support zero or four,
not three; the two-and-two ratios would give support two.

In the nine-support case only one old coordinate is nonzero. To obtain
prescribed old support012, that active coordinate must be position3. If it
is the doubled coordinate, |<a,b>|=2 and D=44. If a new coordinate is doubled,
|<a,b>|=1 and D=47. Thus every eligible pair has squared GS lengths at least
(4,11). These rows are Gauss reduced since |<a,b>|<=2.

Saturation is valid even for the unit-twelve case: choose ANY old unit
coordinate of a and a new coordinate where b is a unit. The old value of b
is irrelevant because a vanishes at the new coordinate, giving determinant
+/-1. For the nine-support case at most one new coordinate is doubled,
so such a new unit coordinate always exists. The complete integer plane
and its modular image therefore have the claimed rank and basis.
